1Glossary

Adeles
A compatible list of local entries, one in each completion of a number field, integral at all but finitely many finite places. The diagonal embedding sends one field element to its value in every completion. Read in context
Almost everywhere
A property holds almost everywhere if the set where it fails has measure zero. Two functions in L² can differ on such a set and still represent the same vector. Read in context
Analytic continuation
An extension agreeing with a given analytic function on its original domain. Agreement on an open subset determines the continuation on a connected domain. Read in context
Annihilator in a character group
The characters of a finite abelian group that are identically one on a specified subgroup. Subgroup averaging selects exactly these characters. Read in context
Annihilator in a ring
For t in a commutative ring, Ann(t) is the ideal of all x with tx = 0. In a ring containing η with η² = 0, this ideal can contain η itself. Read in context
Augmentation of the face complex
The homomorphism from integer combinations of vertices to the integers that sends each vertex to one. Its kernel consists of the zero-dimensional cycles that the edges must fill. Read in context
Automorphic function
A function on an adelic group that descends under its rational subgroup and satisfies the prescribed regularity and other conditions. Square-integrability on the quotient matters for the lifts here. Read in context
Bar chain
A finite linear combination of ordered words in a group. Its boundary takes alternating faces obtained by omitting an end entry or multiplying neighboring entries. Read in context
Bernoulli value of a character
A weighted finite sum of Bernoulli polynomial values at fractions of the conductor. The minus estimate clears its denominators and uses the same algebraic integer at every coefficient prime. Read in context
Bockstein map
In the coefficient sequence for reduction modulo q, lift a cycle to an integral chain. Its boundary is a multiple of q. Dividing that boundary by q gives a class one degree lower, killed by q. This map distinguishes the two torsion orders in the cycle count. Read in context
Character / χ
A homomorphism from a finite group to nonzero complex numbers. A Dirichlet character extends by zero off the units modulo N. Read in context
Class group / Cl(K)
Fractional ideals of the number field K modulo principal fractional ideals. The quotient Cl(K)/ℓCl(K) measures the number of independent characters of order ℓ. Read in context
CM field
A number field that is a totally imaginary quadratic extension of a totally real field. Complex conjugation interchanges the two embeddings over any real place of the base. Read in context
Cocycle
A function c satisfying c(gh)=c(g)+g·c(h). Its class in degree one identifies functions differing by g↦g·a−a. Local conditions impose restrictions on these classes. Read in context
Cocycle test on a prism
A linear functional that vanishes on boundaries. For the residue test, consecutive pairs contribute carries and the last entry contributes an additive index. Read in context
Coincidence point
For two maps with a common source and target, a point of the source at which their values agree. For the identity map paired with a self-map, these are the fixed points of the self-map. Read in context
Coinvariants
Given an operator g on a module M, the quotient M/(g−1)M identifies each element with its translate under g. The quotient by gᵈ−1 maps onto it because gᵈ−1 is divisible by g−1. Read in context
Conductor / f
The least divisor of the modulus from which a Dirichlet character is induced. A primitive character has conductor equal to its modulus. Read in context
Covolume
The volume of a fundamental parallelepiped of a lattice in its real span with the chosen metric. Read in context
Covolume of a lattice
The volume of one fundamental parallelepiped, measured in the chosen ambient coordinates. In the complex plane the lattice generated by α and iα has covolume |α|². Read in context
Critical strip / σ
The region 0 < Re s < 1. Here s = σ+it; σ = ½ is the critical line. Read in context
Cyclotomic layer
A stage in a tower formed by adjoining roots of unity. In the ℓ-adic tower here, layer m has relative degree ℓᵐ over the chosen base field. Read in context
Discrete valuation ring
A ring in which each nonzero element has the form uπⁿ for a unit u, a fixed uniformizer π and a nonnegative integer n. This exponent measures divisibility. For ℤℓ one can take π=ℓ. Read in context
Double coset
For subgroups Γ and K of a group, the double coset of h consists of the products γhk with γ in Γ and k in K. Grouping an integral by these classes preserves the measure of each class. Read in context
Doubling integral
An integral obtained by placing two copies of a unitary group in a larger group and pairing a section there with matrix coefficients on the original copies. Its local factors determine the partial L-function in the theta norm. Read in context
Eisenstein polynomial
A monic polynomial whose nonleading coefficients belong to the maximal ideal of the coefficient valuation ring and whose constant coefficient belongs to the first power of that ideal but not its square. In the period construction its image generates the kernel of Fontaine’s map. Read in context
Elementary divisors
A pair of full local lattices can be put into diagonal form by integral changes of bases. The integer exponents of a uniformizer classify their relative position. Their sum gives the determinant valuation and the sum of their absolute values gives the degree in the split-place series. Read in context
Euler’s constant / γ
The limit of 1+1/2+⋯+1/n − log n; it is the constant Laurent coefficient of ζ at 1. Read in context
Field norm and ideal norm
In a Galois extension the field norm multiplies all conjugates of an element. The ideal norm sends a prime above a base prime to the power of the base prime given by the relative residue degree. For a principal ideal these two constructions agree. Read in context
Formal degree
The positive constant in Schur orthogonality for a discrete series and a chosen Haar measure. For a unit vector v it satisfies ∫|⟨v,σ(g)v⟩|² dg = 1/dσ. Read in context
Formal group and formal logarithm
A group law in power series at the identity and a change of coordinate taking that law to addition. Read in context
Frame height
In the symmetric normalization the two integral Frobenius matrices multiply to the Eisenstein polynomial raised to twice the height, times the identity. The height also sets the scalar in the Frobenius equation for the period basis. Read in context
Frame of a regular Gram fibre
An ordered list of independent vectors whose matrix of mutual Hermitian pairings is nonsingular. Its length can be smaller than the ambient dimension. Read in context
Frobenius and inertia
Inertia is the subgroup of a local Galois group acting trivially on the residue field. A Frobenius class acts there by raising to the residue-field size. Read in context
Generalized eigenspace
For an operator T and a scalar λ, this is the space of vectors killed by some power of T − λ. Its dimension counts the whole nilpotent chain at λ, including vectors that are not eigenvectors. Read in context
Genus mass
For a positive definite lattice, sum 1/|Aut(L)| over one representative of each isometry class in its genus. A weighted average of a count divides the corresponding weighted sum by this mass. Read in context
Genus of a Hermitian lattice
The full lattices in a fixed rational Hermitian space that are isometric to a given lattice at every finite place. A genus may contain several global isometry classes. Read in context
Gram matrix
The matrix of pairwise inner products of a list of vectors. Its diagonal records squared lengths. If the vectors are columns of M in orthonormal coordinates, the Gram matrix is M* M. Read in context
Haar measure
A translation-invariant measure on a locally compact group. Its normalization matters when comparing the two sides of the Petersson identity. Read in context
Hecke operator
An operator formed by summing translates indexed by the cosets in a double coset. The operator in the invariant-period comparison divides by the number of cosets, so it fixes constants. Read in context
Homology and cohomology
A cycle is a chain with zero boundary, and its homology class records it up to addition of boundaries. A cohomology class tests cycles by a cocycle, which vanishes on boundaries. Torsion counts and bounds on the dimension over the reals give different information. Read in context
Idèle modulus
The product of the normalized local absolute values of an invertible adèle. The product formula makes it one on each nonzero field element. Read in context
Isogeny
A surjective homomorphism of abelian varieties with finite kernel. Its degree counts the rank of the kernel group scheme. Read in context
Isotropic flag
A nested sequence of subspaces on which every two vectors have Hermitian pairing zero. Its successive increases in dimension are its rank jumps. Read in context
Jacobi sum
For two multiplicative characters of a finite field, extended by zero at zero, sum χ(x)φ(1−x) over the field. In the nondegenerate cyclotomic case the product with the inverse-character sum is the size of the field. Read in context
Level marking
An identification that assigns a specified torsion point to each label at a chosen level. Permuting the torsion points can change the marking. Read in context
Marked root
An eigenvalue chosen by the positive line of the period datum. Its place in the root list specifies which determinant factors carry the form-degree variable. Read in context
Matrix coefficient
The scalar formed by translating one vector by a group element and pairing it with a second vector. When the vectors coincide, its value at the identity is their squared norm. Read in context
Matrix lattice of a representation
The ℤℓ-module of finite integral linear combinations of the group matrices. It contains the identity, and left multiplication by the group preserves it. Read in context
Mellin transform
Here the integral from 1 to infinity against x^(−s−1) dx. Under x=eᵘ it becomes a Laplace transform. Read in context
Module length
Number of simple quotients in a composition series. For a finite ℤℓ-module of order ℓᵈ the length is d. Its minimum number of generators can be smaller. Read in context
Mollifier for an L-function
A short Dirichlet polynomial chosen to cancel the early coefficients of a product with the L-series. Here its weights are μ(d)χ(d), where μ is the Möbius function. A zero forces the product of the two truncated sums to be small. Read in context
Moving frame
An identification of tangent spaces used to compare a form at different points. In the cone of positive Hermitian matrices, the action carrying the identity to a point pulls the form back to a tensor at the identity. Read in context
Multiplicity / m
At an isolated zero ρ, the integer m≥1 such that L(s)=(s−ρ)ᵐh(s) with h holomorphic and h(ρ)≠0. The balance chapter also uses m for a chain length. Read in context
Nerve of a cover
An ordered tuple of cover labels is allowed when their sets share a witness. Deleting a label gives a face. Alternating deletion gives the boundary on integer combinations of tuples. Read in context
Partition of unity
A collection of smooth nonnegative weights that add to one, with only finitely many nonzero in a neighborhood of each point. Here the weights index translates by the arithmetic group. Read in context
Period of an automorphic form
An integral of an automorphic function over a specified quotient, often after restricting the function to a subgroup or pairing it with a second function. The bottom period here restricts a core function to the row group and integrates their product. Read in context
Petersson pairing
The integral of the product of an automorphic function and the complex conjugate of another on the automorphic quotient, with the chosen measure. On the diagonal it gives a squared norm. Read in context
Poincaré series
A sum of translates of a test function over a discrete group or a coset space. The sum used to separate a row has an identity contribution large enough to dominate the sum of the other contributions after unfolding its period. Read in context
Pole and residue
The residue is the coefficient of (s−ρ)⁻¹ in a Laurent expansion. A simple pole has a nonzero residue and no higher-order singular terms. Read in context
Positive part / u₊
The number max(u,0). In a ramp it cuts off the sum; in the window estimate it keeps the side controlled by the hypothesis. Read in context
Projective module
A module that is a direct summand of a free module. A relation solved in the free module can be projected back to the summand. Read in context
Ramification index
The multiplicity of a prime in the extended prime ideal. It enters the weighting of the finite-place contributions to divisibility. Read in context
Regulator
The volume spanned by logarithms of a basis of free units after deleting one place coordinate. The complex coordinates use log |z|². In a field with unit rank zero this volume is one. Read in context
Relative normalization
For a given generic cover, take all functions on the cover integral over each affine open of the base. Their rings define the normal model used for the source of the correspondence. Read in context
Residue degree
The degree of an extension of residue fields. In an unramified quadratic extension, a split base prime has two primes of residue degree one. An inert prime has one of degree two. Read in context
Restricted product
A family of local coordinates integral at all but finitely many finite places. For the multiplicative group, both a coordinate and its inverse must be integral there. Read in context
Rigidification of a line bundle
A chosen trivialization on a specified subscheme, often the zero section of a group scheme. On a square the polarization bundle here has a trivialization on each zero axis. Read in context
Satake parameters
The unramified eigenvalue data that encode the action of the spherical Hecke operators at one place. The normalization used here specifies their powers of the residue-field size in the local Euler factors. Read in context
Saturation of a lattice
The saturation of Λ in E is the set of x in E such that kx lies in Λ for some nonzero integer k. Modulo Λ it gives exactly the torsion subgroup of E/Λ. Read in context
Schur polynomial
The symmetric polynomial attached to a partition. Its product with the Vandermonde determinant is the alternant with row exponents shifted by that partition. Read in context
Schwartz function
A smooth function on a real vector space whose derivatives of every order decay faster than every inverse power of the norm. A Gaussian is an example. Read in context
Self-dual Hermitian lattice
A full lattice L consisting exactly of the vectors whose Hermitian pairings with L are integral. This condition makes integral adjoints available in the construction of a frame. Read in context
Semisimple representation
A direct sum of irreducible representations. One can compare multiplicities constituent by constituent once an isomorphism of the global representations has been established. Read in context
Sha-type local kernel
The subgroup of degree-one Galois cohomology classes whose restrictions at the specified places vanish. Its module length enters the cohomological window bound. Read in context
Shapiro’s isomorphism
Homology with an induced coefficient module can be computed in the subgroup. Here the module of functions on a finite cyclic quotient turns a homology calculation for G into one for the kernel. Read in context
Siegel parabolic
The subgroup preserving a maximal isotropic subspace in the doubled Hermitian space. Its rational cosets index the Eisenstein series used to identify the normalized theta mean. Read in context
Signature of a real unit
The signs of a unit at all real embeddings. Recording a negative value as one and a positive value as zero turns multiplication of units into addition of bit vectors. Read in context
Similitude
An invertible linear map that multiplies a Hermitian form by a single nonzero scalar. It sends the sphere of one length to the sphere of the scaled length. Read in context
Slope
For a Euclidean lattice of positive rank, its Arakelov slope is the negative log of its covolume divided by its rank. Extremal slopes measure sublattices and quotients. Read in context
Spherical data
At a good finite place the vectors are fixed by the specified maximal compact subgroup. This invariance permits the local integral to be evaluated by Hecke eigenvalues. Read in context
Split sequence
An ordered list of vectors vᵢ with specified linear functionals λᵢ satisfying λᵢ(vⱼ)=1 when i=j and 0 otherwise. Deleting one pair gives a shorter sequence. Read in context
Stabilizer
The subgroup of symmetries that take an object to itself. In the arithmetic face count a finite stabilizer acts faithfully on a finite set of shortest vectors. Its order contributes to the bound for integral homology. Read in context
Stickelberger element
An element of a group ring acting on ideal classes in cyclotomic arithmetic. Its annihilation property supplies a relation on a quotient of the class group in the tower argument. Read in context
Successive minima
For a lattice in a Euclidean space, the jth successive minimum is the least radius of a ball about zero containing j real-linearly independent lattice vectors. In the number-field row argument, independence over the field requires taking the radii in blocks. Read in context
Target slice
For a kernel K(x,y) and a fixed source point x, the function y ↦ K(x,y) on the target. Its square-integral as x varies controls the change of integration order in the theta pairing. Read in context
Tate twist
A change in a Galois action by a power of the cyclotomic character, which records the action on roots of unity. Its exponent matters in a local restriction kernel. Read in context
Theta lift
An integral operator with a theta kernel carrying an automorphic function between spaces for two groups. Read in context
Thick part
The region of positive Hermitian forms where both the primal and dual lattice minima are at least a fixed positive constant. A compact set meets every arithmetic orbit in this region. Read in context
Trace-dual basis
For a finite separable extension, bases (uᵢ) and (vⱼ) are trace-dual when Tr(uᵢvⱼ) is 1 for i=j and 0 otherwise. Then x=∑ᵢ Tr(xuᵢ)vᵢ. Clearing both bases at once produces the split residue calculation. Read in context
Translated trace
For a matrix X and a group representation r, the measurement tr(Xr(g)). Varying g can detect coordinates invisible to tr(X) itself. Read in context
Valuation / vₚ
The exponent of p in a nonzero rational number, extended by vₚ(0)=+∞. The normalized p-adic absolute value is p to the power −vₚ. Read in context
Von Mangoldt function / Λ
Λ(pʲ)=log p for a prime p and integer j≥1; its value at every other positive integer is zero. Read in context
Whittaker coefficient
A Fourier coefficient obtained by integrating along a unipotent matrix variable against an additive character. In the Siegel–Weil calculation, a nonsingular Hermitian matrix specifies the character. At the special parameter the coefficient reads the density of tuples with that Gram matrix. Read in context
Window / Y, U, δ
An interval [Y,Y¹⁺ᵟ] with δ>0; in logarithmic coordinates its endpoints are U=log Y and (1+δ)U. Read in context
Witt index
Largest dimension of a subspace on which the entire Hermitian pairing vanishes. It determines which convergence hypothesis is required for the doubled theta integral. Read in context

2Theorems used in the book

  1. The pole of the zeta function

    The series for ζ(s) on Re s > 1 extends meromorphically to the plane. At its only pole the expansion begins ζ(s) = 1/(s−1) + γ + O(s−1), where γ is Euler’s constant.

    Taking the logarithmic derivative of this expansion specifies the two centering terms in the smoothed prime sum.

    T. M. Apostol, NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, §25.2(i) and (ii), especially (25.2.4).

    Used in From a series to a function · Why the correction terms have these signs · How is the principal character detected?

  2. Euler product for a Dirichlet character

    If χ is a Dirichlet character and Re s > 1, then L(s,χ) is the absolutely convergent product over primes p of (1−χ(p)p⁻ˢ)⁻¹. A prime dividing the modulus contributes the factor 1.

    Taking the logarithmic derivative turns each Euler factor into a sum over powers of its prime. That sum supplies the weights in the Mellin transform.

    T. M. Apostol, NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, (25.15.2).

    Used in From one ramp to a logarithmic derivative · Which disk controls a Dirichlet character at height t? · Why are there exactly J possible blocks?

  3. Dirichlet nonvanishing at one

    For every nonprincipal Dirichlet character χ the continuation of L(s,χ) is holomorphic at 1 and L(1,χ) is nonzero.

    This makes L′(1,χ)/L(1,χ) a finite centering constant. The principal character requires the separate pole calculation.

    T. M. Apostol, NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, §25.15(i) and (25.15.9).

    Used in Why the correction terms have these signs · Reflect from zero to one. · Why are there exactly J possible blocks?

  4. Removing Euler factors at a larger modulus

    If χ modulo q is induced by χ₀ of conductor d, then L(s,χ) equals L(s,χ₀) times the product of 1−χ₀(p)p⁻ˢ for p dividing q. The identity holds meromorphically.

    At Re s > 0 every added factor is nonzero, so changing level preserves zeros there.

    T. M. Apostol, NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, (25.15.4).

    Used in Removing an imprimitive level

  5. Reflection for a primitive Dirichlet character

    For primitive χ of conductor q, put a=0 or 1 according as χ(−1)=1 or −1. The completed function Λ(s,χ)=(q/π)^((s+a)/2)Γ((s+a)/2)L(s,χ) satisfies Λ(s,χ)=εχΛ(1−s,χ̄), where |εχ|=1.

    In the open critical strip the gamma factors are finite and nonzero. A zero on the left therefore gives a zero for the conjugate character on the right.

    T. M. Apostol, NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, §25.15(i), primitive functional equation (25.15.5)–(25.15.6).

    Used in The strip theorem for characters · Reflect from zero to one.

  6. The boundary of the critical strip

    The only zeros of ζ outside 0 < Re s < 1 are the negative even integers. In particular there are no zeros on Re s = 1.

    Once both open halves of the strip have been handled, this identifies the exceptions in the classical statement about all zeros.

    T. M. Apostol, NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, §25.10(i).

    Used in Which zeros? · Why classical RH excludes the negative even integers

  7. Changing the order of integration

    On two σ-finite measure spaces a nonnegative measurable function has the same integral in either order, possibly infinite. For a complex measurable function, finiteness of the integral of its absolute value makes both iterated integrals exist almost everywhere and gives the same value. One may take the completed measures.

    In the Mellin identity one variable has counting measure. In the theta integral a majorant must first control two noncompact variables.

    Terence Tao, An Introduction to Measure Theory, §1.7, Theorem 1.7.18 and Corollary 1.7.23.

  8. Cauchy’s estimate for derivatives

    Suppose g is holomorphic on a neighborhood of the closed disk of radius R about σ, with |g| ≤ M on the circle. For each k ≥ 0, |g⁽ᵏ⁾(σ)|/k! ≤ M/Rᵏ. This follows by bounding the integrand in Cauchy’s derivative formula and multiplying by the circumference.

    Subtracting the principal part of an isolated pole gives such a g. The estimate quantifies the advantage of the pole when its distance from σ is smaller than R.

    NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, §1.9, Cauchy’s integral formula for derivatives, (1.9.31).

    Used in Two incompatible derivative rates · How does a real zero push other zeros away from 1? · How much of a logarithmic derivative comes from nearby zeros? · Why do three different terms appear in the prime moment?

  9. Uniqueness of analytic continuation

    Two holomorphic functions on a connected open set agree throughout it if they agree on a set having a limit point there. For meromorphic functions the same conclusion follows by removing their poles locally.

    This carries an equality from the half-plane where an integral converges into the region where the continued coefficients are evaluated.

    NIST Digital Library of Mathematical Functions, §1.10(ii), Analytic Continuation.

    Used in One determinant derivative moves the convergence line. · Removing an imprimitive level · From a series to a function · Why is the kernel smooth near every point of the group?

  10. Legendre’s formula for a factorial

    For a prime p and integer n ≥ 0, the exponent of p in n! is ∑ⱼ≥₁ ⌊n/pʲ⌋. Each integer from 1 to n contributes once for each power of p that divides it.

    This is the valuation gained when one passes from a Taylor coefficient to a derivative.

    Gennady Eremin, Legendre’s formula and p-adic analysis, §1, formula (1).

    Used in Proving the one-variable estimate · Why is the von Mangoldt sum at most three times the cutoff?

  11. Orthogonality of finite characters

    On a finite abelian group the average of a character is 1 for the trivial character and 0 otherwise. Indeed, translating the sum by a group element multiplies it by the value of the character at that element.

    Applying the identity to a subgroup retains exactly the characters trivial on that subgroup in the arithmetic balance. On a four-dimensional residue space the same identity turns the fibre equation into an additive-character sum.

    Keith Conrad, Characters of Finite Abelian Groups, Theorem 4.1 and Corollary 4.2.

  12. Orbit–stabilizer counting

    When a finite group G acts on a set, the orbit of x is in bijection with the cosets of its stabilizer. In particular the orbit size divided by |G| equals 1/|Stab(x)|.

    The same pairs can be counted by first fixing a lattice or by first fixing a marked subspace. The reciprocal stabilizer order is the weight in the second count.

    Keith Conrad, Group Actions, Theorem 3.16.

    Used in Why average over a genus? · Proof for two factors of rank at least two. · Why mark a maximal lattice before counting smaller ones? · What does the rank of convolution say about the sum of its coefficients?

  13. Product formula in a number field

    For nonzero a in a number field, multiply |a|ᵥ over all places, using the normalized absolute values, with the complex modulus squared at a complex place. The product equals 1.

    The scalar and determinant in a rational similitude contribute cancelling local Jacobians over all places.

    J. S. Milne, Algebraic Number Theory, Theorem 7.15.

    Used in Compute what happens on a fibre. · Why is the global normalization one? · Why a near term has exactly the traces of the identity

  14. Local classification determines a global Hermitian space

    Let E/F be quadratic over number fields. Two nondegenerate Hermitian spaces over E of the same rank are isometric if their localizations are isometric everywhere. At nonsplit finite places the determinant modulo local norms distinguishes the two classes of a given positive rank. At real places becoming complex one uses the whole signature. For a global space the product of the local determinant signs is +1.

    To represent a given nonzero length in a plane, compare it with a diagonal plane whose first vector has the prescribed length. An isometry carries that vector into the given plane. For a definite subspace this classification identifies the rational space after adjoining one determinant line. The product condition also rules out a single nontrivial inert determinant sign.

    Benedict H. Gross, Incoherent definite spaces and Shimura varieties, §3, especially Theorem 3.1.

    Used in Which lengths reach the counting theorem? · From the local lattices to a marked class · Which ideal records the isometry class of a rational subspace? · How many rational subspace orbits can one discriminant order require?

  15. Hasse’s norm theorem

    Let L/K be a cyclic extension of number fields. An element of K× that is a norm at every completion is the norm of an element of L×.

    In the dyadic argument the extension is quadratic. A choice of real signs supplies the norm condition at infinity and the ideal calculation supplies it at the finite places.

    J. S. Milne, Class Field Theory, Chapter VIII, Theorem 3.1.

    Used in How many sign patterns can a dyadic class miss? · Which lengths reach the counting theorem? · From the local lattices to a marked class

  16. Kronecker’s criterion for a root of unity

    A nonzero algebraic integer whose conjugates all have absolute value at most 1 is a root of unity. Its powers have bounded degree and bounded coefficients in their monic minimal polynomials, so two powers coincide.

    A quotient of conjugate generators is a unit. Checking its complex absolute values now restricts the ambiguity to roots of unity.

    J. S. Milne, Algebraic Number Theory, Proposition 5.5 and Corollary 5.6.

    Used in How many sign patterns can a dyadic class miss? · Which ideal does the cyclotomic element generate? · Where does the extra factor ℓ come from?

  17. The logarithmic lattice of units

    In a number field with r real places and s complex places, the logarithms of the units form a full lattice of rank r+s−1 in the product-formula hyperplane. The kernel is the finite group of roots of unity.

    The lattice gives a compact torus when integrating along the center. Its rank also determines how many independent unit directions a determinant can measure.

    J. S. Milne, Algebraic Number Theory, Theorem 5.9 and the preceding description of the logarithmic map.

  18. The S-unit theorem

    Let S be a finite set of places of a number field containing all its infinite places. The elements with valuation zero outside S form a finitely generated group. Modulo roots of unity it is free abelian of rank |S|−1.

    Allowing denominators at primes above ℓ adds one free direction for each such prime in the generator estimate.

    Paul Fili and Zachary Miner, A generalization of Dirichlet’s S-unit theorem, §1, statement of the S-unit theorem.

    Used in Counting the generators before bounding the length

  19. Hilbert’s Theorem 90

    In a cyclic field extension F/K generated by γ, an element v of norm one has the form z/γ(z) for some z in F×.

    After two twisted-norm classes yield the same finite data, their ratio has norm one. Writing that ratio as a coboundary gives the descent used in the kernel count.

    J. S. Milne, Class Field Theory, Chapter II, Corollary 1.23.

    Used in What the detecting cycles can miss · How does the minus class count control the plus classes?

  20. Artin reciprocity and the existence of class fields

    Fix a modulus 𝔪. Let I be the fractional ideals prime to 𝔪, and P the principal ideals whose generators satisfy its congruences and real sign conditions. For every subgroup H with P ⊆ H ⊆ I there is a class field L/K whose Artin map induces I/H ≃ Gal(L/K). A prime outside 𝔪 maps to arithmetic Frobenius.

    For the ordinary ideal class group this gives the Hilbert class field. Prime-ideal classes can therefore be prescribed by choosing Frobenius elements.

    J. S. Milne, Class Field Theory, Chapter V, Theorems 3.5 and 3.6.
