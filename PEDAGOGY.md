# Reading the proof without the formal source

Three parallel readings on 21 November 2026 tested whether a reader with undergraduate analysis and linear algebra could recover specific implications. Each reader followed a different argument through the existing chapters. We checked the proposed changes against the statements they use. This was an editorial audit by agents. Testing with students and external mathematical review remain open.

## Reading the minor count and the dimension bound

| Reading task | What the readers checked | Change in the book |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reproduce the count without opening the source | Several terms can have the same list. At several places, bins for the individual sizes determine an interval for the global size. | The text pays for the terms with the same list once, bounds the number of distinct lists for each profile, and counts the possible profiles for a given global size. [Count by size](book.html#global-minor-label-shells) |
| Count the first two shells by hand | The origin can occur as a zero entry in a list. In the one-entry experiment the minor is required to be nonzero. Points on a circle of radius two belong to the second shell. | The worked example gives 45 − 9 = 36 in the second shell. Enumeration by squared norms and by coordinate symmetries reproduced all six counts and weights. [Gaussian example](book.html#fig-minor-shells) |
| Explain what near-orthogonality says about dimension | The same columns can sit in a larger target by appending zero rows. A one-word Gram bound controls the kernel. Combining words requires a separate estimate for their overlaps and an upper bound for all rows. | A coordinate map losing one of eight directions illustrates the first inequality. The full proof tracks the two traces, then the columns lost at the ramified level. [Kernel](book.html#global-minor-dimension) · [Common image](book.html#global-minor-frame-rank) · [Retained columns](book.html#global-minor-retained-rank) |

Two further readings checked the constants and the row spaces independently. Direct multiplication of a two-word complex matrix gave tr F = 5 and tr(F²) = 17, falling to 3 and 7 after one column was removed. The scalar comparisons were also checked at σ = 1/4 and at (σ, √ρ) = (1/20, 1/2). The text identifies the full automorphic target at both stages and describes the final projection onto the arithmetic rows before the slope count.

## Priorities for the next chapters

1. **Open the analytic estimates for the ambient dimension.** The trace argument compares the full dimension with the identity mass. Give the reader a derivation of the near and far orbital bounds and of the concentration estimate on the deep region. [Projection trace](book.html#global-relative-count)
2. **Derive the integral annihilation of ideal classes.** A surviving coefficient modulo ℓ gives a power relation. To make that relation act on classes one also uses an integral annihilator. Its proof should identify the element, the module it acts on, and the precise step that kills the classes. [Application to the class group](book.html#inputs-descent-class)
3. **Review every bridge with its hypotheses in view.** At the next difficult step, a reader should be able to point to the object being bounded, reproduce one small case, and name the result that licenses the transition. For example, the six and forty-eight Gaussian planes illustrate exact determinant counts, whereas the uniform genus estimate uses the split prime and the mass of the entire genus. [Higher-corank theorem and example](book.html#global-higher-corank-mean)
