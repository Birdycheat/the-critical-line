Local Galois cohomology finiteness and cohomological dimension two over local fields

Why do all Galois cohomology groups of a finite discrete module over a nonarchimedean local field stay finite and vanish identically from degree three onward, and why does this hold over every finite extension K of ℚℓ even when the module’s order is a power of the residue characteristic ℓ? Whenever a finite discrete module M over the absolute Galois group GK = Gal(Ksep/K) has cardinality |M| invertible in K (so char(K) ∤ |M|), multiplication by n = |M| annihilates both M and its Cartier dual M∗ = Hom(M, (Ksep)×). Local Galois finiteness and local cohomological dimension two apply uniformly to every n-torsion module with n ≠ 0 in K, yielding finiteness in every degree and vanishing in all degrees t ≥ 3. In particular, every finite extension K/ℚℓ has characteristic 0, so |M| ≠ 0 in K holds unconditionally—even when |M| is a power of ℓ—supplying the local finiteness input to local Tate duality and the integral localization-kernel criterion.

1. Local finiteness and cohomological dimension two. Let K be a nonarchimedean local field with separable closure Ksep and absolute Galois group GK = Gal(Ksep/K). For a finite discrete GK-module M, define its Cartier dual M∗ = Hom(M, (Ksep)×) with the conjugate Galois action (g · f)(m) = g(f(g−1 · m)). For a prime ℓ, a finite extension K of ℚℓ inside an algebraic closure ℚ̄ℓ, and an integer r ≥ 1, let M be a finite discrete ℤ/ℓrℤ-module carrying a continuous representation ρ of GK.

Proposition (Local finiteness and vanishing above degree two).

(i) If |M| ≠ 0 in K, then for every degree t ≥ 0 both Ht(GK, M) and Ht(GK, M∗) are finite, and

|Ht(GK, M)| < ∞,   |Ht(GK, M∗)| < ∞   (∀ t ≥ 0),   Ht(GK, M) = 0   (∀ t ≥ 3).

(ii) Over any finite extension K of ℚℓ inside ℚ̄ℓ, for every r ≥ 1 and every continuous finite discrete ℤ/ℓrℤ-representation (M, ρ) of GK, the groups H0(GK, M), H1(GK, M), and H2(GK, M) are finite, and Hn(GK, M) = 0 for every n ≥ 3.

Proof. For (i), set n = |M| ≥ 1. By Lagrange’s theorem in the finite abelian group M, n · m = 0 for every m ∈ M. Because n ≠ 0 in K, the top-level local Galois finiteness theorem and the local cohomological dimension bound for n-torsion modules apply directly to M, giving finiteness of Ht(GK, M) for every t ≥ 0 and vanishing Ht(GK, M) = 0 for every t ≥ 3. For the Cartier dual M∗ = Hom(M, (Ksep)×), writing the target group additively, any homomorphism f : M → (Ksep)× satisfies (n · f)(m) = f(n · m) = f(0) = 0 for all m ∈ M, so n · f = 0 on all of M∗. Applying top-level local finiteness to M∗ with the same integer n proves that Ht(GK, M∗) is finite for all t ≥ 0.

For (ii), every finite extension K of ℚℓ inside ℚ̄ℓ inherits a unique nonarchimedean local-field valuation and topology extending that of ℚℓ, with separable closure ℚ̄ℓ (since char(K) = 0). Transporting the continuous ℤ/ℓrℤ-representation (M, ρ) to a finite discrete ℤ[GK]-module A preserves cardinality |A| = |M| ≥ 1. Because K has characteristic 0, the natural number |A| maps to a nonzero element of K regardless of whether ℓ divides |M|. Applying (i) to A in degrees 0, 1, 2 and in all degrees n ≥ 3, then transporting along the degree-by-degree cohomology equivalences between A and (M, ρ) (detailed below), yields finiteness of H0, H1, H2 and vanishing of Hn for all n ≥ 3.

Representation transport over finite extensions of ℚℓ and an explicit check on ℚℓ

In (ii), the valuation extension from ℚℓ to a finite intermediate field K ⊂ ℚ̄ℓ endows K with a nonarchimedean local-field structure whose residue ring satisfies |𝒪K/ℓm𝒪K| = ℓm[K:ℚℓ] and makes ℚ̄ℓ a separable closure of K. The continuous action of GK on the discrete finite ℤ/ℓrℤ-module M corresponds via an additive equivariant isomorphism e : M ≃ A to a finite discrete ℤ[GK]-representation A, inducing additive equivalences Hn(GK, M) ≃ Hn(GK, A) in every degree n ≥ 0 (in particular for n = 0, 1, 2 and all n ≥ 3). Both finiteness and subsingleton (vanishing) properties transfer directly across these bijections.

As a concrete check over K = ℚℓ with trivial module M = ℤ/ℓℤ: when ℓ is odd, H0(Gℚℓ, ℤ/ℓℤ) = ℤ/ℓℤ has order ℓ, local class field theory identifies H1(Gℚℓ, ℤ/ℓℤ) = Homcont(Gℚℓ, ℤ/ℓℤ) ≅ Hom(ℚℓ×/(ℚℓ×)ℓ, ℤ/ℓℤ) of order ℓ2 (since ℚℓ×/(ℚℓ×)ℓ ≅ (ℤ/ℓℤ)2 is generated by ℓ and 1 + ℓ), and H2(Gℚℓ, ℤ/ℓℤ) is dual to μℓGℚℓ = {1} (as ℓ − 1 is coprime to ℓ, so ℚℓ contains no primitive ℓ-th root of unity) and therefore has order 1, while Hn = 0 for all n ≥ 3. When ℓ = 2, ℚ2×/(ℚ2×)2 ≅ (ℤ/2ℤ)3 (generated by −1, 2, 5) gives |H1| = 8, |H0| = 2, and μ2 = {±1} ⊂ ℚ2 gives |H2| = 2, with Hn = 0 for n ≥ 3. In both cases Tate’s local Euler characteristic relation |H1| = |H0| · |H2| · |M|[K:ℚℓ] holds (ℓ2 = ℓ · 1 · ℓ and 8 = 2 · 2 · 2).

Local Tate duality for finite Galois modules via the Brauer invariant and Cartier pairing

Why do the cup-product pairings between a finite discrete Galois module M and its Cartier dual M∗ in complementary degrees r + s = 2 identify each local cohomology group canonically with the Pontryagin dual of the other, and how does this descend to the ℤ/ℓrℤ-valued twisted-dual pairings used in bounding the local degree-two exponent? Set n = |M|. Every homomorphism M → (Ksep)× lands inside the n-th roots of unity μn(Ksep), giving an equivariant isomorphism Hom(M, μn) ≅ M∗ that intertwines the evaluation pairing into μn with the Cartier pairing into (Ksep)×. On degree-two cohomology, the local Brauer invariant invK : H2(GK, (Ksep)×) → ℚ/ℤ agrees on the image of H2(GK, μn) with the ℤ/nℤ-valued local class-formation invariant followed by the embedding ℤ/nℤ ↪ ℚ/ℤ. Naturality of cup products therefore translates local class-formation duality directly into bijectivity of the Cartier pairing, while finiteness from local finiteness yields bijectivity in the reversed variable as well. Over a finite extension K/ℚℓ, identifying μℓr with ℤ/ℓrℤ(1) transports all four cup-product arrows in degrees (0, 2), (1, 1), and (2, 0) into the twisted-dual local Tate duality interface.

1. Cartier duality and cyclotomic twisted-dual cup products. Let K be a nonarchimedean local field with separable closure Ksep, M a finite discrete GK-module with |M| ≠ 0 in K, and M∗ = Hom(M, (Ksep)×) its Cartier dual. For r, s ≥ 0 with r + s = 2, let ⟨·, ·⟩loc : Hr(GK, M) × Hs(GK, M∗) → ℚ/ℤ be the pairing obtained by cupping along M ⊗ M∗ → (Ksep)× and applying the local Brauer invariant invK : H2(GK, (Ksep)×) → ℚ/ℤ. For a prime ℓ, a finite extension K/ℚℓ inside ℚ̄ℓ, r ≥ 1, and a continuous finite discrete ℤ/ℓrℤ-representation (M, ρ) of GK, write ℤ/ℓrℤ(1) for ℤ/ℓrℤ with the mod-ℓr cyclotomic character action χcyc and M(1)∨ = Homℤ/ℓrℤ(M, ℤ/ℓrℤ(1)) for its twisted dual.

Proposition (Local Tate duality).

(i) Cartier pairing over nonarchimedean local fields. For every r, s ≥ 0 with r + s = 2, both adjoint maps of the local duality pairing are bijective:

Hr(GK, M) ≅ Hom(Hs(GK, M∗), ℚ/ℤ),   Hs(GK, M∗) ≅ Hom(Hr(GK, M), ℚ/ℤ).

(ii) Cyclotomic twisted-dual cup products over finite extensions of ℚℓ. For every finite extension K/ℚℓ inside ℚ̄ℓ, r ≥ 1, and continuous finite discrete ℤ/ℓrℤ-representation M of GK, there is a ℤ/ℓrℤ-linear isomorphism H2(GK, ℤ/ℓrℤ(1)) ≅ ℤ/ℓrℤ, and the cup-product pairings induce bijections

Hi(GK, M) ≅ Homℤ/ℓrℤ(H2−i(GK, M(1)∨), H2(GK, ℤ/ℓrℤ(1)))   (i = 0, 1, 2),

together with the bijective flipped pairing in degree (1, 1).

Proof. For (i), set n = |M| ≥ 1, so n · m = 0 for all m ∈ M and n ≠ 0 in K. Every homomorphism f : M → (Ksep)× takes values in the n-torsion subgroup μn(Ksep), producing an isomorphism of discrete GK-modules φ : Hom(M, μn) ≃ M∗. On tensor products, composing 1M ⊗ φ with the Cartier evaluation pairing M ⊗ M∗ → (Ksep)× coincides identically with the μn-evaluation pairing M ⊗ Hom(M, μn) → μn followed by the inclusion ι : μn ↪ (Ksep)×. In degree 2, the local Brauer invariant invK composed with H2(ι) : H2(GK, μn) → H2(GK, (Ksep)×) equals the canonical embedding εn : ℤ/nℤ ↪ ℚ/ℤ composed with the local class-formation invariant ttop : H2(GK, μn) → ℤ/nℤ. By naturality of cup products, for all x ∈ Hr(GK, M) and y ∈ Hs(GK, Hom(M, μn)),

⟨x, Hs(φ)(y)⟩loc = εn(ttop(x ∪ y)).

Because n kills Hom(M, μn), n also kills Hs(GK, Hom(M, μn)), so every additive character into ℚ/ℤ lands in (ℚ/ℤ)[n] = im(εn). Since the local class-formation duality map x ↦ (y ↦ ttop(x ∪ y)) from Hr(GK, M) to Hom(Hs(GK, Hom(M, μn)), ℤ/nℤ) is bijective, transporting along the isomorphism Hs(φ) and the injection εn (detailed below) establishes bijectivity of Hr(GK, M) → Hom(Hs(GK, M∗), ℚ/ℤ). For the flipped pairing, note that μn and hence Hom(M, μn) are finite and killed by n, so local finiteness ensures finiteness of Hs(GK, Hom(M, μn)); flipping the bijective finite ℤ/nℤ-pairing and transporting via εn and Hs(φ) proves bijectivity of Hs(GK, M∗) → Hom(Hr(GK, M), ℚ/ℤ).

For (ii), over any finite extension K/ℚℓ inside ℚ̄ℓ, char(K) = 0 guarantees ℓr ≠ 0 in K, and choosing a primitive ℓr-th root of unity in ℚ̄ℓ yields an equivariant isomorphism ℤ/ℓrℤ(1) ≃ μℓr(ℚ̄ℓ) matching the cyclotomic character χcyc mod ℓr. The local class-formation invariant ttop : H2(GK, μℓr) ≃ ℤ/ℓrℤ therefore induces a ℤ/ℓrℤ-linear isomorphism H2(GK, ℤ/ℓrℤ(1)) ≃ ℤ/ℓrℤ. Transporting (M, ρ) to a finite discrete ℓr-torsion ℤ[GK]-module A identifies M(1)∨ with Hom(A, μℓr) and M with the double dual Hom(Hom(A, μℓr), μℓr). Applying local class-formation duality to A gives bijectivity of the (0, 2) and (2, 0) cup products; applying it to Hom(A, μℓr) gives bijectivity of the (1, 1) cup product, and finiteness of H1(GK, M) from local finiteness yields bijectivity of the flipped (1, 1) pairing.

Transport along ℤ/nℤ ↪ ℚ/ℤ, flipped finite pairings, and verification on ℚ3

To verify the algebraic transport lemma used in (i): suppose β : X → Hom(Y, ℤ/nℤ) is bijective, γ : X′ → Hom(Y′, ℚ/ℤ), e1 : X ≃ X′ and e2 : Y ≃ Y′ are additive isomorphisms, n · Y = 0, and γ(e1(x))(e2(y)) = εn(β(x)(y)). Injectivity of γ follows immediately from injectivity of εn and β. For surjectivity, given any homomorphism χ : Y′ → ℚ/ℤ, n · Y = 0 forces n · (χ ∘ e2) = 0, so χ ∘ e2 factors uniquely as εn ∘ ψ for some ψ : Y → ℤ/nℤ; picking x ∈ X with β(x) = ψ gives γ(e1(x)) = χ. Likewise, for a finite group Y killed by n, bijectivity of β : X → Hom(Y, ℤ/nℤ) implies |X| = |Hom(Y, ℤ/nℤ)| = |Y| and nondegeneracy in the second variable (a nonzero y ∈ Y admits a character Y → ℤ/nℤ not killing y, hence some x with β(x)(y) ≠ 0), so the flipped map Y → Hom(X, ℤ/nℤ) is injective between finite sets of equal cardinality and therefore bijective.

As a concrete check of (ii), take K = ℚ3, ℓ = 3, r = 1, and trivial module M = ℤ/3ℤ, so M(1)∨ = μ3 (with nontrivial Galois action via χcyc mod 3, of order 2). Here |H0(Gℚ3, ℤ/3ℤ)| = 3 matches |H2(Gℚ3, μ3)| = |ℤ/3ℤ| = 3; |H2(Gℚ3, ℤ/3ℤ)| = 1 matches |H0(Gℚ3, μ3)| = 1 (since x2 + x + 1 has discriminant −3 of odd 3-adic valuation and is irreducible over ℚ3); and in degree (1, 1), Kummer theory identifies H1(Gℚ3, μ3) ≅ ℚ3×/(ℚ3×)3 ≅ (ℤ/3ℤ)2 (order 9), pairing nondegenerately via the cubic Hilbert norm residue symbol with H1(Gℚ3, ℤ/3ℤ) = Hom(ℚ3×/(ℚ3×)3, ℤ/3ℤ) (order 9).

Poitou–Tate global duality, Shafarevich–Tate pairing, nine-term exact sequence, and conjugation compatibility

How do global Galois cohomology groups unramified outside a finite set of places S interlock with the product of local cohomology groups at S, why do the locally trivial kernels Ш1 and Ш2 pair perfectly with each other, and why does every normalized family of local invariants automatically respect field automorphisms of the ambient algebraic closure? When S contains all primes dividing a positive integer N and either N is odd or the number field K is totally complex (eliminating 2-torsion obstructions at real archimedean places), reducing along prime-power factors of N combines global Artin reciprocity with local Tate duality to produce compact-support duality in degrees r + s = 3, finite Pontryagin duality between Ш1S(K, M) and Ш2S(K, M′), and the nine-term Poitou–Tate exact sequence—precisely the finite-level global duality driving the integral degree-two length balance. Moreover, once local invariants invA : H2(DA, μℓr) ≃ ℤ/ℓrℤ are normalized by sending Kummer–unramified cup products of local uniformizers to 1, conjugating by any automorphism σ of Ω preserving K transports uniformizers, decomposition groups, inertia, and Frobenius identically from A to A′, forcing conjugation compatibility automatically.

1. Global duality, nine-term exactness, and conjugation compatibility of normalized invariants. Let K be a number field inside an algebraic closure Ω (or ℚ̄), S a finite set of finite places of K containing all primes dividing a positive integer N, and assume that either N is odd or K is totally complex. Write GK,S for the Galois group of the maximal extension of K in Ω unramified outside S, fix a prolongation (valuation subring Av ⊂ Ω with decomposition group Dv and inertia Iv) above each v ∈ S, and let M be a finite discrete GK,S-module killed by N with dual M′ = Hom(M, μN). Define the locally trivial kernels ШiS(K, M) = ker(Hi(GK,S, M) → ∏v∈S Hi(Kv, M)) and the Pontryagin dual X∨ = Hom(X, ℚ/ℤ). When N = ℓr (ℓ prime, r ≥ 1, with ℓ ≠ 2 or K totally complex) and ζ ∈ Ω is a primitive ℓr-th root of unity, call a family of bijective ℤ/ℓrℤ-linear local invariants invA : H2(DA, μℓr) ≃ ℤ/ℓrℤ (indexed by valuation subrings A ⊂ Ω over finite places v of K) ζ-normalized if for every local uniformizer ϖ ∈ 𝔭v ∖ 𝔭v2, every root yℓr = ϖ with Kummer cocycle g(y) = ζκ(g)y, and every unramified homomorphism χ : DA → ℤ/ℓrℤ vanishing on IA and sending arithmetic Frobenius (for residue size Nv) to 1, the cup cocycle z(g, h) = κ(g)χ(h) satisfies invA([z]) = 1.

Proposition (Poitou–Tate global duality, nine-term exact sequence, and automatic conjugation compatibility).

(i) Compact-support and Shafarevich–Tate duality. For every r, s ≥ 0 with r + s = 3, compact-support duality gives a bijection Hrc(GK,S, M) ≅ Hs(GK,S, M′)∨. Both Ш1S(K, M) and Ш2S(K, M′) are finite, and the global Shafarevich–Tate pairing is bijective in both directions:

Ш1S(K, M) ≅ Hom(Ш2S(K, M′), ℚ/ℤ),   Ш2S(K, M′) ≅ Hom(Ш1S(K, M), ℚ/ℤ).

(ii) Nine-term exact sequence. The global-to-local restriction maps, local Tate duals of restriction for M′, and connecting transgression homomorphisms form an exact nine-term sequence:

0 → H0(GK,S, M) → ∏v∈S H0(Kv, M) → H2(GK,S, M′)∨ → H1(GK,S, M) → ∏v∈S H1(Kv, M) → H1(GK,S, M′)∨ → H2(GK,S, M) → ∏v∈S H2(Kv, M) → H0(GK,S, M′)∨ → 0.

(iii) Normalized ℤ/ℓrℤ package and conjugation compatibility. For every prime ℓ, r ≥ 1, (ℓ ≠ 2 or K totally complex), and primitive ℓr-th root ζ ∈ Ω, there exists a ζ-normalized family of bijective local invariants invA yielding, for every finite S containing all primes over ℓ, every choice of prolongations Av, every descended cyclotomic character μ, and every finite discrete ℤ/ℓrℤ[GK,S]-module M, the nine-term exact sequence (with transgression pairings given by invAv), Shafarevich–Tate duality, and Hn(GK,S, M) = 0 for all n ≥ 3. Furthermore, every ζ-normalized family of bijective local invariants is automatically conjugation-compatible: whenever σ ∈ Aut(Ω) restricts to τ ∈ Aut(K) with σ(ζ) = ζc (c ∈ ℤ/ℓrℤ) and maps A onto A′, any 2-cocycles z on DA and z′ on DA′ related by z′(g′, h′) = c · z(σ−1g′σ, σ−1h′σ) satisfy invA′([z′]) = invA([z]).

Proof. For (i) and (ii), let K be a finite subfield of ℚ̄, N ≥ 1, S ⊇ {v ∣ N}, and assume N is odd or K is totally complex. For every prime divisor p of N, the primary factor q = pvp(N) divides N, so S still contains all places above q and either q is odd or K is totally complex; in particular H3(GK,S, μq) = 0, and there exists an open subgroup U0 ≤ GK,S satisfying the compact-support Poitou–Tate duality hypotheses for μq (combining global Artin reciprocity and local Tate duality). Assembling these prime-power base cases establishes the full compact-support global duality hypotheses for N on GK,S, which directly deliver the bijectivity of compact-support duality in degrees r + s = 3, finiteness and mutual Pontryagin duality of Ш1S(K, M) and Ш2S(K, M′), and exactness at all nine spots of the sequence in (ii).

For the first half of (iii), when ℓ ≠ 2 or K is totally complex and S contains all primes above ℓ, N = ℓr satisfies (N odd or K totally complex) and S ⊇ {v ∣ ℓr}. Representing the continuous ℤ/ℓrℤ-representation M by a matching finite discrete ℓr-torsion ℤ[GK,S]-module transports the nine-term exact sequence from (ii) across the degree-0, 1, 2 local and global comparison equivalences and matches the three transgression maps with the cup-product pairings evaluated through the ζ-normalized local invariants invAv (detailed below), packaging nine-term exactness with Shafarevich–Tate duality and vanishing of Hn(GK,S, M) for n ≥ 3.

For automatic conjugation compatibility in (iii), let σ ∈ Aut(Ω) restrict to τ ∈ Aut(K) with σ(ζ) = ζc and map A onto A′ (with A over v). Define the continuous pullback conjugation homomorphism ψ : DA′ → DA by ψ(g′) = σ−1 ∘ g′ ∘ σ. Then ψ maps inertia IA′ into IA, maps arithmetic Frobenius at A′ to arithmetic Frobenius at A, and preserves the mod-ℓr cyclotomic character (since (ℤ/ℓrℤ)× is abelian, conjugating by σ cancels). Moreover, τ maps v to a prime v′ beneath A′ with the same residue-field cardinality Nv′ = Nv, sending any local uniformizer ϖ ∈ 𝔭v ∖ 𝔭v2 to a uniformizer ϖ′ = τ(ϖ) ∈ 𝔭v′ ∖ 𝔭v′2. At (A, v), choose an unramified homomorphism χ : DA → ℤ/ℓrℤ (trivial on IA, sending Frobenius to 1) and a Kummer witness (ϖ, y, κ) with yℓr = ϖ and g(y) = ζκ(g)y; by ζ-normalization, the 2-cocycle z0(g, h) = κ(g)χ(h) satisfies invA([z0]) = 1. Given any 2-cocycle z on DA, put a = invA([z]) ∈ ℤ/ℓrℤ. Because invA is bijective and ℤ/ℓrℤ-linear, [z] = a · [z0], so z − a · z0 = d1(f) for a continuous 1-cochain f : DA → ℤ/ℓrℤ. Transporting via (σ, ψ, c), the 2-cocycle z0′(g′, h′) = c · z0(ψ(g′), ψ(h′)) is the Kummer–unramified cup cocycle attached to (ϖ′, σ(y), c · (κ ∘ ψ), χ ∘ ψ) (since g′(σ(y)) = σ(ψ(g′)(y)) = σ(ζκ(ψ(g′))y) = ζc · κ(ψ(g′))σ(y)), so ζ-normalization at (A′, v′) forces invA′([z0′]) = 1. Meanwhile, the continuous 1-cochain f′(g′) = c · f(ψ(g′)) satisfies d1(f′) = z′ − a · z0′ (using cyclotomic invariance under ψ), whence [z′] = a · [z0′] in H2(DA′, μℓr) and invA′([z′]) = a · 1 = invA([z]).

Normalized nine-term transport, continuity of pullback conjugation, and a complete nine-term check on ℚ at ℓ = 3

In the ℤ/ℓrℤ transport of (iii), every finite discrete ℤ/ℓrℤ-representation (M, ρ) of GK,S gives rise to a discrete ℓr-torsion ℤ[GK,S]-module A with continuous twisted dual M(1)∨; the six comparison equivalences identify the middle local cohomology products ∏v∈S Hi(Dv, M) and the dual global cohomology groups H2−i(GK,S, M(1)∨)∨ (i = 0, 1, 2) with the corresponding terms of the profinite nine-term sequence for A, while three local transgression comparisons verify that composing the local twisted-dual cup products with invAv reproduces the Pontryagin duals of global-to-local restriction. In the conjugation-compatibility proof, continuity of ψ(g′) = σ−1 ∘ g′ ∘ σ in the Krull topology follows directly from finite bases: for any finite subextension E/K with basis {bi}, the finite intersection of open stabilizers of σ(bi) maps into the open subgroup fixing E.

As a complete nine-term numerical verification, take K = ℚ, ℓ = 3 (odd), r = 1, S = {3}, and trivial module M = ℤ/3ℤ, so M′ = μ3. By global class field theory (Kronecker–Weber), the maximal abelian extension of ℚ unramified outside 3 is ℚ(μ3∞) with Galois group ℤ3× ≅ (ℤ/2ℤ) × ℤ3, so H1(Gℚ,{3}, ℤ/3ℤ) ≅ ℤ/3ℤ (order 3, totally ramified at 3). Locally at 3, as computed above, |H0(Gℚ3, ℤ/3ℤ)| = 3, |H1(Gℚ3, ℤ/3ℤ)| = 9, and |H2(Gℚ3, ℤ/3ℤ)| = 1. Globally, |H0(Gℚ,{3}, ℤ/3ℤ)| = 3, |H0(Gℚ,{3}, μ3)| = 1 (since μ3 ⊄ ℚ), and Kummer theory over the principal ideal domain ℤ[1/3] (whose unit group is {±3k}) gives H1(Gℚ,{3}, μ3) ≅ ℤ[1/3]×/(ℤ[1/3]×)3 ≅ ℤ/3ℤ (order 3, generated by 3 since −1 = (−1)3). Because the global cyclotomic cubic extension is ramified at 3, the restriction H1(Gℚ,{3}, ℤ/3ℤ) → H1(Gℚ3, ℤ/3ℤ) is injective (so Ш1{3}(ℚ, ℤ/3ℤ) = 0). Since H0(Gℚ,{3}, ℤ/3ℤ) → H0(Gℚ3, ℤ/3ℤ) is an isomorphism (3 → 3), exactness of the first four terms forces H2(Gℚ,{3}, μ3) = 0 (confirming Ш2{3}(ℚ, μ3) = 0 by Shafarevich–Tate duality). Finally, the cokernel of H1 ↪ H1loc has order 9/3 = 3 = |H1(Gℚ,{3}, μ3)∨|, forcing the arrow H1loc → H1(μ3)∨ to be surjective and hence H2(Gℚ,{3}, ℤ/3ℤ) = 0. The nine term orders are therefore 3, 3, 1, 3, 9, 3, 1, 1, 1, whose alternating product checks out exactly: 3 · 1 · 9 · 1 · 1 = 27 = 3 · 3 · 3 · 1.
