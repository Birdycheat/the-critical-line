What the good arithmetic window controls

One can first think of a degree-one cohomology class as a way of assigning a value to each symmetry. A cocycle satisfies c(gh) = c(g) + g·c(h). Two choices give the same class if they differ by g ↦ g·a − a. For the group of order two acting trivially on ℤ/4, the choice at its generator must double to zero. The two possibilities 0 and 2 give exactly two classes. In the arithmetic problem the group is a Galois group, the action includes a twist and one retains classes whose restrictions vanish at the specified places. This is the reason a kernel of a restriction map occurs below.

We measure a finite ℤℓ-module by its length. Filter it until each successive quotient has order ℓ. If there are d quotients, its order is ℓd. Both ℤ/ℓ² and (ℤ/ℓ)² have length two, although they require different numbers of generators. Later the tower estimate uses both quantities. Over a coefficient ring with residue field of size ℓf, each quotient instead contributes f to the base-ℓ logarithm of the order.

Fix a number field E and a rational prime ℓ. The coefficient module here is the discrete quotient ℚℓ/ℤℓ. A Tate twist changes the scalar by which Galois acts. If the ℓ-adic cyclotomic character is χcyc, the action on this quotient uses χcyc−j for the integer j in the window sum. With this action the cocycle rule above becomes c(gh) = c(g) + χcyc(g)−jc(h). Vanishing on restriction means the restricted cocycle becomes a coboundary.

The source uses the Galois group with ramification allowed in the specified set of primes above ℓ. The set S in LData is precisely the finite places dividing ℓ. One chooses a place of the algebraic closure above each member and restricts to the corresponding decomposition group. Write hℓ,j for the ℤℓ-module length of the subgroup Sha of these locally trivial degree-one classes. The related degree-two group has coefficients ℤℓ(j + 1). Thus the locally trivial subgroup within degree one is the intersection of the kernels of those local restriction maps. The length bounded by VVle is its ℤℓ-module length. Splitting the obstruction by the action of conjugation

There is a different estimate for each of three parts of the cohomological sum. To see where they come from, fix a CM field in the construction, a twist index j and an odd prime ℓ. Complex conjugation on the CM field induces a linear involution c on the degree-one cohomology. Its action preserves the locally trivial subgroup. Put s = (−1)j. Because 2 is a unit in ℤℓ, every locally trivial class v has a decomposition

v = ½(v + scv) + ½(v − scv).

Applying c to the first summand multiplies it by s, and applying it to the second multiplies it by −s. Both still lie in the subgroup. A vector in both eigenspaces satisfies 2sv = 0, hence vanishes. The sum is direct, so the full module length equals the sum of its two eigenspace lengths. The name “plus” in the source means eigenvalue (−1)j; for odd j this is the −1 eigenspace. At ℓ = 2 the projectors involve a forbidden division, and the full module is estimated separately. Direct-sum construction Conjugation and the two parities

From finite subsets of primes to a series

The arithmetic comparison eventually needs a summable sequence of nonnegative terms. Choose the algebraic closure and local data attached to the arithmetic datum. Finiteness of each module length ensures conversion from extended natural numbers to integers recovers its value. For each prime the arithmetic contribution is bounded by its logarithm times the sum of these lengths in the specified range. All finite subsums are bounded by the same B. Their supremum is therefore finite, and equals the sum of the full nonnegative series. This is the use of the universal quantifier over finite sets of primes. The resulting package also keeps the finiteness assertion on the relevant twists. Reading the cohomological estimate into the arithmetic data

Why is an exponential bound small enough for a final estimate near √x? At the same scale x the weight in a single arithmetic measurement is b = exp(xε). The cohomological series has sum at most exp(xε/2) = √b. Since b ≥ 1, it fits within the allowance b in the finite-place lattice estimate. This is a bound on one input to the measurement. Only after the arithmetic comparisons are added does the proof divide the error by the total weight to bound the centered prime sum.
