Descending a transition map to the prescribed level

Keep the moduli schemes and their universal abelian schemes over the standard base field. Write K(N) for the principal subgroup and Λ̂ for the prime-to-p adelic lattice. Fix a power k ≥ 0. A point of the fibre product ZN specifies a point of XN and k points of its abelian variety. At power zero only the point of XN remains.

Transition theorem. Let h be an adelic element. Assume hΛ̂ ⊆ Λ̂ and hK(N′)h−1 ⊆ K(N). Then h induces a map Th from ZN′ to ZN over the ground field. It is independent of the finer level used to construct it. An element of K(N) acts as the identity at level N. For successive admissible elements h and g the composition is Tg ∘ Th = Tgh.

Choosing the common cover. Write d for the denominator used in the preceding isogeny construction. It is prime to p and clears h−1 on Λ̂. Such a d exists because the adelic lattice and its transform are commensurable away from p. If u is congruent to the identity at level Nd, apply u − 1 to dh−1Λ̂ ⊆ Λ̂. Its image lies in NdΛ̂. Applying h and cancelling d puts the image of huh−1 − 1 in NΛ̂. The same calculation with u−1 gives the full subgroup condition. Pull the pair constructed at level Nd back to level M = N′Nd. Its two maps are now defined on a cover of the prescribed source.

Descent and independence. Forgetting level gives a finite étale surjection c from XM to XN′. Two geometric lifts of a point differ by a change of level u in K(N′). The target change huh−1 belongs to K(N), so the transformed objects agree at level N. Rigidity of a polarized isogeny with its action on the prime-to-p Tate modules gives agreement on the abelian varieties as well. For the map on moduli points, the two pullbacks to XM ×XN′ XM therefore agree on all geometric points. This fibre product is reduced, since it is étale over a smooth scheme over a field. The target is separated, so the equalizer is closed and contains this entire reduced scheme. The map descends uniquely along c. On total spaces the forgetful map is the base change of c. The same argument descends the map of abelian varieties, using smoothness of the source and separatedness of the target. The endomorphism, polarization and Tate-module compatibilities descend with it. The Stacks Project, §35.13, descent of morphisms along faithfully flat covers.

Denote the resulting pair by (πh, Φh). Two constructions can be compared after forgetting from a common multiple of their fine levels. There they have the same transformed moduli point and the same map of its abelian variety by the uniqueness just used. Since the common cover is surjective and étale, the descended pairs coincide. The pair for g composed with the pair for h realizes the transformation for gh, by composition of the isogenies and of their level structures. An element of the level subgroup realizes the identity pair. Applying these statements to the base point and then to each of the k coordinates proves the composition and identity laws for the fibre powers.

Which level changes give finite étale covers?

Fibre theorem. Suppose K(M) ⊆ K(N) for two admissible principal levels. For the transition with h = 1, forgetting the finer structure gives a finite étale surjection at every power k. The power at level M is its cartesian pullback from level N. More explicitly,

ZM ≅ XM ×XN ZN.

When N divides M, the map on moduli schemes forgets the additional level structure. Its geometric input is the finite étale surjectivity of the full-level forgetful map. For an inclusion of principal subgroups, the level MN maps to both. The two composites to XN agree by the transition theorem. Finiteness and étaleness thus descend along the finite étale surjection to XM, and every point of XN lifts from level MN. In the forgetful transition the abelian variety is unchanged after base change, so its square with the moduli schemes is cartesian. Giving k points of the pulled-back abelian variety amounts to giving their common base point at level M and their images at level N. This proves the displayed isomorphism. Finite étale surjections remain finite étale and surjective under base change.

The quotient acting on a fibre. Assume K(M) is normal in K(N). Each element u of K(N) acts at level M because it normalizes the finer subgroup. The composition law makes this a left action, and K(M) acts trivially. Given two geometric lifts of the same moduli point, their level structures differ by an element of K(N). If the lifts give the same point downstairs in the fibre power, the cartesian square shows that this element also takes the first lifted tuple to the second. Conversely, an element fixing a tuple fixes its level-M moduli point. Rigidity identifies its action there with the identity on the abelian variety, and the full level structure forces u ≡ 1 modulo M. Thus K(N)/K(M) acts freely and transitively on every geometric fibre.

For an intermediate subgroup K(M) ⊆ H ⊆ K(N), forgetting the choice within an H-orbit gives an intermediate finite étale cover. To see this, pull the cover back along itself. The free transitive action identifies the pullback with a disjoint union indexed by the quotient group. Taking the orbits of H/K(M) gives a finite set of components compatible with descent. The descended cover has that orbit set as its geometric fibre. Here normality of K(M) in K(N) provides the group acting on the fibre. The construction of the intermediate cover imposes no normality assumption on H. The Stacks Project, §95.6, descent of finite étale covers.

A common normal refinement is available for finitely many prescribed levels and translates. For each pair (Nj, hj), the principal-level congruence theorem gives a positive factor cj prime to p such that conjugation by hj carries level Njcj into level Nj. Taking M = N ∏j Njcj imposes all these inclusions. The principal kernel K(M) is invariant under conjugation by the integral level-one subgroup, hence normal in K(N).

We can now count a fibre even when the given inclusion is not assumed normal. Write H ⊆ J for the two level groups and take a common finer level L normal in J. Over a point at level J there are [J : L] lifts to level L by the normal case. Each lift to the intermediate level H has [H : L] such points above it. Dividing gives [J : L]/[H : L] = [J : H], since every H-coset in J is a union of [H : L] cosets of L.

The geometric correspondence uses an intermediate subgroup in a specific way. Given K(N) ⊆ K ⊆ K(1), it takes one copy of ZM for each coset of K(N) in K. Here N divides M and the datum requires (hu)K(M)(hu)−1 ⊆ K(N) for every u in K. On the copy labelled by a representative u, the two arrows are the forgetful transition and Thu. Both have principal level N as their target. This description defines the correspondence using the principal-level models already constructed.
