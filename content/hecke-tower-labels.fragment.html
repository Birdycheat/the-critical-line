How can each adelic point carry an equivariant label?

The correction at fixed weight multiplies a form by a function on a finite group. To evaluate that function at an adelic point, we need a label that respects rational equivalence and follows right translation at the auxiliary place. The starting level matters because a point can have a stabilizer under this translation.

Let G be the definite unitary group of rank a ≥ 1 attached to a CM field E, viewed over ℚ, and let Γ = G(ℚ). Fix a split place w₀ over ℓ. Its local group identifies with GLa(Ew₀). Fix a compact open level K away from this place, embedded with trivial auxiliary component, and a K-fixed algebraic form f. Smoothness gives an identity neighbourhood N in the local group whose elements fix f. Write 𝒪 for the integers of the local field. Choose s ≥ 2 large enough that the matrices congruent to the identity modulo ℓs lie in N. For n ≥ 0 take Jn to be the congruence subgroup of GLa(𝒪) modulo ℓs+n. These compact open subgroups are normal in J₀ and shrink to the identity through a neighbourhood basis.

Two properties of this choice will be used. First, J₀ is torsion-free. If it contained a nonidentity element of finite order it would contain one of prime order p. Write this element as I + X and take the largest ℓ-adic absolute value of an entry as ‖X‖. Then 0 < ‖X‖ ≤ |ℓ|². In the expansion of (I + X)p − I, the linear term has norm |p| ‖X‖. All higher terms have norm at most ‖X‖², which is strictly smaller since |p| ≥ |ℓ|. The sum cannot vanish. Second, the ℓth power sends Jn into Jn+1. The linear term gains a factor ℓ, and every term of degree at least two is already divisible by ℓs+n+1. Thus every element of Hn = J₀/Jn has order dividing ℓn. This quotient is finite because its open cosets cover the compact group J₀. It is an ℓ-group by Cauchy’s theorem, which says that each prime dividing the order of a finite group occurs as the order of an element. To see the latter assertion for a group of order h and a prime p dividing h, cyclically permute the hp−1 ordered p-tuples whose product is 1. Orbits have size p except for the constant tuples (x, …, x) with xp = 1. Their number is divisible by p and includes the identity tuple, so some such x has order p.

Equivariant label theorem. Write Cn = K Jn, using the split local factor inside the finite adelic group. Projection to the auxiliary component followed by reduction gives a surjection λn from C₀ to Hn with kernel Cn. For every n there is a locally constant label Ln on G(𝔸) such that, for all γ ∈ Γ, g ∈ G(𝔸), u ∈ G(ℝ) and c ∈ C₀,

Ln(γguc) = Ln(g) λn(c).

Proof. Choose one representative b per double class modulo Γ on the left and G(ℝ)C₀ on the right. Assign b the label 1 and set Ln(γbuc) = λn(c). If two decompositions give the same point, then b−1γ₂−1γ₁b belongs to G(ℝ)C₀ and has finite component c₂c₁−1. The intersection of Γ with the conjugate of G(ℝ)C₀ is finite. Indeed, this product is compact by definiteness and the compactness of C₀, and rational matrices form a closed discrete subgroup of the adelic matrices. Our element therefore has finite order. Its auxiliary component lies in the torsion-free group J₀, so λn(c₂c₁−1) = 1. The two labels agree. This verifies precisely the stabilizer condition. Every element fixing a class must act trivially on its label. Multiplication on the right now gives the displayed formula. A label is constant on each open set gG(ℝ)Cn. In particular, multiplying f by any function of the label gives a form at the refined level and preserves its archimedean weight.

For example, inside ℚ₂× start with J₀ = 1 + 4ℤ₂ and J₁ = 1 + 8ℤ₂. The two labels are the residue classes 1 and 5 modulo 8. Right multiplication by 5 exchanges them, since 5² ≡ 1 modulo 8. Although this quotient has an element of order two, the element 5 in the starting group has infinite order. Starting instead at 1 + 2ℤ₂ would admit the torsion element −1, so killing the images of rational stabilizers would require a separate check.

When do different Hecke translates give different points?

The finite quotient also counts the points needed to evaluate a form. Choose a finite set S of representatives at level C₀, using the class-number theorem. For each q ∈ Hn choose an auxiliary lift jq ∈ J₀. At every depth the points bjq, with b ∈ S, cover the double classes of level Cn. Indeed, in a decomposition at level C₀, the auxiliary component differs from its lift by an element of Jn. Move the factors supported away from the auxiliary place and the archimedean component past the lift. The remaining factors belong to G(ℝ)Cn. This gives a cover by at most |S| |Hn| points.

Separation at large depth. Let T be a fixed finite set of finite adelic translations trivial at the auxiliary place, such that the cosets tK are pairwise distinct. For all sufficiently large n, for each b ∈ S and every auxiliary lift j ∈ J₀, distinct t, t′ ∈ T give distinct double classes starting from bj. In the notation [·]n for these classes,

[bjt]n = [bjt′]n   ⟹   t = t′.

Proof. A collision gives bjt′ = γbjtuc for some γ ∈ Γ, u ∈ G(ℝ) and c ∈ Cn. All possible γ lie in one compact set because S and T are finite, j lies in the compact group J₀, and c lies in the fixed compact group C₀. Thus only finitely many γ can occur at any depth. At the auxiliary place, the component of b−1γb equals j cw₀−1j−1, which lies in Jn by normality. For γ ≠ 1 this local component differs from 1 because the field embedding into its completion is injective. The tower eventually excludes all these finitely many nonidentity components, uniformly in b, j and the translations. Then γ = 1, and cancellation gives t′ = tuc. Comparing archimedean components gives u = 1. Comparing auxiliary components puts c in K. Hence tK = t′K, which forces t = t′. The threshold may depend on S and T. This is the separation used in counting the representations that fail a local test.
