Why do both radii involve a ratio of lattice sizes?

Both constructions round a matrix between two lattices. Suppose the source has rank a over the CM field E and the target has rank b. Every nonzero source sublattice has normalized size at least L > 0. Every nonzero quotient of the target by a saturated sublattice has normalized size at most H > 0. The supremum over these quotients is called its largest normalized size. Use the specified Hermitian frames, let f be the number of complex places of E (one over each real place of its fixed field), and write d = |disc E|1/(2f). For any family of complex matrices Tᵥ between these frames there is one E-linear map Φ carrying the source lattice into the target, with

‖T − Φ‖ ≤ ab √f d H/(2L).

Here the norm squares and adds all matrix entries at all f places, then takes a square root. A zero source or target rank makes the error zero. For positive ranks, the integral maps form a full lattice of real dimension D = 2fab. To find an integral map near a prescribed point, use Euclidean lattice transference. In dimension D, a full lattice of dual minimum λ has covering radius at most D/(2λ). Thus it is enough to show λ ≥ 2√f · L/(d H).

What does an integral covector measure? It assigns to each integral map Φ the integer TrE/ℚ(tr(ΨΦ)) for a reverse matrix Ψ. The field trace pairing is nondegenerate, so every integral covector has this form after rational extension. If 𝔇 is the different, then δΨ sends the target lattice into the source for every δ in 𝔇. Indeed, evaluating on rank-one maps tests each scalar pairing of Ψ with a target vector and a source covector. Their traces remain integers after multiplication by every element of 𝒪E. By definition these pairings belong to the inverse different 𝔇−1. The lattice is recovered from its integral dual, giving the assertion.

Suppose Ψ ≠ 0. Its image of the target lattice is nonzero. Multiplying any nonzero sublattice of that image by 𝔇 puts it in the source and multiplies its normalized size by d. In rank r, the index factor is Norm(𝔇)r, with Norm(𝔇) = |disc E| and the size takes the (2fr)th root. Thus the least size in the image is at least L/d. For the target lattice B, put K = B ∩ ker Ψ and I = ΨB, of rank r > 0. The kernel is saturated, and B/K maps isomorphically onto I as an arithmetic lattice. In orthonormal coordinates for the quotient metric and the image metric, the real volume factor at a complex place is the square of the absolute determinant, at most ‖Ψv‖2rop. Multiplying over the places and taking the (2fr)th root bounds size(I)/size(B/K) above by the geometric mean of the operator norms. The numerator is at least L/d and the denominator at most H. This proves the lower bound L/(d H) for that geometric mean. The arithmetic–geometric mean inequality and the bound of operator norm by Hilbert–Schmidt norm now give ∑v ‖Ψv‖²HS ≥ f(L/(d H))².

The field trace sums twice the real part at each complex place. In Euclidean coordinates the real dual vector therefore has norm 2(∑ ‖Ψv‖²HS)1/2. Substitution into D/(2λ) gives the displayed bound. For example, over ℚ(i), multiplication by 1/2 already has integral field trace on every Gaussian integer. Its real dual vector has length one. The factor two recovers the ordinary Euclidean dual of the square lattice.

How does the map act on a quotient of the original lattice?

Let S be saturated in A, meaning that A/S has no torsion. Write a for the rank of A/S. Let the target be a sublattice R of rank b. Give the quotient its quotient metric and R its induced metric. An integral matrix on these two lattices lifts to a rational matrix on the ambient space by composing projection to the quotient, the rounded map, and inclusion of R. It kills S and sends A into R. At a real place choose orthonormal bases adapted to the source quotient and target subspace. The lifted error can be taken zero on the orthogonal complement and hence has the same sum of squared entries as its rectangular block. The bound holds for this extension of the error from the span of A. In the central application that span is the whole space. In the first layer only the error on the middle quotient is controlled at this stage.
