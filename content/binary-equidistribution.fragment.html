Recovering the scalar. Under the dilation X ↦ εX, the scalar Gram value becomes ε²β. Source volume scales by ε4d and target volume by ε2d. Consequently ε4d times the integral of Φ(εX) on the fibre at β is (ε²)dG(ε²β). This puts the length sum at exactly the scale δ = ε² above. Integrating the local fibre densities over their targets gives back the test volume V. For each ε the factorization of every length fibre identifies the vector count with κ times the length count. To see why we can cancel V, start with the rational vector (1,1). Anisotropy makes its Gram value nonzero. At infinity choose a continuous nonnegative function equal to one at the vector, with compact support in the regular locus. At each selected finite place choose a compact open neighborhood of its image in that locus. Haar measure is positive on nonempty open sets and finite on compact sets, so their product with the archimedean integral satisfies 0 < V < ∞. The two limits now give V = κV, hence κ = 1.
