Which two exponents can be chosen before selecting a layer?

Fix a finite extension K/ℚℓ, its Breuil–Kisin datum and an order m ≥ 0. Put 𝔖 = W(k)[[u]], A = Ainf and ξ = e(E), where e is evaluation and E is the Eisenstein polynomial. Write θ for Fontaine's map. It identifies 𝔖/(E) with 𝒪K inside 𝒪ℂℓ through θ ∘ e. A witness on a frame has a matrix β over 𝔖 and an exponent a ≥ 0 satisfying

β ≡ ℓaI (mod E),   φ(ε(g)) g(e(β)) ≡ e(β) (mod ξm)   for every g ∈ GK.

Uniform exponents. There are integers c, k₀ ≥ 0 depending only on the fixed datum and m with the following properties. On any integral frame, of any rank and height, the existence of a witness of order m implies the existence of one with exponent c. Also, for every s ∈ 𝔖 there exists a series γ satisfying both

γ ≡ ℓk₀s (mod E),   g(e(γ)) ≡ e(γ) (mod ξm)   for every g ∈ GK.

The first statement allows witnesses from different layers to have the same scalar residue. The second supplies coefficients of a comparison matrix whose evaluations are Galois invariant to the prescribed order. The same choice of γ for a given s satisfies all the Galois congruences. For m = 0 one may take β = I, γ = s and both exponents zero.

Normalizing a frame witness one coefficient at a time

Assume m ≥ 1. Use the element of the Galois group constructed from the period for which g(ξ) = ξw and λ = θ(w) has infinite multiplicative order. For each 1 ≤ i < m, choose ai ≥ 0 with |ℓ|ai ≤ |λi − 1|. A finite choice is possible since the right side is nonzero. With c₁ = 0, put ci+1 = ci + ai and take c = cm. If the exponent a of the original witness is at most c, multiply β by ℓc−a. Both congruences are linear in β, so this gives the required exponent.

Suppose a > c. We construct Γi over 𝔖 with β ≡ ℓa−ciΓi modulo Ei. The residue of β gives Γ₁ = I. For the step from i to i+1, put n = a − ci and write β = ℓnΓi + EiΔ. We claim that

(λi − 1) θ(e(Δ)) ∈ ℓn Matd(𝒪ℂℓ).

To see this, set P = φ(ε(g)) and D(Z) = P g(e(Z)) − e(Z). Reducing the original witness equation by θ and cancelling ℓa gives θ(P) = I. Indeed g fixes the residue of every evaluated series because that residue belongs to 𝒪K. Substitution gives

D(β) = ℓnD(Γi) + ξi(wiP g(e(Δ)) − e(Δ)).

Since ξm divides the left side, ξi divides ℓnD(Γi). All powers of (ξ) are ℓ-saturated in A. For the first power this follows from A/(ξ) = 𝒪ℂℓ being ℓ-torsion-free. For higher powers apply the first case, cancel one ξ in the domain A and repeat. Write D(Γi) = ξiV. Divide the displayed identity by ξi and apply θ. The left side vanishes since i < m. The right side is ℓnθ(V) + (λi − 1)θ(e(Δ)). This proves the claim.

Taking absolute values proves divisibility of each entry of θ(e(Δ)) by ℓn−ai in 𝒪ℂℓ. This quotient belongs to K and has absolute value at most 1, so it lies in 𝒪K. Lifting it through 𝔖/(E) gives Δ ≡ ℓn−aiΔ′ modulo E. Now take Γi+1 = ℓaiΓi + EiΔ′. By substitution this gives the desired congruence modulo Ei+1. At i = m we have β ≡ ℓa−cΓm modulo Em. The quotient 𝔖/(E) is ℓ-torsion-free, so Γm reduces to ℓcI. For every Galois element the ideal (ξm) is stable because (ξ) = ker θ. The original witness equation therefore implies ξm divides ℓa−c times the defect of Γm. Saturation proves the second test for all group elements. The choice of one element controls the losses in detecting the coefficients. The resulting witness satisfies the test for every element.

Constructing an invariant lift of any power-series residue

Write E = P(u), with P(X) monic of degree d₀ ≥ 1. We first lift the single parameter u. Since P is Eisenstein, it is irreducible over the fraction field of W(k). In characteristic zero it is relatively prime to P′. Bézout's identity over that field, after clearing the denominators and absorbing a unit of the discrete valuation ring, gives A₀P + B₀P′ = ℓb with integral polynomials. Write B = B₀(u). For a power s = ℓa define Ps(X) = sd₀P(X/s), a polynomial with integral coefficients.

Start with s = 1 and q = u. At order j ≥ 1 assume q ≡ su modulo E and F = Ps(q) ∈ (Ej). Put h = ℓbsd₀−1, replace s by sh and q by hq − BF. This preserves the residue condition. The identities Psh(hq) = hd₀F and P′sh(hq) = hd₀−1P′s(q) give, by polynomial expansion,

Psh(hq − BF) ≡ hd₀−1(h − BP′s(q))F (mod Ej+1).

The quadratic error contains F². The term in parentheses is divisible by E because P′s(q) ≡ sd₀−1P′(u) modulo E and BP′(u) ≡ ℓb there. This raises the order by one. Stop once Em divides Ps(q). The final s = ℓa depends only on the datum and m.

Why is e(q) invariant modulo ξm? In (A/(ξm))[1/ℓ], both e(q) and g(e(q)) are roots of Ps. Indeed g fixes the Witt coefficients of Ps and preserves (ξ). Both roots reduce modulo ξ to sπ, where π = θ(e(u)) ∈ K. That root is simple because P′s(sπ) = sd₀−1P′(π) ≠ 0 in ℂℓ. Two such roots through a nilpotent ideal coincide. If their difference is t, factor their polynomial difference as t times the derivative at one root plus a multiple of t². The factor multiplying t is a unit modulo ξ and hence a unit in the ring, so t = 0. Clearing the power of ℓ from this equality and using ℓ-saturation of (ξm) proves the integral invariance.

Finally divide a given series z by the distinguished polynomial E. The remainder ∑0≤i<d₀ wiui has coefficients in W(k). Take k₀ = ad₀ and set

γ = ∑0≤i<d₀ wi ℓa(d₀−i)qi.

Substitution of q ≡ ℓau modulo E gives γ ≡ ℓk₀z. The evaluated polynomial is invariant modulo ξm since its coefficients are fixed and its argument is invariant to that order. This construction works for every z with the same k₀. Sharifi, Iwasawa Theory, Proposition 2.2.1, Weierstrass division.

For the parameter calculation alone take ℓ = 3, P(X) = X² − 3 and m = 2. We have −P(u) + (u/2)P′(u) = 3. One step gives s = 3 and q = 3u − (u/2)E. Direct expansion gives q² − 27 = (u²/4 − 3)E². For a remainder w₀ + w₁u the lift is 9w₀ + 3w₁q, which reduces to nine times that remainder modulo E.

Lean correspondence

The integral approximate-root argument compares the two evaluations of the scaled polynomial with its derivative after clearing a power of ℓ. The nilpotent-ideal calculation above proves this comparison after inverting ℓ, then contracts it back by the same saturation statement.
