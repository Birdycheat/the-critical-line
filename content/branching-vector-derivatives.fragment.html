How large is one derivative after the vector has already been differentiated?

The projector onto a shifted summand involves a minor whose entries are differential operators. Applying the first operator can take a vector out of its lowest compact type. To estimate a product of operators, we therefore follow the spaces reached at each order.

Let π be a unitary discrete series of G = U(p,q), with m = p + q ≥ 2, and K = U(p) × U(q). Its Harish-Chandra parameter λ has strictly decreasing coordinates that belong to the same translate of the integers. Assume |λa| ≤ B and |λa − λb| ≥ 100m³ for coordinates with opposite signature. Write ρ for the half-sum of the positive roots ea − eb with a < b. The same-sign roots contribute ρc and the opposite-sign roots contribute ρn. The lowest compact-type and infinitesimal-character theorems give a unique smooth compact type of highest weight Λ = λ + ρn − ρc and the full Casimir eigenvalue ‖λ‖² − ‖ρ‖². We use these statements as inputs. The discrete-series input requires a gap strictly greater than mpq + 1. Since pq ≤ m², the stipulated gap suffices. M. F. Atiyah and W. Schmid, Corollary (6.13) and Theorem (8.5), on infinitesimal characters and lowest compact types.

Denote the lowest compact type by V₀. Write Dab = dπ(Eab) for the complexified derivative in an elementary matrix direction. Define Vk+1 as the span of Vk and DabVk for opposite signs. Compact derivatives preserve each layer. Each layer is finite dimensional and decomposes orthogonally into compact types. The compact weight estimate proved in the chapter on discrete-series derivatives says that each highest weight μ in Vk satisfies ‖μ − Λ‖ ≤ √2k. Each noncompact step can add a root of length √2, so the bound grows with the number of steps.

Energy bound. If x ∈ Vk and 0 ≤ k ≤ S, then

∑a,b ‖Dabx‖² ≤ 64m(B + S)² ‖x‖².

Indeed, Dab∗ = Dba on equal-sign directions and equals −Dba on opposite-sign directions, as identities of pairings on smooth vectors. Write Ω for the full sum ∑a,bDabDba and ΩK for the same-sign part. Then twice the compact sum minus the full sum gives every squared norm a positive sign. On a compact type of highest weight μ, ΩK has eigenvalue ‖μ + ρc‖² − ‖ρc‖². Thus its quadratic form on the whole layer is at most (R² − ‖ρc‖²)‖x‖² for R = ‖Λ + ρc‖ + √2k. Compact Casimir and its extension from each compact type to its span.

The factors in 64 can now be checked. Strict order and the integer differences give m − 1 ≤ 2B, hence m ≤ 4B. Each coordinate of ρn and ρ has absolute value at most m/2. Consequently R ≤ √m(B + m/2) + √2k ≤ 3√m(B + S) and ‖ρ‖² ≤ 4m(B + S)². Substituting the two Casimir eigenvalue estimates and discarding the nonpositive terms gives 2R² + ‖ρ‖² ≤ 22m(B + S)². The stated constant 64 makes the one-step factor after taking square roots equal to 8√m(B + S).

From one derivative to every word in a projector minor

Derivative-word bound. Let v₀ be any vector in the lowest compact type, and let X₁,…,Xr be complex m-by-m matrices with Frobenius norms at most one. For every integer S ≥ r,

‖dπ(X₁) ··· dπ(Xr)v₀‖ ≤ [8√m(B + S)]r ‖v₀‖.

For one direction write dπ(X)x = ∑a,bXabDabx. The triangle inequality followed by Cauchy–Schwarz bounds its norm by ‖X‖F(∑‖Dabx‖²)1/2. This is at most 8√m(B + S)‖x‖ in each layer that is reached. Its value lies in the next layer. Reading the word from right to left gives one such factor per derivative. The empty word leaves the starting vector unchanged. This explains why the exponent is the actual word length r, even when a larger S serves many words.

In the cofactor estimate, set n = m − 1 and S = n. The lowest vector has norm one, each matrix unit has Frobenius norm one, and the branching gap of 1000m³ implies the 100m³ hypothesis above. A product from a minor of size n contains at most n derivatives. Each remaining scalar factor has size at most 4B. Summing over which factors act by differentiation gives (4B + 8√m(B + n))n per permutation. The n! permutations then give the bound used for a single projector entry. The splitting of the signed form and the positive quantity |cs|‖rs(elv₀)‖² come from the signed form identity.

As a check on the order of the operators, take the defining representation of the compact group U(4). Its lowest compact type is the entire defining space, with Λ = (1,0,0,0) and λ = Λ + ρ = (5/2,1/2,−1/2,−3/2). Acting on e₄, the word E₁₂E₂₃E₃₄ gives e₁. Reversing the word gives zero. Here ‖λ‖² − ‖ρ‖² = 9 − 5 = 4, which agrees with ∑‖Eabe₄‖² = 4. With S = 3 and B = 5/2, the estimate permits 88³ for the norm. This compact example tests the word and energy formulas. The indefinite branching pair imposes additional signature and interlacing hypotheses.

How does the chosen translate affect the period derivative?

The vector finally inserted into the period coefficient is v = π(g₀)v₀, where the branching construction chooses g₀. Here the argument uses real Lie derivatives. Write C = 8mB and let v₀ have norm one. For m ≥ 3 and opposite-sign separation at least 1000m³, the independent one-place estimate gives, for a word of length r ≤ m + 2 in real Lie directions Yi, an upper bound ∏i(C²‖Yi‖F) on the norm of its value on v₀.

Indeed the one-place estimate, with its weight budget set to b = √B, gives a cost βP = (√m)B + (3/2)m3/2 + 3P after P derivatives. Here m ≥ 3 implies B ≥ 1, and a word of the indicated length has at most m + 1 previous derivatives. The three terms are bounded respectively by (mB)², (3/2)(mB)² and 2(mB)². Their sum is at most C². Multiplying the bounds for the successive real directions proves the assertion.

Suppose Y is a direction of the smaller unitary group with ‖Y‖F ≤ 1. Its image Y′ in the larger Lie algebra acts as zero on the distinguished line and has the same Frobenius norm. Differentiating exp(tY′)g₀ = g₀ exp(t g₀−1Y′g₀) twice moves both derivatives back to v₀. Put R = ‖g₀‖F. Since g₀−1 = Jg₀∗J for the diagonal signature matrix J, it has the same Frobenius norm. Submultiplicativity gives ‖g₀−1Y′g₀‖F ≤ R². The period argument therefore uses

‖dπ(Y′)²π(g₀)v₀‖ ≤ (C²R²)².

For a check choose one positive and one negative coordinate and set the corresponding block of g₀ to ((5/4, 3/4), (3/4, 5/4)). It preserves diag(1,−1). Fix two further coordinates, one the distinguished positive line. Choose Y′ to be i times the diagonal projector onto the positive coordinate of the block. Its norm is one. Conjugation gives the block (i/16)((25,15),(−15,−9)), of Frobenius norm 17/8. The four-dimensional matrix gives R² = 25/4, so the bound covers this increase. In the period proof the second derivative controls a Taylor remainder for the coefficient. Decay in the subgroup variable then makes the resulting bound integrable.
