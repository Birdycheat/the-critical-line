The degree-two bound for an exceptional twist

In the preceding window argument, a marked twist can approach an ℓ-adic root too closely for its valuation to give a uniform estimate. The proof returns to a finite layer, where it bounds a different quantity. Let ℓ be any prime and L a totally complex number field of degree n and absolute discriminant Δ. Assume L contains a primitive ℓth root of unity for odd ℓ, or a primitive fourth root when ℓ = 2. Let S be all finite primes over ℓ and GS the Galois group unramified outside them. The assertion holds for every algebraic closure, every choice of places above S and every compatible descended cyclotomic character.

For every integer e ≥ 1,   H²(GS, ℤℓ(e + 1)) is finite,   log |H²| ≤ KC(e,n,Δ) = exp((2en)C log(2Δ)).

One exponent works simultaneously for all these choices. Tracing three numerical witnesses in the proof gives C = 150. The theorem returned to the window argument quantifies existentially over C. The numerical estimate applies to log order, so a length bound is obtained by dividing by log ℓ. Finite-level duality, passage to the limit and the inequality

At a place over ℓ the decomposition group has cyclotomic image U of index 1 ≤ h ≤ n in ℤℓ×. An invariant of order ℓk requires ve ≡ 1 modulo ℓk for every v ∈ U. In particular (1 + ℓ²)h belongs to U, and the valuation of (1 + ℓ²)he − 1 is exactly 2 + vℓ(h) + vℓ(e). This controls the local length also at ℓ = 2. For example, with ℓ = 3, h = 2 and e = 3, the tested integer is 10⁶ − 1 = 27 · 37037 and its 3-adic valuation is 3. Summing over at most n places gives

length H² ≤ s(L) + n(2 + ⌊logℓ n⌋ + e).

This proves finite length. Each simple ℤℓ-quotient has ℓ elements, so log order equals length times log ℓ. Since ℓ ≤ n + 1 and ⌊logℓ n⌋ log ℓ ≤ log(n + 1), the local addition to log order is at most n(3 + e) log(n + 1). The source’s numerical majorant absorbs this addition by increasing the exponent from 141 to 150. This is a sufficient exponent read from the proof, with no claim of optimality.
