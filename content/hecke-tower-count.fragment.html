How many forms can fail one Satake test?

Let w be the split place where a nonzero polynomial Φ tests the Satake parameters. The level is hyperspecial there. At a second split place w0 above ℓ, refine the level along compact open subgroups Jn normal in J0, decreasing to the identity and eventually contained in every neighborhood of it. The places w and w0 lie over distinct primes of the maximal real subfield. This allows the test at w to be used at every depth at w0, including when both places divide ℓ.

Write K for the level away from w0, Cn for the full level obtained by adjoining Jn, and Hn = J0/Jn. Let Bw,n be the span of the Cn-fixed forms of weight W in the representations that fail the test at w. The count measures the dimension of this space, including the multiplicities of the contributing forms.

One-place counting theorem. For every ε > 0, at all sufficiently large depths n the space Bw,n is finite dimensional and

dimℂ Bw,n ≤ ε |Hn|.

The local input supplies, for every δ > 0, a Hecke operator with complex coefficients h(t) and finite support T such that ∑t∈T |h(t)|² ≤ δ. Its translations act at the tested prime, have trivial components above the prime under w0, and give distinct cosets tK under right multiplication by K. The operator acts as the identity on the K-fixed vectors of every representation that fails the test. Since K is contained in Cn, the operator therefore fixes Bw,n pointwise. All squared norms in this count use the ordinary complex absolute value. The local operator and its normalization.

Cover and separation. A finite set S0 of representatives suffices at depth zero. At depth n the cover has index set Xn = S0 × Hn. Its label y represents an adelic point py up to the actions of the rational group, the archimedean group and the level Cn. Distinct labels may represent the same double class. What the counting argument requires is that for any one starting point py, the translated points pyt for t ∈ T belong to distinct double classes. For the chosen finite support this holds at all sufficiently large depths, uniformly in the starting label. Covering the tower and separating the translates.

The matrix on evaluations. Choose a basis of the weight space of dimension d and evaluate each form at the d basis vectors and all py. These coordinates determine the form. Indeed, for an arbitrary adelic argument the covering expression removes the rational and level factors, while its archimedean factor acts linearly on the weight vector. For each translated point pyt, choose a covering label π(y,t) and write mij(y,t) for the corresponding weight change, so that its ith evaluation is the sum of the jth evaluations at that label with coefficients mij. All these coefficients have absolute value at most a constant L depending only on the weight and basis. The archimedean group is compact and its weight-matrix entries are continuous.

The matrix M computing the translated evaluations of the Hecke sum has entries

M(y,i),(z,j) = ∑t∈T, π(y,t)=z h(t) mij(y,t).

At sufficiently large depth each sum in an entry has at most one term, by separation at the starting label y. Summing squared absolute values first over the target labels, then over the two basis indices and the starting labels, yields the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm

‖M‖HS2 = ∑y,i,z,j |M(y,i),(z,j)|² ≤ |Xn| d²L² ∑t∈T |h(t)|².

The image of Bw,n under evaluation is fixed pointwise by M. Take an orthonormal basis of this image and extend it to the full coordinate space. The sum of ‖Mv‖² on an orthonormal basis is tr(M∗M), independent of the basis and equal to ‖M‖HS2. Each fixed basis vector contributes one. The injectivity of evaluation therefore bounds dim Bw,n by this same squared norm.

Now choose δ = ε/(1 + |S0|d²L²), select the local operator at this precision, and only then choose a depth separating its finite support. Using |Xn| = |S0| |Hn| gives the asserted estimate at every later depth. For a numerical model of the dimension bound, average the N shifts of a cyclic group with coefficient 1/N each and take the scalar weight. In every row the shifts occupy different columns, the squared norm is N·N·(1/N)² = 1, and the fixed space consists of the constants.
