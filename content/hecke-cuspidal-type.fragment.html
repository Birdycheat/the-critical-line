Why does each nontrivial character force cuspidality?

The cyclic character sum uses every nontrivial character of a single quotient. Some of these characters have small order. To treat them all, we construct an element mapping to the generator of the quotient in the unipotent radical of each proper parabolic, and in every conjugate of that radical.

Local type theorem. Let F be the completion of the number field at the chosen split place w₀ above ℓ, let G = GLa(F) with a ≥ 1, and fix an identity neighbourhood U and an integer m₀ ≥ 0. There exist m ≥ m₀, a torsion-free compact open subgroup J ⊆ U and a surjective homomorphism θ from J to the additive group ℤ/ℓmℤ with the following property. If a smooth irreducible complex representation σ of G contains a nonzero vector on which J acts by χ ∘ θ for some nontrivial character χ of this quotient, then σ is cuspidal. Here cuspidality means that the coinvariants of σ under the unipotent radical of every proper parabolic vanish.

The level and its cyclic quotient. Write 𝒪 for the valuation ring of F, κ for its residue field and v for the valuation normalized to take value 1 on a uniformizer. Start with a ≥ 2. Choose m ≥ max(2, m₀) sufficiently large that J = 1 + ℓmMa(𝒪) lies in U. These are compact open subgroups because 𝒪 is compact and open, all the displayed matrices reduce to the identity over κ, and the ideals ℓm𝒪 shrink to zero. This uses only v(ℓ) > 0, including at ramified places. To see that J is torsion-free, a nonidentity torsion element would have a power of prime order r, say 1 + Y. Let s be the least valuation of its nonzero entries in Y. In (1 + Y)r − 1 the linear term rY has an entry of valuation s + v(r), while every entry of a higher term has valuation at least 2s. Since s ≥ 2v(ℓ) and v(r) ≤ v(ℓ), that entry survives.

Put M = ℓm. The class of 1 in 𝒪/M𝒪 generates a copy of ℤ/Mℤ. Indeed, for a nonzero integer n, its valuation in F is v(ℓ) times its ℓ-adic valuation, so n lies in M𝒪 precisely when M divides n. On this cyclic subgroup send n to n/M modulo ℤ. Divisibility of the additive circle ℝ/ℤ extends this homomorphism to all of 𝒪/M𝒪. Every value is killed by M, so the extension takes values in (1/M)ℤ/ℤ. Identifying n/M with n modulo M gives an additive retraction φ onto ℤ/Mℤ with φ(1) = 1. Stacks Project, Lemma 15.55.1 (extension into a divisible abelian group).

Identify κa with the degree-a extension of κ. Let B̄ be multiplication by a generator of the multiplicative group of that field. If a B̄-stable κ-subspace contains a nonzero element, the successive powers of the generator carry this element through every nonzero element of the field. Thus the only stable subspaces are zero and the whole space. Lift the entries of B̄ to a matrix B over 𝒪 and define θ by the ordinary matrix trace. K. Conrad, Finite Fields, Lemma 1.6 and Theorem 5.1.

θ(1 + MX) = φ(tr(BX) mod M𝒪).

In the product of 1 + MX and 1 + MY, the coefficient of M is X + Y + MXY. Reduction modulo M removes the last term, so θ is a homomorphism. Its kernel contains 1 + M²Ma(𝒪), hence it is open.

An element mapping to 1 in each radical. Fix the unipotent radical N of a proper parabolic and any g ∈ G. The conjugate g−1Ng acts trivially on the successive quotients of its flag. Choose a nonzero proper step W of that flag. Any map X with image in W and vanishing on W lowers the flag. It kills each step up to W and maps every later step into W. Hence 1 + MX belongs to g−1Ng. To place it in J as well, we need integral entries.

Let L = W ∩ 𝒪a. Clearing denominators shows that L spans W over F. The lattice is saturated. Indeed, if a nonzero multiple of an integral vector lies in W, the vector itself lies in W. Thus 𝒪a/L is a finitely generated torsion-free module over the discrete valuation ring 𝒪, hence free. Lifting a basis of the quotient splits off a complement to L. Consequently the image L̄ of L in κa has dimension dimF W, strictly between 0 and a. Stacks Project, Lemma 15.22.11(3) (freeness over a principal ideal domain).

Since B̄ does not preserve L̄, choose y ∈ L for which By reduces outside L̄. In the free quotient 𝒪a/L at least one coordinate of the class of By is a unit. Pull that coordinate back to an integral linear functional f on 𝒪a. Its extension to F vanishes on W, and u = f(By) is a unit. The rank-one operator below therefore has integral entries.

X(z) = y f(z)/u,   X² = 0,   tr(BX) = f(By)/u = 1.

The square vanishes because f(y) = 0. The trace identity follows by summing the diagonal entries of the rank-one map z ↦ (By)f(z)/u. Its construction puts h = 1 + MX in J ∩ g−1Ng, with θ(h) = 1. Since 1 generates ℤ/Mℤ, this also proves surjectivity of θ.

Vanishing of the Jacquet module. Let v be a nonzero vector of σ with character χ ∘ θ. In the space of N-coinvariants we identify σ(n)x with x for all n ∈ N. For each g choose the element h just constructed, so that ghg−1 ∈ N. Writing brackets for the class in the quotient gives

[σ(g)v] = [σ(ghg−1)σ(g)v] = χ(1)[σ(g)v].

Every nontrivial character takes a value different from 1 on the additive generator, whatever its order. Thus each translate of v has zero class. Their span is a nonzero invariant subspace, hence the whole irreducible representation. This proves that its N-coinvariants vanish for every proper parabolic.

For example, take F = ℚ₂, a = 2 and M = 4. Choose B with rows (0, 1) and (1, 1). Its characteristic polynomial modulo 2 is t² + t + 1, which has no root in 𝔽₂. For the upper triangular parabolic and g = 1, choose X with its only nonzero entry equal to 1 in position (1, 2). Then tr(BX) = 1. The order-two character of ℤ/4ℤ takes the value −1 at 1 and the value 1 at 2. Our element therefore gives [v] = −[v] even for this character. An element mapping only to 2 would give the identity [v] = [v] and yield no information.

Rank one. For GL₁(F) there are no proper parabolics, so every smooth irreducible representation satisfies the stated cuspidality condition. Here we may use the requested exponent m = m₀ exactly. Choose d ≥ max(2, m) with J = 1 + ℓd𝒪 inside U. Send 1 + ℓdx to φ(x mod ℓm𝒪). The product formula again makes this a homomorphism, and 1 + ℓd maps to the generator. For m = 0 the quotient is trivial.
