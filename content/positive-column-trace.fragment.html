How can a signed pairing detect every column?

To decide whether a shifted eigenspace is empty, we need a test that detects a nonzero vector even if its signed length vanishes. Write J = diag(ε₁,…,εm), where εb = ±1 records the signature of U(p,q). On an m-tuple of vectors in π put B(f,h) = ∑b εb ⟨fb,hb⟩. We take inner products linear in the second entry. For signature (+,−), the column (x,x) has signed length zero, even for a nonzero x. Pairing it with (x,−x) gives 2‖x‖².

Fix a nonzero vector y in the lowest compact type of π and its formal degree d for the chosen Haar measure. Let A(g) = (g−1)ᵗ on the dual defining representation. For derivative-generated columns define zf,b(g) = ⟨y,π(g)fb⟩ and Φ(f)(g) = JA(g)zf(g). Each coordinate is in L² by the weighted coefficient estimate for the separated original parameter. This use of the weight matters because A(g) may grow. Denote by J also the operation that changes signs coordinatewise on L²(G)m.

⟨Φ(f), JΦ(h)⟩ = (‖y‖²/d) B(f,h).

Indeed A(g)*JA(g) = J. This follows by taking inverses and conjugating the defining relation g*Jg = J, since J is real and J² = I. Thus the signed pointwise inner product reduces to that of zf and zh. Integrate and apply Schur orthogonality in π separately to each coordinate. Finally take the pair (Jf,f) in the identity. If Φ(f) vanishes, then (‖y‖²/d)∑‖fb‖² vanishes as well. This proves injectivity. Duflo and Moore, orthogonality relations for square-integrable representations, in the unimodular case.

Why is the partial trace a sum of ordinary squared norms?

Let P be the projector for one tensor eigenvalue. On generated columns it is idempotent, commutes with compact translations and tensor derivatives, and satisfies B(Pf,h) = B(f,Ph). Suppose its partial trace is cI. We want the sign of c. The ordinary Hilbert space where we can compute norms is the closure HP of the span of Φ(Pf). Right translation R(g) preserves this space. To see why, let V be the finite-dimensional lowest compact type. The starting space ΦP(Vm) is finite-dimensional and compact-stable. It generates every Φ(Pf) by derivatives. Indeed a tensor derivative is the sum of its actions on the matrix slot and the coefficient slot. Subtract the first action, which only mixes matrix coordinates, and induct on the number of coefficient derivatives. The tensor Casimir on these projected generators has scalar cπ+m+2z, where cπ is the original Casimir scalar and z is the tensor differential eigenvalue. Its three terms come from the coefficient slot, the matrix slot, and twice their mixed operator. Symmetry on the unitary coefficient space makes this scalar real whenever the image is nonzero. The derivative-closure theorem applies to their derivatives. In particular the ordinary orthogonal projection Q onto HP commutes with these unitary translations.

Write eby for a column with y in slot b only. Set ub = Φ(Peby), κ = d/‖y‖² and wb = κQJub. Each wb reads one coordinate of a translated vector:

vb(g) = ⟨wb, R(g)v⟩   for almost every g,   v ∈ HP.

For a generator v = Φ(Pf), its translate is the coefficient column of the translated tensor vector, since both are evaluated by replacing x with xg. The components of the translated tensor are in the closed original representation space and its coefficient functions remain in L² by right invariance of Haar measure. These are exactly the hypotheses of the signed pairing calculation. Pair this with JΦ(eby) in the signed column identity. Multiplying by κ gives exactly the value of coordinate b at g. We may first replace eby by Peby. Indeed B((I−P)η,Pf) = 0 by idempotence and signed symmetry, hence JΦ((I−P)η) is perpendicular to the closed space containing the translate. Its orthogonal projection is zero. For a limit of generators the coordinates converge in L², and along a subsequence almost everywhere. On the other side the inner products converge uniformly in g, since R(g) is an isometry. For the subsequence, choose successive L² errors at most 2−2j. The sets with error greater than 2−j then have summable measures, so their tail unions have measure tending to zero.

Computing ⟨y,(ptr P)y⟩ slot by slot gives c‖y‖² = ∑b εb B(Peby,Peby). The signed column identity makes each B on the right equal to ⟨ub,wb⟩. Expand these last inner products by coordinate. The integral comparing (ub)a and (wb)a is the complex conjugate of the integral with a,b exchanged. To see this, use the displayed coordinate formula for both functions and change g to g−1 in Haar measure. Unitarity exchanges the slots of two coordinate readers. For the mixed pair, ⟨wa,R(g)ub⟩ = κ⟨ua,R(g)Jub⟩ = ⟨ua,R(g)wb⟩. In the first equality we may drop Q because R(g)ub belongs to its range. In the second we may insert it because R(g−1)ua also belongs to its range. Conjugation then exchanges the indices at g−1. On summing, the sign εb now acts on the coordinate b inside each vector. Thus

c‖y‖² = ∑a ⟨Jua, wa⟩ = κ ∑a ‖QJua‖²,   c = (1/d)∑a ‖wa‖².

If c = 0, all coordinate readers vanish, so every element of HP has every coordinate zero almost everywhere. In particular Φ(Pf) = 0 for each generated column f. Injectivity of Φ gives Pf = 0. This proves the positivity and the vanishing criterion used at the colliding weight.

When does one shifted representation fill the projected space?

Suppose a copy of a shifted irreducible square-integrable representation has already been placed in HP. Write H′ for this nonzero closed invariant subspace, E for orthogonal projection onto it, and d′ for its formal degree in the same Haar measure as d. We can measure exactly how much of each coordinate reader lies in this copy. For v ∈ H′ every translate belongs to H′, so ⟨wb,R(g)v⟩ = ⟨Ewb,R(g)v⟩. Integrate its squared modulus using Schur orthogonality in H′, sum in b, and choose v nonzero to cancel ‖v‖². This gives ∑‖Ewb‖² = d′. The trace formula and Pythagoras now give an exact remainder:

d c − d′ = ∑b ‖(I − E)wb‖² ≥ 0.

At equality every wb lies in H′. Take v in HP perpendicular to H′. For every g we have ⟨R(g−1)wb,v⟩ = 0, so every coordinate of v vanishes almost everywhere. Hence HP = H′. For a regular lowering the formal-degree product gives d′/d equal to the residue at that shift. This proves saturation in the Hilbert coefficient space once the algebraic embedding has supplied the copy. Identifying individual algebraic tensor vectors in its image additionally uses compact finiteness in this copy.

For a finite example take the four-element group of pairs of bits and normalized counting measure. On the constants together with the character χ(a,b) = (−1)a, the function w = 1 + χ represents evaluation at the identity. Its squared norm is 2. Projection onto the constants leaves a reader of squared norm 1, the formal degree of the trivial representation. The difference 1 measures the second character. Restrict the original space to constants alone and the difference vanishes.
