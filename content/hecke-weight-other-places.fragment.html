Which parts of the original weight survive the projection?

The two matrices in the weight family change under one archimedean factor. At another archimedean place the matrices stay fixed. We show that every nonzero projected component there has the original weight. We then identify the vector giving the required finite level.

Local weights of a constituent. Let U be an irreducible automorphic constituent on the definite unitary group. At any archimedean factor H ≃ U(a), take the span of the H-translates of a nonzero form in U. This span is finite-dimensional by the defining archimedean finiteness of the form. Averaging a positive Hermitian product over the compact group makes it invariant. Orthogonal complements are then invariant, so this space contains an irreducible summand. The Cartan–Weyl classification gives its unique dominant integral highest weight. The decomposition into copies at this factor shows that U is a direct sum of copies of this type when restricted to H. Another irreducible summand projects nontrivially to at least one of these copies. Such an intertwiner between irreducibles is an isomorphism, by looking at its kernel and image. Thus the same dominant weight is obtained however the first vector is chosen. D. Vogan, Theorem 2.12 (Cartan–Weyl for the unitary group).

Keeping the unmodified factors. The original weight space W is the tensor product over the archimedean places of its local weight spaces. Let BU be a nonzero projected family and fix y₁ and y₂ for which the linear map in the weight vector is nonzero. Pure tensors span W, so one pure tensor gives a nonzero value. Choose a place v′ different from the archimedean place of τ. Fix every slot of this tensor except the one at v′. Varying that slot defines a nonzero map from the original local weight space into U.

An element x of the unitary group supported at v′ has matrix equal to the identity at τ. In the archimedean transformation law it changes only the chosen tensor slot. The map just constructed therefore intertwines the two actions at v′. The uniqueness just proved identifies the weight of U there with the original weight. A CM archimedean place has two conjugate embedding labels. Passing to the conjugate embedding conjugates the unitary matrix, negating its torus weights. The dominant member of the opposite extremal orbit is the negative list in reverse order. Applying this operation to the same local weight on both sides gives agreement at the conjugate label as well.

For example, if the original weight at a second real place has label (3, 1), its conjugate label is (−1, −3). Both stay fixed as the weight at the selected place varies. Finally, take any nonzero value of BU. It lies in U and is fixed by the required finite level K, by the projection identities. This supplies the level and the unmodified weights for every retained system. At τ itself the two partial-sum inequalities give the separate entrywise error 2d used to choose the gaps.
