Why do permutations account for every invariant tensor?

Let W be a finite set of places and V = ⊕w∈W ℂN. A separate unitary matrix uw acts on each summand. On V⊗q the resulting operator is D⊗q, where D = diag(uw). To recover the invariant forms, we need the matrices T that commute with every such operator. The proof works for all finite block sizes N and tensor degrees q, including N < q. A basis index I is a word of q pairs (place, coordinate). For a place pattern w = (w₁,…,wq) and a permutation σ, define Pσ,w by its rows and columns.

(Pσ,w)I,J = 1 if place(Ii) = wi for every i and J = I ∘ σ, and 0 otherwise.

These matrices commute with every D⊗q. Indeed, a block unitary preserves the place pattern and the same D acts in every slot. Let 𝒜 be their complex span and 𝒰 the span of the unitary tensor powers. Write 𝒮′ for the matrices commuting with all of 𝒮. The archive proves 𝒜′ ⊆ 𝒰 by interpolation and 𝒜″ ⊆ 𝒜 by orthogonal projection. Thus 𝒰′ = 𝒜. Suppose a complex linear functional φ vanishes on 𝒰. For any block diagonal D, we claim φ(D⊗q) = 0. Start with Hermitian blocks and fix their unitary eigenbases. As the diagonal eigenvalues vary, the expression is polynomial of degree at most q in each variable. The product Pσ,wPτ,v vanishes unless v = w ∘ σ, in which case it is Pσ∘τ,w. The adjoint is Pσ⁻¹,w∘σ, and the sum of all Pid,w is the identity. Thus 𝒜 is a unital algebra closed under adjoints.
