Norm primes in short intervals without an ineffective constant

Why can both the dyadic selection of the two auxiliary primes g0, g1 and the selection of every word’s level prime Tu inside a narrow multiplicative window of relative width 1/(fk) proceed unconditionally, without introducing any ineffective Siegel constant? In an arbitrary rational residue class modulo a large integer, a real Dirichlet character can carry an exceptional zero whose distance from s = 1 is controlled only ineffectively once the exponent drops below one. Here, however, the global lattice construction does not ask for primes in a general rational progression. Instead it seeks rational primes p = ππ̄ that split in the fixed imaginary quadratic field K = ℚ(√(−d)) (with d prime, d ≡ 3 modulo 4 and d ≥ 100) and admit a factor π ≡ 1 modulo M𝒪K. Such primes are governed by the ray class group G of K modulo M𝒪K. For any real character χ of G, the Dedekind–ray-class product series formed by convolving the trivial character with χ has nonnegative Dirichlet coefficients. Testing that series near s = 1 against a nonnegative Bessel-type smoothing kernel yields an effective polynomial lower bound at s = 1 in the conductor scale B = 4dM², and hence an effective zero repulsion away from s = 1.

Theorem (Short-interval norm primes). There exist absolute constants A > 0, c > 0 and c′ > 0 with the following property. Let d be a prime satisfying d ≡ 3 modulo 4 and d ≥ 100, let M ≥ 3 be an integer, put B = 4dM², and let X ≥ 1 and h be real numbers satisfying log B ≤ c√(log X) and exp(−c√(log X)) ≤ h ≤ 1. Then the short interval (X, X(1 + h)] satisfies

#{p ∈ (X, X(1 + h)] ∩ ℕ : p prime, ∃ A0, B0 ∈ ℤ, A0² + dB0² = 4p, M ∣ B0, 2M ∣ A0 − 2 − B0} ≥ c′hX / ((4dM²)A log X).

Proof. Since d ≡ 3 modulo 4, we have −d ≡ 1 modulo 4 and the ring of integers of K = ℚ(√(−d)) is 𝒪K = ℤ[(1 + √(−d))/2]. A rational prime p admits integers A0, B0 satisfying 4p = A0² + dB0², M ∣ B0 and 2M ∣ A0 − 2 − B0 if and only if p = ππ̄ for some algebraic integer π ∈ 𝒪K with π − 1 ∈ M𝒪K. Such a prime is necessarily coprime to M and splits in K into principal-ray-class prime ideals belonging to the trivial class of the ray class group G of K modulo M𝒪K. Injecting G into Cl(𝒪K) × (𝒪K/M𝒪K) and applying Minkowski’s discriminant bound hK ≤ |ΔK| = d ≤ 4d bounds the group order by |G| ≤ hKM² ≤ 4dM² = B, while d ≥ 100 > 3 (so 𝒪K× = {±1}) and M ≥ 3 make generators congruent to 1 modulo M𝒪K unique and give each ray class ideal-count density 2π/(√d M²).

By finite abelian character orthogonality on G, the von Mangoldt prime-power weight restricted to the trivial ray class over (X, X(1 + h)] is the average (1/|G|)∑χ∈Ĝ [ψ(X(1 + h); χ) − ψ(X; χ)] of the ray-class Chebyshev differences. Four analytic properties of the ray-class L-functions L(s, χ) at parameter B = 4dM² control this character sum:

  • Across all χ ∈ Ĝ, L(s, χ) obeys a standard logarithmic zero-free region in terms of B and |Im s|, up to possible real zeros near s = 1.
  • Across the entire dual group Ĝ, at most one real character can vanish in that real exceptional segment, and any such exceptional zero β is unique and simple.
  • For every real character χ ∈ Ĝ, nonnegativity of the quadratic Dirichlet convolution coefficients paired with a positive Bessel-type smoothing kernel produces an effective polynomial lower bound at s = 1, forcing any real exceptional zero β to satisfy 1 − β ≥ δ, where δ = c9/BA1 for explicit constants 0 < c9 ≤ 1 and A1 > 0.
  • The truncated Perron explicit formula on (X, X(1 + h)] extracts the pole contribution hX from the principal character, bounds the magnitude of the optional exceptional-zero term by at most hX(1 − δ/2), and—for sufficiently small c > 0 under log B ≤ c√(log X) and exp(−c√(log X)) ≤ h ≤ 1—bounds the sum of the contour-truncation and zero-sum errors over all |G| ≤ B characters by at most hXδ/4.

Combining the three contributions in the character sum leaves at least hX − hX(1 − δ/2) − hXδ/4 = hXδ/4. Dividing by |G| ≤ B bounds the trivial-class prime-power weight on (X, X(1 + h)] below by hXδ/(4B) = c9hX/(4BA1+1). Subtracting higher prime powers costs O(√X), which is absorbed into half of this main term on the admitted range of B and h. Each surviving prime p ∈ (X, X(1 + h)] splits into at most two prime ideals of K and therefore contributes at most 2 log(X(1 + h)) ≤ 4 log X to the weighted sum. Dividing by 4 log X and setting A = A1 + 1 yields at least c′hX/((4dM²)A log X) norm primes in (X, X(1 + h)].

Quadratic congruence equivalence and prime-power absorption

Write an arbitrary element of 𝒪K = ℤ[(1 + √(−d))/2] as π = u + v(1 + √(−d))/2 with u, v ∈ ℤ, and set A0 = 2u + v and B0 = v. Then 4ππ̄ = (2u + v)² + dv² = A0² + dB0², and conversely any integers A0, B0 solving A0² + dB0² = 4p have the same parity because d is odd, so u = (A0 − B0)/2 and v = B0 are integers defining π ∈ 𝒪K of norm p. Moreover, π − 1 = (u − 1) + v(1 + √(−d))/2 belongs to M𝒪K if and only if M divides both v = B0 and u − 1 = (A0 − 2 − B0)/2, which is equivalent to M ∣ B0 and 2M ∣ A0 − 2 − B0. If a prime ℓ divided both p and M, then π ≡ 1 modulo ℓ𝒪K would force p = ππ̄ ≡ 1 modulo ℓ, a contradiction; hence p is coprime to M.

For the prime-power tail and logarithmic divisor, note that d ≥ 100 and M ≥ 3 give B = 4dM² ≥ 3600, so taking c small enough in log B ≤ c√(log X) forces X ≥ 2 and keeps hB−AX1/2 ≥ exp(−(1 + A)c√(log X) + (1/2) log X) arbitrarily large, absorbing the O(√X) contribution of prime squares and higher powers into half of c9hX/(4BA). Finally, since h ≤ 1 and X ≥ 2, we have X(1 + h) ≤ 2X ≤ X², whence 2 log(X(1 + h)) ≤ 4 log X.

Specializing the theorem to relative width h = 1 on a dyadic interval (Y, 2Y] with log(4dM²) ≤ c√(log Y) absorbs the denominator (4dM²)A log Y into Y1/10 for all sufficiently large Y, producing at least Y9/10 split norm-one primes in (Y, 2Y]. Applied on the global route at base conductor N = QS · q · d (for the positive host integer QS), rank 1 ≤ r ≤ x⁵ (with 1 ≤ k ≤ r) and dyadic scale Y = (kb)⁸, where b = exp(L) and L = xε, at most (3r + 1)(1 + 2r²(Y3/4 + 1)) ≤ 20r³Y3/4 < Y9/10 candidates in [Y, 2Y] can share an exceptional cyclotomic prime divisor through exponent r; this extracts two distinct auxiliary primes g0 ≠ g1 in [(kb)⁸, 2(kb)⁸] with disjoint exceptional divisor sets, each represented by an element of 𝒪K congruent to 1 modulo N𝒪K and satisfying log gi ≤ 9L once 2(kb)⁸ ≤ b⁹.

Selecting each word’s level prime in its short window

Once the base conductor N = QS · q · d, the two auxiliary primes g0, g1, the positive normalized row and column masses D★, du★ and the auxiliary factor Γ > 1 have been fixed, each word u prescribes a target real scale Xu = (D★/du★)1/(fk) and a lower window endpoint Tu* = XuΓ−2/(fk). Why is the relative window width h = 1/(fk) simultaneously wide enough to contain a split ray-class prime and narrow enough to preserve the mass comparison up to a single factor of exp(1)? On the analytic side, the lower word-scale budget forces log Tu* to grow at least as fast as csmL with m ≥ x1/3 and L = xε (0 < ε < 1/52), so √(log Tu*) grows at least like x1/6+ε/2 and polynomially dominates both the log-modulus of N(g0g1)² and log(fk). On the geometric side, raising the upper endpoint (1 + 1/(fk))Tu* to the fk-th power multiplies (Tu*)fk = D★/(du★Γ²) by at most (1 + 1/(fk))fk ≤ exp(1), regardless of the rank product fk.

Proposition (Unconditional word-level prime selection and window bounds). Fix 0 < ε < 1/52, cs > 0 and CY > 0 (with CY = 9 on the global route). Let d ≥ 100 be a prime with d ≡ 3 modulo 4, let N = QS · q · d > 0 be divisible by d with log N ≤ 3xε/5(log x)², let g0, g1 satisfy 1 ≤ gi ≤ exp(CYL) where L = xε, and put M = N(g0g1)². Suppose that the integers m and f, k ≥ 1 satisfy m ≥ x1/3 and 1 ≤ V = fk ≤ x12. For any word u with D★ > 0, du★ > 0, Γ > 1 and csmL ≤ log Tu*, where Xu = (D★/du★)1/(fk) and Tu* = XuΓ−2/(fk), all sufficiently large x ≥ x0 admit a rational prime Tu ∈ (Tu*, (1 + 1/(fk))Tu*] ⊆ [Tu*, (1 + 1/(fk))Tu*], distinct from any two pre-assigned host primes strictly below X′ = exp(xε/3), satisfying Tu ≡ 1 modulo QS · q · d together with an algebraic integer πu ∈ 𝒪E ∩ K of the host CM field E ⊇ K such that πuπ̄u = Tu and πu − 1 ∈ N(g0g1)²𝒪E. Moreover, for Γ ≥ 1, every prime Tu ∈ [Tu*, (1 + 1/(fk))Tu*] satisfies

du★ Tufk ≤ (exp(1)/Γ²) D★,   log Xu − 2 log Γ / (fk) ≤ log Tu ≤ log Tu* + 1 ≤ log Xu + 1.

Proof. Since d divides N and gi ≥ 1, the combined modulus M = N(g0g1)² satisfies M ≥ N ≥ d ≥ 100 ≥ 3 and 4dM² ≤ 4N³(g0g1)⁴. Taking logarithms gives log(4dM²) ≤ log 4 + 3 log N + 4(log g0 + log g1) ≤ log 4 + 9xε/5(log x)² + 8CYxε. On the other hand, csmL ≤ log Tu* and m ≥ x1/3 imply log Tu* ≥ csx1/3+ε, whence √(log Tu*) ≥ √cs x1/6+ε/2. Because 0 < ε < 1/52, the exponent 1/6 + ε/2 strictly exceeds both ε and ε/5. Above a threshold x0 independent of u, we therefore have Tu* ≥ 1, log(4dM²) ≤ c√(log Tu*), and log(fk) ≤ 12 log x ≤ c√(log Tu*), the last inequality placing h = 1/(fk) inside [exp(−c√(log Tu*)), 1].

The short-interval norm-prime theorem applied to (d, M, Tu*, 1/(fk)) now furnishes a prime Tu ∈ (Tu*, (1 + 1/(fk))Tu*] ⊆ [Tu*, (1 + 1/(fk))Tu*] and an element πu ∈ 𝒪K with πuπ̄u = Tu and πu − 1 ∈ M𝒪K. Since K ⊆ E has ring of integers 𝒪K ⊆ 𝒪E ∩ K and complex conjugation on E restricts to quadratic conjugation on K, we obtain πu ∈ 𝒪E ∩ K and πu − 1 ∈ M𝒪E = N(g0g1)²𝒪E. Expanding Tu − 1 = πuπ̄u − 1 shows that M divides Tu − 1 in ℤ, whence Tu ≡ 1 modulo QS · q · d because N = QS · q · d divides M. Once x ≥ max(2, cs−4), we have csm ≥ csx1/4 ≥ 1 and xε/3 ≤ xε = L ≤ csmL, so both pre-assigned host primes lie strictly below X′ = exp(xε/3) ≤ exp(csmL) ≤ Tu* ≤ Tu.

Rational congruence descent and the three window inequalities

Since πu − 1 ∈ M𝒪K and quadratic conjugation preserves M𝒪K, we also have π̄u − 1 ∈ M𝒪K. Consequently

(Tu − 1)/M = (πu − 1)(π̄u − 1)/M + (πu + π̄u − 2)/M ∈ ℚ ∩ 𝒪K = ℤ,

because ℤ is integrally closed in ℚ. Thus M divides Tu − 1 as rational integers, and in particular QS · q · d = N divides Tu − 1.

For the three quantitative estimates on [Tu*, (1 + 1/(fk))Tu*], the definitions Xufk = D★/du★ and Tu* = XuΓ−2/(fk) give (Tu*)fk = D★/(du★Γ²). Using 1 + t ≤ exp(t) at t = 1/(fk) yields (1 + 1/(fk))fk ≤ exp(1), so du★Tufk ≤ du★(1 + 1/(fk))fk(Tu*)fk ≤ (exp(1)/Γ²)D★; when the ramified column dimension satisfies d0(D, u) = du★(Tuk − 1)f ≤ du★Tufk, this gives d0(D, u) ≤ (exp(1)/Γ²)D★ = D★ exp(1 − 2 log Γ), and hence d0(D, u) ≤ D★ exp(−2Cm⁵fL) whenever 1 + 2Cm⁵fL ≤ 2 log Γ. Taking logarithms of Tu ≥ Tu* gives log Tu ≥ log Xu − 2 log Γ/(fk) (losing at most 2 whenever log Γ/(fk) ≤ 1), while Tu ≤ (1 + 1/(fk))Tu* and log(1 + 1/(fk)) ≤ 1/(fk) ≤ 1 give log Tu ≤ log Tu* + 1 ≤ log Xu + 1 because Γ ≥ 1 makes −2 log Γ/(fk) ≤ 0.
