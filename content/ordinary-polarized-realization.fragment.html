How does the Hodge graph give an abelian scheme with an action of the order?

Fix the abelian variety A0 at our geometric point of the unitary moduli problem. Its field k is algebraically closed of characteristic q. Put Δ = k[ε]/(ε²) and extend the map from the coefficient base to k by the inclusion k → Δ. Let Fil be the Hodge subspace of the covariant first de Rham module H of A0. The graph construction starts from an admissible endomorphism D of H and produces a summand G in the crystalline module over Δ. This module records the de Rham homology of a lift before its Hodge subspace has been specified.

Realization with endomorphisms. There is an abelian scheme A over Δ, with its fibre identified with A0, whose Hodge subspace is G under crystalline comparison. Every endomorphism in the prescribed order 𝒪 lifts to A, and these lifts form an action of 𝒪.

Proof. The ideal (ε) has square zero and carries the divided powers with γ1(u) = u and γj(u) = 0 for j ≥ 2. The crystalline deformation theorem says that, on such a thickening in characteristic q, a direct summand lifting the Hodge subspace specifies a lift of the q-divisible group. Its morphism criterion says that a homomorphism on the special fibre lifts precisely when its crystalline map carries the source summand into the target summand. Such a lift is unique for fixed source and target lifts. Here the q-divisible group is the system of kernels of multiplication by q, q² and all higher powers, with their inclusions. The Serre–Tate theorem gives the corresponding statements for abelian schemes with identified special fibre. These results apply also when q = 2 with the divided powers just specified. Messing, The Crystals Associated to Barsotti–Tate Groups and Chai and Oort, Theorems 2.4 and 2.7.

Here the constant abelian scheme A0 ×k Δ already supplies a reference lift for evaluating the crystal. Under this comparison, G is a direct summand and reduces to Fil, so the theorem gives the required A. For a coefficient b ∈ 𝒪, apply its action σ(b) to a graph element x + ε(D(x) + u), with x, u ∈ Fil. Its reduction is σ(b)x ∈ Fil. The difference between its first-order coefficient and D(σ(b)x) belongs to Fil by the order condition. Thus the crystalline endomorphism preserves the graph, so [b]0 lifts uniquely to [b]. The identity, sum and composite of lifted homomorphisms reduce to the corresponding operations on A0. Uniqueness now gives [1] = id, [b + c] = [b] + [c] and [bc] = [b] ∘ [c].

Why does the polarization lift, and what happens to its degree?

The polarization datum on the special fibre is a line bundle P0 on A0 × A0. It is trivial on the two axes through the identity. It is symmetric under exchange of the factors, its pullback to the diagonal is ample, and its adjoint on the order is the prescribed involution b ↦ b∗. For each point y of the second factor the restriction to the first factor gives a point of the dual abelian variety. Denote the resulting homomorphism by λ0 ∶ A0 → A0∨.

Polarized realization. If the graph is isotropic for the crystalline pairing of P0, the lifted abelian scheme carries a bundle P with these same properties and with restriction isomorphic to P0. The corresponding isogeny λ has the same degree as λ0. An integer e prime to q that kills ker λ0 also kills ker λ.

Proof. The dual abelian scheme A∨ represents rigidified families of line bundles algebraically equivalent to zero. Its universal family on A × A∨ is the Poincaré bundle. The associated perfect crystalline pairing identifies the Hodge subspace of A∨ with the annihilator of the Hodge subspace of A. Hence λ0 respects the two lifted filtrations exactly when its crystalline pairing vanishes on G × G. Indeed, for each second argument v in the graph the pairing with u is evaluation of the image of v under the crystalline map induced by λ0 against u. Vanishing for every u in the graph says that this image lies in the annihilator. The morphism criterion now lifts λ0 to λ ∶ A → A∨. Pull back the Poincaré bundle along (id, λ) ∶ A × A → A × A∨ to obtain P. This construction accounts for its restriction and its trivializations along the axes. Robert, Theory of abelian varieties and their moduli spaces, Theorem 2.3.7 on the dual abelian scheme, and Berthelot, Breen and Messing, crystalline duality, Proposition 5.1.10.

Symmetry identifies λ0 with its dual. The two lifts λ and λ∨ are equal by uniqueness, so exchanging the factors carries P to an isomorphic bundle. Similarly the special-fibre relation λ0 ∘ [b]0 = [b∗]0∨ ∘ λ0 lifts to the same identity over Δ. It says that acting on one argument of P by b has the same effect as acting on the other by b∗. Finally the diagonal pullback of P reduces to the ample diagonal pullback of P0. Ampleness is detected on this nilpotent thickening. The Stacks Project, Lemma 32.11.4 on restriction of an ample line bundle.

The kernel. The moduli datum supplies an integer e prime to q annihilating ker λ0. In particular this kernel has zero tangent space, since the differential of [e] is multiplication by the unit e. The differential of λ0 between spaces of the same dimension is therefore invertible. Its lift remains invertible over Δ, and translation gives the same conclusion everywhere. Thus λ is étale and, being proper and quasi-finite, finite. Its kernel is finite étale over Δ, so its rank equals that of its special fibre. This rank is the degree of λ. Moreover, multiplication by e and the zero map on this kernel agree after reduction. A finite étale target has unique infinitesimal lifts, so these two maps agree over Δ. The conclusion concerns the same annihilating integer e for all test schemes. The degree is determined by the special-fibre kernel and need not equal e. This argument uses the invertibility of e also when q = 2. The Stacks Project, finite morphisms and proper quasi-finite morphisms and the infinitesimal criterion for étaleness.

How do the level, the Lie conditions and the prescribed first derivatives fit together?

Realization in the moduli problem. Let the principal level N ≥ 3 be prime to q. The abelian scheme with order action and polarization constructed above admits a level structure lifting the original one and satisfies the two Lie conditions of the original unitary datum. Together these give an object over Δ whose reduction is identified with the starting object. For every x ∈ Fil, the lifted filtration contains a vector with reduction x and first-order coefficient D(x).

The level. If Λ is the lattice of the datum, its original marking sends v ∈ Λ to a section η0(v) killed by N. The differential of [N] is multiplication by N, which is invertible over Δ. Thus [N] is étale and there is a unique N-torsion section η(v) above η0(v). The sum of the lifts for v and w and the lift for v + w reduce to the same section and are both killed by N. They coincide by uniqueness. The same argument applied to [b]η(v) gives compatibility with 𝒪. At every geometric point, the original prime-to-q Tate markings, their transition maps and their Weil pairings are transported across the identified fibre. Indeed a ring map from Δ to a field kills ε, and the line bundle defining the pairing restricts to the original one. Thus η also meets the geometric level condition. The Stacks Project, Lemma 37.8.10 on formal étaleness.

The Lie conditions. Let ω be the quadratic element of the datum, r its selected root and χi the coefficient characters. The first condition is an identity of tangent vectors after every scalar extension Δ → S. Each vector in Lie(AS) on which ω acts as r must be a sum of vectors on which the central coefficients act through the individual χi, with the scalars mapped into S. Over Δ this is the signature calculation in the graph quotient. To pass to S, the projector onto the selected root is (σ(ω) − r′)/(r − r′), where r′ is the other root. Their difference is a unit. On its image the coefficient labels are separated, so the operators selecting the individual characters add to the identity and obey the character identities there. These identities persist after extension of scalars. The tangent module of a smooth group commutes with base change, so the resulting sum is the required decomposition over S, including when S has nilpotents.

The second condition prescribes the dimension m of each labelled tangent eigenspace at every geometric point. Every such point of Spec Δ factors through Spec k, and the fibre identification respects the order action. Its tangent eigenspaces therefore have exactly the dimensions prescribed on A0. In the ordinarity argument m = 1. Decomposition must hold over every test algebra, whereas multiplicity is prescribed on geometric fibres.

The prescribed derivative. Under crystalline comparison the lifted Hodge filtration is exactly G. Taking the element x + εD(x) in this graph realizes the required coefficient. More generally x + ε(D(x) + u) lies in the same filtration for every u ∈ Fil. Thus the intrinsic first-order coefficient lies in H/Fil. At the vector selected in the generic ordinarity proof, D(x) lies outside Fil, so its class is nonzero. A further argument using the generic point supplies a lift of that same vector with zero coefficient in the same filtration. Comparing the two gives the contradiction.
