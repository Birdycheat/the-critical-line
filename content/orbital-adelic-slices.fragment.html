How does cancellation at one place eliminate an adelic orbital term?

At the chosen split place, write the group away from p as GLn(k) × H, and the level as K₀ × K′. Here H carries the remaining coordinates. Let h be a Hecke function at this level. It is invariant under the level on each side and supported on finitely many level double cosets. For each z ∈ H take the slice hz(g) = h(g, z). This is a locally constant function on GLn(k) by right invariance under the open subgroup K₀. Its support lies in the union of the projections of those finitely many double cosets. Each projected double coset K₀gK₀ is compact because K₀ is compact. Assume every slice has vanishing constant terms in the sense of the local theorem. For the Hecke test used in the point count, the compact character supplies this hypothesis.

Let γ have coordinates (a, b), suppose its centralizer is abelian, and assume a has separable reducible characteristic polynomial over k. Write T₁ and T₂ for the centralizers of a and b. Commutation in a product is componentwise, so the centralizer of γ is T₁ × T₂. A double class has representatives (x, y), with x running through T₁∖GLn(k)/K₀ and y through T₂∖H/K′. At (x, y) both the reference group and the stabilizer are products. An index of product subgroups is the product of the two indices, by the bijection on cosets. The orbital weight therefore has the form v₁(x)v₂(y).

∑x v₁(x)v₂(y) h(x−1ax, y−1by) = v₂(y) Oa(hy−1by) = 0.

Here Oa is the local orbital sum at level K₀. Fixing y specifies a single slice and leaves its second-coordinate weight constant. The local theorem makes its orbital sum zero. When the full summand has finite support we may now sum over the finitely many contributing y. The convention for arbitrary support is the one in the local calculation. The argument allows the local test to change with y. For a finite illustration, take three points of weight one in the first coordinate. Two slices with values (1, −1, 0) and (0, 2, −2) both sum to zero. They are linearly independent, so the same cancellation applies even when the test cannot be expressed as a single product of functions of the two coordinates.
