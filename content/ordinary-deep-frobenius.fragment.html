What happens to the lifted tangent after Frobenius?

Let A be the abelian variety at the geometric point of the unitary moduli problem. Its ground field k is algebraically closed of prime characteristic q. We keep the algebra degree m = 1 and level N ≥ 3 prime to q. The chosen residue character χ of 𝒪F has kernel 𝔴 and image of order Q = qf, with f ≥ 1. Denote the action of a coefficient b on A by [b]. Write Fr for absolute qth-power Frobenius on A. We first show why [a] factors through Frf for the coefficient a chosen at 𝔴.

The coefficient and the Hodge space. The local ideal calculation supplies a ∈ 𝔴 and e, s ∈ 𝒪F with e² ≡ e modulo q and a(1 − e) = qfs. Let L be the first Hodge filtration on primitive de Rham cohomology in degree one. The pullbacks ρ(b) = [b]∗ preserve L and give it an action of 𝒪F modulo q. Thus E = ρ(e) is a projector. The unitary Lie algebra calculation gives the following input at the geometric point. Its selected Hodge summand EL is a line on which every b acts as χ(b).

Extracting one Frobenius factor. Suppose θ is a group morphism on A over an automorphism σ of k, and θ∗L = 0. Invariant one-forms are dual to the tangent space at the identity, so θ has zero differential there. Translations give zero differential at every geometric point. Locally the pullback of each regular function is therefore a qth power by the degree-zero Cartier isomorphism for smooth schemes over a perfect field. These roots are unique on the reduced scheme A. They agree on overlaps and define a map θ′ with θ = θ′ ∘ Fr. Uniqueness also gives the group law for θ′, and its action on scalars is λ ↦ σ(λ)1/q. Katz, Nilpotent connections and the monodromy theorem, Theorem 7.2.

Extracting all f factors. Start with ψ0 = [a]. Suppose [a] = ψj ∘ Frj for j < f, with scalar action σj(λ) = λ1/qj. We show that ψj∗ kills both summands of L = (1 − E)L ⊕ EL. Since [q]∗ acts as zero on L, the one-step argument factors [q] through Fr. Taking the jth iterate and using naturality gives [qj] = νj ∘ Frj. It follows that [1 − e] ∘ ψj = [qf−js] ∘ νj. Indeed precomposition with Frj gives [a(1 − e)] on both sides. We may cancel this precomposition because Frobenius fixes the underlying points and is injective on functions. Now q divides qf−js, so pulling back the equality gives ψj∗(1 − E)L = 0.

On EL the first pullback ψ0∗ acts as χ(a) = 0. For 0 < j < f, the same cancellation shows that ψj commutes with every [b], since [a] does. Its pullback preserves the Hodge filtration and commutes with E. The image of a vector of EL therefore lies in EL, with character χ. Semilinearity also gives that image the character σj ∘ χ. A nonzero image would force all Q values of χ to be roots of Xqj − X, whose degree is less than Q. This proves the vanishing and permits the next extraction. At j = f the induction gives [a] = ψ ∘ Frf, where ψ = ψf lies over λ ↦ λ1/Q.

Constancy lemma. Write Δ = k[ε]/(ε²). Suppose the point count has given a nonzero tangent v ∶ Spec Δ → A at the identity with a lift u ∈ A[𝔴](T). The truncated-ring calculation supplies a k-algebra map τ ∶ T → Δ and an injective ring map j ∶ Δ → T satisfying v = u ∘ Spec τ and j(τ(s)) = sQ for every s ∈ T. Then ψ ∘ v is constant in the infinitesimal direction. More precisely, let v0 be the constant tangent given by the identity section over Δ. Then ψ ∘ v = ψ ∘ v0 as maps of schemes.

Proof. Precompose with Spec j. The composite of Spec j with Spec τ is FrSpec Tf. Naturality of absolute Frobenius therefore gives

ψ ∘ v ∘ Spec j = ψ ∘ FrAf ∘ u = [a] ∘ u = 0T.

The last equality uses a ∈ 𝔴. Replacing u by the identity section gives the same calculation for v0. To cancel Spec j, observe that Spec Δ has a single point and Spec T is nonempty since j is injective. Both maps from Spec Δ have the same image point in A. Choose an affine open neighbourhood of it. On the coordinate ring of this neighbourhood the two maps become equal after composing with j, so they were equal already. This proof also accounts for the action of ψ on the ground field. The Stacks Project, §33.36, naturality of absolute Frobenius.

Which Hodge classes does the constant tangent detect?

Put P = H1dR(A/k)prim and L = P ∩ Fil1. A primitive class pulls back along addition to the sum of its pullbacks along the two projections. The classes in L are represented by differential forms. Let ρ(b) = [b]∗ act on P. The coefficient e for 𝔴 satisfies e − 1 ∈ 𝔴, and E = ρ(e) is idempotent. The unitary Lie algebra calculation says that EL is a line and that every coefficient b acts on it by χ(b).

Vanishing on the selected Hodge line. If v has the lift in the constancy lemma, then ψ∗(Ex) = 0 for every x ∈ L.

Indeed the Lie–Hodge pairing identifies tangents at the identity with linear functionals on the classes of invariant one-forms. In the dual P∨, a representative h of the tangent v is defined up to the annihilator L⊥. Its restriction to L is nonzero. A pulled-back form evaluated on v evaluates the original form on the image tangent, with the scalar twist of ψ. The image tangent is constant, so h(ψ∗x) = 0 for all x ∈ L. Pullback preserves L. Oda, The first de Rham cohomology group and Dieudonné modules, Corollary 5.11, for the Hodge exact sequence.

Because v is killed by 𝔴, [e] fixes v. Evaluating forms on this equality gives h ∘ E = h on L. For any functional λ on P, the restriction of λ ∘ E to L is a scalar multiple of h|L. In fact all these restrictions factor through the line EL, and h gives a nonzero one. Finally ψ commutes with [e]. After precomposition with FrAf this follows from naturality and [a][e] = [e][a]. Frobenius on the smooth, hence reduced, scheme A is the identity on points and is injective on functions, which permits cancellation on affine opens. Thus

λ(ψ∗Ex) = (λ ∘ E)(ψ∗x) = 0   (x ∈ L).

Linear functionals separate the points of P. This proves the vanishing on the whole selected line using a single nonzero tangent.

How does Verschiebung supply the last Frobenius preimage?

Pointwise Frobenius inclusion. Let Φ be the q-semilinear Frobenius on P. With the same hypotheses and a nonzero lifted tangent, for every x ∈ L there exists z ∈ P such that

ρ(e)x = ρ(d)Φf(z).

Here d is the coefficient in the Frobenius-span alternative. Two other local coefficients c and c′ satisfy ad = qc and c′ce ≡ e modulo q. Write U = ψ∗. Multiplication by q also factors through Frobenius. The factor is the Verschiebung from the Frobenius twist of A to A. Using the inverse Frobenius automorphism of k to identify the twist as a scheme, write V for its additive pullback on P. For λ ∈ k and v ∈ P, these scalar actions give U(λv) = λ1/QU(v) and V(λv) = λ1/qV(v). We need two relations on P.

UΦf−1ρ(d) = Vρ(c),   ker V ⊆ im Φ.

For the first, denote the scheme map inducing V by ν. The two maps [d] ∘ FrAf−1 ∘ ψ and [c] ∘ ν become equal after precomposition by FrAf. Indeed naturality and the two factorizations reduce their composites to [da] ∘ FrAf−1 and [cq] ∘ FrAf−1. Cancel Frobenius and take pullbacks in reverse order. The second relation is the Frobenius–Verschiebung exactness for the Dieudonné module of A[q], transported to de Rham cohomology. This is the cohomological input. The image of Φ is a k-subspace because k is perfect. A scalar multiple βΦ(w) is Φ(β1/qw). Oda, Corollary 5.11, the Dieudonné comparison, and Devalapurkar and Halliday, Theorem 1.1, Frobenius–Verschiebung exactness.

Following the residue characters. For 0 ≤ j ≤ f, let Pj be the simultaneous generalized eigenspace with character χj(b) = χ(b)qj. All these characters have the same kernel 𝔴. The index here counts Frobenius iterates of the one chosen character. A generalized eigenvector is killed, for each b, by some power of ρ(b) − χj(b)·1. Commuting endomorphisms over the algebraically closed field k give a direct sum of such spaces. Indeed their linear span in End(P) has a finite basis taken from the coefficient operators. Decompose successively into the generalized eigenspaces of those basis elements. Commutation preserves each summand already obtained.

Why do these spaces have the same dimension? Write C = 𝒪F. The characteristic-polynomial comparison for abelian varieties, applied to the prime-to-q lattice of the moduli datum, identifies the characteristic polynomial of ρ(b) with that of multiplication by b on C, raised to the power mn and reduced in k. Here m and n are the parameters of the unitary datum. Choose b by the Chinese remainder theorem so that its residue at 𝔴 is a nonzero generator of the residue field over its prime field and its residues at the other primes above q are zero. The conjugates χj(b) distinguish the embeddings at 𝔴, and they are nonzero. Thus the multiplicity of χj(b) in the characteristic polynomial counts precisely dim Pj. By the comparison, this is mn times the dimension of the corresponding generalized eigenspace for multiplication on k ⊗ℤ C. In this last space the bijection λ ⊗ c ↦ λq ⊗ c carries each character summand onto the next, so their dimensions agree. We have proved equality of dimensions before asking whether Φ carries one space onto the next.

We have Pf = P0 because χ takes values in the field with Q elements. The Hodge–Frobenius identity gives ker Φ = L. Since Φ commutes with the coefficient action, semilinearity sends Pj to Pj+1. On all these spaces E is the identity since E is idempotent with generalized eigenvalue 1. For 1 ≤ j < f, any element of Pj killed by Φ therefore lies in EL, where the character is χ. But χj ≠ χ since the polynomial Xqj − X cannot vanish on all Q field elements. Distinct generalized eigenspaces meet only in zero. Thus Φ is injective on Pj for those indices. Since the spaces have equal dimension and every scalar has a qth root, the image of a basis is a basis. We can therefore lift from P0 back to P1 through f − 1 applications of Φ.

Constructing the last preimage. Put y = Ex. Its character is χ and a ∈ ker χ, so ρ(a)y = 0. The local annihilator relation gives E(ker ρ(a)) ⊆ im ρ(d). Thus y = ρ(d)b since Ey = y. Project b onto P0. This preserves the equality because ρ(d) preserves every simultaneous generalized eigenspace. The preceding lift now gives w ∈ P1 with y = ρ(d)Φf−1(w). For f = 1 take the projected b itself.

By the vanishing on the Hodge line, Uy = 0. Commuting ρ(d) with Φ and using the displayed operator relation gives Vρ(c)w = 0. Frobenius–Verschiebung exactness supplies z0 with Φ(z0) = ρ(c)w. Set z = ρ(c′e)z0. The coefficient action factors through reduction modulo q, so ρ(c′e)ρ(c) = E. Consequently Φ(z) = Ew = w. Substitution gives ρ(d)Φf(z) = y, as required. Together with the torsion count this proves the alternative used in the generic ordinarity argument.
