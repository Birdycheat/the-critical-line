One integer level for all the prescribed places

At each place in a finite set T, we know how close a matrix must be to the identity to stabilize the chosen automorphic vector. We want a single integer M that imposes all those congruences. At a place w where we shall apply a Hecke operator, we also need a level ideal prime to w. Simply removing the entire rational prime below w from M can lose a required congruence at another place above the same prime.

Level and site theorem. Work over the CM field E with its chosen integral lattice and coordinates, the auxiliary prime p and the automorphic vector V of the uniform stabilizer theorem. Its level support S misses the places above p, and the form takes a nonzero value at a point integral above p. Let K = ℚ(x) = ℚ(ω) be the quadratic subfield specified by the integral generator ω. Fix a finite set Q of test places. Each w in Q lies over a good rational prime q ≠ p. Here good means that the quadratic polynomial of ω has two distinct roots modulo q and the trace pairing is perfect at q and the coefficient of the lattice form is a unit there. Write ū for the CM conjugate of a place u. Assume that w is split over the real subfield, that w and w̄ lie outside S, and that neither w̄ nor any ū with u in S has the same restriction to K as w. All test places over the same rational q must restrict to the same place of K. The two places of the quadratic subfield above q.

For every positive integer c prime to p, there is an integer M ≥ 3, prime to p and divisible by c and by every test prime. The principal subgroup of level M stabilizes V in the sense of the uniform stabilizer theorem. At every test place w we can also choose a site, consisting of its rational prime q, an integer root r modulo q, an auxiliary ideal 𝔞 and the subgroup of the associated ideal level. This subgroup has the same stabilizer property. Sites above the same rational prime use the same integer representative r and the same auxiliary ideal 𝔞.

From a depth to an integer. The uniform stabilizer theorem supplies a finite T away from p and a depth e ≥ 0. Each member of T belongs to S or has a nonintegral change of coordinates. Write 𝔭u for the ideal of u and ℓu for its residue characteristic, so ℓu ∈ 𝔭u. Take B to be p + 3 plus the largest test prime, with the last summand zero for an empty list. A prime divisor t of B! + 1 exceeds B, since every prime at most B divides B!. Any prime larger than B will do. Form the products

P = (∏u∈T ℓu)(∏w∈Q qw),   b = e|T| + e + 1,   M = ctPb.

All factors are positive and prime to p. The factor t gives M ≥ 3 even if both products are empty. The exponent b is at least 1, at least e and at least e|T|. Thus every test prime divides M, and ℓue divides M for every u in T. In particular (M) ⊆ 𝔭ue. For any nonzero ideal 𝔟 inside all these powers, a matrix in the subgroup of level 𝔟 belongs to the integral level-one subgroup. Its difference from the identity has entries in the extension of 𝔟 to each local integer ring away from p, measured in the frame coordinates. Those entries lie in 𝔭ue at every u in T, so the uniform depth applies. Taking 𝔟 = (M) proves the assertion for the principal level.

Keeping the congruences at the other places over q. Fix a test prime q. Choose one test place over it and choose an integer rq lifting the residue of ω on its side. The quadratic splitting criterion gives the same root at every test place over q. Let Tq be the places of T above q and let M(q) be the integer left after removing the full q-power from M. Set

𝔞q = ∏u∈Tq 𝔭ue,   𝔟q = (M(q)) 𝔞q.

Three containments explain this choice. First, (M) ⊆ 𝔟q. Indeed q divides P, so its exponent in M is at least b ≥ e|Tq|. For each u in Tq we have (q) ⊆ 𝔭u. Multiplying those inclusions, each raised to e, places the removed q-power in 𝔞q. Multiplication by M(q) gives the claim. In particular 𝔟q is nonzero.

Second, 𝔟q ⊆ 𝔭ue for every u in T. Above q, the right-hand ideal occurs in the product defining 𝔞q. Away from q, ℓue still divides M(q). The subgroup of level 𝔟q therefore satisfies the same stabilizer condition as the principal subgroup.

Third, (q, ω − rq)e|Tq| ⊆ 𝔞q, while 𝔞q is prime to each test ideal 𝔭w over q. To see both assertions, take u in Tq. The frame is integral over a good prime, so u must belong to S. Its conjugate cannot be on the side of w by hypothesis. There are only two sides, and conjugation exchanges them. Hence u is on the side of w, and q and ω − rq both belong to 𝔭u. Multiplication gives the required power containment. Since w is outside S, each 𝔭u is a maximal ideal distinct from 𝔭w. Their powers and their product are consequently prime to 𝔭w. This also covers the empty product, when 𝔞q is the unit ideal.

Finally M(q) is prime to p and q, and it is at least 3 because it retains t. The local pairing condition holds by goodness of q, and ω reduces to rq at w. These facts give all the required site data. Their construction uses q alone for the shared root and auxiliary ideal. The eigenvalues for the geometric correspondence still require the spectral and Hodge arguments.

An example with two places on one side. Let E = ℚ(ζ), where ζ is a primitive eighth root of unity, and take ω = i = ζ². At q = 17 the polynomial X² + 1 has distinct roots 4 and −4. The ideals 𝔭w = (ζ − 2) and 𝔭u = (ζ + 2) each have index 17, since the absolute determinant of multiplication by their generator on the integer lattice is its norm, 24 + 1 = 17. They are distinct since their generators differ by 4, a unit at primes above 17. The index calculation uses the ideal norm formula. Both residues send i to 4. The field discriminant is prime to 17 since the power basis of ζ has discriminant 256, the norm of the derivative 4ζ³. For example the integral trace pairing twisted by −i is perfect at 17, and the real coefficient −(ζ + ζ−1) is a unit there since its square is 2.

To illustrate the ideal construction, take T = {u}, Q = {w}, e = 2, p = 5, c = 2 and t = 29. Then P = 17², b = 5 and M = 58 · 1710. Removing the 17-part leaves 58, which is a unit at both places. The site ideal (58)𝔭u² reinstates depth two at u and remains a unit at w. The shared quadratic root is represented by 4. This calculation takes the stabilizer support and depth as inputs.
