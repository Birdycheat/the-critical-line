Lifting a translation to a line bundle

The comparison of polarizations uses a scalar attached to two division points. Translations by the points commute on the abelian scheme. Their lifts to a line bundle can differ when composed in the two possible orders. We first construct the lifts and then compute their difference.

Let A → S be an abelian scheme with line bundle ℒ. Work over a test scheme T → S and fix a section x with kx = 0 for some positive integer k. Write π for the projection of AT to T, and ℒa for the pullback along a point a. A lift of translation by x is an isomorphism Uₓ from the translate of ℒ⊗k back to ℒ⊗k. At a variable point z it goes from the line over z + x to the line over z.

To construct it, form the normalized difference D(a,z) = ℒa+z ℒa−1 ℒz−1 ℒ₀, writing tensor products by juxtaposition. The theorem of the cube makes this difference additive in a under tensor product. Thus D(x,z)⊗k ≅ D(kx,z) = D(0,z) ≅ 𝒪. Rearranging gives the line of possible lift maps on AT.

Hom(tₓ*ℒ⊗k, ℒ⊗k) ≅ π*(ℒ₀⊗k ⊗ ℒx⊗(−k)).

Here tₓ denotes translation by x and the star denotes pullback. The right side comes from a line on T. Choose a generator wherever that line is trivial. Its pullback generates the Hom line, so it gives an isomorphism at every point above this open set. This proves local existence in the base, enough to compare two lifts on overlaps. The theorem of the cube.

How the eight factors give additivity

Expand the quotient of D(a + b,z) by D(a,z) D(b,z). Its four numerator lines lie at a + b + z, a, b, z. The four denominator lines lie at a + b, a + z, b + z, 0. The factors cancel in pairs on each coordinate face through zero. Apply the cube theorem on the triple product, then pull back to the chosen points.

For an arbitrary base, the proof of the cube theorem constructs an open and closed subscheme of the third copy of A on which this tensor product comes from the third factor. It contains the zero section by the third face identity. Each fibre of A is connected, so the locus is all of A. Restricting to (0,0,z) trivializes the remaining line. Why the two orders give a scalar on the base

Let x and y be killed by the same integer k. Choose lifts on a common open set of T. Starting on the line over z + x + y, we can first remove y and then x, or first remove x and then y. The two composites are isomorphisms to the line over z. There is a unique invertible scalar relating them. Define its sign by the following equation, with composition read from right to left.

Uₓ(z) Uᵧ(z + x) = c(x,y) Uᵧ(z) Uₓ(z + y).

At first the scalar is a regular unit on the whole abelian scheme over the chosen open set. Properness, flatness and geometrically connected reduced fibres give π∗𝒪 = 𝒪 on that open set, also after base change. Applying this to the scalar and its inverse shows that it comes from a unique base unit. In particular it is independent of the variable z. Global functions and base change.

Two choices for Uₓ differ by a unit on the abelian scheme and hence by a base unit a. Likewise a new Uᵧ differs by a base unit b. The two sides acquire the same factor ab. Thus the scalars agree on overlaps and glue to a unit on T. Pulling back the displayed equation gives the equation for the pulled-back lifts, so uniqueness gives the same scalar after every base change.

In the square below the lifts are fixed. Changing the basis of the line at each vertex changes the numbers written on the edges. The intermediate basis cancels in each composite, leaving the same endpoint factor on both paths. This is the coordinate version of comparing the same two maps.

Why the commutator takes values in roots of unity

Write X, Y, Z for local lifted translations by x, y, z. The composite XY lifts translation by x + y. Moving Z successively past Y and X multiplies by c(y,z) and c(x,z). Both are base units and commute with every lift. Comparison with the scalar for XY proves multiplicativity in the first variable. Reversing the two paths in the square inverts their ratio. This gives multiplicativity in the second variable as well. Taking the same lift twice gives c(x,x) = 1.

c(x,y)k = c(kx,y) = c(0,y) = 1.

The last equality uses the identity lift at zero. On A[k] ×S A[k] the two projections give a universal pair of torsion sections. Their scalar therefore defines a morphism to μk, the scheme of k-th roots of unity. Every pair over T pulls back this one morphism. Over ℂ its values are discrete, precisely the input used to transport the polarization along a connected parameter ball.
