Why does one inertia value suffice?

At a finite place of residue characteristic p ≠ ℓ, let I be inertia and P its wild subgroup. The orbit count uses one element γ ∈ I per representative place. Every locally constant additive character I → 𝔽ℓ is determined by its value on γ. The choice of γ precedes the choice of character. The reason is that the image of γ generates a dense subgroup of tame inertia I/P, while an ℓ-primary character kills P. The proof uses that every nonzero y ∈ Ω is algebraic over ℚ. In an integral polynomial relation for y, at least two nonzero terms attain the largest absolute value, so |y|m = |p|k for some m > 0 and integer k. What forces wild inertia into the kernel? For an additive character φ to a discrete ℓ-primary group, local constancy gives a finite field extension whose fixing subgroup intersects I inside ker φ. A positive power of any wild σ fixes that extension. Write the exponent as ℓab, with ℓ ∤ b. A wild automorphism τ that fixes x after k iterations already fixes x whenever p ∤ k. Indeed, if y = τx − x is nonzero, the telescoping sum of τiy for 0 ≤ i < k is zero. Dividing by y and reducing modulo the maximal ideal gives k = 0 in the residue field, a contradiction. Apply this with τ = σb and k = ℓa. Then bφ(σ) = 0, so φ(σ) = 0. If φ(γ) also vanishes, its closed kernel contains P and γ and hence all of I. Two values and four values at the same place

Over ℚ₅ take the extension generated by a primitive 25th root ζ. The polynomial Φ₂₅(1 + X) is Eisenstein at 5, with reduction X²⁰ and constant term 5. Thus π = ζ − 1 is a uniformizer and the extension is totally ramified of degree 20. Its finite inertia group is (ℤ/25)×, with a acting by ζ ↦ ζa. The residue of σa(π)/π is a modulo 5. The wild subgroup is therefore the five elements congruent to 1 modulo 5. The element 7 maps to the generator 2 modulo 5 and satisfies 7² ≡ −1 modulo 25. For this finite inertia quotient, evaluation at 7 gives the two values of 𝔽₂, or the four values 0, ¼, ½, ¾ in ℚ₂/ℤ₂. In both cases the wild subgroup contributes a single possible restriction, the zero character.
