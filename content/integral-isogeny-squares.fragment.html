What has to extend from the generic fibre?

At a marked point the source of a Hecke correspondence forgets part of the marking. Its target takes a quotient abelian variety and equips that quotient with a specified marking. Both operations must make sense over the local integer ring, so that they can be reduced at its closed point. We first identify the maps whose extension will suffice.

Write R for the discrete valuation ring and K for its fraction field. On the integral side let p₁ ∶ A₁ → X₁ and p₂ ∶ A₂ → X₂ be the two universal families. A point of X₁ specifies a marked abelian variety. A point of A₁ over it additionally specifies a point on that variety. Thus an extension consists of a map t ∶ X₁ → X₂ over R and a map Φ ∶ A₁ → A₂ with p₂ ∘ Φ = t ∘ p₁.

On the generic tower, denote the known base map by π. The known map of families first applies the isogeny ψ on the source family and then the comparison φ into the family over the target. For each level i = 1, 2 write ji for the map from the generic tower base into Xi and ui for the corresponding map of families into Ai. Agreement with the given translation means

t ∘ j₁ = j₂ ∘ π,   Φ ∘ u₁ = u₂ ∘ φ ∘ ψ.

Passage to fibre powers. The first equality identifies the underlying marked object at the two ends of the calculation. For an ordered tuple of k points on that object, the second equality identifies the image of each coordinate. By the defining property of the fibre product, maps to a fibre power agree if their base maps and all their coordinate maps agree. This proves compatibility in every positive power. In power zero the family is just its base, and the first equality suffices. The Stacks Project, fibre products of schemes.

When one step has label h and the next has label h′, their action on a lattice vector is v ↦ h′(hv). The product label is h′h. This order also affects points on the family. For an elliptic curve over ℂ and a point P of order three, consider the maps (X,Y) ↦ (2X,Y) and (X,Y) ↦ (X + Y,Y) on the product of the curve with itself. Applied in the stated order to (0,P), they give (P,P). In reverse order they give (2P,P). Both composites lie over the same point of the base. The second square detects which one we have constructed.
