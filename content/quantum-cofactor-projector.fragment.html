Why do the columns of the differential minor have successive shifts?

The shifted tensor estimate needs an explicit entry of a projector. Its numerator is a determinant-like sum of differential operators. Since their order matters, begin with the exact convention. Let V be the common complex space on which the operators Eab act, for 0 ≤ a,b ≤ N. In the application V is the space of smooth vectors and the matrix size is m = N + 1. The complexified elementary generators satisfy the matrix-unit commutation laws

[Eab, Ecd] = δbcEad − δdaEcb.

Here [A,B] = AB − BA, and δ is the Kronecker delta. Set Tab(u) = Eab + δabu Id. For row and column lists a and b of length k, let Q(a,b∣u) be the sum over row permutations σ of their signs times the ordered product of the factors Taσ(h),bh(u − h), in the order h = 0,…,k − 1 from left to right. The rightmost operator acts first. The empty minor is the identity. Reindexing σ gives alternation in the rows.

To interchange two adjacent columns c,d, keep fixed every factor before and after these positions and pair the two assignments of their rows p,q. Write x and y for the two column parameters. The sum of the old alternating pair and the pair with its columns interchanged has the following middle factor

Tpc(x)Tqd(y) − Tqc(x)Tpd(y) + Tpd(x)Tqc(y) − Tqd(x)Tpc(y).

Expanding the factors shows exactly what the shift pays for. Put L = δpcEqd − δqdEpc − δqcEpd + δpdEqc. The purely scalar terms cancel. The mixed terms give (x − y)L. The terms with two generators give [Epc,Eqd] + [Epd,Eqc] = −L. Consecutive parameters satisfy x − y = 1, so the middle factor vanishes with the surrounding factors in their original order. Adjacent swaps generate all permutations. Thus the minor is alternating in its columns and a repeated column makes it zero. A. I. Molev, Yangians and their applications, §2.6, formulas (2.24)–(2.25) and the quantum minors.

Which side of the matrix does the comatrix multiply?

Let D(u) be the full minor on the ordered lists 0,…,N. Define a matrix C(u) of operators by putting in entry (i,j) the factor (−1)i+j times the minor obtained by deleting row j and column i, keeping the remaining lists in increasing order. Its size-N minor always starts with parameter u. Let E denote the matrix with entries Ejl acting on columns in Vm. Then the identity used in the projector is

C(u) ((u − N) Id + E) = diag(D(u),…,D(u)).

Fix an output row i and an input column l. Form the full minor with its column list equal to 0,…,N with i omitted, followed by l. Group the permutations according to the row j assigned to that last column. Moving row j to the last position takes N − j switches, and the last operator stays on the right. The contribution is (−1)N−j times the minor without row j and column i, multiplied on the right by Tjl(u − N). Multiplying the whole expansion by (−1)N−i gives precisely the (i,l) entry on the left above.

If l ≠ i, the chosen column list contains l twice, so its full minor vanishes. If l = i, moving the last column back to its original position takes N − i switches. That sign cancels the factor outside the sum, leaving D(u). This proves the claimed multiplication identity without commuting the cofactor past its last factor. A. I. Molev, Definition 2.26 and Proposition 2.27, equations (2.30)–(2.31).

For a two-by-two check, the upper-right entry of the product is (u + E₁₁)E₀₁ − E₀₁(u − 1 + E₁₁) = [E₁₁,E₀₁] + E₀₁ = 0. Using u in both columns would leave −E₀₁. This is already nonzero for the defining representation.

How does evaluation select one shifted summand?

Now specialize the algebraic identity to the signed discrete series in the smooth tensor decomposition. Its infinitesimal character gives the scalar action D(u) = ∏j(u + tj) Id with tj = λj − N/2. This scalar action is the representation-theoretic input in this step. One can read off the shifts on a highest-weight vector of weight μ. In a nonidentity row permutation, take the rightmost column j whose row has moved. Its assigned row is less than j. The columns to its right have fixed rows and act diagonally on the vector. The raising operator in column j therefore kills that term. The identity permutation gives the scalar ∏j(u + μj − j). In the Harish-Chandra parameter λ = μ + ρ the jth coordinate of ρ is N/2 − j, which yields the displayed factors. The centrality of the coefficients and the infinitesimal character of the discrete series make this same scalar applicable on its smooth vectors. The tj have distinct values. A. I. Molev, Casimir elements for glN, formula (4.37) and Corollary 4.6.2, the Harish-Chandra image of the Capelli determinant.

On any nonzero eigenvector Y of E with eigenvalue κ, the left factor in the comatrix identity is multiplied by u − N + κ. Setting u = N − κ forces one of the factors on the right to vanish, so κ = N + ts for a unique s. On the corresponding eigenspace the identity becomes (u + ts)C(u)Y = ∏j(u + tj)Y. For u ≠ −ts cancellation gives C(u)Y = ∏j≠s(u + tj)Y. Both sides are vector-valued polynomials of degree at most N, since every cofactor contains exactly N affine factors. Their coefficients lie in a finite-dimensional span. In each coordinate these polynomials agree at infinitely many points, so they also agree at −ts.

Applied to a vector in a different shifted summand, this same product still contains u + ts and vanishes there. On the s-summand it is the nonzero scalar below. Decomposing a smooth tensor x into its summands thus gives

psx = C(−ts)x / ∏j≠s(tj − ts).

The differences are the nonzero integers λj − λs, so the reciprocal denominator has absolute value at most one. For an input supported in one coordinate the numerator is a single signed minor, the one estimated by the differential word bound. The construction uses a direct sum of eigenspaces on smooth tensors. A repeated root would invalidate this division.

A projector with a negative denominator. Take the defining two-dimensional representation, with the usual elementary matrices on basis e₀,e₁. Its full minor is (u + E₀₀)(u − 1 + E₁₁) − E₁₀E₀₁ = (u² − 1) Id. Thus (t₀,t₁) = (1,−1). On a column (v,w) the operator E gives (v₀ + w₁)(e₀,e₁), where the subscripts denote coordinates. Its eigenvalues are 2 and 0. The sum of the two diagonal elementary matrices equals Id, whence C(−1) = −E. Dividing by t₁ − t₀ = −2 gives the projector E/2. It sends (e₀,0) to (e₀,e₁)/2 and kills (e₀,−e₁).
