How one slope carries the prescribed determinant

The relation among cycles singles out a slope t₀ with unit coefficient. How can its gluing map carry one chosen determinant when all the maps must connect the same two lattices? Write C = ℤ/q, q = ℓν, m = 2e + 1 and κ = 1 + ℓc. Let t run from 0 through m. On the coordinates indexed by C², each slope t groups indices (u,v) by the weight u + tv. Why can the final factor have integer coefficients?

Put w = ℓc and H = Xw − 1. Substitution X ↦ Xκ sends H to H times a geometric sum congruent to κ modulo H. It fixes every polynomial modulo H. Thus if Hj divides f, then Hj+1 divides f(Xκ) − κjf(X). Apply the m steps to f = X. The result is ∑ piXκi, divisible by Hm. Set X = [1] in the integer group algebra of C. Each nonzero slope difference t₀ − s has absolute value at most m < w. Its ℓ-power part divides w and the remaining factor is invertible modulo q. Consequently [t₀ − s] − [0] divides [w] − [0] by the geometric sum identity. The product of all m differences therefore divides the desired projection, and each translate [s] in [t₀] − [s] = [s]([t₀ − s] − [0]) is invertible. Lift the resulting integer quotient along x ↦ (x,0). Use the multiplicities to assign divisor η(u,v)D to each coordinate. At an unwanted slope the divisor sum in every weight block vanishes. The lattice isomorphism theorem supplies maps of determinant 1 there. At the selected slope, γ−i sends ζκi back to ζ, collecting all the prescribed weights at weight 1. To identify the determinant there, put a = κe and Pe(T) = P(T)/(T − a) = ∑ biTi. The element β = ∏i γ−i(α)bi has exactly the required divisor. Indeed ordP′(γ−iα) equals ordγiP′(α). By the divisor condition on α, this is D(γi+1P′) − aD(γiP′). Multiplying by bi and summing gives coefficient bi−1 − abi = pi on D(γiP′), taking missing coefficients as zero. The same lattice theorem now realizes β as determinant in the weight-one block and 1 in all the others.
