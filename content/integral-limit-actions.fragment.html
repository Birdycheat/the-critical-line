How does Frobenius act on the integral limit lattice?

Write 𝔖 = W(k)[[u]], Ainf = W(𝒪F) and W = W(F), where F = ℂℓ♭. We identify Ainf with its image in W. Write e and eW for the two evaluations of 𝔖, ξ = eW(E) for the image of the Eisenstein polynomial, and t for the image of the primitive period τ. The tower has a common height H. The basis of the integral intersection gives T invertible over W and Y over Ainf. The opposite comparison supplies the integral matrix Y′. Together they satisfy Y = tHT in W and YY′ = τ2HI over Ainf.

Frobenius on the limit lattice. There are unique matrices A, A′ over 𝔖 such that

ξHφ(T) = T eW(A),   AA′ = E2HI.

Thus, on the lattice with basis Y, Frobenius on a column Y e(s) acts by Y e(Aφ(s)). The companion A′ shows that the 𝔖-span of the Frobenius images contains E2H times the whole lattice. In rank one and height zero this says that the Frobenius coefficient is a unit of 𝔖.

First consider the basis T∗ over W supplied by the étale comparisons. Its Frobenius coefficient C∗ = T∗−1φ(T∗) and its inverse have entries in 𝒪ℰ. Here is the approximation that proves this assertion. At precision n, put Bn = ξ−HeW(An), the coefficient of the layer, so φ(Tn) = TnBn. Its inverse is ξ−HeW(A′n). These are étale coefficients because the monic E has nonzero reduction and its evaluation is a unit of 𝒪ℰ. In the basis T∗, write Rn and R′n for the original étale comparison matrices. The forward matrix was denoted Cn in the change of basis. Their defining congruences are ΠnTn ≡ T∗Rn and ΠnTnR′n ≡ T∗. Applying φ to the second, substituting the layer identity and then the first congruence gives

C∗ ≡ RnBnφ(R′n),   C∗−1 ≡ φ(Rn)Bn−1R′n (mod ℓn).

For the second congruence use Tn = φ(Tn)Bn−1. Both approximants have entries in 𝒪ℰ, which is stable under φ and closed for the ℓ-adic topology in W. Write T = T∗U for the integral choice of columns, with U invertible over 𝒪ℰ. The new coefficient is C = U−1C∗φ(U). Both C and C−1 therefore still have étale coefficients.

Since t is a unit in W, the identity for YY′ gives Y′ = tHT−1 in that ring. From φ(t) = ξt, the two integral products satisfy

Y′φ(Y) = t2HξHC,   φ(Y′)Y = t2HξHC−1.

The scalar descent lemma below applies to every entry. It produces A and A′ with evaluations ξHC and ξHC−1. Multiplication and the injectivity of eW give AA′ = E2HI. The first identity follows from φ(T) = TC. Both matrices are uniquely determined after evaluation because T and ξ are invertible in W.

Descending an étale coefficient with bounded period denominator

Suppose w ∈ 𝒪ℰ and a = tbw belongs to Ainf, for b ≥ 0. The coefficient-ideal calculation gives a 𝔖-linear form f with f(τb) = ℓr. At any precision n, the definition of 𝒪ℰ gives eW(u)jw ≡ eW(s) modulo ℓn. Multiply by tb. The resulting difference has integral entries, and divisibility by ℓn contracts from W to Ainf. Indeed, the shift of Witt coordinates under multiplication by ℓ forces the quotient of such an integral entry to be integral, one division at a time. Applying f gives ujf(a) ≡ ℓrs in 𝔖. After evaluation, cancel the unit eW(u)j in W to get eW(f(a)) ≡ ℓrw modulo ℓn. This holds for all n. Separatedness gives equality, and contraction now puts w in Ainf.

Finally, Ainf ∩ 𝒪ℰ = e(𝔖). In fact, reduction of an element of the intersection belongs both to the tilt valuation ring and to the Laurent series field k((u)), hence to k[[u]] because the image of u has positive valuation. Lift this residue to 𝔖, subtract its evaluation, and divide by ℓ. Both rings are ℓ-saturated in W, so the quotient lies in the intersection again. The lifted digits sum in the ℓ-adically complete 𝔖 and evaluate to w. This proves the claimed descent.

How do the integral Galois corrections pass to the limit?

The Frobenius construction also gives ρ(g)g(T) = T for every g ∈ GK fixing the chosen tilt element, the first Witt coordinate of e(u). Indeed such a g fixes the étale coefficient ring pointwise. Apply g to T∗ ≡ ΠnTnR′n and multiply by ρ(g). The equivariance of Πn modulo ℓn and the fixed-vector identity at level n give T∗ back modulo ℓn. Separatedness gives equality on T∗, and g fixes the change U of étale coordinates.

Integral correction for the full Galois group. Assume now that every layer is a frame with integral matrices εm(g) satisfying ρm(g)g(Tm) = Tmεm(g) for every g ∈ GK. Assume the comparisons with the same limit basis T have the uniform exponent r. Then each g has a unique ε(g) in GLd(Ainf) such that

ρ(g) g(T) = T ε(g).

The only possible candidate in W is Dg = T−1ρ(g)g(T). Fix a precision n and choose m ≥ n, together with R and G over 𝔖, such that ΠmTm ≡ T eW(R) and RG ≡ ℓrI modulo ℓn. Work in W/ℓn. Apply g to the first congruence. It fixes the entries of Πm in ℤℓ. Also Πm intertwines the actions at this precision because it does so modulo ℓm. Consequently

T Dg g(eW(R)) ≡ ρ(g) Πm g(Tm) ≡ Πm Tm εm(g) ≡ T eW(R) εm(g).

Cancel T on the left. On the right multiply by g(eW(G)). The order matters because R is rectangular and the known product is RG. Since g fixes ℓ, we obtain in W

ℓrDg ≡ e(R) εm(g) g(e(G)) (mod ℓn).

Every entry on the right belongs to Ainf. To recover a Witt coordinate of Dg, apply this congruence at n = i + r + 1. For any entry x of Dg, coordinate i+r of ℓrx equals (xi)ℓr in F. This follows by iterating the Witt formula ℓ(x₀,x₁,…) = (0,x₀ℓ,x₁ℓ,…). Congruence modulo ℓn gives equality of the first n coordinates, so this power belongs to 𝒪F. Its valuation is ℓr times the valuation of xi. Hence xi also belongs to 𝒪F. Applying the argument to every i and every matrix entry puts Dg in Matd(Ainf). This constructs ε(g). Brinon–Conrad, §4.2, on the Witt coordinates of a perfect ring.

The defining equation gives ε(gh) = ε(g) g(ε(h)), since ρ(h) has coefficients in ℤℓ. In particular ε(1) = I and g(ε(g−1)) is the inverse of ε(g). Uniqueness follows by cancelling T. For the subgroup fixing the tilt element the opening identity gives ε(g) = I. This completes the integral Galois action. Passing the de Rham witnesses to this frame, or proving divisibility of ε(g) − I by e(u) from crystalline layers, requires the corresponding layer hypotheses and separate arguments.
