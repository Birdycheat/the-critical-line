Which rational endomorphisms come from matrices over the CM field?

Let 𝒪 be the ring of integers of the CM field E. The lattice is Λ = 𝒪n, but its rational vector space V = ℚ ⊗ℤ Λ is initially only given over ℚ. The geometric datum has to recognize precisely which rational endomorphisms are matrices with coefficients in E.

Sending t ⊗ v to the column tv gives a rational isomorphism β ∶ V → En. Indeed, an integral basis of 𝒪 is a rational basis of E, and its copies in the n coordinate positions give corresponding bases on both sides. Transport ordinary multiplication on columns through β and call the resulting matrix action ρ. A matrix is determined by the images of the standard columns, so ρ is faithful. Integral matrices act on Λ by their original multiplication.

ρ(Mn(E)) = {T ∈ Endℚ(V) ∣ TMb = MbT for all b ∈ 𝒪},

where Mb denotes multiplication by b on the lattice, extended to V. Every matrix over E commutes with these scalars. Conversely suppose T commutes with each Mb. If d ∈ 𝒪 is nonzero, Md is invertible on V and T also commutes with its inverse. Since every element of E is a fraction b/d, the transported map βTβ−1 is E-linear. Its columns therefore give the required matrix.

Why test all integral scalars?

In rank one over ℚ(i), complex conjugation is rational-linear. It sends i · 1 to −i, while i times the conjugate of 1 is i. Thus this rational endomorphism fails the scalar test at i and cannot be multiplication by a Gaussian field element.

Which matrix is adjoint for the alternating trace form?

Use the alternating trace form F on rational columns and its diagonal Hermitian matrix H, with real nonzero diagonal entries hj. For a matrix A over E, write A† for its conjugate transpose. The adjoint relative to F is

A∗ = H−1A†H,   F(Ax,y) = F(x,A*y).

To see the order of the factors, the coefficient of xj in the Hermitian expression for F(Ax,y), before multiplying by δ and taking trace, is ∑k hkAkjyk. In F(x,z) that coefficient is hjzj. Matching them gives exactly z = A∗y, since conjugation fixes each hk. Taking the weighted trace gives the displayed identity. Nondegeneracy of F makes the adjoint unique. In particular, for b ∈ 𝒪 the scalar matrix bI has adjoint bI. Both preserve the lattice, so F(bx, y) = F(x, by) already holds on integral vectors.

Where can a denominator appear in the adjoint?

Take E = ℚ(i), δ = −i and c = −2, so H = diag(2, −4). Let Ae₂ = ie₁ and Ae₁ = 0. The adjoint sends e₁ to (i/2)e₂. Both F(Ae₂,e₁) and F(e₂,A∗e₁) equal Tr(2) = 4. Using the ordinary conjugate transpose instead gives Tr(−4) = −8 in the second slot. The adjoint is rational even when an integral matrix acquires a denominator at a prime dividing c.

How do the local embeddings and the trace lattice give one geometric datum?

Fix a rank n ≥ 1 and a good prime p for the trace lattice. The marked family of local embeddings selects one embedding of E into a p-adic algebraic closure. At infinity the positive complex structure was first specified as an operator on the real vector space. The geometric datum requires it to come from the real extension of the rational matrix algebra.

Write Φ for the embeddings selecting the same root of the quadratic generator as τ₀. In the identification E ⊗ℚ ℝ ≃ ∏τ∈Φ ℂ, multiplication is coordinatewise. Applying this identification entry by entry takes the real matrix algebra isomorphically to ∏τ∈Φ Mn(ℂ). Thus the previously prescribed diagonal multipliers come from one element J of that algebra. If Jτ is its τ-component,

Jτ = diag(i sign τ(h₀), …, i sign τ(hn−1)),   J² = −1,   J∗ = −J.

The square is −1 in every component. Conjugate transpose negates the diagonal entries, and conjugation by the diagonal Gram matrix preserves them, giving the stated adjoint. Its action on real columns is precisely the complex structure for which F(Jx,x) is positive when x ≠ 0. At τ ∈ Φ the matrix −iHτJτ is diagonal with entries |τ(hj)|. At the conjugate embedding τ′, the entries of H agree and the multipliers of J change sign. Thus +iHτ′Jτ′ has the same strictly positive diagonal, as in the real polarization calculation.

Write s for the marked global embedding of E into an algebraic closure of ℚ. Its complex realization is τ₀, so the infinite place of s is the unique place of positive index one. Its p-adic realization is exactly the chosen representative in the local family. This identifies both markings on the lattice carrying the integral scalar action, the rational matrix action and the positive orientation. In the choice of auxiliary prime the lattice is fixed first. Each finite exclusion set can then be avoided by a good split prime for that same lattice. When the rank is odd and the real degree is even, the compactness calculation supplies the additional conditions for the spectral application.
