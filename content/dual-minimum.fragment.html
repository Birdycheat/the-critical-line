How the correction keeps both lattice minima positive

The compactness argument needs a constant c > 0 that works for both minima at every input h. Adding positive forms helps its primal minimum but can make its inverse smaller. Why does the dual minimum survive? The proof tests the correction in a direction chosen by an integral covector. Along this direction a whole range of scales vanishes exactly. Where the two bounds enter the partition

Fix the field L and rank N ≥ 1. Write qh for squared length summed over the infinite places, and λ and λ∨ for the minima on nonzero integral vectors of h and its dual h∨, whose matrix at each place is the inverse transpose. Let R be the corrected form. Its base term is μh, where μ is a smooth approximation to λ∨(h). At scale d = 4j, a lattice vector v contributes a rank-one form with local matrix (hwvw)(hwvw)*. Give it weight θ(qh(v)/d), where 0 ≤ θ ≤ 1, θ = 1 on arguments ≤ 1 and θ = 0 on arguments ≥ 4. Divide the weighted sum by d²Zd, where Zd is the sum of the weights, to obtain Td. The zero vector has weight 1, so this denominator is positive. Then R = μh + ∑d θ(dμ)Td. Two cutoffs and the normalization at one scale

The primal minimum. Let u attain λ(R) and set a = qh(u). If μa ≥ 1/16, the base term suffices. Otherwise choose a power of four with 4a ≤ d ≤ 16a. Its outer weight is 1. For every v with qh(v) ≤ d/4, both v and v+u have inner weight 1, by the parallelogram identity. Put B = Re ∑w uw*hwvw. Translation by u changes B to B+a, and B²+(B+a)² ≥ a²/2. By Cauchy–Schwarz, each square is at most the number r of infinite places times the corresponding rank-one value at u.

Let M count the vectors in the inner ball of squared radius d/4. The positive weighted numerator at u is at least Ma²/(4r). Indeed, each of the two lists v and v+u has no repetitions, so together they can charge the numerator at most twice. The counting estimate with dilation 16 gives Zd ≤ #{v ∣ qh(v) ≤ 4d} ≤ C₁₆M. Since d ≤ 16a, this one scale proves

λ(R) ≥ min{1/16, 1/(1024rC₁₆)}.

Finding a dual test direction. Now assume L is totally complex. Choose an integral covector f realizing Q = λ∨(R). Among vectors with f(v) ≠ 0, choose v₀ minimizing m = qh(v₀). Such a vector exists because f is nonzero on at least one coordinate vector, and only finitely many lattice vectors have squared length at most that of this first candidate. At some place w we have |f(v₀)w| ≥ 1, because the absolute field norm of this nonzero algebraic integer is at least 1. At that place take the unique y satisfying y*hwx = fw(x) for all x. Set b = y*hwy > 0. The source’s local pairing inequality gives 1 ≤ 4bm. The local inequality, bilinear pairing and integral norm · Choice of covector, vector and place

The transference bound supplies a lattice point within squared distance ρ = CT/λ∨(h) of any real vector. Apply it at the vector z supported only at w, with component ty where t²b = 5ρ. If the nearby v belonged to the kernel of f, testing z−v against y would give (tb)² ≤ 4bρ. Its left side equals 5bρ. Hence f(v) ≠ 0. The parallelogram identity gives qh(v) ≤ 2(5ρ)+2ρ, so

1 ≤ 4bm,   m ≤ 12CT/λ∨(h).

What the correction does to this direction. When 4d ≤ m, a vector with positive inner weight has qh(v) < m. It lies in the kernel of f by minimality. Thus its rank-one form at w evaluates to |y*hwvw|² = 0. Choose d₀ = 4j₀ ≤ m < 4d₀. Only scales d ≥ d₀ need be counted. For the surviving terms use Cauchy–Schwarz in the original metric. A vector of positive weight has qh(v) < 4d, hence |y*hwvw|² ≤ 4bd. The weighted average divided by d² is at most 4b/d. Their sum is ≤ 4b · (2/d₀) ≤ 32b/m by a geometric series. If μ ≤ Cμλ∨(h), the base term contributes at most 12CT|Cμ|b/m. Put A = 12CT|Cμ|+32. Testing the same covector and vector in the corrected metric now gives

b² = |fw(y)|² ≤ 4Q (y*Rwy) ≤ 4QA b/m,   λ∨(R) = Q ≥ bm/(4A) ≥ 1/(16A).

The selected smoothing permits Cμ = 4. For each fixed field and rank the smaller of the primal and dual bounds works for all positive forms, including the normalized inputs to the partition. One scale can raise one direction and leave another untouched. Take ℤ[i]², h = diag(1,64), u = e₁, and use f(v) = v₂ as a test covector. Then m = 64, y = e₂/64 and b = 1/64. At d = 4 the outer weight is 1 since μ ≤ 4λ∨(h) = 1/16. Every vector with positive inner weight is (z,0) with |z|² < 16. There are 45 such Gaussian integer candidates, so Z₄ ≤ 45. The four points at each squared norm 1, 2 and 4 carry full weight. They contribute 4(1+2+4) = 28 to the numerator at e₁. Hence T₄[e₁] ≥ 28/(16·45) = 7/180, while T₄[y] = 0 exactly. This checks the two estimates on one scale without needing the value of the smooth cutoff between its plateaus.
