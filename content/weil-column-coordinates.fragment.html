How does a column of a period matrix give a period of the representation we want?

The integral operator acts on a lattice of matrices of rank d. The local Weil trace is taken on a representation of dimension a. A column of a lattice matrix has precisely a entries. Taking its ith column will carry us from the first space to the second.

Let K be the local base field and fix a finite coefficient field L over ℚℓ containing the entries of the matrices B₁, …, Bd in the chosen lattice basis. On a layer take the component of each basis matrix in the representation under consideration. Let r be its action on La and ρ the lattice action. Put vki equal to column i of Bk. Equivariance of the matrix lattice says that r(g)vki = ∑k′ ρ(g)k′k vk′i. This is left multiplication on each matrix, so it holds separately in each column.

Let Bst be the semistable period ring. Write c for the embedding of Bcris as constant polynomials in Bst. In this description Frobenius and the Galois action on a constant agree with their crystalline actions. A semistable period is an element of the tensor product La ⊗ℚℓ Bst fixed by the diagonal action of every g ∈ GK. The space of such periods is denoted by Dst. Brinon and Conrad, §9.2, construction of the semistable period ring.

mji = ∑k vki ⊗ c(Ukj)   ∈ Dst.

Here i selects one of the a physical columns of each basis matrix, j selects one of the d period columns of U, and k runs over the d basis matrices. The coefficient attached to vk′i after acting by g is c(∑k ρ(g)k′k g(Ukj)). This is c(Uk′j) by invariance of the period columns. The tensor product is over ℚℓ, so moving the entries of ρ(g) across the tensor sign in this calculation is legitimate. Frobenius acts on the period factor. After n iterations the displayed sum has c(φn(Ukj)) in its second factor.

Now let Ω be the algebraic closure of ℚℓ, F its maximal unramified subextension, and set S = Ω ⊗F Bst. The finite field extension L supplies the matrix coefficients through its inclusion in Ω. The field F specifies which period scalars we identify with elements of Ω. The coordinate map ψ₀ takes a simple tensor v ⊗ b to the vector with entries vi ⊗ b in S. It is additive in each variable. Rational ℓ-adic scalars can pass from one factor to the other in S, which proves that this prescription descends to the tensor product over ℚℓ. We can now read all a coordinates of φn(mji) in one ring.

How does the Weil action change the Frobenius exponent in those coordinates?

The complex Weil representation is built from Dpst, the periods fixed after passing to some finite extension of K. Thus each mji above already belongs to Dpst. Fix the coefficient isomorphism ι ∶ Ω → ℂ. When we extend scalars from L to ℂ, we must also match the action of the unramified coefficients F on periods. Concretely start with ℂ ⊗L Dpst, where L acts on ℂ through ι, and quotient by the relations

z ⊗ (u·m) = zι(u) ⊗ m   (u ∈ F, z ∈ ℂ).

Here u acts on the period factor of m. Denote the quotient by W and the class of 1 ⊗ m by [m]. Put γ(z) = ι−1(z) ⊗ 1 in S. The rule z ⊗ m ↦ γ(z)ψ₀(m) respects the first tensor product since multiplication by a coefficient in L multiplies each vector coordinate by that coefficient. It respects the displayed relation because S is itself a tensor product over F. This constructs the coordinate map ψ ∶ W → Sa, with ψ([m]) = ψ₀(m) and ψ(zy) = γ(z)ψ(y).

For a potentially semistable representation Frobenius on Dpst is invertible. The Weil action at σ is the local Galois action composed with φ−α. The two actions commute. For a semistable period m, the local Galois component of σ fixes m. With x = [φ₂(m)], the exponent therefore changes by precisely −α. Writing Tσ for the operator on W, we obtain

Tσ[φn₂(m)] = [φn₂−α(m)] = [φn₁(m)].

For example at α = 2 the input is [φ²(m)] and its image is [m]. At α = −2 the input is [m] and its image is [φ²(m)]. Thus ψ reads the same physical column before and after the action using φ₂(U) and φ₁(U), respectively. This identifies the two sets of coordinates that enter the measurement of the trace. Brinon and Conrad, §9.3, the Frobenius module of a semistable representation.
