Where does the Hermitian form have a positive direction?

Let E be the CM field and τ₀ the chosen complex embedding. Write [τ] for the infinite place represented by τ and its complex conjugate. The rank-n trace form on 𝒪n uses the Hermitian matrix H = diag(−c, −c², …, −c²). Its signature records the numbers of positive and negative eigenvalues, with multiplicity. For every n ≥ 1,

sig(Hτ) = (1, n − 1) at [τ₀],   sig(Hτ) = (0, n) elsewhere.

Indeed c belongs to the real subfield, so conjugate embeddings give the same matrix. The first diagonal entry −τ(c) is positive exactly at [τ₀] by the choice of signs. Each remaining entry is the negative square of a nonzero real number. The coordinate vectors form an eigenbasis, so counting the entries proves the formula. In rank one the first entry is the entire matrix.

Which complex structure makes the trace form positive?

Let Φ consist of the embeddings τ with τ(ω) = τ₀(ω). For τ ∈ Φ write τ′ for the conjugate embedding, so τ′(b) is the complex conjugate of τ(b). The two roots of the quadratic polynomial of ω are nonreal conjugates. Thus Φ contains one embedding from each infinite place. Use its embeddings as coordinates on ℝ ⊗ℤ 𝒪n, with one copy of ℂ per pair (τ, j), 0 ≤ j < n. Let hj be the j-th diagonal entry of H. Define J on a coordinate by multiplication by +i at (τ₀, 0) and by −i everywhere else. On the conjugate type every multiplier is conjugated. This prescription gives a real operator with J² = −1 that commutes with the scalar action of 𝒪.

Write F for the real extension of the alternating trace form. Each conjugate pair contributes twice the real part of its summand in the field trace. In F(Jx, x) the multiplier in the first argument is i times the sign of τ(hj). Since τ(δ) is purely imaginary, this gives

F(Jx, x) = 2 ∑τ∈Φ, j (−Im τ(δ)) |τ(hj)| |xτ,j|².

The choice of δ makes each coefficient strictly positive. A nonzero vector has a nonzero coordinate, so F(Jx, x) > 0. Applying J to both arguments gives F(Jx, Jy) = F(x, y) because each multiplier has modulus one. Interchanging the arguments reverses the sign by alternation. Equivalently, at τ ∈ Φ the diagonal entries of −iHτJτ are |τ(hj)|. At the conjugate embedding +iHτ′Jτ′ has the same entries.

Real coordinates and the two traces of a scalar

Choose a primitive element of E/ℚ. Over ℝ its minimal polynomial factors into distinct irreducible quadratics, one for each conjugate pair of embeddings. The Chinese remainder theorem then identifies E ⊗ℚ ℝ with ∏τ∈Φ ℂ by evaluation. Extending scalars on the full lattice gives n copies of this decomposition. Stacks Project, Lemma 9.19.1 (primitive element) and Lemma 10.15.4 (Chinese remainder).

For b ∈ 𝒪 let Mb denote multiplication by b. Regard the real space as a complex space via J. On a coordinate where J acts by +i, the complex eigenvalue of Mb is τ(b). Where J acts by −i, conjugating the coordinate makes J multiplication by +i and changes the eigenvalue to τ′(b). There is exactly one exceptional coordinate. Thus the complex trace is S(b) = n ∑τ∈Φ τ′(b) + τ₀(b) − τ₀′(b). Multiplication by a complex number z has real trace 2 Re z, so

trℝ(Mb) = 2 Re S(b),   trℝ(JMb) = −2 Im S(b).

For example take E = ℚ(i, √2), ω = i, δ = −i and c = −√2. Choose τ₀ fixing i and taking √2 to its positive root. For n = 3 the two Gram matrices at the embeddings above i are diag(√2, −2, −2) and diag(−√2, −2, −2). On their six complex coordinates JMi acts once as −1 and five times as +1. Its real trace is 8, in agreement with S(i) = −4i.

Where do the parity hypotheses enter the geometric construction?

Now assume that n is odd and that the maximal real subfield E⁺ has even degree over ℚ. The arithmetic compactness criterion used here has a sufficient numerical condition, 2a < [E∶ℚ], where a counts the distinguished complex embeddings of the PEL datum. Our datum has the single distinguished embedding τ₀ and the scalar order 𝒪 has multiplicity one. On the group side, the signature calculation likewise gives a single infinite place with positive index one.

Write s = [E⁺∶ℚ]. It is positive and even, hence at least two. A CM field is quadratic over its maximal real subfield. The degree formula gives the required strict inequality,

[E∶ℚ] = 2s ≥ 4 > 2 = 2a.

This proves the compactness condition on the datum. The unitary group has rank n, so the assumed parity of n gives the odd rank used by the spectral trace test. Both the signature and the real polarization were obtained for every n ≥ 1. In the construction at the geometric prime, the split quadratic polynomial and the perfection of the integral trace form are additional finite-place inputs.
