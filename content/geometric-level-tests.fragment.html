How does one level carry all the requested spectral tests?

A test of Hodge weights needs good reduction at its place. A polynomial test needs the Hecke operators at its place. Write H for the finite set of places of the first kind and C for those of the second. We shall require H and C to be disjoint. We now construct the family of operators used for both purposes and compute its polynomial.

Hypotheses on the field and representation. The CM field E contains x with x² = −d for a positive integer d. The element −d is a square in ℚℓ, the maximal real subfield E⁺ has even degree over ℚ, and each finite place nonsplit over E⁺ is unramified over it and lies away from ℓ. Fix an identification ι of the algebraic closure of ℚℓ with ℂ. The representation U is irreducible automorphic on the definite unitary group of odd rank a. At a designated place w₀ above ℓ it has a cuspidal type. Here a torsion-free compact open subgroup maps onto a cyclic group of ℓ-power order. A nontrivial character of this quotient occurs on a nonzero vector of U, and every smooth irreducible local representation containing that character is cuspidal.

Write K = ℚ(x). Each place in H ∪ C is split over E⁺ and spherical for U, has a different restriction to E⁺ from w₀, and lies over a rational prime q for which −d is a square in ℚq. At a common rational prime the test places must all induce the same place of K. Above ℓ this must be the place induced by w₀.

Assembly theorem. Given the integral group datum, its automorphic vector and the spectral and local comparison inputs specified below, there is one level with one family of correspondences and one system of scalar values. It passes the group-side good-reduction and weight tests at every place in H and the polynomial test at every place in C. It also passes the polynomial test at all eligible spherical places away from ℓ outside one finite exception set. Here the group-side tests are the hypotheses that the geometric transfer theorem uses to obtain its conclusions about a tower.

Choosing the form and its level. Call a place u allowed if its conjugate has a different restriction to K from every requested place. At least one of u and its conjugate is allowed in each split pair. Indeed, if both were forbidden, two requested places of the same residue characteristic would induce opposite primes of K, contradicting the quadratic residue test and the compatibility hypothesis. The place w₀ is allowed for the same reason. The automorphic vector construction with this choice of sides supplies a nonzero form, an auxiliary character κ and a finite exceptional support S. At each requested place w, sphericity and separation from w₀ keep w and its conjugate outside S. The choice of sides also keeps the conjugates of the supported places away from the required prime of K. These are the freedom conditions used by the level construction.

Choose the geometric prime p outside the residue characteristics of the requested places, those of the full support S, and those where the argument of the nonzero evaluation is nonintegral. The spectral construction supplies a second prime r₀, distinct from p and from the requested residue characteristics. At this point only the prime and the spectral assertion for levels divisible by it are fixed. Apply the integer-level theorem with c = r₀. It gives a level N divisible by r₀ and a compatible site at every requested place. Their stabilizer property permits the spectral trace test at that level.

Choosing the operators. Include the eligible deck transformations (changes of level structure) with value 1. Include each eligible local Hecke correspondence at a place outside H whose site is compatible with all the requested sites. For each such entry record the scalar provided by the local action on the chosen form. The spectral assertion applies to this entire indexed family. It supplies a test function f on whose convolution words the operators commute. For each polynomial nonzero at the recorded scalars, a further polynomial multiplier gives an admissible convolution with nonzero trace. The simultaneous eigenclass in cohomology is obtained later by geometric comparison.

At a place w in C, write Q for its residue field size and B for any multiset of spherical parameters of U there. Write ei(B) for the elementary symmetric polynomial of degree i, with e₀ = 1. For each i from zero to a there is an eligible local operator. Its scalar is a subgroup index times ι(ϑi), where

ι(ϑi) = Qi(a−i)/2 ei(B) κ(w)−i.

The index counts the cosets in the correspondence and is removed in the local congruence. The coefficient of Xa−i in its Hecke polynomial is (−1)iQi(i−1)/2ι(ϑi). The two exponents of Q add to i(a−1)/2. This is precisely the exponent obtained by multiplying each of the a parameters by Q(a−1)/2 before evaluating the elementary symmetric polynomial. Thus the roots are the algebraically normalized parameters divided by κ(w), with their multiplicities. Here ι(ϑ₀) = 1, so the constructed polynomial is monic.

For a rank-three check, take Q = 5, B = {1, 2, 3} and κ(w) = 5 in the coefficient formula. The four values are 1, 6, 11/5 and 6/125. The Hecke polynomial multiplies the last two by 5 and 125 before applying the alternating signs. It gives X³ − 6X² + 11X − 6, whose roots are 1, 2 and 3.

At a place v in H, all the non-deck operators in our family come from different places and compatible sites. These are the hypotheses of the group-side good-reduction criterion. Deck transformations at its site have scalar 1. Indeed the site subgroup stabilizes the chosen vector and lies in the level at r₀, because r₀ divides the part of N prime to the residue characteristic of v. The weight comparison for the automorphic vector then supplies, at each embedding above v, all the shifted exponents required for the Hodge test. Such embeddings induce places over ℓ through ι. For a place in H away from ℓ this exponent condition is empty, while the good-reduction and unramified-character conditions still apply.

Finally exclude places whose residue characteristic divides Nep, together with those whose characteristic occurs in S. Here e is the defect integer of the arithmetic datum. The exception set is finite because the factorization of each rational prime has finitely many prime ideal factors. At every remaining eligible spherical place away from ℓ the local site may use the principal subgroup of level N. Its residue characteristic differs from those of all the prescribed sites, which divide N. The same coefficient calculation applies there. The direct calculation at each w in C is essential because its characteristic was deliberately made to divide N. The arithmetic datum, the choice of automorphic vector and frame, and the geometric comparisons still have their own existence theorems.
