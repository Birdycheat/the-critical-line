How accurately must a rectangular Frobenius equation hold?

The comparison of Weil traces requires one integral operator on the target lattice and one on each layer. These lattices can have different ranks, so the error in a comparison can be rectangular. We first determine how accurately a Frobenius equation must hold to make that error divisible by a prescribed power of ℓ.

Fix a prime ℓ, a finite extension K/ℚℓ and its Breuil–Kisin datum. Let R = Acris and let v be the image of the power-series parameter u in R. Frobenius φ acts entrywise on matrices, fixes ℓ and sends v to vℓ. The ring R is ℓ-torsion-free. All matrix congruences below are entrywise. Brinon and Conrad, CMI Summer School Notes on p-adic Hodge Theory, §9.1 and Proposition 9.1.1.

Finite-precision lemma. Fix nonnegative integers c and M. There exists an input precision n that works for all dimensions d, d′, all d by d′ matrices D over R, and all square matrices F, F′ over R of sizes d, d′. If D ≡ 0 modulo v and ℓcD ≡ Fφ(D)F′ modulo ℓn, then D ≡ 0 modulo ℓM.

Proof. Let b be the degree of the Eisenstein polynomial E. The image η of E is congruent to vb modulo ℓ, and its divided power ηℓ/ℓ! belongs to R. Hence vbℓ ∈ ℓR. Choose j₀ with ℓj₀ − 1 ≥ bℓ(cj₀ + M) and put n = cj₀ + M. We have ℓn ∣ vℓj₀−1. Write the second hypothesis as an exact equality by introducing an integral rectangular matrix G.

ℓcD = Fφ(D)F′ + ℓnG.

For each j ≥ 0 we shall find integral matrices Aj, Bj satisfying

ℓcjD = vℓjAj + ℓnBj.

For j = 0 choose an entrywise quotient A₀ with D = vA₀ and put B₀ = 0. For the induction step, multiply the original Frobenius equation by ℓcj. In its first term substitute the Frobenius image of the induction hypothesis for ℓcjφ(D). The new coefficients are

Aj+1 = Fφ(Aj)F′, Bj+1 = Fφ(Bj)F′ + ℓcjG.

Every new error still carries ℓn. At j = j₀, write vℓj₀−1 = ℓny. The claim becomes ℓcj₀D = ℓcj₀+M(vyAj₀ + Bj₀). Cancellation of ℓcj₀ in the torsion-free ring gives D ∈ ℓM Matd×d′(R). This choice of input precision depends only on the fixed datum, c and M.

How much precision can be lost? We can see the loss in the smaller ring ℤ[v]/(v⁸), with ℓ = 3 and φ(v) = v³. Take scalar matrices, F = F′ = 1 and c = 1. For D in (v), the second iterate φ²(D) vanishes because φ²(v) = v⁹. If 3D − φ(D) = 27G, applying φ once and substituting gives 9D = 27(3G + φ(G)). The ring is free over ℤ with basis 1, v, …, v⁷, so we may divide the coefficients by 9 and conclude that 3 divides D. Precision 9 at the input can fail. Indeed D = 3v + v³ gives 3D − φ(D) = 9v, while the coefficient of v³ in D is 1. Thus even a single entry can require two extra powers of 3.

Where does the exponent 3H enter the comparison of two sections?

Let 𝔖 = W(k)[[u]] be the Breuil–Kisin power-series ring and H, M nonnegative integers. Write f for Frobenius on 𝔖 followed by evaluation in R, and f₀ for the same map applied to the constant term. Both commute with Frobenius. Write f(E) = ℓw and f₀(E) = ℓw₀, with w, w₀ units. The normalization lemma supplies these units.

Comparison theorem. There is an n depending only on the datum, H and M. Take any common scalar exponent k and any ranks d, d₁. Let A and A₁ be square Frobenius matrices of those sizes over 𝔖. Assume the layer has a companion A₁′ satisfying A₁A₁′ = E2HI. Assume both matrices have integral crystalline section pairs (Q, Q′) and (Q₁, Q₁′) at height H and exponent k. In each pair the product is ℓ2kI, and each member reduces to ℓkI modulo v. Each section also satisfies the Frobenius identity for its matrix. For a rectangular C over 𝔖 satisfying CA₁ ≡ Aφ(C) modulo ℓn, we have

f(C)Q₁ ≡ Qf₀(C) (mod ℓM).

Proof. Set X = f(C), X₀ = f₀(C) and D = XQ₁ − QX₀. Reducing first modulo v gives ℓk(X − X₀), since the two scalar residues have the same exponent. For every entry z of C we have z − z(0) ∈ u𝔖, so f(z) − f₀(z) is a multiple of vℓ. This gives the first factor v in the defect.

To obtain its equation, write α = f(A), β = f₀(A), and use subscripts 1 for A₁. Put β₁′ = f₀(A₁′), s = (ℓw)H and t = (ℓw₀)H. The two section identities are sQβ = tαφ(Q) and sQ₁β₁ = tα₁φ(Q₁). The companion at level 1 gives β₁β₁′ = t²I. Define the two evaluated errors

L = Xα₁ − αφ(X), L₀ = X₀β₁ − βφ(X₀).

They are the images of CA₁ − Aφ(C) under f and f₀, so both are divisible by ℓn. Substitute their definitions into φ(D) and use both section identities. This gives the exact equality

tαφ(D) = sDβ₁ − tLφ(Q₁) + sQL₀.

Multiply on the right by β₁′. The coefficient of D is now st² = ℓ3HwHw₀2H. Put μ = (wHw₀2H)−1. Multiplication by this unit gives

ℓ3HD − (μtα)φ(D)β₁′ = μtLφ(Q₁)β₁′ − μsQL₀β₁′.

Every entry on the right is a multiple of ℓn. The finite-precision lemma with c = 3H, F = μtα and F′ = β₁′ now gives the assertion. The scalar example at 3 shows why a recurrence that loses a power on each iteration needs stronger input precision. One factor of height H comes from the section equation and two from the companion product on the right. The comparison uses the given pair of sections and their common residue. The next step obtains the required congruence for C from a comparison of the two frame bases.

How does a comparison of two frames descend to their integral period matrices?

Consider a target frame and a layer frame over the same Breuil–Kisin datum, with ranks d and d₁ and common height H. Write W = W(ℂℓ♭) for the Witt ring of the tilt field. There are two smaller coefficient rings in this proof. The integral periods belong to Ainf, the Witt ring of the tilt valuation ring. The Frobenius matrices have entries in 𝔖. Denote the evaluation 𝔖 → Ainf by e, and its composite with the inclusion into W by eW.

Let T and T₁ be the two invertible basis matrices in W. Take the common primitive period τ and put Y = τHT and Y₁ = τHT₁ in W, assuming as in the integral comparison that both matrices have entries in Ainf. Set ξ = eW(E). The frame equations are ξHφ(T) = T eW(A) and ξHφ(T₁) = T₁eW(A₁).

Descent of a comparison at a given precision. Let Π and C be d by d₁ matrices over ℤℓ and 𝔖 respectively. Suppose that at precision h ≥ 0 the projected frame bases satisfy

ΠT₁ ≡ T eW(C)  (mod ℓh in W).

Then the integral period matrices and the Frobenius matrices satisfy the following congruences in their respective rings.

ΠY₁ ≡ Y e(C)  (mod ℓh in Ainf),
CA₁ ≡ Aφ(C)  (mod ℓh in 𝔖).

Proof. Multiply the input comparison by τH. The entries on both sides now lie in Ainf. Divisibility by ℓh in W descends to this ring. Indeed divisibility of an integral Witt vector by ℓ in W means its first Witt coordinate vanishes. It therefore vanishes in the tilt valuation ring as well. Over a perfect ring, the kernel of the first Witt coordinate is ℓ times the Witt ring, so we can divide the original entry by ℓ in Ainf. Cancelling ℓ in W and repeating gives the assertion at precision h. See the Witt coordinate formula for multiplication by ℓ. This gives the first congruence.

For the second, write ΠT₁ − T eW(C) = ℓhG in W. Frobenius fixes Π entrywise and commutes with eW. Expand the two frame equations, then subtract. One obtains the identity

T eW(CA₁ − Aφ(C)) = ℓh(ξHφ(G) − G eW(A₁)).

In fact the left term T eW(C)eW(A₁) can be written as (ΠT₁ − ℓhG)eW(A₁). Replacing T₁eW(A₁) and T eW(A) by their Frobenius expressions gives the displayed identity. Multiply by T−1 on the left and descend from W to Ainf as above.

It remains to descend through e. Modulo ℓ the map from k[[u]] to the tilt valuation ring is injective. A nonzero series is a power of u times a unit, and its image is a power of the nonzero tilt parameter times a unit. Thus if e(s) is divisible by ℓ, then s = ℓs′ in 𝔖. If e(s) is divisible by ℓh with h ≥ 1, cancelling ℓ in Ainf gives the same condition at precision h − 1 for s′. Induction on h gives the second congruence in 𝔖.

Comparison after adjoining the sections. Fix output precision M. Take the input n of the section comparison for height H and order M, and take h = max(n, M) in the frame comparison. For any two integral sections Q and Q₁ with the same scalar exponent k, let y, y₁ be the images of Y, Y₁ under Witt Frobenius followed by inclusion into R = Acris. Write Z = yQ and Z₁ = y₁Q₁. The first congruence, transformed by this map, gives Πy₁ ≡ y f(C) modulo ℓM. The second gives the hypothesis needed for f(C)Q₁ ≡ Qf₀(C) modulo ℓM. Combining in this order gives

ΠZ₁ − Zf₀(C) = (Πy₁ − y f(C))Q₁ + y(f(C)Q₁ − Qf₀(C)) ≡ 0  (mod ℓM).

The projection on the left has entries in ℤℓ. The matrix on the right is the constant-term evaluation of C. This distinction matters when applying different powers of Frobenius to the two period matrices in the Weil operator.
