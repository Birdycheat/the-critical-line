How much of a logarithmic derivative comes from nearby zeros?

A zero of order a gives a term a/(s−ρ) in f′/f. To move a contour near many zeros, we need to bound both their total multiplicity and the part of the derivative left after removing them. Both follow from the same comparison of the size of f at the centre of a disk with its size on the boundary.

Local zero expansion. Let f be analytic on a neighbourhood of the closed disk of centre z₀ and radius r > 0. Suppose f(z₀) ≠ 0 and |f(z)| ≤ B|f(z₀)| throughout the disk, with B ≥ 1. Let Z consist of all zeros at distance at most r/3 from the centre. Write aρ for the order at ρ and N for the sum of these orders. At every s within r/6 of the centre where f(s) ≠ 0,

N ≤ log B / log 2,   |f′(s)/f(s) − ∑ρ∈Z aρ/(s−ρ)| ≤ 72 log B / r.

The zeros in the smaller closed disk are finite, since an accumulation point there would force f to vanish identically. Divide f by the polynomial P(z) = ∏(z−ρ)aρ. At each zero its Taylor expansion shows that the quotient g extends holomorphically with a nonzero value. At the centre, |P(z₀)| ≤ (r/3)N. On the outer circle each zero is at least 2r/3 away. There |P| ≥ (2r/3)N, hence |g| ≤ B 2−N|g(z₀)|. The maximum-modulus principle carries this inequality back to the centre. Cancelling |g(z₀)| gives 2N ≤ B. Maximum-modulus principle, DLMF §1.10(v).

On the disk of radius r/3 the quotient is zero-free. Choose the analytic logarithm H of g/g(z₀) with H(z₀) = 0. Its real part is at most log B by the preceding bound. The Borel–Carathéodory inequality gives |H| ≤ 6 log B on the disk of radius r/4, since 2(r/4)/(r/3−r/4) = 6. For |s−z₀| ≤ r/6, the circle of radius r/12 about s stays in that disk. Cauchy’s estimate bounds |H′(s)| by 72 log B/r. Since f′/f = P′/P + H′, this is exactly the remainder in the displayed formula. Siu, the Borel–Carathéodory theorem · Cauchy’s integral formula for derivatives, DLMF §1.9(iii).

For example, take f(z) = exp(z)(z−1/9)² on the unit disk about zero. At the centre f′/f = −17. The zero contributes 2/(0−1/9) = −18, and the remaining 1 is the logarithmic derivative of the exponential. Counting the zero once would leave an extra pole in the remainder.

Which disk controls a Dirichlet character at height t?

Fix an integer m ≥ 3, any Dirichlet character χ modulo m, and any real t. Set z₀ = 101/100 + it and r = 2/5. For the principal character take F(s) = (s−1)L(s,χ), continued at 1. For the other characters take F = L(·,χ). All these functions are analytic on the outer disk. For σ = Re s > 1 the Euler product gives |1/L(s,χ)| ≤ ζ(σ) ≤ 1 + 1/(σ−1), the last inequality by comparison with an integral. Hence |L(z₀,χ)| ≥ 1/101. In the principal case |z₀−1| ≥ 1/100. The uniform centre bound is therefore |F(z₀)| ≥ 1/10100. Dirichlet series, analytic continuation and Euler products, DLMF §25.15(i).

The whole disk lies in σ ≥ 1/2. For a nonprincipal character the complete period sums to zero, so |∑n≤xχ(n)| ≤ m. Partial summation expresses L(s,χ) as s times the integral of this partial sum against x−s−1 on [1,∞), giving |L| ≤ 2m|s|. For the principal character the finite factors ∏p∣m(1−p−s) have product of absolute values at most ∏p∣mp ≤ m. Applying the same partial summation to the integer count ⌊x⌋ = x−{x} gives ζ(s) = s/(s−1) − s∫₁∞ {x}x−s−1 dx. The integral is bounded by 1/σ. After removing the pole we obtain |F(s)| ≤ C₀m(1+|s|)² with an absolute constant C₀ ≥ 1.

Since |s| ≤ |t|+2 on this disk, the preceding lemma applies with B = 10100C₀m(|t|+3)². Its logarithm is at most a fixed multiple of log(m(|t|+2)). Thus the disk of radius 2/15 contains at most that order of zeros, with multiplicity. For |s−z₀| ≤ 1/15, s ≠ 1 and F(s) ≠ 0, the sign needed in the contour integral is

−L′(s,χ)/L(s,χ) = 𝟙χ principal/(s−1) − ∑|ρ−z₀|≤2/15, F(ρ)=0 aρ/(s−ρ) + E(s),   |E(s)| ≤ 180 log B.

If the contour stays at distance at least δ from each listed zero, the pole sum costs at most N/δ. In the smoothed progression formula, only zeros with real part at least 9/10 need to be avoided. The contour has real part at least 19/20, so all other zeros are already at least 1/20 away. To the right of 107/100 the absolutely convergent series for −L′/L supplies a uniform bound.

To count the zeros in the window |Im ρ−t| ≤ 1 and 9/10 ≤ Re ρ < 1, take the fifteen centres with real part 101/100 and heights t+k/7 for −7 ≤ k ≤ 7. Choose the nearest of these heights to Im ρ. Its vertical distance is at most 1/14 and the horizontal distance is at most 11/100. Since (11/100)²+(1/14)² < (2/15)², the zero belongs to one of the counting disks. Summing the fifteen disk bounds counts every zero at least as many times as its multiplicity. Their heights differ from t by at most one, so the window count is still bounded by a constant times log(m(|t|+2)). This gives the short-interval multiplicity count. The power-sum argument uses the disk lemma with centre 26/25 + it and radius 12/25, then differentiates the analytic remainder by Cauchy’s formula.
