The fixed-witness estimate bounds the number of flags once their witness lattice is given. To average this bound we also need to count witnesses. Check both counts in one Gaussian lattice

Take the identity Hermitian form on ℤ[i]³. A primitive normal vector v determines U = v⊥, and four unit multiples give the same hyperplane. Put d = ⟨v,v⟩. Primitivity gives a vector w pairing to one with v. A basis of P followed by w is a basis of the full lattice. The squared length of the projection of w onto the normal line is 1/d. Factoring the Gram determinant of that basis thus gives 1 = det Gram(P)/d. The Gaussian index of the dual quotient, counted as a finite abelian group, is the absolute norm of this determinant, namely d².

At d = 3 the three coordinate norms are 1,1,1 or 0,1,2 up to permutation. In the first case the four phases per coordinate give 4³/4 = 16 hyperplanes. In the second there are 6 orders, four phases for the norm-one entry and four for the norm-two entry, giving 6·4²/4 = 24. Every integral isometry permutes the three coordinate axes and assigns a Gaussian unit to each, so the group has order 6·4³ = 384. The two stabilizers have orders 24 and 16. Counting marks at the lattice gives 40/384. Counting by orbits gives 1/24 + 1/16, the same 5/48. This example checks the change of order for one ambient lattice.

What is left when a hyperplane is fixed? Split the space orthogonally into U and a line. A lattice L gives a pair of intersections, one in each factor. Put t = Tuf. In this genus the ambient discriminant module is isomorphic over 𝒪E to 𝒪E/(Tu). One generator suffices over that ring. As an additive group it has t² elements and Tu kills every element. The hyperplane intersection has discriminant order d². The line intersection has discriminant order between 1 and d²t². For fixed intersections the line-gluing estimate permits at most t²d² ambient lattices. The count therefore uses the product of two masses, one for each factor, multiplied by this gluing allowance.
