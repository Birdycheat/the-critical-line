A bound for a centered prime sum can survive a surprisingly large error in a single arithmetic measurement. The proof takes measurements at many places and along a chain, then divides by their total weight. This chapter follows that division and accounts for each power of the scale that it costs.

Choose a > 0, a translation τ ∈ H, weights wρ > 0 for ρ ∈ Φ, and an integer cutoff N ≥ a/wρ for every such ρ. The host data distinguish a prime d and a finite set S containing 2 and the auxiliary parameter q1, with d outside S. Outside S and d inertia is trivial. At primes of S, both inertia and the specified Frobenius element lie in Φ. At d, the inertia elements are exactly zero and the involution sgc0 = ((1,0),0). Inertia here records the local symmetries invisible on the residue field. Frobenius describes raising residues to the residue-field size. The source packages these assumptions as HostFactsS. Write A for the weighted prime-power count at one sample and one chain stage. To assign a weight to g outside Φ, pair it with ρ = ((1,1),0) + g + ((0,0),τ) inside Φ. At n = pi the weight is (a/n − wρ)+, where the subscript means the positive part. At an unramified prime the character sum is 2 if g equals the ith Frobenius power and zero otherwise. Its contribution is therefore twice log p times the weight when that power lies outside Φ. At a prime of S the Frobenius coset lies inside Φ and contributes zero. At d the inertia subgroup has order two, so averaging replaces 2 by 1 at each matching element outside Φ. Each such weight contributes log d times its value. Which characters survive?

Let A0 be a subgroup of H. For ψ a character of E, put Sψ = ∑u ∈ A₀ ψ(0, −u). Translation by any v ∈ A0 permutes this sum and multiplies it by ψ(0, −v). Thus the sum vanishes whenever one of these multipliers differs from one. Otherwise it equals |A0|. For e1(τ) = ((1,0),τ) and s ∈ A0, the corresponding shifted sum acquires the phase ψ(e1(τ)). Summing over s ∈ T2 ⊆ A0 and averaging over subgroup translations therefore contributes a density δ2/2 with δ2 = |T2|/|H|. The two in the denominator comes from |Φ| = 2|H|. Translation and density identities

For example, take H = ℤ/4ℤ and A0 = {0, 2}. On H the four characters are ψj(h) = exp(2πijh/4) for j = 0, 1, 2, 3. Averaging ψj over A0 gives (1 + (−1)j)/2. The odd indices cancel and the even ones survive with value 1. If T2 contains one element, its contribution occupies one of the eight positions in Φ. The factor 1/8 comes from that count, independently of which surviving character is evaluated.

At a scale x ≥ 3, fix a loss ε > 0, a real sampling weight c with |c| ≤ 1, and a finite sample T ⊆ H. The survivors form the annihilator Ψ of A0. A further selection K ⊆ Ψ retains characters used in the target. For these the source imposes ψ(sgc0) = 1 and includes the trivial character. Among retained characters, a character trivial on all of H receives weight 1. The others receive c. Call these weights ωψ. For retained characters nontrivial on H, the sum over T of ψ(0,τ) differs from c|T| in norm by at most M0x−1+ε|T|, where M0 also bounds |Ψ|. The constants bounding ℓ on retained nontrivial and discarded characters are respectively K0 and CE log x. The sum of norms of the discarded centered ramps is denoted Σ.
