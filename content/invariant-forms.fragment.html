Why does one trace per complex place suffice on the filtered cycles?

The transported mean gives a form ω invariant under every archimedean change of basis. To integrate it on a homology class, we first need its integral to vanish on boundaries. Write M = ω(I) for its value at the identity tuple and Y₁,…,Ym for Hermitian tangent vectors. All matrix operations below are taken place by place. For a positive Hermitian tuple x, the matrix g = x−1 takes I to x², and sends Y to xYx. Invariance therefore gives ω(x²)(xY₁x,…,xYmx) = M(Y₁,…,Ym). Near I the squaring map has a smooth inverse because its derivative is twice the identity. Solving this formula for ω by undoing each tangent map gives smoothness near I. To obtain the derivative, put x = I + tX in the preceding identity. The base point moves with velocity 2X and the jth argument with velocity XYj + YjX. The right side is constant. To obtain the exterior derivative, alternate the left side of the displayed formula in its m + 1 inputs. Swapping X and the selected Yj preserves their sum of products and reverses the sign. Every term cancels. Invariance carries dω = 0 from I to every positive tuple. Stokes’ theorem now makes the integral of ω over the boundary of every affine simplex zero. Its periods therefore descend to homology. The two Hecke estimates then show that the transported mean has the periods of the original detecting cocycle. Count the independent block labels. At I, M is invariant under unitary conjugation at each place. The tensor invariant theorem expresses it as a real linear combination of alternating real or imaginary parts of products of traces. Each product is indexed by a permutation of the m inputs. A cycle (i₁ … iℓ) contributes tr(Yi₁,w ⋯ Yiℓ,w) at a single place w. This trace sums over matrix indices around a closed loop. Replace each matrix by d identical diagonal blocks. A nonzero matrix entry must stay within one block, so the entire loop chooses a single block in d ways. With j permutation cycles there are dj choices. In odd degree m, a single cycle orders all the matrices at one place. Relabeling the inputs of its alternating trace changes only the sign. Reversing their order conjugates the trace because each matrix is Hermitian. Reversal has sign (−1)m(m−1)/2, so the alternating trace is real for m ≡ 1 modulo 4 and purely imaginary for m ≡ 3 modulo 4. This leaves at most one real functional per place. The filter on the selected cycles kills every weight from 2 to m. Any surviving invariant period is thus a linear combination of these r₂ one-place evaluations. Why do these trace products span all invariant tensors?

After allowing complex scalars, the tensor at the identity is represented by a matrix T acting on a tensor power. Unitary invariance says that T commutes with every tensor power of a placewise unitary matrix. Consider the operators that permute the tensor slots and select a place in each slot. The first finite-dimensional matrix result says that every operator commuting with all of them is a linear combination of those unitary tensor powers. Thus T commutes with all such operators. The second matrix result says that this puts T itself in the span of the slot permutations with place selections. In one such term, a matrix entry links index ai to aπ(i). Summing around each cycle gives its trace. If the place changes across a link, the entry vanishes. Taking real parts and alternating recovers the real tensor M. Check the sign and the weight in degree three

At one place take X = [[0,1],[1,0]], Y = [[0,−i],[i,0]] and Z = diag(1,−1), where i² = −1. These three Hermitian matrices anticommute in pairs, and XY = iZ. Thus tr(XYZ) = 2i. Each of the six permutations changes the trace by its own sign, so the signed sum is 12i. The real functional supplied by its imaginary part takes value 12. Two identical blocks give 24 through the same calculation. These are values on tangent vectors at I.
