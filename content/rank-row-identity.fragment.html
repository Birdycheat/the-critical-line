When does the tangent algebra contain a whole row of a group matrix?

Write A for the unital algebra generated by the tangent Lie algebra 𝔤, and let Vi be the image of the weight projector pi. For an operator G, its ith row means the map piG from the whole space V to Vi. The corner piaGpi first applies G to vectors in Vi, then a, then projects back onto that same space.

Proposition. Work over an algebraically closed field K of characteristic zero. Suppose each group matrix has minimal polynomial of degree at most m. Take the m nonzero orthogonal projectors of a tangent matrix with lacunary weights, with sum equal to the identity. Use the tangent-algebra properties proved in the matching of projectors and the separation of cycles. In particular, the projectors belong to A, its diagonal corners are scalar, it is normalized by the group, its off-diagonal corners lie in 𝔤, and each nonzero b in A admits a in A such that ab is nonnilpotent. Then for every G = ρ(g) and every i,

if  piaGpi ≠ 0  for some a ∈ A,   then  piG = piw  for some w ∈ A.

Put qj = G−1pjG. The matching of the two projector systems gives an invertible v in A and a permutation π satisfying vpj = qπ(j)v. This matching keeps the label i fixed whenever qiApi is nonzero. To apply this clause, take the a from the proposition and put b = G−1aG ∈ A. Then qibpi = G−1(piaGpi) ≠ 0. Consequently π(i) = i.

Factor v = ud into a product u of elementary matrices and an invertible diagonal scaling d of the weight spaces. Both factors belong to A. Since d commutes with the projectors, Gu induces π on the weight spaces. Apply the separation of cycles with the full group as the subgroup of finite index. It gives another invertible diagonal scaling t such that u and t lie in the image closure, and the operator y = Gut has disjoint spectra on different cycles. Here the separation applies to every eigenvalue on each cycle. The construction excludes collisions for all pairs of eigenvalues of the return maps. Since the closure is closed under multiplication, y lies there as well. The polynomial equations for the degree bound therefore give a nonzero polynomial P of degree at most m with P(y) = 0.

On each cycle of length k, the cyclic eigenvector construction gives k distinct eigenvalues, each with an eigenvector supported on that cycle. Their union Λ has size m because the cycles partition the labels and have disjoint spectra. Every λ ∈ Λ is a root of P, as one sees by applying P(y) to its eigenvector. Hence P is a nonzero constant times ∏λ∈Λ(T − λ). This counts roots of an annihilating polynomial even when the original space has dimension greater than m.

The fixed label i contributes a single eigenvalue c. On Vi, every y − λI with λ ∈ Λ other than c is invertible. A nonzero vector in its kernel would give the same eigenvalue on this fixed space and on another cycle. Cancel these invertible factors in P(y) = 0 on Vi. The remaining factor gives y = cI there. Since y commutes with pi at a fixed label, we have piy = cpi on all of V. Multiplying on the right by (ut)−1 now gives the promised row,

piG = piw,   w = c (ut)−1 ∈ A.

Indeed t−1 is the sum of the projectors weighted by the reciprocal scalars. The inverse of each elementary factor I + N of u is I − N because N² = 0. Thus the witness lies in the tangent-generated algebra itself. This supplies the row identity used in the passage to the span of the products aρ(g). The scalar-corner and nilpotence properties of A enter separately in the matching of the projectors.

A fixed plane. Let G act as the identity on a plane and exchange two additional coordinate lines. Take u = I and let t act by 3, 1 and 4 on the three weight spaces. On the exchanged lines (Gt)² = 4I. Thus Gt has eigenvalues 2, −2 and 3, and its minimal polynomial is (T² − 4)(T − 3). For the projection p onto the plane, the choice a = I gives pGp = p. The formula gives w = 3t−1, whose row pw equals p. Replacing the action of Gt on the plane by a size-two Jordan block at 3 would preserve these three eigenvalues and raise the degree of the minimal polynomial to four. The bound of three rules out precisely that extra factor.
