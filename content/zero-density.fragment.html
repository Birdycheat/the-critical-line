How can one count characters whose L-function has a zero?

Write Q for the height H of the box in the preceding prime selection. Consider all primitive characters of conductor at most Q that have at least one zero ρ with Re ρ > 399/400 and |Im ρ| ≤ Q. The number of these characters is at most CQ1/4 for every real Q ≥ 3 and a single absolute constant C. One zero, chosen separately for each character, suffices. The argument turns it into a large finite sum on one interval, then bounds how many characters can give large sums on the same interval. Detecting one zero. For large Q take X = ⌈Q⌉, Y = X⁴, J = 8(⌊log₂ X⌋ + 1) and N = 2J. Here Y is the length of an auxiliary sum within this proof. Multiply the first N terms of L(s,χ), denoted P(s), by M(s) = ∑d≤Y μ(d)χ(d)d−s, summing over positive integers. The Möbius function μ is zero on multiples of squares of primes. On a product of k distinct primes it is (−1)k, including μ(1) = 1. In the product the coefficient multiplying χ(n)n−s is

a(n) = ∑db=n, 1≤d≤Y, 1≤b≤N μ(d).

For 2 ≤ n ≤ Y every divisor occurs, since Y ≤ N. The sum is zero by expanding (1−1)k over the distinct prime divisors. At n = 1 it is one. Thus PM = 1 + D, where D uses only Y < n ≤ YN. For a short calculation set Y = 3 and N = 4. At 6 only (2,3) and (3,2) qualify, so a(6) = −2. At 4 only (1,4) and (2,2) occur, and their contributions cancel. Truncating the product can leave either outcome beyond the initial range. Why must at least one block be large at a zero?

For a primitive character of conductor q > 1, complete periods of χ sum to zero. The resulting partial-sum bound and partial summation give, at the chosen zero with σ = Re ρ, |P(ρ)| ≤ 2(q+1)(1+|ρ|/σ)N−σ. Every term of the second polynomial has modulus at most one, so |M(ρ)| ≤ Y. When Q ≥ 20 we have q+1 ≤ 2X and 1+|ρ|/σ ≤ 3X. Also N ≥ X⁸ and σ > 15/16, so Nσ ≥ X⁷√X ≥ 4X⁷. Multiplication by Y = X⁴ gives |PM| ≤ 3/X ≤ 1/2. Since PM = 1 + D, the reverse triangle inequality forces |D| ≥ 1/2. Split (Y,YN] into J successive blocks (U,2U] with U = Y2j. At least one block sum has modulus ≥ 1/(2J). How many characters can select the same block?

Write σ₀ = 399/400. Suppose T characters selected one block (U, 2U]. Place their values in rows vχ(n) = χ(n)n−(ρχ−σ₀), and put b(n) = a(n)n−σ₀. Multiplying each row by this same column gives its selected large block. The squared norm of a row is at most U. For two different rows the character χψ̄ at their common product modulus is nonprincipal: equality of the induced characters would identify their conductors and then the original primitive characters. Complete periods of this product sum to zero, so its partial sums have modulus at most the product of the conductors, at most Q². Partial summation against the complex power gives the bound 2Q²(1+|w|) for the inner product, with w = ρχ−σ₀ + conj(ρψ−σ₀). Each of these nonprincipal zeros has real part below 1, and the two imaginary parts have absolute values at most Q. Thus the inner product has modulus at most 8Q³.
