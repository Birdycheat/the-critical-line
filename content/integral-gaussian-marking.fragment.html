How can the same quotient give the wrong marking?

On the elliptic curve E = ℂ/ℤ[i], a Gaussian integer x labels the m-torsion point ηm(x) = x/m modulo ℤ[i]. Two labels give the same point precisely when they are congruent modulo mℤ[i]. A marking remembers which label gives each point. For the complex-lattice description, see Milne, Elliptic Curves, “Elliptic Curves over the Complex Numbers”, Proposition 3.3.

Take h to be multiplication by 1 + i. It is onto, since every complex representative has a preimage under division by 1 + i. Its kernel is (1 + i)−1ℤ[i]/ℤ[i]. Multiplication by 1 + i identifies this group with ℤ[i]/(1 + i), a field with two elements since i ≡ −1 and 2 ≡ 0. The two kernel points are 0 and (1 + i)/2. Thus h identifies the quotient by this subgroup with E. The next task is to recover a prescribed marking of level seven on that quotient.

The source has level fourteen. We want to lift a label x modulo seven to level fourteen and obtain η₇(x) after the isogeny. The factor of two between the levels prescribes F = 2/h = 1 − i. For any Gaussian integer representative z of x,

h(η₁₄(Fz)) = (1 + i)(1 − i)z/14 = z/7 = η₇(x).

The intermediate point can depend on the representative. Replacing z by z + 7y changes it by (1 − i)y/2. Applying h gives y, which is zero on the curve. Both kernel elements occur as changes, for y = 0 and 1. This explains why the expression gives a well-defined point on the quotient.

Try using 1 + i in place of F. It generates the same subgroup of differences, since 1 + i = i(1 − i) and i is a unit. The output is now iz/7 because (1 + i)² = 2i. The label 1 gives i/7 in place of 1/7. Multiplication by i permutes the 49 points of level seven, so a count of those points would also miss the error. The two kernel points count the degree of this isogeny. The 49 labels describe the target marking.

For the general lattice the element κh−1 acts on the completed lattice. The two lattice congruences give finite-level formulas for the kernel and the target labels, just as 1 − i did here.
