How much of the fundamental domain can lie in the deep region?

Choose a point x using Haar measure on the fundamental domain divided by its volume V. The question is now about the point itself. If its least normalized sublattice size is below the deep cutoff τ∗, how often can this occur? Since τ∗ < ½, it suffices to bound the probability that some step of the lattice filtration occurs below ½.

Short-flag probability. For the finite-unramified CM data of the dimension count, let the unitary group have signature (p,q) at each of the f real places, and put n = p + q. Suppose log n ≥ 15, q² ≤ n, the root discriminant d satisfies log d ≤ exp(n) and log f ≤ √n. With U = nf log n,

Pr(least size < ½) ≤ exp(−U/3),   μ(deep) ≤ V exp(−U/3).

By the filtration criterion, a least size strictly below ½ is equivalent to a nonzero step strictly below ½. Its canonical flag ends in a totally isotropic subspace. The positive jumps j₁,…,ja in its ranks add to the rank J of that subspace, so 1 ≤ J ≤ q. The successive sizes satisfy 0 < τ₁ < … < τa < ½. Thus the event is covered by measurable sets indexed by ordered compositions of these ranks. This cover lets us bound its volume with the same flag density that appeared in the near orbital integral.

What is the probability of one ordered rank type?

The flag-measure theorem bounds that probability by κj times the integral of the size density over the ordered simplex. Its arithmetic input is a comparison of quotient volumes: unfold a marked flag of rank J, use the residual Hermitian space of signature (p−J, q−J), then divide the resulting bound by the full volume. The arithmetic estimate for the resulting coefficient is κj ≤ exp(−JU/2).

For the volume alone there is no orbital weight multiplying the density. All the size factors λ from the fixed-type calculation lie between zero and one. Retain the factors τi raised to 2fji(n−2J) and bound the other factors by one. Here n − 2J ≥ 1, since J ≤ q ≤ √n and log n ≥ 15. Combining the retained powers with the factors 2fji/τi bounds the density by ∏i cᵢτicᵢ−1, where cᵢ = 2fji. Enlarge the domain to the unit cube. Each coordinate integrates to [scᵢ]01 = 1. This proves

Pr(flag of type j) ≤ κj ≤ exp(−JU/2).

For f = 1 and rank jumps (1,2), the cube majorant is (2s)(4v³). On 0 < s < v < ½ its integral is ∫0½ 4v⁵ dv = 1/96. It becomes 1/64 on the half-size square and 1 on the full unit square. This computes only the majorant. The factor κj carries the arithmetic dependence on n.

Where does the factor exp(−JU/2) in the flag coefficient come from?

The explicit coefficient contains discriminant and power-of-two factors, a ratio of compact unitary volume products, a power of the Dedekind zeta residue, a product of zeta values at positive integers ≥ 2, and a reciprocal product of quadratic-character factors. The large positive index p in the compact volume ratio gives the dominant decrease. The following scalar comparison displays how much of it survives the other factors.

Put s = √n, L = log n and c = log(2π). Express each upper bound as exp(fJ times a cost). The discriminant and power of two cost at most n/3 and 0. The compact-volume comparison costs at most c − (n − 2s)(L − c − 1) + 3.49 + sL/4. The residue costs at most 2(3 + n), the zeta product at most 2, and the inverse quadratic-character product at most 1. These are the arithmetic and compact-volume comparisons used here. Since 0 ≤ c ≤ 2, their sum is at most

−nL + (9/4)sL + (16/3)n + 14.49.

Here s ≥ 100 and L ≥ 15. The three positive terms divided by nL are at most 9/400, 16/45 and 14.49/150,000. Their sum is below ½, giving a total cost at most −nL/2. Multiplication by fJ gives the claimed exponent.

From one flag to the volume lost in the trace

Write z = exp(−U/2). Each of the 2J−1 compositions of J contributes at most zJ. Taking the sum of the measures of the covering sets gives

Pr(least size < ½) ≤ ∑J=1q 2J−1zJ ≤ z/(1 − 2z) ≤ 2z ≤ exp(−U/3).

Indeed U ≥ 15 gives z ≤ ¼ and 2 ≤ exp(U/6). Returning to the fundamental domain multiplies the probability by V. The identity term has constant value c₀ there, so its omitted integral is at most c₀V exp(−U/3) ≤ M exp(−U/4). This supplies the deep-region identity contribution in the four-term estimate. Bounding the integral of the full projection kernel on these same points also requires control of how concentrated its basis functions can be.
