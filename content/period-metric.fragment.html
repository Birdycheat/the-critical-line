Why the root discriminant appears

Write n = [E : ℚ], h = [E : K0] = n/2. Let b be the absolute norm of the product of the host fractional ideal with the relative different of E/K0. The ideal-index calculation gives I(s)n bDM = |NM/ℚ(s)|n. Taking logarithms and summing the local metrics eliminates the norm of s. Hence

Ω̄τ = (1/DM) ∑ρ log(Aρ/π) − (2/n) log b.

Set B equal to the absolute norm of the relative different. Its half-different identity gives b² = B. The discriminant tower formula gives |disc(E)| = B(dK)h. Since rd(E) = |disc(E)|1/n, the correction (2/n) log b equals log rd(E) − log √dK. For the area term the proof compares the conjugate lattices with a family indexed by ideal classes. Over a common extension their homotheties give 6 log Aoriginal = 6 log Afamily + log |ρ(u)|, with u a discriminant unit. The unit contributes zero to the sum over embeddings. Averaging over the extension gives the same mean as over M, because each embedding occurs with the same multiplicity. The Chowla–Selberg class-sum formula then evaluates the area mean as γ + log(2π/√dK) − dχ, where dχ = Re(L′(1,χ)/L(1,χ)) for the host quadratic character. This dχ is the scalar denoted d in the main-term calculation above. Subtracting the correction cancels log √dK. For every compatible M and every τ this gives

Ω̄τ = γ + log(2π/rd(E)) − dχ = Ωlog.

This identifies the scalar that occurs earlier with v = rd(E)/2. The calculation uses the CM family of period lattices, the Chowla–Selberg evaluation and the ideal-index formula as inputs. From embeddings to column heights

The coefficient embeddings ρ and the infinite places w of the field of the column matrices index different lists. The kernel estimate chooses one field M containing the coefficients and the generators aτ, and nonnegative weights Wwρ. For each w the weights sum to one. For each ρ their average over w equals 1/DM. Define the period at a matrix place by pτ(w) = ∑ρ Wwρ[−mρ(aτ)]. Interchanging two finite sums gives

meanw pτ(w) = ∑ρ (meanw Wwρ)[−mρ(aτ)] = Ωlog.

Each place can have its own period. Only its mean is prescribed. The kernel theorem inserts these local numbers into its logarithmic bounds for the matrix factors. It treats the vacuum theta norm separately by an upper bound on its mean. The construction also needs the character and weight identifications, the field of definition, the trivial twist and the sharp local data of the selected column. An independent estimate for the final evaluation map and positive definite row metrics then turn the bounds into framed rows with an invertible ramified frame at every infinite place.
