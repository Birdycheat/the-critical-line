The lattices that carry the comparison

Over a number field K, an arithmetic lattice in a finite-dimensional K-vector space V is a finitely generated module over the ring of integers of K spanning V. Why the slope bound contains a logarithm

Think of a lattice through its finite list of Harder–Narasimhan slopes, each repeated according to rank. A step in its filtration contains the directions whose slopes meet a given threshold. For a slope λ of the underlying real lattice the arithmetic normalization is 2λ + cK, with the same field constant for all words and the target. The resulting list has arithmetic mean equal to the lattice's mean slope. For the source lattices subtract each height allowance and concatenate the lists over all words. Their mean is s and every entry is at most s + bF. If a fraction p lies below s − τ, for τ ≥ 0, then the mean is at most p(s − τ) + (1 − p)(s + bF). Rearranging gives p ≤ bF/(bF + τ) when the denominator is positive. If both quantities vanish, all slopes equal s and the strict lower tail is empty. This bounds the rank lost when we discard low-slope steps in the source.

At a fixed infinite place, let Ai be the word maps on all D rows, with each positive frame weight absorbed into its map by multiplying by the square root of the weight. Write r for the sum of their source dimensions, after the ramified restriction and before the slope cutoff. Both r and D are positive. The retained source step for word i spans a complex subspace Ui. Its codimension di counts the discarded source slopes for that word, so ∑i di = pr. The rank estimate from the minors and the choice of frame errors give the following hypotheses on the maps before this cutoff. Put Ki = Ai∗Ai and Q = ∑i AiAi∗.

∑i ‖Ki − I‖HS² ≤ er,   D tr(Q²) ≤ (1 + η)(tr Q)²,   0 ≤ e ≤ 1/4,   η ≥ 0.

Rank bound for the selected subspaces. Let S = ∑i Ai(Ui). Its missing fraction x = codim(S)/D is at most η + 4p + 4√e. For one identity map and a retained subspace of codimension k, the errors vanish and the missing fraction is exactly p = k/D. This example exhibits the linear dependence on the discarded dimension.

Why the discarded dimensions bound the missing rows

Let P project orthogonally onto S⊥ and let Pi project onto Ui⊥. Write t = tr Q. Applying Hilbert–Schmidt Cauchy–Schwarz to ∑i tr(Ki − I) gives |t − r| ≤ √r √(er). Indeed, the squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm of each identity is its source dimension. Thus t ≥ r(1 − √e) ≥ r/2.

Only discarded directions can contribute to the trace on the missing rows. Each PAi vanishes on Ui, and orthogonal projection decreases lengths. Hence tr(PQ) = ∑i ‖PAiPi‖HS² ≤ ∑i tr(PiKi). Splitting each Ki into I and its error gives a main term ∑i di = pr. Cauchy–Schwarz bounds the remaining term by √(pr) √(er), since ‖Pi‖HS² = di.

For a lower bound on the same trace, subtract the mean eigenvalue from Q. The second trace hypothesis gives ‖Q − (t/D)I‖HS² = tr(Q²) − t²/D ≤ ηt²/D. As tr P = ‖P‖HS² = Dx, Cauchy–Schwarz gives

t(x − √(ηx)) ≤ tr(PQ) ≤ r(p + √(pe)).

Divide by t and use r/t ≤ 2 and 2√(ηx) ≤ η + x. Rearranging gives x ≤ η + 4p + 4√(pe). Since 0 ≤ p ≤ 1, this implies the stated bound.

Now project S onto the Dar arithmetic rows. This projection loses at most D − Dar independent directions, so its image has codimension at most Dx among the arithmetic rows. At the chosen place each source basis change carries its retained subspace to the corresponding arithmetic filtration step. All words use the same target basis change. It therefore carries the sum of their projected images into the complex span of the arithmetic images. The nonzero scalar attached to each word preserves its image subspace. The slope inequality and the height allowance for each word put its retained image in the target filtration step at s − τ. The count of target slopes strictly below that threshold is the codimension of this step. Divide by Dar ≥ (1 − δ)D, where 0 ≤ δ < 1, and substitute the source bound on p. The resulting upper bound for the fraction is

ω(τ) = [η + 4bF/(bF + τ) + 4√e]/(1 − δ).

At bF = τ = 0, use zero for the source fraction because the strict source tail is empty.

Let ν run through the normalized target slopes with multiplicity. The lower-slope bound says ν ≥ s − H. Consequently the interval of τ in [0, H] for which ν < s − τ has length max(s − ν, 0). Average these lengths over the finite list. Their integral representation, followed by the bound above, gives

s − mean(ν) ≤ mean[max(s − ν, 0)] = ∫0H fraction{ν < s − τ} dτ ≤ ∫0H ω(τ) dτ.

For example, set s = 4 and take the three numbers 1, 3, 5 with H = 3. Two lie below 4 − τ for 0 ≤ τ < 1, and one lies below it for 1 ≤ τ < 3. The integral of the fraction is (2/3)·1 + (1/3)·2 = 4/3. This equals the average of the positive deficits 3, 1, 0. The signed mean deficit is only 1, since the last value exceeds 4. This finite list illustrates the integration step.

For bF > 0, integrating bF/(bF + τ) gives bF log(1 + H/bF). For bF = 0 this contribution vanishes. The two constant terms in ω integrate to (η + 4√e)H/(1 − δ). Since mean(ν) = μ(R∨), the calculation actually proves the bound without subtracting log |W|.
