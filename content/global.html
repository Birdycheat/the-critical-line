Why the slope bound contains a logarithm

Think of a lattice through its finite list of Harder–Narasimhan slopes, each repeated according to rank. A step in its filtration contains the directions whose slopes meet a given threshold. For a slope λ of the underlying real lattice the arithmetic normalization is 2λ + cK, with the same field constant for all words and the target. The resulting list has arithmetic mean equal to the lattice's mean slope. For the source lattices subtract each height allowance and concatenate the lists over all words. Their mean is s and every entry is at most s + bF. If a fraction p lies below s − τ, for τ ≥ 0, then the mean is at most p(s − τ) + (1 − p)(s + bF). Rearranging gives p ≤ bF/(bF + τ) when the denominator is positive. If both quantities vanish, all slopes equal s and the strict lower tail is empty. This bounds the rank lost when we discard low-slope steps in the source. The filtered source ranks Bounds for the mean and maximum
