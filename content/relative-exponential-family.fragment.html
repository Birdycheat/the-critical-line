Linearizing the group law near the identity

Let A → X be an abelian scheme of relative dimension g over a complex base locally of finite type. Pull it back along a holomorphic family of base points y(b), with b in an open set of ℂm. Write Ab for the complex fibre over y(b), and fix b₀. The first step towards a family of exponentials is to find coordinates in which multiplication of nearby points of Ab becomes addition. These coordinates must be holomorphic in b as well as in the point.

Smoothness gives g relative coordinates near the identity of Ab₀. Locally the remaining coordinates satisfy equations with invertible Jacobian in the variables to be eliminated. After substitution of y(b), the holomorphic implicit function theorem solves for those variables on one neighbourhood of the central point. Subtract the coordinates of the identity section on each fibre. The resulting chart takes the identity to 0, has a holomorphic inverse there, and represents multiplication by a holomorphic map Mb(u,v). Since Mb(u,0) = u and Mb(0,v) = v, its linear part in the fibre variables is u + v. Smooth relative coordinates · The inverse theorem with parameters.

The doubling map u ↦ Mb(u,u) has derivative 2I at zero. Let εb be its local inverse fixing zero. Its linear part is u/2. Halving repeatedly moves a point into a region where the linear term dominates. Multiplication by 2j then recovers its coordinate in the additive group.

ℒb(u) = limj→∞ 2j εb∘j(u),   ℒb(Mb(u,v)) = ℒb(u) + ℒb(v).

Here the superscript ∘j denotes iteration. The estimate below gives joint holomorphy and derivative I at zero. For additivity, commutativity makes doubling a homomorphism. Its inverse therefore respects products sufficiently close to zero. Put uⱼ = εb∘j(u) and likewise for vⱼ. The difference Mb(uⱼ,vⱼ) − uⱼ − vⱼ is bounded by a constant times (‖uⱼ‖ + ‖vⱼ‖)². After multiplication by 2j it tends to zero by the same geometric estimate. This proves the displayed identity on smaller balls where the products and inverse branches are defined. The inverse of ℒb now gives a jointly holomorphic local exponential with derivative I in the centred chart.

Why the halving limit is holomorphic in the parameter

On smaller parameter and fibre balls the Taylor estimate is uniform, ‖εb(u) − u/2‖ ≤ C‖u‖². Indeed the second derivatives of the holomorphic inverse are bounded on a smaller closed product of balls. Choose a fibre radius r with Cr ≤ 1/8. Then ‖uⱼ‖ ≤ (5/8)j‖u‖, so all iterates stay inside the same ball. Consecutive approximants satisfy

‖2j+1uj+1 − 2juⱼ‖ ≤ 2C‖u‖² (25/32)j.

The square in the error is decisive since 2(5/8)² = 25/32 < 1. Summing the bound gives locally uniform convergence and ℒb(u) = u + O(‖u‖²). On a smaller polydisc the limit inherits the Cauchy integral formula from the jointly holomorphic approximants, hence is itself holomorphic. Cauchy’s formula on a polydisc.

For a one-dimensional local model take M(u,v) = u + v + uv. Then 1 + M(u,v) = (1 + u)(1 + v). The halving branch satisfies 1 + ε(u) = (1 + u)1/2 near 1, so the approximants are 2j((1 + u)2−j − 1). Their limit is the usual logarithm log(1 + u).

Extending the exponential over every fibre

Write fb(z) for the point of the group obtained by applying the inverse logarithm and the inverse of the centred chart. After shrinking around b₀, choose one ball of radius r in which the inverse identities and the local addition law hold for every parameter. For arbitrary z choose a positive integer k with ‖z‖/k < r. Define

Fb(z) = fb(z/k)k.

For two eligible integers k and l, subdivide instead into kl pieces. The partial sums from z/(kl) to z/k stay in the ball, so the local addition law recovers the k-piece formula. Grouping the pieces in the other order gives the l-piece formula. Choosing a common subdivision for z, w and z + w proves the group law globally. Around each (b,z) fix a single admissible k. Its strict inequality persists on a neighbourhood, where the formula is holomorphic. Common subdivision makes these local formulas agree on overlaps.

The image contains a neighbourhood of the identity because the local logarithm is its inverse. It is therefore an open subgroup. The complex fibre is a connected abelian variety. Its open cosets partition the fibre, so the subgroup is the entire fibre. On the common ball, Fb(z) = 1 implies z = 0 by applying the logarithm. To match a prescribed exponential at the centre, precompose by its invertible derivative T in centred coordinates, as in the central normalization. The radius r/(1 + ‖T‖) works for all corrected exponentials eb(z) = Fb(Tz). Connectedness of the complex points of a smooth connected fibre.

The same inverse lifts sections through a specified point. If x(b₀) = eb₀(z₀), then x(b)eb(z₀)−1 is close to the identity for nearby b. Apply the local inverse there and add z₀. Taking x to be the identity section and z₀ in the central period lattice gives exactly the continued vectors in the kernel proof.

Restoring the order action in the continued frame

Let Pb be the continued periods on the marked integral lattice L. At the centre the order acts on tangent coordinates by the prescribed matrices ρ(a). In the preliminary exponential coordinates the action on a nearby fibre lifts to a complex-linear map Db(a). To see this, lift a(eb(z)) near zero by the preceding inverse. Its additivity defect lies in the kernel and is small near zero, hence vanishes there. Its derivative at zero is therefore the lift itself near zero, and subdivision gives the lift globally. Differentiating the joint local lift in z gives holomorphic matrix entries in b.

For fixed a and x ∈ L, the difference Pb(ax) − Db(a)Pb(x) is continuous and belongs to the kernel for every b. On a connected parameter ball its zero set is closed and also open by the common radius. Since it contains b₀, the difference vanishes throughout the ball. This identifies the action on the abstract lattice. To restore the fixed complex matrices, one needs a holomorphic change of frame hb satisfying hbDb(a) = ρ(a)hb for every a.

There is a short construction for these full period lattices. Let Jb be the unique real-linear map taking each central period to its continued value. The preceding identity gives Jbρ(a) = Db(a)Jb on a real basis. Since the order matrices on both sides are complex-linear, the complex-linear part Ub(z) = (Jb(z) − iJb(iz))/2 satisfies the same identity. Its columns are fixed complex combinations of the continued period values and hence are holomorphic. At the centre U = I. Shrink to where its determinant is nonzero and put hb = Ub−1. Then

P′b = hbPb,   e′b(z) = eb(hb−1z).

The first formula gives P′b(ax) = ρ(a)P′b(x). The inverse in the second formula preserves the actual group points, including every division point represented by Pb(x)/k. These are the points on which the marking and the polarization are tested.

The same change of frame from the algebra generated by the order

In an integral basis form the complex matrix algebra S generated by the order matrices on the lattice. The real part of the central positive Hermitian form gives a positive Gram matrix on that basis. The order involution gives the adjoint of each generator for this Gram matrix, so S is closed under adjoints. Every invariant subspace has an invariant orthogonal complement. Consequently the faithful lattice representation decomposes as a direct sum of simple modules, and the radical of S, which kills every simple module, is zero. The complex Wedderburn theorem writes S as a finite product of matrix algebras. The Wedderburn decomposition.

Let Vb have columns the continued periods in the integral basis. It is surjective over ℂ. The lattice identity shows that each generator preserves the kernel of Vb. Sums and products preserve it as well. Thus every element of S acts on the quotient ℂg, independently of the representative, through a representation πb. It varies holomorphically. Indeed, if R₀ is a right inverse of V₀ (the period matrix at b₀), then Rb = R₀(VbR₀)−1 is a holomorphic right inverse nearby, and πb(s) = VbsRb. For each block α choose matrix units Eαij, with index 1 in that block. Put

Kb = ∑α,i πb(Eαi1) π0(Eα1i),   πb(s)Kb = Kbπ0(s).

Here π0 denotes the central action. Testing the identity on a matrix unit Eαpq leaves the term πb(Eαp1)π0(Eα1q) on both sides. At the centre the sum reduces to the sum of the diagonal units, hence to I. Thus Kb−1 is another holomorphic choice for the required change of frame.

What stays fixed at division points

Let the family carry a relatively ample line bundle and marked sections η(a) killed by a positive integer N. Assume the central marked period description realizes those sections and its alternating form E gives the prescribed Weil phases for all pairs of lattice vectors at every division level. For each k > 0 and x ∈ L, the section sₓ(b) = eb(Pb(x)/k) is holomorphic and is killed by k, since raising it to the k-th power gives eb(Pb(x)) = 1. The simultaneous changes of period and exponential leave this section fixed.

Over ℂ, multiplication by k on an abelian scheme has finite étale kernel. Analytically this gives disjoint local sheets. Two continuous k-torsion sections agreeing at one point of a connected parameter ball agree everywhere. Their equality locus is open by local uniqueness on a sheet and closed by separation. With k = N this proves that the section represented by Pb(t(a))/N is the prescribed η(a) throughout the ball. Finite étale torsion.

The commutator pairing attached to the line bundle is compatible with pullback. On the fibre product of the torsion cover with itself there are two universal torsion points. Their pairing is a regular unit. Pulling it back along the pair of holomorphic sections gives a holomorphic unit on the parameter ball. The theta-group commutator is multiplicative in either torsion argument, so its k-th power is the value at the identity, namely one. The roots of unity form a finite discrete set, so its value equals its central value on the entire connected ball. The same ball works for every k because the argument starts with globally defined sections on that ball and treats each level there. The commutator convention and the Weil phase.

On a nearby fibre the ample line bundle supplies a positive Hermitian form with integral alternating form Eb. The Weil formula at level k now gives the same phase for Eb(x,y) and E(x,y). As in the fibre comparison, every positive integer divides their difference, so the two integer values agree. This proves that the corrected periods and exponential realize the original polarization, order action and marking throughout the chosen neighbourhood. The dimension comparison can therefore use an actual local period family on the fixed marked lattice.
