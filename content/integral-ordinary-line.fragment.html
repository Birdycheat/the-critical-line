Why do the even and odd correspondences give the same residue points?

The local congruence counts each residue point with its multiplicity. An equality of total degrees would allow an excess at one point to cancel a deficit at another. To prove the identity point by point, we follow the reduction of a single subgroup through the map that divides out the ordinary line.

Ordinary subspace lemma. Let H have dimension n over a field with Q elements, and let C be a line in H. Write π for the quotient map to H/C. Let F be a self-map of a set of points. Suppose that each subspace U of the quotient determines a point A(U). For each subspace D of H of dimension j, its residue point is A(πD) if D misses the line, and F(A(πD)) if D contains it. Apply F another n − j times to this residue point and give it weight Qj(j−1)/2. Then the multiset obtained from even j equals the one obtained from odd j.

Proof. Fix a subspace U of dimension r downstairs. Inside π−1(U) there is exactly one subspace mapping onto U and containing C. It is the entire inverse image, of dimension r + 1. The other subspaces mapping onto U meet C only in zero. Projection identifies each of them with U, so each is the image of a unique linear section. To count the sections, lift one basis of U to get a first section. Every other section is obtained from it by adding a linear map U → C. For each of the r basis vectors one can choose any of the Q elements of the line. This gives Qr subspaces of dimension r.

The second set of subspaces starts at A(U) and receives n − r further applications of F. The single subspace containing C starts one application ahead and receives one fewer thereafter. They arrive at the very same point Fn−r(A(U)). The accumulated weights agree because

Qr · Qr(r−1)/2 = Qr(r+1)/2.

The two dimensions r and r + 1 have opposite parity. This pairs their contributions for the chosen U. All subspaces occur exactly once as U varies. At r = 0 the pair consists of the zero space and the line itself. At r = n − 1 the unique containing space is H. Even if distinct U give the same final point, adding their equal multiplicities preserves equality there.

For instance, let H be three-dimensional over 𝔽₃ and take U a line in the quotient plane. There are three lines above it that miss C and one plane above it that contains C. The three lines supply one copy each of F²(A(U)). The plane supplies three copies of that same point. In the experiment a three-cycle makes the two different numbers of remaining applications of F visible.

In the integral model the set of subspaces of dimension j parametrizes the sections of the j-th source correspondence over a valuation-ring point. The kernel of reduction supplies C, and the marked quotient calculation supplies A and the rule for the residue twist. Thus the lemma proves the pointwise congruence for every enumeration of the sections. The local congruence construction explains where these inputs enter.
