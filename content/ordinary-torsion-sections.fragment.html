Which torsion points can be followed through reduction?

Fix a point of the moduli scheme over a valuation ring V, with algebraically closed fraction field K and residue field κ. Let A be the abelian scheme it classifies. As above the residue characteristic is q, and q is nonzero in V. Write 𝔽 = 𝒪F/𝔴 and Q = |𝔽|. A section belongs to A[𝔴] if multiplication by every element of 𝔴 sends it to zero. We shall compare three sets: these sections over V, their generic points over K and their reductions over κ.

Torsion and reduction lemma. For the rank-n unitary datum of algebra degree one, the sections H = A[𝔴](V) form an n-dimensional 𝔽-vector space. Restriction to the generic fibre identifies H with A[𝔴](K). Reduction maps H onto all the points of A[𝔴](κ). This last assertion does not require ordinarity.

Generic points. The field K has characteristic zero. Indeed q is nonzero there, and every other rational prime is already a unit in the base discrete valuation ring. The characteristic-zero torsion theorem for this moduli problem gives |A[𝔴](K)| = N(𝔴)n = Qn. Its exponent comes from the scalar isogeny degree. For each nonzero scalar c ∈ 𝒪F, the norm-degree theorem applied to the torsion module gives |NF/ℚ(c)|n kernel points. The tangent kernel vanishes: a nonzero integer multiple of the identity factors through c and is invertible on the tangent space.

To pass from a scalar to 𝔴, take a positive power 𝔴e = (c) that is principal, using finiteness of the ideal class group (Minkowski’s ideal bound). The torsion filtration for a divisible 𝒪F-module gives |A[𝔴e](K)| = |A[𝔴](K)|e. Divisibility here follows because each nonzero scalar divides a nonzero integer, and multiplication by that integer is surjective on an abelian variety over an algebraically closed field. The scalar count at c gives Qen. Taking the positive e-th root proves the claimed count. The following calculation gives the filtration identity.

Why a new power of 𝔴 contributes the same number of points

Put Tj = A[𝔴j](K), with T₀ = {0}. Choose a in 𝔴 outside 𝔴², so (a) = 𝔴𝔟 for an ideal 𝔟 coprime to 𝔴. Multiplication by a sends Tj+1 to Tj. Its kernel is T₁: an element of Tj+1 killed by a is killed by (a) + 𝔴j+1 = 𝔴(𝔟 + 𝔴j) = 𝔴. The reverse inclusion holds since a ∈ 𝔴.

For surjectivity take y ∈ Tj and choose a point x with ax = y by divisibility. Write 1 = v + u with v ∈ 𝔟 and u ∈ 𝔴j+1. Then vx is killed by 𝔴j+1, since 𝔟𝔴j+1 = (a)𝔴j kills x. Moreover a(vx) = vy = y, as uy = 0. Each fibre of multiplication by a has |T₁| elements, so induction gives |Te| = |T₁|e. Here the groups up to index e are finite since they are contained in the already counted kernel of (c) = 𝔴e.

Properness of A gives a unique V-section extending each K-point. If multiplication by an element of 𝔴 kills the generic point, its product with the section and the zero section have the same generic value. Uniqueness makes them equal over V. Hence restriction identifies the two torsion groups. The action of 𝒪F on either factors through 𝔽. A space of dimension d over 𝔽 has Qd elements, by the unique expansion in any basis. Thus H has dimension n. Stacks Project, valuative criterion for properness.

Lifting a prescribed special point. The torsion subgroup scheme A[𝔴] is finite flat over V by the finite flat torsion theorem for the marked abelian scheme. Write it as Spec B, and let χ ∶ B → κ be the prescribed point. Flat going down gives a prime 𝔮 ⊆ ker χ over the zero prime of V. The domain B/𝔮 is finite over V, so its fraction field is a finite extension of K. Since K is algebraically closed, this field is K itself. A valuation ring is integrally closed in its fraction field, hence B/𝔮 = V under this identification. The quotient map B → V gives a section. Its reduction equals χ since χ factors through this same quotient and restricts to the residue map on V. Different sections may thus have the same prescribed reduction. Stacks Project, Lemma 10.39.19 and Lemma 10.50.3.
