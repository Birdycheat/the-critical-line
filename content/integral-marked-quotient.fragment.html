Which marked points form the kernel of a lattice translation?

Let Λ be the integral lattice and Λ̂ its completion at the finite primes away from p. Fix an admissible generic translation h and its isogeny ψ₀. The integral source and target have integer levels Nκ and N, with N ≥ 3 and κ > 0. The levels are prime to the residue prime q and to p. Assume κΛ̂ ⊆ hΛ̂ ⊆ Λ̂. We write η₁ and η₂ for the generic markings at the larger levels M₁ = Nκc₁ and M₂ = Nc₂, respectively. Thus the marking at level Nκ on the source is x ↦ η₁(c₁x).

Kernel lemma. There is an additive map F ∶ Λ → Λ whose reduction modulo Nκ represents κh−1. On each geometric fibre the kernel of ψ₀ consists exactly of the values η₁(c₁NFx) with x ∈ Λ. Distinct sections in this subgroup remain distinct on every geometric fibre.

Proof. The first inclusion makes κh−1 integral on Λ̂. Reducing its values on a ℤ-basis modulo Nκ and lifting each column gives F. This is possible because Λ/(Nκ)Λ → Λ̂/(Nκ)Λ̂ is an isomorphism. In each coordinate this is the Chinese remainder theorem, since Nκ is prime to the omitted prime p. Applying h to the first congruence and using hΛ̂ ⊆ Λ̂ gives the second, for every x ∈ Λ. The Stacks Project, Chinese remainder theorem.

κh−1x ≡ Fx,   hFx ≡ κx   (mod NκΛ̂).

The generic marking identifies the action of ψ₀ on torsion with h on the corresponding lattice residues. At level Nκ, the second congruence sends Fx to κx. Multiplying a point of level Nκ by κ evaluates its label at level N. Compatibility up the two towers of markings therefore gives ψ₀η₁(c₁Fx) = η₂(c₂x). Replacing x by Nx proves that every displayed kernel section is killed.

For the converse, the lattice inclusion gives a companion χ to ψ₀ satisfying χψ₀ = [κ] and ψ₀χ = [κ]. Consequently any point z killed by ψ₀ is killed by κ. On a geometric fibre choose a label a ∈ Λ for z at level κ. The marking criterion for ψ₀ says that ha = κv for some v ∈ Λ̂. Choose x ∈ Λ with v ≡ x modulo NκΛ̂. Since κh−1 preserves Λ̂, we obtain a = κh−1v ≡ Fx modulo NκΛ̂. In particular a and Fx have the same residue in Λ/κΛ. Written at level M₁ this point is η₁(c₁NFx). Finally, if η₁(c₁NFx) vanishes at one geometric point, the marking gives c₁NFx ∈ M₁Λ. The section therefore vanishes everywhere. Applying this to the difference of two sections proves the last assertion.

Why can the companion be chosen with multiplier κ?

The generic translation comes with a reverse isogeny δ for some positive integer d prime to p, with δψ₀ = [d] and ψ₀δ = [d]. Thus its kernel lies in the finite étale d-torsion over the characteristic-zero generic base. A geometric kernel point of label a at level d satisfies ha ∈ dΛ̂. Applying κh−1 gives κa ∈ dΛ, so [κ] kills this point. To pass from points to the kernel scheme, observe that ψ₀ is finite étale. It is surjective by ψ₀δ = [d], proper with finite fibres by δψ₀ = [d], and its differential is invertible by the two composite identities. The two maps from the kernel, [κ] and zero, take values in the finite étale d-torsion. Their equalizer is open and closed and contains every geometric point, proving equality on the kernel scheme. Then [κ] on the source is invariant under translation by the kernel and descends along ψ₀ to χ. The first identity is the factorization just constructed. The second follows by composing with the surjection ψ₀ and cancelling it. The Stacks Project, proper maps with finite fibres and faithfully flat descent of morphisms.

How does quotienting by these sections extend the marked isogeny?

Integral translation theorem. Keep the two lattice inclusions and the levels of the kernel lemma. Work on the locally noetherian source model over the local integer ring of the marked unitary moduli problem. Its ideal level 𝔟₁ is contained in the target ideal level 𝔟₂, and qe belongs to 𝔟₁ for some e ≥ 0. Assume the two models represent their marked moduli problems, with the matching generic PEL data. Then the quotient of the universal abelian scheme A by the sections η(NFx) gives the integral target object. Its classifying map and its map of abelian schemes restrict to the prescribed generic translation. Here η denotes the integral marking at level Nκ.

The quotient. The sections H = {η(NFx) ∣ x ∈ Λ} form a finite group since their labels factor through Λ/κΛ. The geometric marking at level Nκ shows that a section specializing to zero is zero, by the same divisibility argument as above. Hence the constant group scheme with group H acts freely by translations. The polarization supplies a relatively ample line bundle on A by restriction to the diagonal. Locally on the base this puts each finite orbit in an affine open. The finite-group quotient theorem now gives a scheme A′ and a finite étale surjection ψ ∶ A → A′ with these orbits as fibres. Addition descends because changing either summand by an element of H changes the sum by an element of H. The identity and inverse descend as well. Smoothness descends along the étale cover, properness descends from the proper source through the finite surjection, and the geometric fibres remain connected. Thus A′ is an abelian scheme. The Stacks Project, Proposition 66.14.1 and Lemma 66.14.2 on free finite actions.

The marking at integer level. Each element b of the coefficient order commutes with F modulo Nκ, since it commutes with the adelic action. Thus H is stable under b and the order action descends. The target marking is

η′(x) = ψ(η(Fx)).

It is additive. The error in F(bx) − bFx is an Nκ-multiple, which η kills. This gives coefficient linearity. Also η′(Nx) = 0 because η(NFx) belongs to H. These identities explain why the marking has the target level. Its full geometric Tate compatibility and its polarization require the pairing on Λ as well.

Which pairing and signature conditions enter the quotient?

Let c′ be the positive prime-to-p integer multiplying the generic polarization, so the similitude factor of h is c′ times an adelic integral unit away from p. The normalized action κh−1 has pairing multiplier κ² divided by this factor. The integral similitude calculation gives a positive integer c with cc′ = κ². At every modulus b prime to p, take additive lifts Fb of this action and a unit ub modulo b. They respect the order modulo b, are compatible under reduction, scale the lattice pairing by cub modulo b and have image containing κΛ modulo b. For moduli also divisible by p, one uses κ times the identity and unit c′ on the p-part, and combines the two residues by the Chinese remainder theorem. This extends the system to the moduli prime to q used on the integral base. Its lift at Nκ has the same residue as F. The Stacks Project, Chinese remainder theorem.

Write P for the source polarizing bundle on A × A over the model base, trivialized along the two coordinate axes. The PEL pairing theorem places the sections in the translation stabilizers of P⊗c′ and gives a trivial commutator pairing on them. The corresponding linearization supplies the descent datum on the square of ψ. Faithfully flat descent then produces P′ with (ψ × ψ)∗P′ ≅ P⊗c′. The polarization descent results give the two axis trivializations, symmetry, ampleness on the diagonal, the Rosati identity and a polarizing kernel killed by an integer prime to q. As ψ is étale, its differential identifies the Lie modules with their order actions, preserving both signature conditions. The geometric marking theorem for the compatible system supplies the Tate similitude on each geometric fibre. These pairing and marking results use the unitary moduli datum. The Stacks Project, faithfully flat descent for quasi-coherent sheaves and the infinitesimal lifting criterion for étale morphisms.

The ideal level. In addition to η, the model records an étale marking λ of the 𝔟₁-torsion on geometric points. The quotient transports this marking since the kernel is killed by κ and the marked points are killed by qe. Indeed [κ] on A factors through ψ, giving a reverse map with both composites [κ]. Multiply this reverse map by an inverse of κ modulo qe to invert ψ on the indicated torsion. If e = 0 this torsion is trivial. The level 𝔟₂ selects the subgroup killed by the larger ideal. To label it, use the lattice map ρ induced on the quotients by their marking kernels. The target marking is λ′(y) = ψ(λ(ρy)).

How is the change of ideal labels checked?

For the generic levels, choose ideal representatives ai with (ai, Mi)𝔟i = (Mi). Their ideal arithmetic gives M₁ = d m₁, M₂ = d m₂, d ∣ qe and a₂ = m₂a, with (a, d)𝔟₂ = (d). Since d is prime to κ, h induces an isomorphism modulo d by the two lattice inclusions. Lift its inverse to an additive map g on Λ. The label quotient at level 𝔟i is Λ/𝔟iΛ. The lattice generation theorem for the ideal markings gives a choice of representatives ρ with

a₁ρ(y) ≡ m₁g(ay)   (mod M₁Λ).

Indeed the right-hand side is killed modulo M₁ by every coefficient of 𝔟₁, since 𝔟₁ ⊆ 𝔟₂ and a𝔟₂ ⊆ (d). The generation theorem expresses every such class as a multiple of a₁. The same theorem identifies the label kernels. Thus ρ is well defined and order-linear on the label quotients, and its image is the part killed by 𝔟₂. Applying the source and target markings at common level d gives ψ₀η₁(a₁ρ(y)) = η₂(a₂y). This is the compatibility needed in addition to the integer-level marking formula.

Identification on the generic fibre. The target model represents the marked moduli problem, so the integral quotient with both its markings gives a classifying map and a map of universal abelian schemes over it. Pull back to the generic marked source. Its prescribed isogeny and the pulled-back quotient have the same subgroup of translations by the kernel lemma. Each factors through the other quotient and the factorizations are inverse, as one checks after composing with the quotient surjections. This isomorphism preserves the order action and integer marking by their descent formulas. On the square the two polarizing bundles have the same pullback. The injectivity theorem for bundles trivialized on both axes under the square of this quotient gives their isomorphism downstairs. The formula for λ′ identifies the ideal markings. Uniqueness in the marked moduli problem therefore identifies the two classifying maps and the two maps of universal families. This is the integral translation used for the good-reduction correspondence.
