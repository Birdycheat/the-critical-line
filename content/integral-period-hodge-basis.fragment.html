How do filtration jumps become exponents in a period matrix?

Work on one local layer. Its rational representation V has dimension d over ℚℓ. Let K be the local base field and GK its absolute Galois group. The de Rham period field BdR contains a discrete valuation ring A = B+dR. Fix a generator u of its maximal ideal, so filtration degree h in the period field means membership in uhA, even for negative h. Here u measures the filtration. The powers of ℓ used to compare layers measure a different precision.

Because V is de Rham, its invariant period space D = (BdR ⊗ℚℓ V)GK has dimension d over K. Choose coordinates on V and give D the induced decreasing filtration. Its degree-h subspace consists of the invariant vectors whose coordinates all belong to uhA. On this layer the filtration is all of D at −H and is zero at H + 1.

A finite-dimensional exhaustive, separated filtration has only finitely many jumps, since each strict decrease lowers the dimension. Construct a basis by descending through those degrees. At degree h, keep the vectors already chosen in degree h + 1. Extend them by lifts of a basis of the quotient FilhD/Filh+1D. After reaching −H we have a basis x₁,…,xd. Record the degree at which xj was added as hj. For each integer k, the vectors with hj ≥ k form a basis of FilkD. This gives the full multiplicity of each degree and shows |hj| ≤ H.

Write Q for the matrix with these period vectors as its columns. The coordinates of xj belong to uhjA. Thus the matrix W whose jth column is u−hjxj has entries in A. We claim that its inverse also has entries in A. Invertibility over the period field alone would allow the inverse to have poles.

The de Rham comparison isomorphism respects the filtrations in both directions. For any standard coordinate vector ei, apply its inverse in degree zero. This expresses ei as a sum of terms b z in which z is an invariant vector of some degree k and b belongs to u−kA. Expand z in the adapted basis. A term at xj can occur only if hj ≥ k. Then u−kA ⊆ u−hjA, so the total coefficient of xj lies in the latter module. Here scalars in K preserve each filtration subspace. Consequently ei is an A-linear combination of the rescaled columns of W. For example, the two integral columns (1,0) and (1,u) each have degree zero, yet their difference only gives (0,u). The inverse comparison above ensures that even (0,1) is in the span of the normalized columns. Applying the argument to each coordinate vector gives a right inverse to W over A. Its determinant is a unit, so the square matrix is invertible over A. O. Brinon and B. Conrad, CMI Summer School Notes on p-adic Hodge Theory, Proposition 6.3.7.

Q = W diag(uh₁,…,uhd),   W ∈ GLd(A),   ρ(g) g(Q) = Q for g ∈ GK.

The last identity expresses invariance of the original columns. Here g acts on the period entries and ρ(g) acts on the representation coordinates. For the integral construction one may use as u the image in A of a generator of the kernel of Fontaine’s period map θ from Ainf to the ring of integers in the completed algebraic closure of K. This image generates the maximal ideal of A. Replacing a generator by a unit multiple only rescales each column of W by a unit. Brinon and Conrad, Propositions 4.4.3 and 4.4.6.

To write the factorization using nonnegative integer powers, choose q = ∑j|hj| and pj = q + hj. The common shift can depend on the dimension of the layer. The bound needed at the next step is on the differences, for which the same H works throughout the tower.

uqQ = W diag(up₁,…,upd),   |pj − q| = |hj| ≤ H.

For a calculation with formal Laurent series, take the columns (u−1,0) and (u²,u²). Their degrees are −1 and 2. Rescaling gives the columns (1,0) and (1,1). The second coordinate vector equals the second rescaled column minus the first. In the original basis its coefficients are −u and u−2, precisely the two allowed filtration orders. Here q = 3 and (p₁,p₂) = (2,5), so the differences are still −1 and 2. The next period-theoretic input constructs integral Breuil–Kisin frames from semistable periods and controls their height with this bound. The limit argument also requires comparison across different layers.
