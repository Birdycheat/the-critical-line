Why a near term has exactly the traces of the identity

The near part of the trace count tests stretching at each archimedean place. A rotation can have zero stretching and still move every nonzero vector. To detect that motion we use the finite level of the same rational isometry. Let G be its matrix over the CM field E, of size n ≥ 1, and put f = [F ∶ ℚ] for the totally real field F below E. Take a positive integer level g0 > 4n for which (g0) in 𝒪E is radical, meaning that no prime ideal occurs twice in its factorization.

At a point x of the adelic quotient, take a rational term with nonzero test coefficient. The finite support condition says that the conjugated finite matrix preserves the reference lattice and its difference from the identity is nilpotent modulo g0 with exponent at most n. Let Λx consist of the rational vectors that enter that finite lattice after applying xf−1. The finite-lattice construction makes it a finitely generated 𝒪E-module spanning En. Moving the conjugation from the matrix to the vector gives

GΛx ⊂ Λx,   (G − I)nΛx ⊂ g0Λx.

For example, to check the second inclusion on a rational vector v ∈ Λx, apply xf−1 to (G − I)nv/g0. The finite support condition puts the result in the reference lattice, which is exactly the membership test for Λx.

Localize this lattice at a prime ideal 𝔭. It is free of rank n there, since it is finite and torsion free over the ring of integers. The matrix of G in a local basis is integral. Its power traces are therefore integral at every prime. For 𝔭 dividing (g0), the reduction of G is I + N with Nn = 0. In a basis adapted to the kernels of the powers of N, it is triangular with diagonal entries 1. Every power still has trace n in the residue field. Since (g0) is the product of its distinct prime divisors, these congruences give, for every j ≥ 1,

tr(Gj) − n = g0 aj,   aj ∈ 𝒪E.

The Stacks Project, lemma 15.22.11, local freeness over a Dedekind domain.

Now impose the archimedean test. Choose 0 < c ≤ 10−5 and set ε = c/(n8f2). At each real place the Euclidean length of the vector of logarithms of the singular values of the conjugated matrix A is at most ε. Every singular value is then at most exp(ε), so ‖Aj‖op ≤ exp(jε). Each diagonal entry has absolute value at most this operator norm. For 1 ≤ j ≤ n, we have jε ≤ 1/4 and hence |tr(Aj) − n| ≤ n(exp(1/4) + 1) < 3n. Trace is invariant under conjugation. The CM embeddings above each real place form a conjugate pair, so this bound holds for every conjugate of tr(Gj) − n.

It follows that every conjugate of aj has absolute value at most 3n/g0 < 1. A nonzero algebraic integer has a nonzero integer field norm, the product of all its conjugates. Such a product cannot have absolute value less than 1. Thus tr(Gj) = n for 1 ≤ j ≤ n.

From the power traces to a distance from the identity

Write det(I − zG) = ∑k=0n bkzk, with b0 = 1. Over a splitting field, differentiate the product of the factors 1 − λz, one for each eigenvalue λ with multiplicity. Its logarithmic derivative is −∑j≥1 tr(Gj)zj−1. Multiplication by the original polynomial and comparison of coefficients give Newton’s recursion

k bk = −∑j=1k bk−j tr(Gj),   1 ≤ k ≤ n.

The first n power traces coincide with those of I. Since the field has characteristic zero, division by k gives the same coefficients successively. The characteristic polynomial of G is therefore (T − 1)n. Cayley–Hamilton states that a matrix satisfies its characteristic polynomial, giving (G − I)n = 0. This identity passes to the finite and infinite components and survives conjugation. The Stacks Project, lemma 10.16.1, Cayley–Hamilton.

Unipotent matrices can still have large entries. To get the distance actually needed in the count, use both the first trace and the stretching bound. In the polar decomposition write A = U exp(X) with U unitary and X Hermitian. The eigenvalues of X are the logarithms of the singular values, so its Frobenius norm is at most ε. Write B = exp(X) − I. The exponential series gives ‖B‖F ≤ exp(ε) − 1 ≤ 2ε. Since tr(A) = n, the identity A = U + UB gives n − Re tr(U) ≤ |tr(UB)| ≤ √n ‖B‖F by Cauchy–Schwarz on the diagonal. For unitary U, expanding ‖U − I‖F2 gives exactly 2(n − Re tr(U)). As √n ≤ n, this yields ‖U − I‖F ≤ 2√(nε). Adding ‖UB‖F ≤ 2ε ≤ √(nε) proves

‖A − I‖F ≤ δ = 3√(nε).

This radius satisfies δ < 1/4. For a signature index q with q2 ≤ n, substitution gives δqnf = 3√c · q/n5/2 ≤ 3√c/n2 < 1/100. The one-type count uses c = 10−6. Every contributing rational matrix now belongs to the set defined by the finite lattice condition and the archimedean δ-bound.

A Gaussian transvection. In dimension two over ℚ(i), give the basis e, f zero norms and pairing ⟨e,f⟩ = 1. The matrix G fixing e and sending f to f + ie preserves this form and the Gaussian lattice. Here (G − I)2 = 0, and the level 13 > 8 splits into the distinct Gaussian primes 3 + 2i and 3 − 2i. Its first two traces are both 2. Newton’s recursion gives b1 = −2, then 2b2 = −((−2)·2 + 2) = 2.

Take the finite coordinate of x to be the identity and its real coordinate to scale e,f by R,R−1 with R = 20,000. The upper entry of x−1Gx is now ia, with a = R−2 = 2.5 × 10−9, so its Frobenius distance to I is a. Its squared singular values have product 1 and sum 2 + a2, the determinant and trace of A*A. Thus its logarithmic singular values are ±arsinh(a/2). Their Euclidean length is less than a/√2 < 1.77 × 10−9. For c = 10−6 the permitted ε is 3.90625 × 10−9 and δ ≈ 2.65 × 10−4. This verifies the lattice and metric bounds on a nonidentity matrix. On the same space −I also has zero stretching, but its first trace differs from 2 by −4, failing the congruence modulo 13.
