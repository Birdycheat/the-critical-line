Write C = ℤ/q additively, and let [a|b] denote a basis vector, the ordered pair of group elements in a degree-two chain. Its boundary is [b] − [a + b] + [a]. In any degree, drop the first entry, merge each successive pair, then drop the last entry, with alternating signs throughout. Define a chain in degree 2e by summing the words

z = ∑j∈Cᵉ [j₁ | 1 | j₂ | 1 | … | je | 1]   with coefficients in ℤ/q.

For e = 1 the first faces give q[1] = 0 and the other faces telescope around the circle. The same two cancellations work in higher degree. Dropping the first entry leaves a word repeated q times. At any interior pair, the merge of ja with 1 gives the same output as the next merge after replacing (ja, ja+1) by (ja + 1, ja+1 − 1). The signs are opposite and the substitution permutes the summation indices. At the last pair only the first shift is needed, since the final face drops the 1. Hence ∂z = 0. Why can it be generated by only 2e + 1 elements? Consider projection of a finite group G onto C with kernel K. On the module of functions W from C to A = ℤ/q, the forward difference Δf(j) = f(j + 1) − f(j) has kernel the constants. Its image consists exactly of functions with sum zero. For the converse add successive values starting at zero. The partial sums give a periodic primitive because the whole sum is zero. This proves exactness of 0 → A → W →Δ W →sum A → 0. Translations permute the entries of W transitively, and the stabilizer of one entry is K. Shapiro’s isomorphism identifies the homology of G with coefficients in W with the homology of K with coefficients in A. Split the displayed sequence at the image of Δ. Each of the two long exact homology sequences bounds the number of generators of a middle term by those needed on either side. In particular, in passing from degree n to degree n + 2 for G, one may add generator bounds for K in degrees n + 1 and n + 2. For G = C, the kernel of the identity projection is trivial, so the positive-degree homology of that kernel vanishes. Degrees zero and one for C need at most one generator each, hence so does every higher degree. For G = D, project onto the first factor. Now K is cyclic, so each two-degree step adds at most two to the bound of one in degree zero. After e steps the bound is 2e + 1. Lift the 2e + 2 classes to a free abelian group of rank at most 2e + 1 mapping onto this homology. There is a nonzero integer relation upstairs. Divide its coefficients by their common powers of ℓ while they are all divisible. Division preserves the relation in a free abelian group. This precaution matters downstairs. For example, 5 times a generator of ℤ/5 vanishes, while the generator itself survives. Project the resulting relation to homology and reduce its coefficients modulo q = ℓν.
