How can one period witness work for the whole Galois group on the limit?

Assume every layer of the tower satisfies the integral de Rham test. Fix a required order M and put h = M + 1. We want one matrix β over 𝔖, scalar modulo E, for which every group element gives zero twisted defect modulo ηM, where η = e(E). We first solve these equations at one ℓ-adic precision n with a scalar exponent independent of n. Then we choose one matrix satisfying all precisions together.

Three numbers enter the scalar. The bounds at order h give c for the scalar residue of a layer witness and k₀ for lifting a coefficient so that its evaluation is invariant to order h. The comparison of lattices gives the loss r in its right inverse. All three numbers are fixed before the choice of n. Write δj(g) = φ(εj(g)) for the Frobenius transform of a layer correction and use the subscript ∗ for the target.

At precision n, choose a level m ≥ n and comparison matrices R, G with RG ≡ ℓrI modulo ℓn. Take a layer witness β′ at order h with scalar residue ℓcI. For each entry s of φ(G), the invariant-lift lemma gives γ(s) ≡ ℓk₀s modulo E with g(e(γ(s))) ≡ e(γ(s)) modulo ηh for every g. Assemble these lifts in a matrix Γ of size dm by d. The candidate in the target has size d by d:

βn = φ(R) β′ Γ,  k = c + k₀ + r.

Reduce first modulo (ℓn, E). The middle factor becomes ℓcI and the last becomes ℓk₀φ(G). The product φ(R)φ(G) is φ(RG) and Frobenius fixes ℓ, so βn ≡ ℓkI. For the Galois condition use the intertwining relation for the corrections: ε∗(g) g(e(R)) ≡ e(R) εm(g) modulo ℓn. Apply Frobenius to that relation modulo ℓn. Evaluation commutes with Frobenius, and the Witt action of g also commutes with Frobenius. In the quotient by (ℓn, ηh) this lets δ∗(g) pass the first factor as δm(g). The layer witness then removes δm(g) from the middle factor, and invariance of Γ removes the action on the last. Thus

βn − ℓkI ∈ (ℓn, E),  δ∗(g) g(e(βn)) − e(βn) ∈ (ℓn, ηh) for every g, entrywise.

The candidates at different n were chosen separately. To pass to one candidate, consider their two tests as conditions on matrices modulo (ℓn, Eh). They depend only on the residue: η generates the Galois-stable kernel of Fontaine’s map, so the ideal (ℓn, ηh) is stable under every g. At each precision the set of good residue matrices is finite and nonempty. Reduction sends good matrices to good matrices, since the exponent k has been fixed.

Here is why finite levels suffice even if an arbitrary good matrix has no lift. Set D = deg E. By division by the monic distinguished polynomial Eh, every class modulo this polynomial has a unique representative of degree less than Dh. Thus the matrix residue set before imposing the tests has |kres|nDh d² elements, where kres denotes the finite residue field of the local field. Starting with the unique class at precision zero, keep a child that has descendants at arbitrarily high precision. A child of this kind exists: otherwise the finite set of children would have a common last level, contrary to the choice of their parent. Continue one level at a time. This gives compatible good classes. The coefficients of their degree-bounded representatives give compatible Witt residues, hence limits in W(kres). The resulting polynomial matrix β over 𝔖 satisfies both tests at every n. Sharifi, Proposition 2.2.1 on division. Stacks Project, §10.86.

It remains to remove ℓn from the two ideals. For the scalar condition the quotient 𝔖/(E) is the local ring of integers and is ℓ-adically separated. Therefore β ≡ ℓkI modulo E. For the defect we use ℓ-adic closedness of ηhA. To see it, the first Witt coordinate of η is the nonzero image of uD in the tilt field. Hence η is a unit in the large Witt ring. If an integral element x agrees with ηhan modulo ℓn for every n, divide there by ηh. The first n Witt coordinates of the quotient are integral for all n, so x/ηh belongs to A. Apply this to each entry of the defect for each g. The same β works for all g by the definition of a good class. Its defect is divisible by ηh, hence by ηM.

We have obtained a height-H integral de Rham frame on the limiting lattice. If the layers also satisfy the crystalline congruence, that very frame satisfies both conditions. The integer k may change with M. The construction of the frame and the loss r in its lattice comparisons are common to every order.
