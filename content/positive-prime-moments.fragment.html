How does a real zero control the mass of prime powers?

Fix a nonprincipal quadratic character χ₁ modulo m ≥ 3. At the real point σ > 1, the sum −L′(σ,χ₀)/L(σ,χ₀) − L′(σ,χ₁)/L(σ,χ₁), where χ₀ is the principal character, has coefficient Λ(n)(χ₀(n) + χ₁(n)). It is nonnegative. We want to keep track of its size on entire intervals. For this purpose introduce the divisor sum r(n) = ∑b∣n χ₁(b), the coefficient of ζ(s)L(s,χ₁). The Dirichlet convolution formula gives this identity and the multiplicativity of r. See DLMF (27.5.1).

For n = ph, r(n) = 1 + χ₁(p) + ⋯ + χ₁(p)h. It equals h + 1 when χ₁(p) = 1. When χ₁(p) = −1 it alternates between zero for odd h and one for even h. At a prime dividing m it equals one, while both characters evaluated at ph vanish for h ≥ 1. These three cases prove 0 ≤ Λ(n)(χ₀(n) + χ₁(n)) ≤ 2(log n)r(n). For other positive n, Λ(n) = 0 and r(n) ≥ 0 by multiplicativity.

∑y<n≤x r(n)/n ≤ |L(1,χ₁)| log(x/y) + 400m(1 + log y)/√y   (1 ≤ y ≤ x).

The divisor sum over an interval. Write F(N) = ∑n≤N r(n)/n. Each pair ab ≤ N contributes χ₁(b)/(ab). Choose U = ⌊√N⌋. For b ≤ U the inner sum over a is the harmonic number of index ⌊N/b⌋, within 2b/N of log N + γ − log b. For b > U, sum first over b at each a. Every full period of χ₁ sums to zero. Partial summation therefore bounds the tail of ∑χ₁(b)/b beyond U by 4(m + 1)/U. This controls the inner sums and also allows the truncated sum in the main term to be replaced by L(1,χ₁). These estimates give

F(N) = (log N + γ)L(1,χ₁) − T(U) + R(N),
T(U) = ∑b≤U χ₁(b)(log b)/b,   |R(N)| ≤ 26(m + 1)(1 + log N)/√N.

Subtract this identity at N₀ = ⌊y⌋ and N₁ = ⌊x⌋. The constant γ cancels. For V ≥ U ≥ 1, another partial summation against the bounded character sums gives |T(V) − T(U)| ≤ 4(m + 1)(1 + log(U + 1))/(U + 1). Thus both endpoint errors and this difference cost at most 86(m + 1)(1 + log N₀)/√N₀. Replacing the integer endpoints by x and y uses log(y/N₀) ≤ 2/y, √y ≤ 2√N₀ and |L(1,χ₁)| ≤ 5(m + 1). This yields the displayed interval bound, even at y = 1. A related hyperbola estimate for the same divisor sum appears in Koukoulopoulos, The Distribution of Prime Numbers, Exercise 12.3(b).

Which prime powers survive for the quadratic character modulo 3?

The character has χ₁(2) = −1 and χ₁(7) = 1. At 2 and 4 the divisor sums are 0 and 1, and the prime coefficients are 0 and 2 log 2. At 7 the divisor sum is 2 and the prime coefficient is 2 log 7. At 3 the divisor sum is 1 but the prime coefficient is zero. Thus the divisor sum can count more mass while still detecting the cancellation at 2.

Why do three different terms appear in the prime moment?

Suppose χ₁ has a real zero 1 − 1/ℓ ≤ β < 1, with ℓ = log(3m). Put λ = 1 − β, a = σ − 1, and assume 0 < a ≤ 1. For an integer k ≥ 0, differentiating the absolutely convergent series k times multiplies each term by (−log n)k. After changing sign by (−1)k and dividing by k!, we obtain the positive moment

Pk = (1/k!) ∑n≥1 (log n)k Λ(n)(χ₀(n) + χ₁(n))/n1+a.

The first term comes from small integers. Set v₀ = ⌈8 log m⌉ ≤ 9ℓ. Up to exp(v₀) the character coefficient is at most two. The elementary Mertens bound ∑n≤z Λ(n)/n ≤ CT log z gives a contribution at most 2CTv₀k+1/k!. To verify the Mertens bound, put ψ(z) = ∑n≤z Λ(n). Each prime power in (N,2N] contributes log p to the logarithm of the central binomial coefficient, since the corresponding floor difference in the factorials is one and all other floor differences are nonnegative. Hence ψ(2N) − ψ(N) ≤ log((2N)!/(N!)²) ≤ 2N log 2. Summing over powers of two and rounding z up to the next power gives ψ(z) ≤ 4(log 2)z. Partial summation bounds ψ(z)/z + ∫1z ψ(t)dt/t² by 4(log 2)(1 + log z), at most CT log z when z ≥ 2.

For the rest, group the integers by v < log n ≤ v + 1, v an integer ≥ v₀. The bound on the prime coefficient spends one extra power of log n. Together with n−a ≤ exp(−av), this leaves a weight Wv = (v + 1)k+1exp(−av). Set A = |L(1,χ₁)|. The interval estimate bounds the block’s contribution before division by k! by 2Wv[A + 400m(v + 1)exp(−v/2)].

For the term with A, sum the weights. If p = k + 1, split a as pa/(p + 1) + a/(p + 1). Bound the polynomial against the first exponential using tp ≤ p!exp(t). Summing the remaining geometric series gives ∑v≥v₀ Wv ≤ 2e²(p + 1)p!/ap+1. Here the real zero enters.

Throughout [β,1] the bound |L′(t,χ₁)| ≤ CDℓ² follows from Cauchy’s estimate on a circle of radius 1/(2ℓ), where |L| ≤ 38e3/2ℓ. To check this circle bound, a point w on any of the circles satisfies Re w ≥ 1 − 3/(2ℓ), Re w ≥ ¼ and |w| ≤ 2, since ℓ ≥ log 9 > 2. Through n = m, the Dirichlet series is bounded by e3/2∑n≤m 1/n ≤ e3/2(1 + ℓ). For the remainder, the character sums are bounded by m + 1. Summation by parts at m bounds the endpoint and tail by (1 + |w|/Re w)(m + 1)1−Re w ≤ 9e3/2. Adding gives the stated circle bound, and Cauchy’s estimate divides it by the radius. Integrating from the zero to 1 gives A ≤ CDλℓ². The circle estimate uses Cauchy’s integral formula for derivatives, DLMF (1.9.31).

For the error in the block mass, take the supremum of Wv, at most e · p!/ap by the same exponential-series inequality. The remaining sum of (v + 1)exp(−v/2) is at most 20exp(−v₀/4), by comparing it with the geometric series 4exp(−v/4). The choice v₀ ≥ 8 log m now gives m exp(−v₀/4) ≤ 1/m. Dividing the three bounds by k! where needed, and allowing ℓ² ≥ 1 in the last term, gives an absolute C with

Pk ≤ Cλℓ²(k + 2)²/ak+2 + (Cℓ)k+1/k! + C(k + 2)ℓ²/(mak+1).

The three terms record respectively the value at 1, the small integers, and the error in the large blocks. In the zero moment comparison, substitute k = j − 1 and a = Δ. Multiplication by Δj leaves just one inverse power of Δ in the first term. This is where the extra height logarithm enters.
