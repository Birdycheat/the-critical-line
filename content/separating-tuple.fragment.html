The base-coordinate contradiction

Let Pq be the base quotient map and iℂ its injective realization on the quotient coordinates. The coefficient identity says that a root of unity times a nonzero product of local constants times ψb(iℂ(emb(Pqy))) equals κ times a matrix coefficient for every input y and every reading b. If κ = 0, separation gives iℂ(emb(Pqy)) = 0 for all y. Positive rank supplies a coordinate vector e₀, and surjectivity supplies y with Pqy = e₀. Injectivity would give e₀ = 0, whose zeroth coordinate reads 1 = 0. Thus κ ≠ 0, and the common matrix can be normalized with base phase one. The quantitative estimate for discarded types, the theta adjoint input, and the bottom period, ascent and top weight-gap theorems enter as suppliers in this argument.
