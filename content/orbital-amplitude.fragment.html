Where does the half-power of the last minor come from?

Keep one nonidentity term t that survives the character test in the diagonal subseries. Its two flags determine a positive rank j and an arithmetic list of minors δ. Consider the last minor of that list at each of the f infinite places. In the coordinates adapted to the flags, let Dτ be its positive normalized absolute size. The comparison of Gram minors identifies the local factor in the normalized ideal norm Δ of the last minor with Dτ². Consequently the product P of the Dτ is positive and Δ = P². The local amplitude bound contains one inverse power of the absolute size for each of the k − 1 − j occurrences. Multiplication over the places gives

∏τ Dτ−(k−1−j) = P−(k−1−j) = Δ−(k−1−j)/2.

For an arithmetic check of the exponent, assign the two local sizes 2 and 3 and take k − 1 − j = 3. Their inverse cubes multiply to 1/216. The norm is 36, and 36−3/2 gives the same 1/216. These numbers check the product identity. A realized flag pair also has to satisfy the level, normal-form and size hypotheses. For all nonzero minors up to and including the last, divide the absolute sizes at each place by the common scale (2H₁)2r. Take their maximum and 1, whichever is larger. The product of these maxima gives the label size S. Taking an inverse power reverses the local comparison and gives the upper-bound factor S−Cₛk. How large can one orbital term be before summing the row?

The representative of a term can be changed by its stabilizer. Bounding the orbital integral therefore has two costs. One measures the volume of a fundamental set for that stabilizer. The other measures the size of the integrand at the infinite places. Write a for the norm of the term vector, w for its weight, V for the volume of the word’s unipotent fundamental set (the same for every term in this word), U for the volume of the term’s stabilizer fundamental set with its adapted measures, and J for the product of the local amplitude norms. Unfolding gives aV ≤ wUJ. The volume estimate uses L = (8v)2(r+1)²f. Since 1 ≤ VL, multiplication by L gives a ≤ wUJL. In each adapted normal form N the volume estimate gives U ≤ ωN vk(r−j)fL, where ωN is its index weight. This accounts for both copies of L in the scalar estimate. Here r is the flag dimension and v is the arithmetic volume parameter.
