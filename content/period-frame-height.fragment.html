How do the filtration jumps control both Frobenius matrices?

The adapted basis gives an integer hj for each column of the de Rham period matrix. Suppose |hj| ≤ H on a local representation of rank d. We will use the same H as a height for its integral Frobenius matrices. The initial period construction supplies the starting data. Begin with a de Rham frame of some height N for this representation over the finite extension K/ℚℓ.

Write 𝔖 = W(k)[[u]], where k is the finite residue field, and let E be the chosen Eisenstein polynomial. Evaluation in Ainf sends E to a generator η of ker θ, where θ is Fontaine’s map to the ring of integers 𝒪ℂℓ. Write ξ for the image of η in the discrete valuation ring R+ = B+dR. In the preceding de Rham factorization the generator of the filtration was called u. Here we call it ξ. The variable u in 𝔖 maps to the chosen tilt parameter, and it is E(u) that maps to ξ in R+. A bar over an integral matrix will mean its image in R+. The two Frobenius matrices A and A′ have entries in 𝔖 and satisfy AA′ = E2NI. We seek a power of E that divides both matrices entry by entry.

Factorization over the period ring. Suppose the integral frame and the adapted basis describe the same action of GK in the same representation coordinates. In the adapted basis write Q = W diag(ξhⱼ), with W invertible over R+. In the notation with a common denominator ξq, its column exponents are hj = pj − q. There are invertible matrices P and V over R+ for which, in its fraction field BdR,

Ā = V diag(ξN + hⱼ) P−1,
Ā′ = P diag(ξN − hⱼ) V−1.

If H ≤ N, every exponent in both rows is at least N − H. Multiplication on either side by a matrix over R+ preserves entrywise divisibility by ξN − H. It is essential here that P−1 and V−1 also have entries in R+. The two-column example exhibits the opposite shifts in the two rows.

Why are the changes of basis invertible before passing to the fraction field?

There are two period inputs at one level. Choose the primitive period τ ∈ Ainf of the integral datum. It satisfies φ(τ) = ητ, and its de Rham image is a unit. More explicitly, φ(τ) is a unit times [εcyc] − 1 for a compatible primitive system of ℓ-power roots of unity, where brackets denote the Teichmüller lift. Applying inverse Witt Frobenius shows that the residue of τ in ℂℓ is a nonzero unit factor times ζℓ − 1, for a primitive ℓth root ζℓ. Hence its image is invertible in R+.

The bounded-poles theorem for an integral frame supplies matrices Y, Y′ over Ainf. In the Witt ring of the tilt field, Y represents τN times the frame basis T, and YY′ = τ2NI. This makes Ȳ invertible in R+. The invariant-basis comparison for a de Rham frame supplies an invertible matrix B over R+ and an invariant period basis Qf over BdR satisfying (ξτ̄)NQf = ȲĀB. Its hypothesis on the frame is the integral de Rham condition recalled below.

Let C = Qf−1Q. Both bases satisfy ρ(g)g(Q) = Q with the respective basis in place of Q. Cancelling the common action gives g(C) = C. By the fixed-field theorem BdRGK = K, the entries of C and its inverse lie in K ⊂ R+. Thus P = BC and V = τ̄NȲ−1W are invertible over R+. Substitute Q = QfC into the two expressions for Q to get the first row. The identity ĀĀ′ = ξ2NI gives the second row on multiplying by Ā−1 in BdR. The injection R+ ↪ BdR identifies identities between integral entries in the two rings. Brinon and Conrad, CMI Summer School Notes on p-adic Hodge Theory, Theorem 4.4.13.

How does divisibility return to the integral coefficient ring?

The factorization measures a matrix entry in B+dR. To divide an entry in 𝔖 we must show that the quotient belongs to 𝔖. Write ev for the evaluation 𝔖 → Ainf. The integral datum identifies 𝔖/(E) with 𝒪K, and θ ∘ ev induces the natural injection 𝒪K ↪ 𝒪ℂℓ. Recall that η = ev(E) generates ker θ and has image ξ in R+ = B+dR.

Divisibility lemma. For every m ≥ 0 and s ∈ 𝔖, the three membership tests below agree. Write s̄ for the image of s in R+. Applying the ring maps proves the implications from left to right. We prove the other direction in two steps.

s ∈ Em𝔖   ⇔   ev(s) ∈ ηmAinf   ⇔   s̄ ∈ ξmR+.

First take x ∈ Ainf with image divisible by ξm in R+. By construction R+ is the completion of Ainf[1/ℓ] at (η). The projection of R+ to Ainf[1/ℓ]/(ηm) has kernel (ξm). Thus x is zero in that quotient. Clearing an ℓ-power denominator gives ℓbx = ηmy with y ∈ Ainf. The localization map is injective because Ainf is a domain. Brinon and Conrad, Proposition 4.4.6.

We can remove every power of ℓ from this test. Indeed Ainf/(η) = 𝒪ℂℓ has no ℓ-torsion. If m ≥ 1, reducing the displayed identity modulo η therefore gives x = ηx₁. Substitute this expression and cancel the nonzero η to get ℓbx₁ = ηm−1y. Repeating the argument gives x ∈ ηmAinf. Brinon and Conrad, Corollary 4.4.5.

Now set x = ev(s). If η divides ev(s), then θ(ev(s)) = 0. Injectivity on 𝔖/(E) gives s = Es₁ in 𝔖. When ηm divides ev(s), cancelling η in Ainf gives ηm−1 dividing ev(s₁). Induction from m = 0 produces s ∈ Em𝔖. The converse follows by applying the two ring maps to an equality s = Emv. Applied entry by entry with m = N − H, this proves the required divisibility of both A and A′ in their original ring.

How does one change the height while preserving the Galois action?

Change of height. Given the de Rham frame of height N and an adapted basis with |hj| ≤ H, there is a frame of height H with the same basis matrix T and the same Galois matrices ε(g) for every g ∈ GK. It retains the integral de Rham condition and, whenever the starting frame is crystalline, its crystalline condition as well.

To check the Frobenius equation, let evW denote ev followed by the inclusion Ainf ↪ W(ℂℓ♭), the Witt vectors of the tilt field. Set e = evW(E). This element is nonzero. Indeed ev(E) generates the nonzero ideal ker θ, and the Witt inclusion is injective. Frobenius φ on a matrix acts entry by entry. The two equations involving the height are

AA′ = E2NI   in 𝔖,
eN φ(T) = T evW(A)   in W(ℂℓ♭).

Suppose first H ≤ N and put k = N − H. By integral divisibility we may write A = EkA₀ and A′ = EkA₀′. Substitution in the first equation permits cancellation of E2k in the domain 𝔖, leaving A₀A₀′ = E2HI. In the second equation cancel ek in the Witt domain, leaving eH φ(T) = T evW(A₀). For N ≤ H the construction goes in the other direction. Multiply both A and A′ by EH−N. Their product acquires the square of this factor, and the right side of the Frobenius equation acquires eH−N. When the two heights agree, both operations use the factor 1.

The descent equation is ρ(g) g(T) = T ε(g) in the Witt ring. The separate invariance condition for elements fixing the chosen tilt parameter also involves only ρ and T. Hence the old T and ε satisfy the full Galois conditions at height H.

Which integral de Rham and crystalline conditions are preserved?

For each m ≥ 0 the de Rham condition gives an integer b ≥ 0 and a matrix β over 𝔖 with β ≡ ℓbI modulo E. For every g ∈ GK it requires the entrywise congruence in Ainf

φ(ε(g)) g(ev(β)) ≡ ev(β)   (mod ηm).

At the new height the same b and β work at each m. The crystalline condition requires ε(g) ≡ I modulo ev(u) in Ainf for every g. Here u is the power-series variable of 𝔖. The construction of the crystalline correction establishes it for the appropriate choice of starting periods.

On a tower whose filtration jumps all lie in [−H, H], apply the change separately to each available de Rham frame. Their initial heights may vary. The theorem gives the same height H at every level. The construction at each level specifies the semistable integral periods that supply the initial frames. The comparison maps to a limiting frame require a separate argument.
