How does the residue identity give the initial integral congruence?

Fix the place w and write Q for the size of its residue field and q for its residue characteristic. Thus Q = qf. Choose a uniformizer ϖ of the valuation ring and the integral model with its marking. There is one generic tower mapping to its fibre power. The group-side congruence has already specified an operator γj for every j between 0 and the rank n, as well as a scalar ϑj. We need the actual correspondences on that model and an identity involving their residue points.

Integral congruence theorem. Use the compact unitary datum, a standard base, matching primes under the field identification, and the integral group and level tests at w. Assume the integral moduli and isogeny theorems for this datum, the comparison of sections with subspaces and the ordinary reduction theorem for geometric generic points of the special fibre. Under the group-side congruence conditions, the same model carries n + 1 integral correspondences. Their source maps are finite, and over the open where q is invertible the j-th has finite étale degree equal to the number of j-dimensional subspaces in rank n over the residue field. They satisfy the weighted point identity on the uniformizer divisor. The tower compares with the j-th correspondence with positive multiplicity mj and eigenvalue θ(γj) = mjϑj.

Constructing the legs. At the underlying integral principal level the moduli theorem for isogenies of type j supplies a correspondence with its universal isogeny. Lift the auxiliary marking to it by the marked isogeny theorem. On a fibre power the source retains the tuple on the source abelian scheme, and the target applies the isogeny to each coordinate of that tuple. The source is the pullback of the source on the underlying moduli space. The finite and étale base-change theorem for these correspondences therefore gives the stated degree where q is invertible. Both legs lie over the same point of the valuation ring because the isogeny is defined over that point.

Reading a section at the special fibre. Test the identity at a valuation-ring point as in the definition of the residue comparison. Its fraction field is algebraically closed, q is nonzero in the valuation ring, and its closed point maps to a generic point of the uniformizer divisor. Flatness carries this last condition to the underlying moduli model. At such a point the ordinarity theorem provides the ordinary structure. Comparing the kernels of the isogenies then identifies the sections of the j-th source with j-dimensional subspaces of a single rank-n space over the field with Q elements. There is one distinguished line C for the whole family.

The geometric comparison supplies two equalities of residue targets. For a fixed image U modulo C, any two subspaces missing C give the same target. A subspace containing C gives its residue twist by F. These equalities on a fibre power include the marked moduli point and the images of each coordinate. They determine the entire target point. Choose one subspace missing C over U, for example the image of a linear section, and call its target A(U). The first equality makes this definition independent of the section. The second gives exactly the input to the ordinary subspace lemma. That lemma proves equality of the two weighted multisets for every allowed test point. The comparison of isogeny kernels and their residue targets is a geometric input here.

Reading the tower coefficients. The group test chooses γj with element hj and fine level Mj. Set Hj = K₀ ∩ hj−1K₀hj. It requires that Qj divide Mj, that [K₀ : Hj] give the indicated Grassmannian count, and that the deck transformations occur among the eigenvalue-one operators. By the comparison of Hecke fibres,

mj = [Hj : K(Mj)] > 0,   θ(γj) = mjϑj.

The second equality is part of the original coefficient test. The counting argument identifies the geometric multiplicity with the integer used in that test. This constructs the initial congruence with precisely those coefficients. Restricting the integral model to the components reached by the tower gives the surjective generic cover used later.
