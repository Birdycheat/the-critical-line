How the finite prime sum controls the centered ramp

The prime-selection argument has found a prime p for which the finite sums are small throughout the required interval. There is still a term to estimate for its twist χ(n) = ξ(n)(n/p). Write an = Λ(n)χ(n) and D(s) = −L′(s,χ)/L(s,χ). For a height y and a cutoff Z ≥ y > 0, set

R(y) = ∑1≤n≤y an(y/n − 1),   SZ(y) = ∑1≤n≤Z an min(1,y/n),   PZ = ∑1≤n≤Z an/n.

For n ≤ y, subtracting 1 from y/n gives the ramp weight. For y < n ≤ Z, the two weights in yPZ − SZ(y) cancel. Hence the centered ramp satisfies an exact identity, with a tail that is a single complex number for the whole interval. Ramp, finite sum and tail · Finite splitting at the cutoff

Gχ(y) = R(y) − yD(1) = −SZ(y) − y[D(1) − PZ].

How close is the finite slope PZ to D(1)? Measure the same increment ΔR = R(Z + h) − R(Z) in two ways. First, every term already present at Z advances by exactly han/n. The new terms have Z < n ≤ Z + h. For Z ≥ 1 and h > 0, there are at most h + 1 of them, each with ramp weight ≤ h/Z and |an| ≤ log(Z + h). Thus |ΔR − hPZ| ≤ h(h + 1) log(Z + h)/Z. A prime power entering the ramp. Take the nontrivial character modulo 3, with values 1, −1, 0 on residues 1, 2, 0. From Z = 7/2 to Z + h = 5, the old slope is PZ = −log(2)/2. The ramp goes from −(3/4)log 2 to −(5/4)log 2, so ΔR − hPZ = (1/4)log 2. The entire correction comes from 4 = 2², since χ(4) = 1 and 5/4 − 1 = 1/4. The new prime 5 has weight zero at the endpoint. This calculation checks the finite difference for arbitrary cutoffs.

The same increment from a contour. Suppose χ is nonprincipal of modulus N ≤ Q, with Q ≥ 2, and L(s,χ) has no zero for Re s > 1 − 1/400 and |Im s| ≤ Q. For u ≥ 1, Perron inversion writes R(u) as the integral of D(s)us/[s(s−1)] on Re s = 2, with measure ds/(2πi). Move the segment of height |Im s| ≤ T = Q/2 to σ = 1 − 1/400 + δ, where 0 < δ ≤ 1/800. The rectangle lies inside the zero-free box and stays to the right of 0. Its only possible pole is at 1. Multiplication by s−1 leaves D(s)us/s, whose value there is uD(1). At u = Z + h and u = Z, subtracting these residues gives hD(1). Open left edge of the zero-free box · Perron integral for the ramp · Residue at one · Rectangle with empty zero list

Put h = ηZ with 0 < η ≤ 1. On the left edge, differentiate us with respect to the positive variable u. This gives |(Z + h)s − Zs| ≤ |s|ηZσ. The factor |s| cancels one denominator. Integrating 1/|s−1| over the segment costs at most 4 log(1 + 800T). The other logarithm comes from |D(s)| ≤ Cδ log Q on the smaller box Re s ≥ σ, |Im s| ≤ Q − 1. In the local formula for L′/L, the nearby zeros stay at least δ away and their total multiplicity is bounded by a constant times log Q. On the two horizontal sides the denominator has size at least T². On the unshifted right tails the integrand decays as 1/|Im s|². Together these give a constant Aδ independent of χ, Z, η and Q. Why use the 400th power? Fix 0 < ε ≤ 1/4, K ≥ 1 and τ > 0. For sufficiently large Y, the selection stage has Yε/6 ≤ log Q ≤ Yε/4. Choose x = YK, the largest required height, and set Z = x400, η = x−2, h = x398, δ = ε/(1600K). Subtract the errors ΔR − hPZ and ΔR − hD(1). Their difference is h[D(1) − PZ], whose size is bounded by the sum of the two errors’ absolute values. Multiply by x/h. The left-edge term now contains xZσ−1 = x400δ = Yε/4. Thus the width 1/400 of the zero-free strip pays for the entire factor x. The resulting upper bound for x|D(1) − PZ| has three terms. One bound now covers every y ∈ [Y,x]. Since y ≤ x, the tail in the centered ramp is at most Yε. For Y ≥ 16 this is at most y1/2+τ/2. The surviving prime already satisfies |SZ(y)| ≤ y1/2+τ/2 throughout the interval, by the exclusion of large finite sums. Adding gives |Gχ(y)| ≤ y1/2+τ. The selection applies this calculation to the full primitive twist with Q = FU, the product of the base conductor bound and the prime cutoff. Every prime passing the two tests thus supplies a centered estimate at every required height.
