Why only one real test per complex place survives

In a single Bockstein fiber, the counting proof lifts differences to integral homology in odd degree m = 2e + 1. For a totally complex field L′ with r₂ pairs of complex embeddings, it needs a bound on the number of independent filtered classes. The precise theorem, bStarOdd, gives rank at most r₂ when m < r₂ and the initial matrix size n is at least 2m + 4. The proof uses the published homological stability statement KStability. Over the Dedekind ring 𝒪L′ its stable-range parameter is 2, so enlarging the matrices by identity blocks induces an isomorphism on degree-m integral homology in this range. Start with a class z0. At step t, take its image under the homomorphism that repeats a matrix in ℓ diagonal blocks, then subtract ℓt+2 times its image under ordinary stabilization to the same size. Call the resulting class zt+1. A linear test of weight j multiplies by ℓj under block repetition and is unchanged by stabilization. After m − 1 steps its value has therefore been multiplied by

∏i=2m(ℓj − ℓi).

Every weight from 2 through m has a zero factor. To see what is left, suppose a real linear combination of the resulting cycles represents a nonzero real homology class. Stability preserves it at a larger matrix size, where a closed cochain detects it. The invariant-form comparison represents that cochain on cycles by integration. In odd degree its evaluation is a sum of the higher-weight tests and one weight-one test τw for each infinite place w. The latter comes from an alternating cyclic trace at that place. Reversing the cycle conjugates the trace. After alternation one of its real and imaginary parts vanishes. If every τw vanished on our combination, the cochain could not detect it. Thus any two cycles with the same test values give the same real homology class. The real homology image is a quotient of the image of the r₂ tests, so its dimension is at most r₂. Independent integral classes stay independent after extension of scalars to ℝ, yielding the rank bound. Degree three. For L′ = ℚ(ζ15), the eight embeddings form four complex pairs. Take n = 10 and ℓ = 2. Two steps give sizes 10, 20, 40. The weight-one multiplier is (2 − 4)(2 − 8) = 12; weights two and three disappear. Any five filtered classes are therefore dependent over ℝ. The coefficient 12 is 4 times an odd unit modulo 2ν, so multiplying the filtered evaluation by the inverse of 3 recovers four times the original evaluation. In a target killed by q = 2ν, the free part contributes at most q4 possible values; torsion is counted separately. The general count uses the same bound in each fiber and later cancels its factor qr₂.
