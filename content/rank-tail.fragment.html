Why each orbit is a product of residue groups

At each selected place the residue field has T elements. Its unit group H has T − 1 elements. The level-zero forms admit a model as complex functions on a finite set X. An element of H in one coordinate acts by a local dilation, also called a diamond operator. The operator Nv sums all these dilations in the coordinate v. Under the model, its kernel consists of functions whose sum along that coordinate is zero at every point. The ramified subspace is the intersection of these kernels. Freeness uses arithmetic. Suppose a tuple of local multipliers fixes a level class. Represent it by an adelic element δ, whose components at the two auxiliary type primes are the identity. Its (T − 1)st power belongs to the level subgroup, since the residues of all its multipliers have that power equal to 1. The diamond operator normalizes the level, so the same power of δκ belongs to the level for every level element κ. Fixing the class supplies a rational element γ from the double-coset relation. The admissible stabilizer gives γT−1 = 1. At each of the two distinct auxiliary primes p, the local reduction calculation kills every power of γ of order coprime to p. To see the force of having two primes, write T − 1 = pam with p ∤ m. The first condition kills γpa, whose order divides m. Now γ has order dividing pa, which is coprime to the second prime. Hence γ = 1 and δ itself lies in the level subgroup. Each local multiplier fixes a nonzero level functional killed by T. A multiplier c must therefore satisfy c ≡ 1 mod T. The tuple is the identity. Counting the independent values

Keep only the entries indexed by tuples with every coordinate different from the identity. They determine a function with vanishing line sums uniquely. To recover an entry with an identity coordinate, it must be the negative of the sum of the other entries on that line. Each of those entries has one fewer identity coordinate, so induction recovers the full table. Conversely, to extend a unit value at such a tuple a, use the product over coordinates v of (1hv=av − 1hv=1). It sums to zero along every coordinate and restricts to the unit value at a and zero at every other retained tuple. Taking linear combinations extends arbitrary data. From an exact count to a uniform error

For a nonzero full space and T ≥ 3, Bernoulli’s inequality gives

1 − dram/dfull = 1 − (1 − 1/(T − 1))f ≤ f/(T − 1) ≤ 2f/T.

The scale conditions give T ≥ bm/2 ≥ b, with b = exp(xε), b ≥ 3 and m ≥ 2. Thus dfull − dram ≤ de dfull for de = 2f/b. The undivided inequality also holds if the full space is zero. What controls the variation of the prime level?

The word-to-word part of Count5 uses a Vandermonde product V(z) = ∏a<b(za − zb) in the decreasing real parameters at a place. Its logarithm is a sum over pairs. For the first row a = 0 the two compared gaps are each at least 4kΔ and change by at most Δ. The variation of each logarithm is at most 1/(4k). For a ≥ 1 the gaps are at least 3(b − a)Δ and change by at most 2Δ, giving 2/[3(b − a)] per pair. Both bounds follow by integrating 1/t between the gaps. A harmonic sum over b bounds the logarithmic variation at a place by 1/4 + (2/3)k(1 + log k). Summing over f places and applying the factor 1/(fk) in the level parameter leaves variation of order log k. The shared auxiliary factor cancels when the two word windows are compared. The absolute bounds for each word also use a torus-pair estimate involving the mass, volume and formal degree.
