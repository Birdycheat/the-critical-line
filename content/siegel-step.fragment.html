How a volume becomes a sum over lattice classes

The formula for the word scale uses the volume of a unitary group on the row side and a Hermitian genus mass on the column side. To compare ranks, we need both in the same units. A genus consists of lattices isometric at every finite place. Its mass is the sum of 1/|Aut(Λ)| over its global isometry classes. The proof starts in positive definite rank two, where the automorphism groups are finite. Definition of genus mass · The two inputs used in Count5

Let Γ be the rational unitary group, G its adelic group and K the subgroup preserving the completed lattice at finite places and unrestricted at infinity. In the definite case the prescribed archimedean measures are probabilities. Applying the product normalization to the whole compact group at each infinite place gives μ(K) = 1. Pick representatives gi of the finite double quotient Γ∖G/K. In the class of gi, exactly |Γ ∩ giKgi−1| translates of a point by Γ land in giK. Weight that coset’s indicator by the reciprocal. Its sum over Γ is one on that class. Integrating the weighted indicators over G now gives the volume of a fundamental domain. The stabilizers are the lattice automorphism groups, so the result is the genus mass. A finite model. Take G = S₃ and Γ = K = {1, (12)}. The two double cosets have representatives 1 and (13), with stabilizer orders 2 and 1. Their weights add to 3/2. Directly, μ(K) = 1 gives mass 1/2 to each element and a Γ-fundamental domain has three elements. This explains the inverse stabilizer in the formula. A deeper finite level K′ ⊂ K has measure 1/[K:K′]. Renormalizing that subgroup to measure one therefore multiplies the covolume by its index. In the word count the row uses its own index and the column uses its own, so their quotient is row index divided by column index. Why adjacent ranks have a predictable mass ratio

Write M for the new rank, f for the number of conjugate pairs of complex embeddings of the CM field, and v for half its root discriminant. The quadratic extension of its totally real subfield is unramified at every finite prime. For a totally positive definite integral Hermitian lattice Λ, count vectors whose squared lengths are at most j² at every embedding. Sum these counts over the genus using the reciprocal automorphism weights above. The zero vector contributes the mass μΛ. Grouping the other vectors by their represented value gives the same sum, now expressed through representation counts. Divide by j2fM and let j grow. The two ways of counting give

μΛ (πM/M!)f / (vfM δ) = (cM−1 λΛ/δ) M−f Lη(M) / √((2v)f).

On the left, πM/M! is the volume of a unit ball in ℂM, and δ is the positive square root of the absolute norm of the lattice discriminant ideal. On the right, cM−1 is the mass constant for the preceding rank, λΛ is a product of local lattice corrections, and Lη(M) is the Euler product of the CM field. This second limit uses the representation-density formula and the asymptotic sum of its local factors. The zero vector’s separate contribution tends to zero after division. Cancel δ and use M!/M = (M − 1)!. For a lattice with an orthonormal integral basis every local lattice correction is one. The two standard masses consequently satisfy the following identity for the ranks M ≥ 3 used here. For instance, take M = 3 and a finite prime of the totally real subfield of residue size 2. If it is inert, the unique prime above it has norm 4 and its Euler factor is (1 + 2−3)−1 = 8/9. If it splits, its two primes of norm 2 each contribute (1 − 2−3)−1/2, giving 8/7 together. These are factors at a single prime. Across the rank increments, the same positive rank-one constant occurs in both standard masses and cancels in their ratio. To reach the indefinite row, the normalized unit-vector unfolding supplies the same ratio for a space and the orthogonal complement of a vector of norm +1 or −1. From definite rank two, use +1 to compare successive positive definite ranks. For a constant signature (P, Q) with P ≥ 2, induction on Q uses a −1 vector, whose complement has signature (P, Q − 1). The two inductions identify the row volume with the standard mass of its rank r. In the word-scale formula the column mass then cancels the common ranks, leaving exactly the increments M = k + 1, …, r. These are the factors estimated in the lower bound for each word.
