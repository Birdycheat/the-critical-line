When does a map on the generic cover extend to the integral base?

The elementary correspondence now has an integral source and target. A point on the generic tower specifies a point of this correspondence. To obtain an operator on the integral model we must first forget the auxiliary tower marking and then extend over the closed fibre. The following lemma does both.

Finite descent and extension lemma. Let X be smooth over a discrete valuation ring R with fraction field K. Let V → XK be finite étale and surjective. Suppose X maps to B, Y is finite over B, and a map y ∶ V → Y respects the given maps to B. If every two points of V over the same algebraically closed field and the same point of XK have identical images as geometric points of Y, there is a unique B-map X → Y inducing y.

Descending to the generic fibre. The fibre product V ×XK V parametrizes pairs of lifts. It is étale over XK, hence smooth over K and reduced. The two maps to Y obtained from such a pair have a closed equalizer because the finite map Y → B is separated. By the hypothesis every geometric point belongs to the equalizer. Locally, each function in its defining ideal therefore belongs to every prime ideal. Such a function is nilpotent and reducedness makes it zero. The maps agree on the whole fibre product. A finite étale surjection is faithfully flat and quasi-compact, so descent of morphisms gives a unique map XK → Y over B. The Stacks Project, Lemma 33.25.3 on smooth schemes over a field, Lemma 26.21.5 on the equalizer and Lemma 35.13.7 on descent of morphisms.

Extending across the closed fibre. Pull Y back to X. It is finite over X, and the generic map gives a section there. Smoothness over R gives flatness and normality. Flatness makes a uniformizer ϖ a nonzero divisor. On each integral component the generic fibre is consequently dense. Work on an affine open of one component, with ring A. It is an integrally closed domain. The finite pullback corresponds to a finite A-algebra D. Our section is a homomorphism D → A[1/ϖ]. Each element of D satisfies a monic polynomial over A because D is finite. Its image lies in the fraction field of A and satisfies that polynomial, so already belongs to A. This gives an extension on the affine open. The injection A ↪ A[1/ϖ] forces any two extensions to coincide. The local extensions therefore glue. Locally Noetherian normal schemes are disjoint unions of integral normal components, so the argument covers all of X. The Stacks Project, Lemma 10.163.9 on smooth algebras over a normal ring and Lemma 28.7.7 on normal components.

Two small tests of the hypotheses

Over ℂ the squaring map on the multiplicative group is a finite étale cover of degree two. The coordinate function u takes opposite values at the lifts 1 and −1 of 1. It cannot descend. The function u² is constant on every pair of lifts and descends to the coordinate on the base.

For the ring in the extension argument, take A = ℂ[t²,t³] and D = ℂ[t]. The latter is finite over A, generated as a module by 1 and t. After inverting t² the rings coincide since t = t³/t². Any extension of this generic section would have to send t to t in the common fraction field. The ring A contains no polynomial with a nonzero term of degree one. This shows exactly where integral closedness entered the affine proof.

Which two maps descend for the elementary operator?

Keep the split place of the integral model. Its residue prime q differs from the prime p omitted in the generic adelic lattice Λ̂. Put 𝔲 = (q, ω − r) and fix a prime 𝔴′ containing 𝔲. The source and target models X₁, X₂ have the same integer level N₀ ≥ 3 prime to p and q. Their ideal levels satisfy 𝔲d ⊆ 𝔟₁ ⊆ 𝔟₂𝔴′ for some d ≥ 0. We keep the multiplicity-one datum. Let h preserve Λ̂ and have elementary type at 𝔴′ with index 0 ≤ j ≤ n. Use compatible marked generic levels M₁, M₂ prime to p, with Nm(𝔴′)j dividing M₁. The finite residue field at 𝔴′ has degree f′ ≥ 1 over 𝔽q. The multiplier of the correspondence is therefore c = qf′j.

First descend the kernel. Forget the source ideal marking to obtain X₁ → M into the common moduli scheme of integer level N₀. Let H be the integral elementary correspondence. Its source projection s ∶ H → M is finite. A generic tower point specifies the elementary quotient and hence a candidate point of H above the source. To use finite descent, take two lifts of the same geometric point of X₁. For each point killed by 𝔴′ on the common abelian variety, the ideal marking of X₁ gives a common source label. At integer level M₁ this has the form v = a₁y, where a₁ is the coefficient representing the ideal marking and y is a lattice label. Here the inclusion 𝔟₁ ⊆ 𝔴′ allows the source marking to read 𝔴′-torsion. In each lift membership in the proposed kernel is equivalent to the generic isogeny killing the section labelled v. The marking criterion is hv ∈ M₁Λ̂, independent of the lift.

Why does this identify the two points of H? For either isogeny ψ on this characteristic-zero fibre, let D = ker ψ ∩ A[𝔴′]. Its order is Nm(𝔴′)j by the elementary-type condition. That condition also says that every point of ker ψ killed by a power of 𝔲 is already killed by 𝔴′. Conversely 𝔲 ⊆ 𝔴′, so every point of D is killed by 𝔲. Thus the test identifies the distinguished part of the kernel. This part is finite étale over the characteristic-zero field, so equality at geometric points gives equality as subgroup schemes. To recover the full kernel, apply the polarized full-kernel lemma to the two isogenies of the same multiplier c. The split coefficient action decomposes a c-torsion point into two parts. The polarization and its adjoint identity determine membership of the conjugate part from the distinguished one. Hence the two full kernel functors agree on every test scheme. The isomorphism of the marked polarized quotients identifies the two points of H by its representing property. Descent now gives a map b ∶ X₁ → H. Pulling the universal isogeny back along b gives a quotient family whose target projection is a map X₁ → M.

Then descend the target marking. The target with its additional ideal marking is finite over M. The generic tower already maps to it. Over a fixed integral source point the preceding step fixes the underlying quotient. To identify its ideal marking we must test each target label y. Choose coefficients a₁, a₂ representing the two ideal levels at the corresponding generic integer levels, so that (ai,Mi)𝔟i = (Mi) for i = 1, 2. The lattice calculation supplies a source label x with

h(M₂a₁x) ≡ M₁a₂y   mod M₁M₂Λ̂.
Why is there a source label for each target label?

In the rational completed lattice put z = a₂y/M₂, viewed modulo Λ̂. The ideal 𝔟₂ kills this class since a₂𝔟₂ ⊆ (M₂). Factor 𝔟₂ = (𝔴′)m𝔠, with 𝔠 coprime to 𝔴′. By the Chinese remainder theorem write 1 = α + β with α ∈ 𝔠 and β ∈ (𝔴′)m. The class βz is killed by 𝔠, while αz is killed by (𝔴′)m. Since 𝔠 contains a power of 𝔲, the congruence-to-identity clause of the elementary type fixes the first class modulo Λ̂. For the second, its local clause supplies a scalar γ outside 𝔴′ such that γ𝔴′Λ̂ ⊆ hΛ̂. Choose γ′ with γ′γ ≡ 1 modulo (𝔴′)m. Then h−1(γ′γαz) is a preimage of αz modulo Λ̂. It is killed modulo Λ̂ by (𝔴′)m+1, since multiplying αz by (𝔴′)m puts it in Λ̂ and multiplying by γ𝔴′ puts it in hΛ̂. Their sum is a preimage of z killed by (𝔴′)m+1𝔠 and hence by 𝔟₁ ⊆ 𝔟₂𝔴′.

To obtain an integral source label, choose a representative u of this preimage in the rational adelic lattice. Every b ∈ 𝔟₁ satisfies bu ∈ Λ̂. Since (a₁,M₁)𝔟₁ = (M₁), there are b, b′ ∈ 𝔟₁ with M₁ = a₁b + M₁b′. Multiplying by u gives M₁u = a₁(bu) + M₁(b′u) with both parenthesized vectors integral in Λ̂. Because M₁ is prime to the omitted prime p, choose x ∈ Λ representing bu modulo M₁Λ̂. Then M₁u ≡ a₁x modulo M₁Λ̂. Applying h, which preserves Λ̂, and using hu ≡ z modulo Λ̂ gives the claimed congruence after multiplication by M₂. Milne, Algebraic Number Theory, Theorem 3.7 on ideal factorization and the Chinese remainder theorem.

Evaluate both labels at the common level M₁M₂. Compatibility of the tower markings with multiplication identifies their values with those at their original levels. The congruence says exactly that the image of the source section with label a₁x is the target section with label a₂y. The source section and the descended elementary isogeny agree on both lifts. Thus every labelled target section agrees. The target marking is determined by these sections, so the second descent produces t ∶ X₁ → X₂. Pullback of the target universal family along its forgetting map, together with the descended quotient isogeny, gives Φ over t. The defining property of that pullback verifies the second of the two generic squares. This completes the elementary step needed for the whole family of operators.
