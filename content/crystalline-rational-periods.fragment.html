Why does the crystalline congruence give an exactly invariant section?

Let K be a finite extension of ℚℓ with residue field k. Take a height-H Breuil–Kisin frame over 𝔖 = W(k)[[u]], with Eisenstein polynomial E and matrices AA′ = E2HI. Write e for evaluation in Ainf, ι for the map to R = Acris and v = ι(e(u)). The frame satisfies the integral crystalline congruence ε(g) ≡ I modulo e(u) for every g ∈ GK. We shall use it to show that one and the same Frobenius section Q is fixed by the corrected action of every g.

Fix g, write σ for its action on R and put ε′ = ι(φ(ε(g))). All maps on matrices act entrywise. For a series s put f(s) = ι(φ(e(s))) and f₀(s) = f(s(0)), viewing its constant term as a constant series. The section comes with an integral matrix Q′ and an exponent κ ≥ 0 such that QQ′ = ℓ2κI and both matrices reduce to ℓκI modulo v. This lets us measure the difference between ε′σ(Q) and Q inside the integral ring. Define

D = Q′ ε′ σ(Q) − ℓ2κI.

Every entry of D is divisible by v. Indeed φ(v) = vℓ, so the crystalline congruence gives ε′ ≡ I modulo vℓ. Also σ(v) generates the same ideal as v. If e(u) = [b] is the Teichmüller lift of the chosen tilt parameter, then g(b)/b has valuation zero. Its lift is a unit and multiplies v to σ(v). Thus σ(Q) still reduces to ℓκI, and the two terms defining D agree modulo v.

To see what Frobenius does to this factor of v, first work in R[1/ℓ]. There Q−1 = ℓ−2κQ′. Put B = f(E)−Hf(A) and B₀ = f₀(E)−Hf₀(A). Both denominators are powers of ℓ times units, as proved in the construction of the section. Its intertwining identity is QB₀ = Bφ(Q). The Frobenius–Galois compatibility of the frame gives ε′σ(B) = Bφ(ε′). The Galois action fixes the constant evaluations in B₀ and commutes with φ. For Z = Q−1ε′σ(Q) these identities give

B₀φ(Z) = Q−1Bφ(ε′)σ(φ(Q)) = Q−1ε′σ(Bφ(Q)) = ZB₀.

Since D = ℓ2κ(Z − I), it satisfies the same identity. Clear the denominator in B₀ and multiply on the right by f₀(A′). The resulting product of constant matrices is f₀(A)f₀(A′) = f₀(E)2HI. Dividing by the unit w₀2H, where w₀ = f₀(E)/ℓ, leaves integral matrices on both sides. The map R → R[1/ℓ] is injective, so the identity also holds in R. Brinon and Conrad, Proposition 9.1.1, flatness of the crystalline ring.

ℓ2HD = F₀ φ(D) F₀′,   F₀ = f₀(A),   F₀′ = w₀−2Hf₀(A′).

Start from D = vN₀. Apply φ and multiply by F₀ on the left and F₀′ on the right. Each step raises the power of v by a factor of ℓ and costs the same power ℓ2H. Induction gives an integral matrix Nn at every step, with

ℓ2HnD = vℓnNn,   Nn+1 = F₀ φ(Nn) F₀′.

Apply the growth estimate with c = 2H. Given M, choose n large enough for vℓn−1 to be divisible by ℓ2Hn+M. For example, at ℓ = 3, deg E = 2 and H = 1 we have v⁶ ∈ 3R. After four iterations the exponent of v is 81. Keeping one factor of v leaves 80, enough for thirteen powers of 3. Cancelling the eight powers paid for the iterations still leaves at least five. In general, cancel ℓ2Hn in the iterated identity to obtain D ≡ 0 modulo ℓM. The ring R is ℓ-adically separated, so D = 0. Multiplying its defining identity on the left by Q and cancelling ℓ2κ yields ε′σ(Q) = Q for every g. Brinon and Conrad, §9.1, the separated completion. Proposition 9.1.3, divided powers.

At height zero in rank one, taking both Frobenius coefficients equal to 1 gives the recursion D = φ(D). The constant D = 1 satisfies it. The crystalline congruence supplied the first factor of v in our defect, and its successive Frobenius images force vanishing.

Which columns give the crystalline periods of the representation?

Let ρ be the action of GK on the rank-d lattice of this frame, and V its scalar extension to ℚℓ. Work with the crystalline period ring B = Bcris for the same local field and the compatible maps from Ainf and Acris. In a basis of the lattice, a column c in B ⊗ℚℓ V is a crystalline period if ρ(g)g(c) = c for every g. The ring action can move each entry of such a column. We seek d fixed columns that form a basis over B.

Write fB for Witt Frobenius on Ainf followed by the map to B. The integral frame supplies matrices Y, Y′ with YY′ = τ2HI, where τ is the chosen primitive period. Its defining conditions include φ(τ) = h([ζ] − 1), with h a unit of Ainf and ζ a nontrivial compatible system of ℓ-power roots of unity with untilt 1. The image of [ζ] − 1 is invertible in B, as explained below. Thus w = fB(τ)H is a unit. Put X = fB(Y), X′ = fB(Y′), and extend Q, Q′ from R to B. Take the columns of

U = w−1XQ.

The columns form a basis. Indeed XX′ = w²I and QQ′ = ℓ2κI. Multiplication in the reverse order yields UQ′X′ = ℓ2κwI. Dividing Q′X′ by this unit gives a right inverse for U. Taking determinants shows that det U is a unit, so the adjugate formula gives a two-sided inverse.

To check the action, fix g and put Γ = fB(ε(g)). The Galois identity of the frame gives wρ(g)g(X) = g(w)XΓ. The vanishing of the defect gives Γg(Q) = Q. Since g also acts on the denominator of U, the two scalar factors cancel and

ρ(g)g(U) = g(w)−1ρ(g)g(X)g(Q) = w−1XΓg(Q) = U.

Every column can now be written uniquely as Ua. Its transform is Ug(a), so the column is fixed exactly when all the entries of a belong to the fixed ring. For crystalline periods this ring is K₀ = W(k)[1/ℓ], the maximal unramified subfield of K. Thus the columns of U form a K₀-basis of the fixed vectors. Their dimension is d = dimℚℓ V, which proves that the rational representation attached to the frame is crystalline. Brinon and Conrad, Theorem 9.1.5 and the fixed-field calculation preceding Proposition 9.1.6.

Why does the cyclotomic factor become a unit?

Put μ = [ζ] − 1 and choose a generator of the module of compatible ℓ-power roots of unity. The logarithm of its Teichmüller lift is Fontaine’s period t. Since ζ is nontrivial, the logarithm of [ζ] is a nonzero ℤℓ-multiple of t. This follows from the ℤℓ-linearity of the logarithm on these lifts. Write its series as μ times the sum of (−1)m+1μm−1/m for m ≥ 1. The latter sum belongs to Acris[1/ℓ]. Indeed μ is in ker θ, so μm−1/(m−1)! is integral, and the ℓ-adic valuation of (m−1)!/m tends to infinity. The ring Bcris inverts ℓ and t. There the product of μ with this sum is a unit, which makes μ itself a unit. Brinon and Conrad, §4.4, the cyclotomic logarithm. Proposition 9.1.3 and Definition 9.1.4, divided powers and crystalline localization.

A rank-one calculation over the Gaussian rationals

Take conjugation g on ℚ(i) over ℚ and ρ(g) = −1. Set w = i, X = 1 + i, Q = 1 − i and Γ = −i. Then Γg(Q) = (−i)(1 + i) = 1 − i, and the two sides of the frame identity both equal −1 − i. The product XQ = 2 goes to −2 under the twisted action. Dividing by w gives U = −2i, which the twisted action fixes. More explicitly, a Gaussian rational a + bi satisfies −(a − bi) = a + bi exactly when a = 0. This fixed line is ℚ · U. The column calculation lets us change the factors and test the same identity.
