Why averaging the square over primes saves a power of the height

Choose 0 < ε ≤ 1, K ≥ 1 and a sufficiently large Y. Set Z = (YK)400. Fix one height y ∈ [Y,YK] and one complex sequence |κ(n)| ≤ 1 for the entire progression P of primes √U < q ≤ U, q ≡ a mod m. With N = ⌊Z⌋ and cn = Λ(n) min(1,y/n), expand the square of Sq = ∑n≤N cnκ(n)(n/q). Each ordered pair (n,n′) contributes a coefficient cncn′κ(n)κ(n′) times a sum of quadratic symbols. We can take the absolute value of each such sum after summing over q. This order retains cancellation among primes. Sum and fixed-height quantifiers · Expansion and interchange of the sums

For a product nn′, let s be the product of primes outside m whose exponent in nn′ is odd. For an admitted progression and sufficiently large Y, its lower cutoff √U is at least N² and hence every q exceeds N², so a square factor has symbol +1. The primes dividing m contribute a fixed sign on the progression. Indeed, 8 divides m, which fixes the symbol of 2 and the sign in quadratic reciprocity. For odd primes dividing m it also fixes the residue of q modulo that prime. Hence for a sign σ depending on the pair and the progression,

(n/q)(n′/q) = σ(q/s),   σ ∈ {−1,+1}.

For example, 53 and 101 are both 5 modulo 24. The pair (4,3) has s = 1 even though 12 is not a square. Its symbol is −1 at both primes. For (3,5) the symbol is −(q/5), giving +1 at 53 and −1 at 101. These two terms cancel exactly. The example illustrates the correlation on two primes. The squarefree part and positive weights · The common sign

Why should ∑q∈P(q/s) be small for s > 1? Split the progression into reduced classes b mod ms above a. Their ordinary prime-counting main terms cancel because the nontrivial character (b/s) sums to zero. A real zero β of a quadratic character χ can add a term proportional to ∑b(b/s)χ(b). Multiplying every class by a unit u ≡ 1 mod m permutes the sum. If χ(u) differs from (u/s), this multiplies it by −1, forcing it to vanish. If the characters agree on these units, the logarithmic integral li(Uβ) is small enough unless β > 1 − c₀/log(mY800K). Page’s zero-free result makes any two such near-one characters agree on common units. Distinct squarefree s would give different symbols on one of those units. The Chinese remainder theorem lets us choose a nonresidue at one prime dividing just one of them and 1 at every other prime involved, as well as modulo m. Thus at most one integer s★ escapes the bound |∑(q/s)| ≤ η|P|, η = Y−(K+1). It is chosen for the progression before y and κ. The remaining task is to count the pairs where cancellation can fail. Since Λ vanishes off prime powers, each index has the form pj. It contributes the single prime p to the parity calculation when p ∤ m and j is odd, and contributes nothing otherwise. Let R be the total weight of indices contributing nothing, sp the total weight of all powers of p, B a common upper bound on sp, and T the total weight. All weights in these definitions are cn, for 1 ≤ n ≤ N.

  • For s = 1 the two indices either contribute nothing or contribute the same prime twice. Their weight is at most R² + ∑psp².
  • If s★ is a prime r, the source covers same-base pairs by sr². For distinct bases, one index contributes r and the other contributes nothing, costing at most 2srR in the two orders. If s★ is composite, only a product of two distinct primes p, r can occur, at cost ≤ 2spsr. Thus 2B² + 2BR covers either case.
  • Every other pair with s > 1 costs at most η|P| times its weight. Summing all positive weights gives an upper bound η|P|T².
∑q∈P|Sq|² ≤ |P| [∑psp² + R² + 2B² + 2BR + ηT²].

Put w = Yε/5. The modulus condition requires each prime divisor of m to be at most w or greater than Z. Among these divisors only the first group can contribute to R, with at most w bases of weight ≤ B each. Every other index in R is a square. The squares have total weight bounded by a constant times √y by summing Λ(j) min(1,y/j²). Consequently R ≤ C₁(√y + w log y) ≤ 5C₁√y. The other estimates are B = C₂ log y, ∑psp² ≤ C₃y log y, and T ≤ C₄y log Z. For large Y, (log Z)² ≤ Y, and y ≤ YK gives ηT² ≤ C₄²y. The displayed bound is therefore ≤ C y log y |P| with one absolute C. Once ε and K are fixed, the threshold for Y works for every admitted progression satisfying this prime-divisor condition, every y ∈ [Y,YK] and every common κ. The separate argument for heights chosen by individual primes uses this conclusion twice, for the sum and for its derivative.
