Can one choose the block representatives at every scale at once?

For one block of rank j ≥ 1, let Γ be the group of automorphisms of its coefficient lattice Λ. Its elements act by left multiplication on tuples g of invertible complex j × j matrices, one matrix per embedding pair. We want one representative of each Γ-orbit. We also want the representative of the orbit of rg to be r times the representative of g, for every real r > 0. This lets a single unit interval of logarithmic determinants measure every other interval.

First minimize the largest absolute value of an entry, taken over all matrices in the tuple, as the tuple varies over its orbit. A minimum exists because only finitely many translates can have size bounded by any fixed number. Indeed, choose nonzero integers a, b in O with aOj ⊂ Λ ⊂ b−1Oj. For γ ∈ Γ the entries of abγ are integral. A bound on γg bounds γ after multiplication by the fixed inverse g−1. At every embedding these integral entries now lie in a bounded set. The simultaneous embedding of O is a lattice, so only finitely many entries, and hence matrices, occur.

Break a tie by dividing each matrix tuple by its size and assigning the normalized tuple a real number in a fixed, injective, measurable way. For instance, enumerate the open balls with rational centers and radii in its coordinate space and use ternary digit 2 for membership in a ball, 0 otherwise. These balls distinguish points. On the finite set of minimizers, choose the least such number. Conditions of the form “its size and tie breaker precede those of γg” are Borel, and Γ is countable, so the selected points form a measurable set D. Positive scaling multiplies all sizes by the same number and preserves all normalized coordinates. Hence D is stable under positive scalars. Left multiplication on invertible matrices is free, so the translates of D partition the matrix group.

  • Finiteness under bounds at every complex embedding
  • Only finitely many translates have bounded matrix size
  • A scale-invariant tie breaker and the orbit representatives
  • A unique measurable choice on each orbit
  • Integration on the fundamental domain and the quotient
  • One fundamental domain for every positive scalar
Why does a unit slab determine the whole determinant measure?

Set s(g) = log ∏v|det gᵥ|². For γ ∈ Γ the product is one because it is the absolute real determinant of a lattice automorphism. The function s thus descends to the quotient. Multiplying g by r > 0 adds 2fj log r to s. This map preserves D and Haar measure. Let b be the mass of 0 ≤ s < 1 in D.

Here is the averaging argument for any nonnegative measurable test F. For every real s, the set of shifts t with t ≤ s < t + 1 has length one. Insert its indicator and integrate first over g in the translated slab. The substitution g = exp(t/(2fj))h moves this slab back to the original one. The inner integral over t then has the same value at every h, because it integrates all real translates of F.

∫D F(s(g)) dg = ∫{h∈D, 0≤s(h)<1} (∫ℝ F(s(h) + t) dt) dh = b ∫ℝ F(u) du.

Tonelli’s theorem justifies both orders of integration. The block argument only requires a totally complex field. For a Gaussian line, Γ consists of the four units acting on ℂ× by quarter-turns. Choose the sector 0 ≤ arg z < π/2. With Haar measure dx dy/|z|², a unit slab in log |z|² has log-radius width 1/2. Its mass is (π/2)(1/2) = π/4. The general block uses b with its specified matrix Haar normalization. Changing from s to the flag size now gives the factor 2fj dτ/τ in the marked-slice integral.
