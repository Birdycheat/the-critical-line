How many nonzero vectors of norm zero enter a shrinking box?

Let E/F be a CM extension, d = [F ∶ ℚ], and J a nondegenerate Hermitian form on EM, with M ≥ 3. Write G for the group of adelic isometries and Γ for the rational isometries. Check the exponent in a ternary Hermitian space

Take F = ℚ, E = ℚ(i) and a hyperbolic plane perpendicular to a line of norm 1. Here d = s = 1. Shrinking the radius from 1 to e−1 multiplies the scalar prefactor r² by e−2 and moves the first shell up by one integer. The geometric tail then gains a second factor e−2. Together they multiply the majorant by e−4, as predicted by the fourth power of the radius.

Why does a compact set have finite mean on a nonzero norm fibre?

Fix t ∈ F× and a compact adelic set Ω. An unrepresented norm contributes zero. If ⟨e,e⟩ = t for a rational e, the line Ee is nondegenerate. Its orthogonal complement has dimension M−1. The stabilizer G1 of the vector e is the adelic unitary group of this complement. Witt’s extension theorem identifies every rational vector of norm t with γ−1e for some γ ∈ Γ. Two choices give the same vector precisely when they differ by left multiplication by an element of Γ1 = Γ ∩ G1. Thus the classes are Γ1γ in Γ1 ∖ Γ. Unfolding replaces the sum integrated over Γ ∖ G by the integral of f(h) = 𝟙Ω(h−1e) over Γ1 ∖ G, before division by V. The compact representative theorem supplies a compact set C of isometries such that every norm-t vector in Ω has the form ce with c ∈ C. It uses adelic Witt extension and compact lifting on the closed orbit. Consequently, whenever f(h) = 1, we have h−1e = ce and hc fixes e. Thus the support lies in G1C−1.
