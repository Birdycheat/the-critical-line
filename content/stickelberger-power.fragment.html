How does a surviving coefficient bound a power of γ − 1?

The nonvanishing theorem detects a twisted sum at one fixed projection depth d, however far up the tower the source lies. The class-group bound needs an integral ideal membership with a single exponent at all those levels. The bridge between them is a polynomial in one nilpotent variable.

Read the order of vanishing in the cyclic direction. At level n let G be the units modulo fℓn. Write [g] for the basis element of its group algebra and set T = [γ]−1. Work over an algebraic closure k of 𝔽ℓ. The proof constructs a homomorphism p from G into the cyclic subgroup generated by γ. It preserves the shallow projection π, sends complex conjugation J to 1, and γ has order dividing ℓn. Consequently Tℓn = [γℓn]−1 = 0. For an odd character χ the twist Φ formed with p is a polynomial in T, since every [p(g)] is a nonnegative power of 1+T. The map induced by π takes Φ to the known nonzero twist and sends Tℓd to zero. Choose a polynomial representing Φ and factor out its lowest nonzero power, writing Φ = Tru(T) with u(0) ≠ 0. The polynomial u(T) has an inverse. After division by its constant term it is one plus a nilpotent, whose inverse is a finite geometric sum. If r ≥ ℓd, the image of Φ in the shallow quotient would vanish. A detectable coefficient which has no inverse

In the group algebra of a cyclic group of order eight over 𝔽₂, write T = [γ]−1. The elements 1, T, …, T⁷ form a basis and T⁸ = 0. Project to the cyclic group of order four, where T⁴ = 0. The element Φ = T³(1+T) has nonzero image T³. It has no inverse in the original ring, but it divides T⁴. Multiplying Φ by T(1+T)⁷ gives T⁴ because (1+T)⁸ = 1. This is a check of the polynomial step.

Recover the untwisted relation. The part discarded by p has exponent dividing φ(fℓe) = ℓam with ℓ ∤ m. Here e is 1 at odd ℓ and 2 at ℓ = 2. Raising g/p(g) to ℓa defines a homomorphism b with image D of order prime to ℓ. In the algebra k[G]/(Θ,1+[J]), split the action of D into characters. For an eigenvector v ≠ 0 of character ψ, take χ satisfying χ(g)ℓa = ψ(b(g)), using inverse Frobenius in k. As [J] acts by −1 and p(J)=1, this χ satisfies χ(J)=−1, precisely the required sign. Removing the remaining ℓ-part from the complement · Eigenvectors and inverse Frobenius

Taking the ℓa-th power of the coefficient sum for Θ raises each term to that power in characteristic ℓ. On an eigenvector the one-term identity is [g]ℓav = [p(g)]ℓaψ(b(g))v. Thus the result on v equals the ℓa-th power of the cyclic twist Φ acting on v. Since Θ itself is zero in this quotient, that power of Φ kills v. Raising its divisibility relation to the same power shows that Tℓd+a kills v. Character orthogonality recovers the identity. Summing the character projectors before normalization gives |D| times 1, and |D| is invertible in k. Hence this power of T is itself zero in the quotient. Lift back to ℤ. Put N = ℓd+a. Write the relation over k as TN = uΘ + v(1+[J]). Choose an 𝔽ℓ-linear map k → 𝔽ℓ fixing 1. Apply it to every coefficient of the equation. Linearity suffices because the factors Θ and 1+[J] have integral coefficients. Lift the two multipliers from 𝔽ℓ to ℤ. Every coefficient of the remaining difference is divisible by ℓ, giving the third generator of the ideal. Both d and a were fixed before the source level n. In the dyadic example, f = 3 gives φ(3·4) = 4 and a = 2. Taking the sufficient depth d = 7 from the difference calculation gives N = 2⁹ = 512 for every n ≥ 7. The calculation modulo 8 tests a single character at a smaller depth. The exponent 512 uses a depth that works for every allowed character in every later layer.
