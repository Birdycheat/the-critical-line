How elliptic torsion puts a unit coefficient within degree p²

Why must the reduction of e(T) = [p](T) have a nonzero term by degree p²? Fix the good elliptic model above, with p ≥ 5. Its formal group law gives the integral series e by adding a point to itself p times. Its constant term is zero. To bound the degree, represent the curve by the corresponding complex torus ℂ/Λ. Its p-torsion is (p−1Λ)/Λ, with p choices in each of the two lattice directions. Take one representative P of each of its p² − 1 nonzero points. Write xP for its Weierstrass ℘-value transported into ℂp by the chosen field isomorphism. The representatives P and −P each contribute a factor. For each factor, the maximum coefficient norm is max(1, |xP|p). It is first attained in degree 0 if |xP|p ≤ 1, and degree 2 otherwise. In a product the first degrees attaining the maxima add. At their sum the product of the first maximal coefficients is the unique summand of largest norm. Thus G attains its maximum coefficient norm M = |p²|p ∏P max(1, |xP|p) by degree 2(p² − 1). What forces that maximum to equal one? Choose a nonzero half-period and let h be its transported ℘-value. It solves 4h³ − g₂h − g₃ = 0. Good reduction makes h integral and 3h² − g₂/4 a unit, since the reduced cubic has distinct roots. The torsion product identity at the half-period gives |p²|p ∏P|h − xP|p = 1. Each factor is bounded by max(1, |xP|p), so 1 ≤ M. Now reduce the squared multiplication formula modulo the maximal ideal. Multiplication by U preserves the first nonzero degree, and T² adds two. Hence the first nonzero degree of the reduced e² is at most 2(p² − 1) + 2 = 2p². Squaring doubles the first degree of a nonzero series over a field, giving the claimed bound. The two descents at an inert CM prime separately force that first degree to be a positive multiple of p². Together the arguments identify it exactly. A good curve whose first unit occurs before degree 25

At p = 5 take y² = x³ − x. Its discriminant 64 is a unit. Write x = u−2 and y = −u−3√(1 − u⁴), taking the square root with constant term 1. Then T = u + u⁵/2 + O(u⁹) and the invariant differential gives dλ = dx/(2y) = (1 + u⁴/2 + O(u⁸)) du. Integration and substitution give λ(T) = T − 2T⁵/5 + O(T⁹). Write [5](T) = 5T + aT⁵ + O(T⁹). Comparing degree five in λ([5](T)) = 5λ(T) gives a = 2 · 5⁴ − 2 = 1248 ≡ 3 modulo 5. Here the first unit occurs at degree 5. The inert curve at the same prime reaches degree 25.
