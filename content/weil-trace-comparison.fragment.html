How can one test an algebraic trace with unramified scalars?

A local Weil trace takes values in the algebraic closure Ω of ℚℓ. The crystalline period ring contains the maximal unramified extension F of ℚℓ. To compare the traces inside that ring, we will apply an F-linear functional λ ∶ Ω → F. A single calculation involves only finitely many errors. We need λ to keep those errors small and still detect a specified nonzero difference.

Finite-set lemma. Give Ω its ℓ-adic absolute value, with |ℓ| = ℓ−1. Suppose x₁, …, xn and z all have absolute value at most one. There is an F-linear functional such that

|λ(xi)| ≤ 1   (1 ≤ i ≤ n),   |z| ≤ |λ(z)| ≤ 1.

First construct a linearly independent family of integral elements ej in whose span every listed element has coordinates of absolute value at most one. Process the list, including z, one element at a time. An element outside the current span becomes a new basis vector. For an element y = ∑ ajej already in the span, keep the basis if all its coefficients are integral. Otherwise choose t for which |at| is largest and replace et by y. We can solve for the removed vector since at ≠ 0, so the new family is a basis of the same span. The coordinates of a previously listed element change by

c′t = ct/at,   c′j = cj − ctaj/at   (j ≠ t).

Here |at| > 1 and |aj/at| ≤ 1. The ultrametric inequality keeps every old coordinate integral. The new element y has coordinate 1 in its own position. For instance, if the first listed element is 3 and the second is 1 in ℚ₃, the initial basis (3) gives 1 the coordinate 1/3. Exchanging 3 for 1 gives the integral coordinates 3 and 1.

If z = 0 take λ = 0. Otherwise write z = ∑ bjej. Since |ej| ≤ 1, at least one coefficient has |bt| ≥ |z|. Project onto that coordinate and extend the functional linearly to Ω by completing the independent family to a basis. This proves the lemma. Its bounds hold on the prescribed finite list, which is allowed to change with the precision. Stacks Project, §11.2, on the basis-extension argument.

Where do the three errors in a Weil trace come from?

Fix a local Weil element σ. Let p denote the trace at σ of the target Weil representation, transported to Ω, and θ the scalar predicted by the joint approximation. We shall compare them at a chosen precision M. Write R = Acris, B = Bcris, and F for the maximal unramified extension of ℚℓ in Ω. For an F-linear functional λ ∶ Ω → F, write L(x) for the image of λ(x) under the embedding F → B. This is how algebraic trace coefficients enter the period calculation.

In the target matrix lattice let e be the coordinate vector of the identity and ti the ordinary matrix trace of its ith basis element. At one finite layer the corresponding vector is e′. The jth basis element there is a tuple of matrices, and t′jk is the trace of its component in auxiliary representation k. The Weil trace of representation k at σ is pk, and its weight in the chosen approximation is ck. The projection Π sends layer coordinates to target coordinates.

The construction on a frame gives integral square matrices V on the target and V′ on the layer and a common integral scalar s whose image is invertible in B. The rectangular comparison selects a layer on which the operators commute with Π modulo ℓM. The coordinate trace formula applied to that layer gives both identities below, for every λ. In formulas over B we also use s, V and V′ for their images.

sL(p) = ∑i (Ve)i L(ti),   sL(pk) = ∑j (V′e′)j L(t′jk).

The integers in Π allow a coefficient of Π to pass through L. The weights ck, however, may lie outside F. To weight the kth auxiliary identity, apply it with the functional x ↦ λ(ckx). Its right side then contains L(ckt′jk). For example, take ℓ = 3 and the ramified quadratic extension F(√3). If λ selects the constant coefficient in the basis (1, √3), then λ(√3) = 0 while λ(√3·√3) = 3. Moving a weight √3 outside this functional would lose that contribution.

Subtract the identities in three stages. The projection can miss the image of the identity vector by b = Πe′ − e. Set C = VΠ − ΠV′ and a = Ce′ − Vb. Expanding gives a = Ve − ΠV′e′. This first error is divisible by ℓM when both b and C are. The other two errors come directly from the approximation on basis traces and the additional scalar coordinate.

δj = ∑i Πijti − ∑k ckt′jk,   η = θ − ∑k ckpk.

Insert Ve = a + ΠV′e′ in the target identity. Interchanging the two finite sums pairs each component of V′e′ with ∑i Πijti. Subtract the weighted auxiliary identities and then subtract sL(η). This gives the exact equality in B

sL(p − θ) = ∑i aiL(ti) + ∑j (V′e′)jL(δj) − sL(η).

The matrix and identity-vector congruences bound the first term in R. The bounds |δj|, |η| ≤ ℓ−M refer to elements of Ω. The finite-set lemma converts them to divisibility in the same integral ring.

For a rank-one check, use ℓ = 3, M = 1 and s = 1. Take e = e′ = t = t′ = c = 1, Π = 4, V = 2, V′ = 5, and let λ fix these rational scalars. The two realized traces are p = 2 and p₁ = 5. For θ = 8 the three differences are a = 2 − 4·5 = −18, δ = 4 − 1 = 3 and η = 8 − 5 = 3. The formula gives −18 + 5·3 − 3 = −6, precisely p − θ. This one precision allows a nonzero difference. To force equality we need the same target trace and scalar s at arbitrarily high precision.

Why does agreement at arbitrarily high precision determine the trace?

Suppose the common scalar s and target matrix V give the period trace identities. The scalar s is integral and becomes a unit in Bcris. For each precision M, take a layer where Π intertwines the operators modulo ℓM, the identity vectors agree to that precision, and the basis and scalar approximations have errors of norm at most ℓ−M. The trace p, the proposed value θ and the finitely many target basis traces ti are independent of the layer.

Assume p ≠ θ. Fix an integer c₁ ≥ 0 making all ℓc₁ti and z = ℓc₁(p − θ) integral in Ω. Because |z| > 0 there is another integer c₂ ≥ 0 with ℓ−c₂ ≤ |z|. These two choices precede the choice of a layer.

At precision M, apply the finite-set lemma to z and to the list of ℓc₁ti, δj/ℓM and η/ℓM. All are integral. Put q = λ(z) ∈ F. The lemma makes the images of the list integral, with ℓ−c₂ ≤ |q| ≤ 1. Although λ and q can change with M, the same c₂ works at every precision.

An integral element of the maximal unramified extension F has an integral representative in R = Acris with the specified image in B = Bcris. Indeed it belongs to a finite unramified extension. Choose a residue-field basis over 𝔽ℓ and lift its members by Teichmüller representatives. These lifts form a ℤℓ-basis of the ring of integers. Reduction gives independence modulo ℓ, and subtracting the lifted digits successively gives an expansion by completeness. Each nonzero lift is a root of unity of order prime to ℓ and has a compatible sequence of ℓ-power roots, since raising to ℓ permutes that finite group. The corresponding Teichmüller elements in Ainf give the required lifts in R.

Multiply the three-error identity by ℓc₁. In its first sum, ai is divisible by ℓM in R and L(ℓc₁ti) has an integral representative. In the other two terms, the functionals on δj/ℓM and η/ℓM have integral representatives, and the entries of V′e′ and s are already integral. Thus s times the image of q equals the image of ℓMY for some Y ∈ R. Because |q| ≥ ℓ−c₂, the scalar ℓc₂/q has an integral lift y′ too. Multiply by its image and use the injection R → B to get

ℓc₂s = ℓMYy′   in Acris, for every M ≥ 0.

The right side can change with M. Its fixed left side belongs to every ℓMR, so ℓ-adic separatedness gives ℓc₂s = 0. The ring has no ℓ-torsion. Hence s = 0, contradicting the fact that its image in B is a unit. We conclude p = θ. To see why the choice of s must be fixed, consider the sequence sM = ℓM. It satisfies the indicated divisibility at each individual precision and every term is nonzero. Brinon and Conrad, §9.1 and Proposition 9.1.1, on the completed crystalline ring.

For the application, global trace approximation and the common filtration jumps furnish the crystalline frame on the target. For each σ, choose its signed exponent and form the common scalar before varying the layer. The measurement on the target and auxiliary representations identifies the quantities to which the argument applies. Repeating it at σn gives each of the power sums in the recovery of the local polynomial.
