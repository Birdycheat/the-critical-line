How do all the translated forms acquire a common level and bound?

In the sum defining the weight family, each term uses a form of the original weight W. Which term recovers the original form f depends on the adelic point. We construct a family of forms Ft, indexed by a compact group C, such that each is fixed by C and a single ℓ-adic bound applies to every one of them.

The common level. Let G be the definite unitary group of rank a ≥ 1 attached to the CM field E. Write Γ = G(ℚ) and G∞ = G(ℝ). As in the weight-variation theorem, let w₀ and v be split places above ℓ with distinct underlying primes in the real subfield. Denote the component at v by prv. The level K away from w₀ contains the full integral subgroup supported at v. Let Kv be open in GLa(Ev), and assume that f is fixed by K ∩ prv−1(Kv).

By smoothness, an identity neighbourhood at w₀ fixes f. Choose a torsion-free compact open subgroup J inside it by the congruence subgroup construction. Embed J with trivial components away from w₀ and put C = KJ. The two factors commute because they are supported at disjoint places. This gives a compact open level in the finite adeles. Put C′ = C ∩ prv−1(Kv). For c = kj in C′ the v-component is that of k. Hence k belongs to the initial level fixing f, and j also fixes f. Thus C′ fixes f.

The image prv(C) equals GLa(𝒪v). It is compact and contains this integral group by hypothesis. To see the reverse inclusion, use elementary divisors over the local field to write any matrix of the image as two integral invertible factors around a diagonal matrix of uniformizer powers. The diagonal matrix and all its positive and negative powers then lie in the compact image. If any diagonal exponent were nonzero, some sequence of these powers would have an unbounded entry. All exponents therefore vanish. This supplies the integral matrices and inverses required for the polynomial construction.

Reading the finite component from a double coset. Suppose γg = gcx, where γ ∈ Γ, c ∈ C and x ∈ G∞. The element γ lies in the intersection of Γ with a conjugate of the compact subgroup CG∞. This intersection is finite since Γ is closed and discrete in the adeles. Thus cx has finite order. Its w₀-component belongs to the torsion-free J, and consequently equals the identity. The same holds for the conjugate γ. A matrix over E equal to the identity in one completion is the identity over E, so γ = 1. Taking finite components of cx = 1 gives c = 1. This is the stabilizer condition of the equivariant label construction for the entire group C.

Choose a representative s for each double coset in Γ \ G(𝔸) / (CG∞). Write g = γskx and set c(g) = k, where k ∈ C and x ∈ G∞. Two decompositions using the same s give a stabilizer relation at s whose finite component is the quotient of their two k factors. The preceding argument makes that quotient trivial. With the representatives fixed, the label satisfies c(γg) = c(g), c(gx) = c(g) and c(gk) = c(g)k.

Translation to a prescribed label. Fix t ∈ C and send g to g c(g)−1t. This point has label t. For a weight vector u put

Ft(u, g) = f(u, g c(g)−1t).

Replacing g by gk cancels k against the first factor of c(gk)−1. Thus every Ft is fixed by C. It is rationally invariant because the label is. At the archimedean places, the finite correction c(g)−1t commutes with x ∈ G∞ and is unchanged by g ↦ gx. Hence Ft(u,gx) = Ft(W(x)u,g). This gives archimedean continuity and places the archimedean translates in the image of the finite-dimensional weight space. Together with the open finite level and rational invariance these are the defining conditions for an automorphic form of weight W on this definite group.

If t−1c(g) ∈ C′, then its inverse also lies in C′ and fixes f on the right. This gives Ft(u,g) = f(u,g) for every u. In particular, this equality holds when t represents the polynomial piece containing c(g), since each such piece is a left coset of a subgroup of C′.

One bound before choosing the pieces. Use the integral evaluation frame, with test vectors b(g,i). For every form F of weight W the frame gives a bound on |F(b(g,i),g)|ℓ uniform in g and i but possibly dependent on F. To bound all Ft, choose a compact open finite level C₀ fixing the original form. Such a level exists by smoothness, taking a common stabilizer of a basis of the weight space inside a compact open subgroup. Set L = C ∩ C₀. This is open in the compact group C, so the left cosets tL form a finite set. For h ∈ L, right invariance of f gives Fth = Ft. Choose representatives tj and frame bounds Rj for their forms. Then

|Ft(b(g,i),g)|ℓ ≤ R,   R = 1 + ∑j |Rj|.

The cosets of L give a positive R covering every t in C. The subsequent polynomial construction chooses its cosets inside C′ using the tolerance obtained from R. We can therefore prescribe η = min(1, ε/R) before choosing the cosets used for the polynomial approximation. At each point, the error contributed by the selected piece and the contribution of every other piece have absolute value at most Rη ≤ ε.
