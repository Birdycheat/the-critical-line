From formal contractions to a valuation of the lift

Why does Δ control an actual Taylor coefficient? For a fixed frame monomial Xm, assign a multi-index κ of derivatives to H in the product rule. The rest must differentiate matching − and + coordinates, with h derivatives of each sign at a coordinate that occurs h times in the exponential. Its coefficient has a denominator h!, while the two derivative factorials give (h!)². One h! remains in the numerator. Let nt count the matched pairs at label t. After extracting the derivative of H and the factor ∏t(−Sut)nt, the remaining coefficient A is an integer, a signed sum of binomial choices and the surviving factorials. In the symbol polynomial its exact identity is

(∏t nt!) A = [XmZκ] (∏t Δtnt) G.

The brackets extract a monomial coefficient. The factorial on the left counts the possible orders of the paired differentiations at each label. If one nt exceeds its cap, commuting the operators brings that power next to G. The right side vanishes, so A is zero in ℤ. For every surviving term |A|p ≤ 1, the derivative of H is integral, and the absolute value of the remaining scalar is at most p(∑t Ct)/2. The ultrametric triangle inequality gives the same bound on the finite sum. With vp(p) = 1, every nonzero coefficient has valuation at least −½∑t Ct.
