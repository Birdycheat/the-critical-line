When do the two coefficient-kernel formulas survive on a module?

Let q be a rational prime, C a commutative ring and k a field of characteristic q. Write B = C/qC and S = k ⊗ℤ C. Suppose the classes of e, a and d satisfy the two annihilator formulas in B and the coefficient identities e² = e, ed = d and ad = 0. If dx = 0, then x = au + (1 − e)v for some u, v in B. If ax = 0, then ex = du for some u in B.

Projective module lemma. For every projective S-module M, these relations determine the two indicated submodules. Each coefficient acts through its image 1 ⊗ c in S.

ker(d ∣ M) = aM + (1 − e)M,
e ker(a ∣ M) = dM.

Proof. First pass from B to S. Since q vanishes in k, there is a natural identification S = k ⊗𝔽q B. Choose a basis of k over 𝔽q. Every element of S is a unique finite sum ∑ βj ⊗ xj in that basis. Multiplication by d applies to each xj. If the result is zero, the ring formula writes each coefficient as auj + (1 − e)vj. Summing supplies the required two elements of S. With a in place of d, apply the second ring formula to each coefficient to get ex = du in S.

Next embed M into a free S-module by a map j with an S-linear retraction π, so πj is the identity. Such maps exist by projectivity. If dx = 0 in M, all coordinates of jx are killed by d. Apply the formulas over S on its finite support, using zero witnesses elsewhere. This gives jx = au + (1 − e)v in the free module. Applying π gives the desired expression in M. The same coordinate argument applied to ax = 0 gives ex = dπ(u). For the reverse inclusions, d kills aM and (1 − e)M. Each dy is killed by a and fixed by e. The Stacks Project, Lemma 10.77.2, projective modules as summands of free modules.

A quotient where both formulas fail. Take the ramified local factor S = 𝔽₂[η]/(η²), with e = 1 and a = d = η. On S, multiplying u + vη by η gives uη, so both its image and kernel are (η). On the quotient M = S/(η), the same multiplication has image zero and kernel all of M. The loss of flatness is visible in the injection (η) ↪ S. Tensoring with M sends the nonzero class of η in (η)/(η)² to zero in M.

Why do the coefficient-kernel formulas hold on primitive de Rham cohomology?

Let A be the abelian variety at a geometric point of the unitary moduli problem, with algebra degree m = 1 and level N ≥ 3 prime to q. Its field k is algebraically closed of characteristic q. The labelled coefficient map χ ∶ C = 𝒪F → k has kernel 𝔴. Here r = v𝔴(q) determines the local power d = ear−1, and the residue degree f gives |C/𝔴| = qf. Write P for primitive first de Rham cohomology and H = P∨ for first de Rham homology. A coefficient c acts by pullback ρ(c) on P. Its action on H sends a functional ℓ to ℓ ∘ ρ(c).

In degree one every de Rham class on A is primitive. Indeed, the Künneth formula identifies a class on A × A by its restrictions to the two coordinate axes, since H0dR(A) = k. The group law restricts to the identity on each axis. Its pullback of a class therefore equals the sum of the two projection pullbacks. This identifies the cohomology in the crystalline comparison below with P. The Stacks Project, Lemma 50.8.2, de Rham Künneth formula.

Cohomological annihilator lemma. Put E = ρ(e), U = ρ(a) and D = ρ(d). Then the two kernel identities hold on P.

ker D = UP + (1 − E)P,   E(ker U) = DP.

The geometric input. First de Rham homology with its coefficient action is projective over S = k ⊗ℤ 𝒪F. Here is how the arithmetic in this theorem handles ramification. Over the perfect field k, the lift Λ = W(k)/(q²) from the Witt vectors has residue field k and ker(q ∣ Λ) = qΛ. The crystalline module of the abelian variety gives a finite free Λ-module T with a coefficient action and an equivariant identification T/qT ≅ H. One may also obtain this lift by taking the Λ-dual of the Dieudonné module of A[q∞] reduced modulo q². Its reduction in degree one is functorial for endomorphisms. These crystalline freeness and comparison statements are the geometric inputs. Chai and Oort, Theorem 4.33 and Remark 4.34 on the Dieudonné module of a divisible group. Oda, The first de Rham cohomology group and Dieudonné modules, introduction and Corollary 5.11.

How the lift gives projectivity over the coefficient ring

At every prime of C above q, choose a local uniformizer. By the Chinese remainder theorem modulo the prime powers in (q²), choose t and z in C agreeing locally with the uniformizer and q divided by that uniformizer. Thus tz ≡ q modulo q². The reduction of t is nilpotent modulo q. Killing it leaves a product of finite residue fields, one for each prime over q. They are separable over 𝔽q, so after extending scalars to k this quotient is a product of fields. In each local factor S′ of the Artinian ring S the maximal ideal is therefore generated by t. The Stacks Project, Lemma 10.15.4, Chinese remainders.

Suppose tx = 0 in H and lift x to u in T. Then tu = qv for some v in T. Since tz acts as q on T, we get t(u − zv) = 0, hence q(u − zv) = 0 by multiplying by z. Freeness over Λ gives u − zv ∈ qT. Reducing shows x = z·(v mod qT), with tz = 0 in S.

Pass to a local factor and write H′ for its summand. The ideal (t) is the quotient of S′ by the annihilator of t, via multiplication by t. Thus the map (t) ⊗S′ H′ → H′ sends the class of x modulo Ann(t)H′ to tx. The preceding calculation gives injectivity. The exact sequence of the maximal ideal now gives Tor₁S′(S′/(t), H′) = 0. The local criterion for flatness applies because S′ is Noetherian and H′ is finite. Finite flat modules over this Artinian local ring are free. Taking the product of these local summands gives projectivity over S. The Stacks Project, Lemma 10.99.7, local criterion for flatness and Lemma 10.78.2, finite projective modules.

Transferring the two identities to P. Denote transpose on H by a superscript ∗. The projective module lemma applies to E∗, U∗ and D∗. To prove that a vector belongs to a subspace, we test it against all functionals vanishing there. This test works because any nonzero vector in the quotient can be extended to a basis and detected by a coordinate functional.

Take x ∈ ker D. If ℓ vanishes on UP + (1 − E)P, then U∗ℓ = 0 and ℓ = E∗ℓ. The second identity on H writes E∗ℓ = D∗μ for some μ. Therefore ℓ(x) = μ(Dx) = 0. This proves the first inclusion on P.

To test Ex for x ∈ ker U, take ℓ vanishing on DP. Now D∗ℓ = 0, so the first identity on H writes ℓ = U∗μ + (1 − E)∗ν. Evaluating at Ex gives zero because UEx = EUx = 0 and (1 − E)E = 0. Hence Ex ∈ DP. The reverse inclusions follow from UD = 0 and ED = D.

The first identity supplies the kernel estimate used to find the separating eigenvector. The second supplies a preimage under D for a projected Hodge vector killed by U. Continuing that preimage through the successive Frobenius maps uses a further fact. The simultaneous generalized eigenspaces of χ and of c ↦ χ(c)qj have equal dimensions for every j ≥ 0. The geometric proof compares the characteristic polynomials of the coefficient action on de Rham and prime-to-q Tate modules. Projectivity alone allows different ranks on distinct local factors.
