The correcting series φ and its congruence under formal negation

How does the correcting series φ(T) translate the analytic sigma product into a factorization in the local curve parameter T = −x/y? Let W be a Weierstrass equation over a characteristic-zero field K, with standard invariant b₂ = a₁² + 4a₂. In the formal uniformization variable z, the shifted coordinate series ℘reg,W(z) = xZ(z) + b₂/12 removes the constant Laurent term, leaving z−2 + P(z) with P(0) = 0. Write S(z) for the negative double formal integral of P(z) normalized by S(0) = S′(0) = 0, so that S″(z) = −P(z). For any scalar s₂ ∈ K, the normalized formal sigma series is σ*(z) = z exp(S(z) − ½s₂z²). If l(T) = T lU(T) with lU(0) = 1 denotes the formal logarithm of W in the parameter T, substituting z = l(T) factors out T times a unit exponential:

σ*(l(T)) = T exp(−φ(T)),   φ(T) = −S(l(T)) + ½s₂l(T)² − log lU(T).

Because S(0) = 0, l(0) = 0 and log lU(0) = 0, the correcting series satisfies φ(0) = 0. Fix a subring R ⊆ K. Two formal power series a, b over K with zero constant term are congruent modulo integral exponentials, written a ≡ b, when exp(a − b) has all coefficients in R. Since exp(a − b) then has constant term 1, its inverse exp(b − a) also has coefficients in R; Cauchy multiplication and formal composition make the zero-constant series congruent to 0 an additive subgroup preserved under substitution of zero-constant series with coefficients in R.

Assume now that the subring R ⊆ K contains all Weierstrass coefficients of W and ½ ∈ R, while s₂ ∈ K remains arbitrary. Write [−1]l(T) = l−1(−l(T)) for formal negation on W, and put q−1(T) = φ([−1]l(T)). Then q−1 ≡ φ. This congruence is proved as the instance α = ᾱ = −1, with empty nonzero kernel representative set, of the unified norm theorem for an integral endomorphism datum. Because W has coefficients in R, formal negation is an integral power series of the form [−1]l(T) = −T v(T) with v(T) ∈ 1 + TR[[T]]. Since P(z) is even, both S(z) and ½s₂z² are even, so σ*(−z) = −σ*(z). Evaluating at z = l(T), using l([−1]l(T)) = −l(T), and squaring both sides gives

([−1]l(T))² exp(−2q−1(T)) = T² exp(−2φ(T)).

Substituting [−1]l(T) = −T v(T) and cancelling T² in K[[T]] yields exp(−2(q−1 − φ)) = v(T)−2 ∈ 1 + TR[[T]], and hence exp(2(q−1 − φ)) = v(T)² ∈ 1 + TR[[T]]. For any series g with constant term 1, the identity g − 1 = ½(g² − 1 − (g − 1)²) expresses each positive-degree coefficient of g − 1 in terms of ½ ∈ R, the corresponding coefficient of g², and products of strictly lower positive-degree coefficients of g − 1; strong induction on degree therefore shows that g² ∈ 1 + TR[[T]] implies g ∈ 1 + TR[[T]]. Applying this square-root step to g = exp(q−1 − φ) places exp(q−1 − φ) in 1 + TR[[T]], proving q−1 ≡ φ; the same halving step handles simultaneously every integral endomorphism of unit norm once the squared sigma product supplies integrality of the doubled exponential.

The formal parallelogram and biadditivity of the addition defect

Taken individually, φ(T) acquires arbitrarily large rational denominators from both the double integration in S and the formal logarithm. Why does its addition defect βφ(X, Y) = φ(F(X, Y)) − φ(X) − φ(Y) under the formal group law F(X, Y) = l−1(l(X) + l(Y)) nevertheless become additive in each variable modulo integral exponentials? The formal sigma addition identity turns the two-variable parallelogram combination of φ into the logarithm of the integral one-unit governing chord intersection on the Weierstrass cubic, cancelling every polar and logarithmic denominator at once, and the invertibility of 2 then upgrades that two-variable parallelogram congruence into the three-variable cocycle of the cube.

Let K be a characteristic-zero ultrametric valued field whose closed unit ball R = {a ∈ K : |a| ≤ 1} satisfies |2| = 1, and let W be a Weierstrass equation with coefficients in R and arbitrary scalar s₂ ∈ K. For every indexing set of formal variables and all zero-constant power series x, x′, y with coefficients in R,

βφ(F(x, x′), y) ≡ βφ(x, y) + βφ(x′, y).

1. Formal sigma parallelogram via the wave equation. In two independent uniformization variables u, v, compare the parallelogram series S(u + v) + S(u − v) − 2S(u) − 2S(v) with log Q(u, v), where clearing the poles sets D(u, v) = −u²v²(℘reg,W(u) − ℘reg,W(v)) = u² − v² + u²v²(P(v) − P(u)), and evenness of P factors P(u) − P(v) = (u² − v²) RP(u, v) so that D(u, v) = (u² − v²) Q(u, v) with Q(u, v) = 1 − u²v² RP(u, v) ∈ 1 + (u, v)K[[u, v]]. The one-dimensional wave operator ∂u² − ∂v² annihilates every formal series in u + v or u − v, and since S″ = −P it sends the parallelogram of S to 2P(u) − 2P(v). On the other side, applying the cleared logarithmic wave operator L(H) = H(∂u² − ∂v²)H − ((∂uH)² − (∂vH)²) to D(u, v) and substituting the cleared reduced Weierstrass equations (℘reg,W′)² = 4℘reg,W³ − g₂℘reg,W − g₃ and ℘reg,W″ = 6℘reg,W² − ½g₂ yields L(D) = (2P(u) − 2P(v))D²; since L((u² − v²)Q) = (u² − v²)²L(Q), cancelling (u² − v²)² gives L(Q) = (2P(u) − 2P(v))Q², hence (∂u² − ∂v²) log Q(u, v) = 2P(u) − 2P(v) as well. Both series vanish together with their first v-derivatives along v = 0, so uniqueness for the formal Cauchy problem in characteristic zero makes them equal. Exponentiating and noting that ½s₂z² cancels in (u + v)² + (u − v)² − 2u² − 2v² = 0 gives

σ*(u + v) σ*(u − v) = −σ*(u)² σ*(v)² (℘reg,W(u) − ℘reg,W(v)).

2. Cancellation of poles by chord factorization on the cubic. In two formal curve parameters X₀, X₁, the shift b₂/12 cancels in ℘reg,W(l(X₀)) − ℘reg,W(l(X₁)) = x(X₀) − x(X₁). Writing A(T) = T²x(T) ∈ 1 + TR[[T]] for the integral coordinate series of constant term 1 (which agrees with T²℘reg,W(l(T)) up to b₂T²/12 and therefore gives the identical cross-difference), chord intersection on the integral Weierstrass cubic factors the two-variable series in R[[X₀, X₁]] as

X₁²A(X₀) − X₀²A(X₁) = c(X₀, X₁) F(X₀, X₁) F(X₀, [−1]l(X₁)),

where c ∈ R[[X₀, X₁]] has constant term c(0, 0) = −1 by comparing quadratic terms X₁² − X₀² = −(X₀² − X₁²). Substitute u = l(X₀) and v = l(X₁) into the sigma parallelogram, so that u + v = l(F(X₀, X₁)) and u − v = l(F(X₀, [−1]l(X₁))), and expand each factor via σ*(l(T)) = T exp(−φ(T)). The factors X₀², X₁², F(X₀, X₁) and F(X₀, [−1]l(X₁)) cancel identically in the integral domain K[[X₀, X₁]], leaving Πφ(X₀, X₁) = φ(F(X₀, X₁)) + φ(F(X₀, [−1]l(X₁))) − 2φ(X₀) − 2φ(X₁) = −log(−c(X₀, X₁)). Since −c has coefficients in R and constant term 1, both its inverse exponential −c = exp(−Πφ(X₀, X₁)) and (−c)−1 = exp(Πφ(X₀, X₁)) have all coefficients in R, hence Πφ(X₀, X₁) ≡ 0.

3. Three-variable cube combination. Because |2| = 1 in K, the doubling series [2]l(T) = F(T, T) = 2T + O(T²) ∈ R[[T]] has a unit linear coefficient, so its compositional inverse [½]l(T) = l−1(½l(T)) has zero constant term and coefficients in R. In R[[X₀, X₁, X₂]], set B = [½]l(F(X₁, X₂)), C = [½]l(F(X₁, [−1]l(X₂))) and A = F(X₀, B), so that F(B, C) = X₁ and F(B, [−1]l(C)) = X₂.

How four instances of the parallelogram give the three-variable defect

Adding and subtracting formal logarithms identifies the eight shifted arguments: F(A, B) = F(F(X₀, X₁), X₂) and F(A, [−1]l(B)) = X₀; F(A, C) = F(X₀, X₁) and F(A, [−1]l(C)) = F(X₀, X₂); F(B, B) = F(X₁, X₂) and F(B, [−1]l(B)) = 0; and finally F(B, C) = X₁ and F(B, [−1]l(C)) = X₂.

Using φ(0) = 0, the signed combination Πφ(A, B) − Πφ(A, C) − Πφ(B, B) + Πφ(B, C) eliminates φ(A), φ(B) and φ(C) with weights −2 + 2 = 0, −2 + 4 − 2 = 0 and +2 − 2 = 0, leaving φ(F(F(X₀, X₁), X₂)) − φ(F(X₀, X₁)) − φ(F(X₀, X₂)) − φ(F(X₁, X₂)) + φ(X₀) + φ(X₁) + φ(X₂) = βφ(F(X₀, X₁), X₂) − βφ(X₀, X₂) − βφ(X₁, X₂).

Because A, B and C are zero-constant series over R, substituting (A, B), (A, C), (B, B) and (B, C) into Πφ ≡ 0 produces four series congruent to 0, whose signed sum proves βφ(F(X₀, X₁), X₂) ≡ βφ(X₀, X₂) + βφ(X₁, X₂) universally. Substituting any zero-constant R-integral series (x, x′, y) for (X₀, X₁, X₂) preserves the congruence and completes the proof; combined with q−1 ≡ φ, this yields the quadratic recurrence qm ≡ m²φ used in extracting the integral unit above.
