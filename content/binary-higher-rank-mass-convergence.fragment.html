Higher-rank finite-field quadric counts and local density bounds

The binary local mass calculation rested on a four-variable quadratic equation over a finite residue field and a two-step valuation recurrence in rank two. To feed the higher-rank Siegel–Weil induction and supply the uniform unit-length estimate across every Hermitian rank, both mechanisms must be carried from four variables to an arbitrary even dimension 2M. Over an odd-characteristic finite field, diagonalizing a nondegenerate symmetric form of even rank factors its quadratic Gauss sum into 2M one-variable character sums. Rescaling the quadratic form by any nonzero scalar multiplies each one-variable sum by the quadratic character of that scalar; because the number of variables is even, all 2M character factors multiply to one, leaving the Gauss sum independent of the nonzero scaling parameter and yielding exact square-root cancellation on every nonzero target.

Proposition (Sharp even-rank quadric count and local density bounds). Let k be a finite field of odd characteristic and cardinality q, let M ≥ 1 be an integer, and let B be a nonsingular symmetric 2M × 2M matrix over k defining Q(x) = ½ xtBx on k2M. For every nonzero s ∈ k×, the solution count N(s) = #{x ∈ k2M : Q(x) = s} satisfies

|N(s) − q2M−1| ≤ qM−1,

with equality. Now let v be a finite place of a number field F with |2|v = 1, ring of integers 𝒪v, uniformizer π, residue cardinality qv, and normalized additive Haar measure λv with λv(𝒪v) = 1. Assume B ∈ M2M(𝒪v) is symmetric with |det B|v = 1. For every rank M ≥ 1 and every local unit s ∈ 𝒪v×, there exists a constant D ≥ 0 such that

|D − 1| ≤ 2/qvM   and   λv2M({x ∈ 𝒪v2M : Q(x) ∈ A}) = D · λv(A)

for every measurable set A ⊆ {z ∈ Fv : |z − s|v < |s|v}. Applied to the trace form of a nondegenerate M × M Hermitian matrix J over a CM extension E/F with an F-basis b of E, this yields at all but finitely many finite places v the local Gram-fibre mass bound |mv(s)(Λv) − 1| ≤ 2/qvM for every unit s ∈ 𝒪v× on the standard coordinate lattice Λv ⊂ (Fv ⊗F E)1×M, and in particular |mv(s)(Λv) − 1| ≤ 2/qv2 in the binary case M = 2. Furthermore, for every higher rank M ≥ 3 and every nonzero integral target s ∈ 𝒪v ∖ {0}, there exists a constant D ≥ 0 satisfying

|D − 1| ≤ 2/qvM−1   and   λv2M({x ∈ 𝒪v2M : Q(x) ∈ A}) = D · λv(A)

for every measurable set A ⊆ {z ∈ Fv : |z − s|v < |s|v}.

Proof of the higher-rank quadric count and local density bounds

Even-dimensional Gauss sum invariance. Choose a nontrivial additive character ψ : k → ℂ× and let χ be the quadratic character of k extended by χ(0) = 0. Because char k ≠ 2, each element u ∈ k has 1 + χ(u) square roots in k. Grouping by u = y2 and using ∑u∈k ψ(au) = 0 for a ≠ 0 yields ∑y∈k ψ(ay2) = ∑u∈k χ(u) ψ(au). Replacing a by ta for t ∈ k× and substituting u ↦ tu with χ(t)2 = 1 gives ∑y ψ(tay2) = χ(t) ∑y ψ(ay2). Since B is symmetric and invertible in odd characteristic, its bilinear form admits an orthogonal basis v1, …, v2M with all diagonal entries wi = vitBvi nonzero. Expanding in this basis factors G(t) = ∑x∈k2M ψ(tQ(x)) = ∏i=12M ∑y∈k ψ(½ twiy2) = χ(t)2M G(1) = G(1) for every t ∈ k×.

Sharp finite-field point count. Character orthogonality gives qN(s) = q2M + ∑t≠0 ψ(−ts) G(t) = q2M + G(1) ∑t≠0 ψ(−ts). Because s ≠ 0, the character sum over t ≠ 0 equals −1, whence qN(s) − q2M = −G(1). Since |G(1)| = qM (as verified in the residue-count argument by squaring the modulus and translating), taking absolute values and dividing by q gives |N(s) − q2M−1| = qM−1.

Unit-length primitive lift. At a finite place v with |2|v = 1 and unimodular integral symmetric B, the residue field kv has odd characteristic and cardinality qv, and the reduction B̄ modulo π is symmetric and nonsingular. For a unit s ∈ 𝒪v×, let Nf be the solution count of Q̄(y) = s̄ in kv2M and N ≤ Nf ≤ N + 1 the count of nonzero residue solutions (for a unit target, 0 is not a solution so N = Nf, and keeping the uniform inequality N ≤ Nf ≤ N + 1 gives |N qv−(2M−1) − 1| ≤ 1/qvM + 1/qv2M−1 ≤ 2/qvM since 2M − 1 ≥ M for M ≥ 1). Partitioning the primitive vectors {x ∈ 𝒪v2M : ∃i, |xi|v = 1} into pairwise disjoint residue classes a + π𝒪v2M over a ∈ kv2M ∖ {0}, Hensel lifting assigns measure qv−(2M−1) λv(A) to each class with Q̄(a) = s̄ and measure 0 to all other classes whenever A ⊆ s + π𝒪v. Summing over the N contributing classes produces the primitive density D = N qv−(2M−1). Moreover, any non-primitive integral vector lies in π𝒪v2M and satisfies |Q(x)|v ≤ qv−2 < 1 = |s|v, so no non-primitive vector can enter A ⊆ {z : |z − s|v < |s|v}. Thus the full integral pushforward has the same constant density D with |D − 1| ≤ 2/qvM. For a Hermitian form J of rank M over a CM extension E/F of degree 2 over F, its trace-form matrix B in an F-basis b of E has size 2M × 2M and nonzero determinant; excluding the finitely many places where 2, det B, or an entry of B fails integrality or unitness matches the pushforward density with the local Gram-fibre mass mv(s)(Λv). Setting M = 2 delivers the binary unit-length bound 2/qv2 used in the low-rank Siegel densities assembly.

Strong valuation induction for higher ranks. Now assume M ≥ 3 and let s ∈ 𝒪v ∖ {0} have valuation k = ordv(s) ≥ 0. We induct strongly on n ≥ k in steps of two. Split the preimage of A ⊆ {z : |z − s|v < |s|v} in 𝒪v2M into its primitive and non-primitive parts; the primitive set is measurable as a finite union of open unit spheres. The general primitive-density bound supplies a constant Dp ≥ 0 with |Dp − 1| ≤ 1/qvM−1 + 1/qv2M−1 on all measurable A ⊆ s + π𝒪v. If k ≤ 1, then every z ∈ A satisfies |z|v = |s|v ≥ qv−1, whereas every non-primitive vector x = πy has |Q(x)|v = |π2 Q(y)|v ≤ qv−2; thus the non-primitive preimage is empty and D = Dp satisfies |D − 1| ≤ 1/qvM−1 + 1/qv2M−1 ≤ 2/qvM−1. If k ≥ 2, write s = π2 s′ with s′ ∈ 𝒪v ∖ {0} and ordv(s′) = k − 2 ≤ n − 2. The induction hypothesis yields a constant D′ ≥ 0 with |D′ − 1| ≤ 2/qvM−1 on the scaled target A′ = {z : π2 z ∈ A} ⊆ {z : |z − s′|v < |s′|v}. Because multiplication by π scales λv2M in the inverse-image direction by qv2M while multiplication by π2 scales λv in the inverse-image direction by qv2, the non-primitive preimage has measure (qv2/qv2M) D′ λv(A) = qv−(2M−2) D′ λv(A). Setting u = qv−(M−1) and r = qv−1, the hypothesis M ≥ 3 and qv ≥ 2 gives u ≤ 1/4, r ≤ 1/2, and D = Dp + u2 D′. Consequently D − 1 ≤ (u + u2 r) + u2(1 + 2u) ≤ u + 2u2 ≤ (3/2)u ≤ 2u and D − 1 ≥ −(u + u2 r) + u2(1 − 2u) = −u + u2(1 − r − 2u) ≥ −u ≥ −2u, completing the induction.

Worked check in rank three. Take M = 3 and q = 3 with Q(x) = x12 + … + x62 over ℤ3 (corresponding to B = 2I6, unimodular at 3). Modulo 3 each coordinate square is 0 once and 1 twice. Representing 1 requires 1 or 4 nonzero coordinates, giving N(1) = 6·2 + 15·16 = 252, so N(1) − 35 = 252 − 243 = 9 = 32 = qM−1, and the unit density is D(1) = 252/243 = 28/27 with |D(1) − 1| = 1/27 ≤ 2/27. Representing 0 primitively requires 3 or 6 nonzero coordinates, giving N×(0) = 20·8 + 1·64 = 224 and Dp(9) = 224/243 (note 1 + 224 + 252 + 252 = 729 = 36). At target s = 9 = 32·1, the non-primitive shell scales by 32/36 = 1/81, yielding D(9) = 224/243 + (1/81)(28/27) = 2044/2187, whose deviation 143/2187 lies strictly inside 2/9 = 486/2187.

Global product compatibility, Euler convergence and higher-rank induction

Local mass estimates become a global product formula only once three structural bridges are in place: first, every unitary-invariant global measure supported on a nondegenerate Gram fibre must factor into local Gram-fibre measures on every finite set of places; second, the local deviations |mv(T)(Λv) − 1| for an arbitrary invertible n × n target T must decay quadratically in qv so that the infinite Euler product converges absolutely; and third, adding a rank-one orthogonal block must preserve the exact global normalization constant 1 while propagating any local representation obstruction to zero. Alongside these global structural theorems, the binary scalar average requires a combinatorial powerset sieve coupling unit and nonunit local bounds to product-ideal counts, and the low-rank theta-Eisenstein comparison requires extending Euler-product integration from compactly supported functions to integrable archimedean tests.

Theorem (Global product compatibility, higher-rank convergence, block induction and Euler integrability). Let E/F be a CM extension over a totally real number field F with conjugation σ and F-basis b, let J be a nondegenerate M × M Hermitian matrix over E, and let T be an invertible n × n Hermitian matrix over E. Equip 𝔸F with standard local additive Haar measures λ∞ and λv and let m∞, mv be the associated local Gram-fibre families.

Finite-place orbit factorization. Let I be any locally finite U(J, 𝔸F)-invariant Borel measure on (𝔸F ⊗F E)n×M supported on {X : gram(X) = T}. For every finite set S of finite places, there exists a constant cS ≥ 0 such that for all measurable nonnegative functions φ∞ on (A∞ ⊗F E)n×M and φv on (Fv ⊗F E)n×M (v ∈ S),

∫∀v∉S, Xv∈Λv φ∞(X∞) ∏v∈S φv(Xv) dI(X) = cS · (∫ φ∞ dm∞(T)) · ∏v∈S (∫ φv dmv(T)).

More generally, for any finite product of continuous group actions whose local orbits are transitive and whose invariant measures are locally finite on open invariant subsets U0, Ui carrying full measure, the same scalar product factorization holds via pullback along the open embedding.

Higher-rank local mass convergence. Whenever n = 0 or n + 2 ≤ M, every local lattice volume mv(T)(Λv) is finite, and there exists an explicit constant Cn = 3n − 1 such that at all but finitely many finite places v,

|mv(T)(Λv) − 1| ≤ Cn / qv2,   whence   ∑v |mv(T)(Λv) − 1| < ∞.

Rank-one block induction and anisotropic vanishing. Suppose n + 2 ≤ M and t ∈ F×. If the global Siegel–Weil measure identity (with Euler-product constant 1) holds for (J, T), then it holds for the block-extended pair (diag(t, J), diag(t, T)): for every sufficiently large finite set S and nonnegative measurable pure tests,

∫∀v∉S, Xv∈Λv φ∞(X∞) ∏v∈S φv(Xv) dθdiag(t,J), diag(t,T) = (∏v∉S mv(Λv)) · (∫ φ∞ dm∞) · ∏v∈S (∫ φv dmv).

Moreover, if n + 3 ≤ M and t ∈ F× is not represented globally by J, then the global Siegel–Weil measure identity holds for (J, diag(t, T)) with both sides equal to zero.

Powerset ideal-norm sieve and complex Euler integrability. Let K ⊂ A∞ be compact, N ≥ 1 an integer, and suppose nonzero scaled rational elements in N−1 𝔞 ∩ δ−1 K satisfy the ideal-norm count bound with constant C0. For any finite set S of places, any constant C ≥ 0, local functions gv with |gv(s) − 1| ≤ C/qv on 𝒪v ∖ {0} and |gv(s) − 1| ≤ C/qv2 on 𝒪v× for all v ∉ S, any δ > 0, any finite prime set D disjoint from S, and any finite sample B ⊂ F× of S-outside integral elements with NB ⊂ 𝒪F and δ B ⊂ K,

δ[F:ℚ] ∑x∈B ∏v∈D |gv(x) − 1| ≤ max(C0, 0) · ∏v∈D (2C/qv2).

Finally, if I is a constant-one Euler-product measure with locally finite local factors m∞, mv, if Φ∞ is continuous and m∞-integrable, and if Φf = ∏v Φv is a pure finite-adelic test (each Φv continuous of compact support, equal to 1Λv outside a finite set), then Φ = Φ∞ Φf is I-integrable and ∫ Φ dI = (∫ Φ∞ dm∞) · ∏v (∫ Φv dmv).

Proof of orbit factorization, higher-rank convergence, block induction, sieve and Euler integrability

Orbit factorization on closed and open regular loci. First consider a product of locally compact second-countable groups G0 × ∏i Gi acting continuously on W0 × ∏i Wi, preserving closed orbits Z0, Zi on which each factor acts transitively and carries a locally finite invariant measure m0, mi supported on Z0, Zi (nonzero whenever the orbit is nonempty). If the product orbit is empty, any invariant measure P supported on it vanishes and c = 0 works. Otherwise the product measure m0 × ∏i mi is nonzero, locally finite, invariant, and supported on the single transitive orbit Z0 × ∏i Zi, so uniqueness of invariant measures for proper transitive actions forces P = c · (m0 × ∏i mi) for a constant c ≥ 0, and Tonelli splits product integrals. When transitivity and local finiteness hold only on open invariant subsets U0, Ui carrying full measure, the inclusion E : U0 × ∏i Ui ↪ W0 × ∏i Wi is an open measurable embedding preserving local compactness and second countability; pulling P, m0, mi back along the open embedding preserves invariance, finiteness on compacts, non-vanishing, and integrals, so the closed-orbit result applies on the open subspace and pushes back with the same scalar c. For the adelic measure I and finite set S, let P be the pushforward of I restricted to {X : ∀v ∉ S, Xv ∈ Λv} under projection to W∞ × ∏v∈S Wv. Given any compact set K in the target, compactness yields positive integers Nv scaling each v-projection of K into Λv; multiplying by ∏v∈S Nv embeds the restricted preimage of K into a compact adelic tuple box, proving P is locally finite. Surjectivity of the adelic unitary projection onto U(J, A∞) × ∏v∈S U(J, Fv) with identity outside S makes P invariant under the finite-place product group. Local Witt extension over the Artinian rings A∞ and Fv supplies local transitivity on the nondegenerate T-fibre, and local Gram-fibre positivity provides non-vanishing and local finiteness, yielding the factorization for P.

Higher-rank local mass induction and Euler convergence. We prove |mv(T)(Λv) − 1| ≤ Cn/qv2 for all but finitely many places v by induction on n. For n = 0 the fibre measure is the unit point mass on the zero-tuple space, so mv(T)(Λv) = 1 and C0 = 0. For n + 1 with n + 3 ≤ M, write M = M1 + 1 (so M1 ≥ n + 2 ≥ 2 and M ≥ 3). Because σ is nontrivial on the CM extension E/F and det T ≠ 0, polarization forbids hT(x, x) from vanishing identically, so T represents some nonzero scalar t ∈ F× and splits by congruence as T ≃ diag(t, T1) with det T1 ≠ 0. Set J′ = diag(t, IM1). For all but finitely many v, congruence invariance replaces T by diag(t, T1), local isometry replaces J by J′, and block-one factorization splits mv,J′,n+1(diag(t, T1))(Λv) = mv,J′,1(t)(Λv) · mv,IM1,n(T1)(Λv). Since M = M1 + 1 ≥ 3, the higher-rank local density bound bounds the rank-one factor by 2/qvM1 with M1 ≥ 2, while the induction hypothesis bounds the rank-n factor by Cn/qv2. Writing e = 1/qv2 ≤ 1 and expanding xy − 1 = (x − 1)(y − 1) + (x − 1) + (y − 1) gives |xy − 1| ≤ (2e)(Cne) + 2e + Cne ≤ (3Cn + 2)e, yielding Cn+1 = 3Cn + 2 = 3n+1 − 1. Because each lattice Λv is compact and each mv(T) is locally finite, every individual volume mv(T)(Λv) is finite; comparison with the convergent prime-square sum ∑v qv−2 < ∞ yields ∑v |mv(T)(Λv) − 1| < ∞.

Rank-one block induction and anisotropic vanishing. Assume n + 2 ≤ M, t ∈ F×, and the global Siegel–Weil identity for (J, T) with local families m′∞, m′v. Fix a sufficiently large finite set S containing all bad places for (J, T), the rank-one space (J′ = diag(t, J), t), the scalar-multiplication scaling factors c∞, cv of y ↦ ty on Fw ⊗F E, and the local block-factorization identities. Powerset ideal-norm sieve and complex Euler integrability. For nonnegative errors e(v, x) ≤ av + 1P(v,x) bv with bv cv = av and subset counts w · #{x ∈ B : ∀v ∈ t, P(v, x)} ≤ C′ ∏v∈t cv for every t ⊆ D, expanding ∏v∈D (av + 1P(v,x) bv) over all 2|D| subsets t ⊆ D and exchanging sums yields w ∑x∈B ∏v∈D e(v, x) ≤ ∑t⊆D C′ (∏v∈t bv cv)(∏v∈D∖t av) = 2|D| C′ ∏v∈D av = C′ ∏v∈D (2av). Archimedean coordinates, Haar scaling and Schwartz fibre integrability

To feed an archimedean Schwartz test function φ into the complex Euler-product integration formula and complete the regular-fibre continuity assembly, one must transport both φ and the archimedean Gram-fibre measures mS into Euclidean space ℝN where Schwartz seminorms and dominated convergence live. Right multiplication by an invertible n × n matrix g over A∞ ⊗F E acts linearly on tuple coordinates and by congruence S ↦ g*Sg on Hermitian Gram matrices. Expressing this action in real coordinates and tracking the two continuous positive Haar Jacobian multipliers transforms the varying family of fibre measures mS near any nondegenerate Hermitian matrix into a smoothly scaled linear pushforward of a single fixed fibre measure.

Theorem (Archimedean real coordinates, Haar Jacobians and Schwartz fibre continuity). Let E/F be a CM extension over a totally real number field F with [E:F] = 2, let n, M ≥ 0 be integers, and set N = 2nM[F:ℚ].

Real coordinate intertwining. There exist an additive homeomorphism eV : (A∞ ⊗F E)n×M ≃t ℝN and a continuous map L : Mn(A∞ ⊗F E) → ℬ(ℝN, ℝN) into continuous real-linear operators such that

eV(Xg) = L(g)(eV(X))   for all X ∈ (A∞ ⊗F E)n×M and g ∈ Mn(A∞ ⊗F E).

(X ↦ Xg)* μ = A(g) · μ   and   (S ↦ g*Sg)* ν = C(g) · ν.

Schwartz decay, local congruence transport and low-rank closure. Every Schwartz function g ∈ 𝒮(ℝN, ℂ) satisfies, for every integer k ≥ 0,

|g(y)| ≤ Bk (1 + ‖y‖)−k   with   Bk = 2k supm≤(k,0) pm(g).

Consequently, for any archimedean Gram-fibre family mS disintegrating μ over ν along a nondegenerate M × M Hermitian form J, any archimedean Schwartz test φ = g ∘ eV, and any local continuous congruence section h : O → GLn(A∞ ⊗F E) with h(S)* s0 h(S) = S on an open neighborhood O of an invertible c ∈ Hermn(A∞ ⊗F E), the pushed-forward fibre measures obey (eV)* mS = C(h(S)) A(h(S))−1 · L(h(S))* ((eV)* ms0) with continuous invertible operators L(h(S)), L(h(S)−1) and continuous positive factor C(h(S)) A(h(S))−1. Feeding this coordinate transport into the archimedean fibre continuity assembly proves integrability of φ against mS and continuity of S ↦ ∫ φ dmS and S ↦ ∫ |φ| dmS on all nondegenerate S, closing the low-rank Siegel densities theorem together with the binary unit-length estimate and the anisotropic binary one-vector formula.

Proof of archimedean coordinates, Haar scaling and Schwartz fibre transport

Real coordinate homeomorphism and operator continuity. Because F is totally real, the archimedean adele ring admits a topological ring isomorphism A∞ ≃t ℝ[F:ℚ]. Fixing an F-basis of E of length d = [E:F] = 2 gives an A∞-linear topological isomorphism A∞ ⊗F E ≃t A∞d. Indexing coordinates by the finite product set I = {1, …, n} × {1, …, M} × {1, …, d} × {real places of F} of cardinality N = 2nM[F:ℚ] composes these identifications into an additive homeomorphism eV : (A∞ ⊗F E)n×M ≃t ℝN. For each g ∈ Mn(A∞ ⊗F E), right multiplication X ↦ Xg is additive and jointly continuous in (X, g); conjugating by eV therefore defines a continuous additive endomorphism of ℝN, which is automatically ℝ-linear and bounded, giving L(g) ∈ ℬ(ℝN, ℝN) with eV(Xg) = L(g)(eV(X)). Because every fixed y ∈ ℝN maps continuously under g ↦ eV(eV−1(y)g) and ℝN is finite-dimensional, g ↦ L(g) is continuous in the operator norm.

Strict positivity of the continuous Haar multipliers. Continuous scaling functions A, C : GLn(A∞ ⊗F E) → ℝ≥0 satisfying (X ↦ Xg)* μ = A(g) μ and (S ↦ g*Sg)* ν = C(g) ν arise directly from uniqueness of additive Haar measure on the finite-dimensional real spaces (A∞ ⊗F E)n×M and Hermn(A∞ ⊗F E). Both maps X ↦ Xg and τ(S) = g*Sg are continuous (hence measurable). If A(g) vanished for some invertible g, then the pushforward measure would vanish, forcing μ of the whole space to be zero and contradicting strict positivity of a Haar measure on nonempty open sets; the identical argument proves C(g) > 0.

Polynomial decay and local congruence transport.
