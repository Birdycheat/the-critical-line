Why do two lifts that avoid the ordinary line give the same reduced quotient?

Fix an ordinary valuation-ring point and write κ for its algebraically closed residue field of characteristic q. Write Q = qf for the order of the finite field acting on the torsion space H. Specializing its points gives a map ρ whose kernel is the ordinary line C. A section of the isogeny correspondence, labelled by a subspace D of H, specializes to an isogeny ψD ∶ A → BD of the abelian varieties in the moduli problem. We shall compare two such maps with the same source A. Write tD for the moduli point of the target.

Equality of quotient targets. Suppose D and D′ both meet C only in zero and have the same image U in H/C. Then tD = tD′. For every geometric point of the source abelian variety, the two isogenies also give the same image in the universal abelian variety over this target. These two equalities are needed when the correspondence acts on a tuple of points.

Which part of the kernel is counted? Let KD be the part of ker ψD killed by some power of the distinguished ideal in the split coefficient order. The kernel comparison for the integral isogeny gives two facts. Its coordinate algebra over κ has dimension Qdim D, and its κ-points identify with ρ(D). Indeed, the integral kernel is finite flat of the specified rank. Its points in the special fibre lift to valuation-ring points, and the torsion labels of those lifts are precisely the elements of D. The torsion and lifting argument gives this last passage. Reduction satisfies ρ(x) = ρ(y) exactly when x − y ∈ C. The image ρ(D) is therefore determined by U.

Set r = dim U and d = Qr. Since both chosen spaces avoid the line, each maps bijectively onto U. Both kernel parts have length d and the same d distinct points in A(κ). A finite κ-algebra is a product of its local Artinian factors. Each geometric point contributes a factor of dimension at least one. Here there are d factors and their dimensions sum to d, so every factor is κ. Consequently the two kernel parts are the same reduced closed subscheme of A. This argument is the reason the count of points suffices in this case. Stacks Project, decomposition of an Artinian ring into its localizations.

Recovering the whole quotient. The step from this part to the full kernel uses the coefficient action and polarization. For the polarized isogenies in question the polarization multiplier is the same, say qe, and a complementary isogeny makes the composite multiplication by qe in either order. On the resulting torsion the split coefficient action supplies a scalar α with α + ᾱ ≡ 1 modulo qe. Thus a point x in either kernel decomposes into αx + ᾱx. The first term belongs to the distinguished part. For the second, the polarization moves α across the pairing as ᾱ. To write the factorization, call the two isogenies ψ₁ and ψ₂, with targets B₁ and B₂, and denote their complementary maps by ψ₁′ and ψ₂′. Set c = qe. For y in B₂[c], ψ₂′(y) belongs to ker ψ₂. Its α-part belongs to the common distinguished kernel, so ψ₁ ∘ α ∘ ψ₂′ kills B₂[c]. Division through multiplication by c gives a map ν ∶ B₂ → B₁ with ψ₁ ∘ α ∘ ψ₂′ = ν ∘ [c]. Composing with ψ₁′ and cancelling [c] gives ψ₁′ ∘ ν = α ∘ ψ₂′. The adjoint identity now makes the image of ᾱx under ψ₂ pair trivially. Nondegeneracy up to an integer prime to q shows that this integer kills the image. The multiplier qe also kills it, so Bézout’s identity makes the image zero. The full-kernel lemma below records the hypotheses of this argument.

Full-kernel lemma for the polarized datum. For two isogenies of the prescribed kind with the same source, the same multiplier and the coefficient, polarization and level structures of the datum, equality of their distinguished kernels on every test scheme gives equality of their full kernels on every test scheme. The division lemma for abelian schemes supplies the divisibility by the common multiplier used in the adjoint calculation. Equal full kernels then identify the two quotient objects by a structure-preserving isomorphism that intertwines the quotient maps. These assertions use the polarized quotient and rigidity theorems of this moduli problem.

Apply the lemma to ψD and ψD′. The common moduli space represents quotient objects with these structures, so the identified objects give the same map from Spec κ. Uniqueness of their comparison with the universal object intertwines the two maps on points as well. For instance, if H = 𝔽₃² and C is the second coordinate line, the lines generated by (1, 0) and (1, 1) project to the same quotient line. The difference between these two chosen generators lies in C. Each distinguished kernel has length 3 and the same three reduced points. This is the arithmetic count used above, independent of a choice of splitting of H → H/C.

Why does a lift containing the line produce a Frobenius twist?

Keep the same ordinary point, and fix an r-dimensional subspace U of H/C. Choose a lift D₀ missing C, and let D₁ be the full inverse image of U. Write ψ₀ ∶ A → B₀ and ψ₁ ∶ A → B₁ for the specialized isogenies. Both distinguished kernels have the same d = Qr geometric points. Their lengths are d and Qd, respectively. The extra length is the information lost by counting only the geometric points.

Frobenius comparison. Put φ(a) = aQ on κ. Write B₀(Q) for the pullback of B₀ along Spec φ and Frel ∶ B₀ → B₀(Q) for relative Frobenius. There is an isomorphism β of quotient objects satisfying

β ∶ B₁ ≅ B₀(Q),   β ∘ ψ₁ = Frel ∘ ψ₀.

In particular the target for the containing space is the twist of the target for the avoiding space. On κ-valued points of the moduli scheme the formula is t₁ = t₀ ∘ Spec φ. The residue field of the base discrete valuation ring R has Q elements. Its image in κ is therefore fixed by φ. Hence the twisted moduli point is still over the same base point.

Which Frobenius is used? The absolute Frobenius iterated f times raises every local function to its Q-th power, including the scalars in κ. Its factorization through the projection B₀(Q) → B₀ gives the κ-morphism Frel. For any κ-scheme S and any point x ∶ S → B₀, the condition Frel ∘ x = 0 means that x and the zero section agree after precomposition with absolute Frobenius on S, iterated f times. This follows from naturality of absolute Frobenius and the defining pullback of the twist. Over an algebraically closed field the power map on scalars is invertible, so the only geometric point killed is zero. On a nonreduced test scheme the same criterion detects more. For the additive group and S = Spec κ[ε]/(ε²), the nonzero point ε is killed because εQ = 0. Stacks Project, absolute and relative Frobenius.

The kernel at the identity. The distinguished kernel of ψ₁ has d geometric points. Translation by those points identifies all its local factors, so the local factor at the identity has length Qd/d = Q. The rank-one conditions on the two auxiliary parameters of the unitary datum (m = a = 1) give a tangent-space bound for the ambient distinguished torsion at the identity. Its cotangent space therefore has dimension at most one. In its Artinian local ring the maximal ideal is therefore principal, by Nakayama’s lemma. If its generator is t, the successive nonzero quotients (tj)/(tj+1) have dimension one over κ. Indeed, an element of (tj) outside (tj+1) is tj times a unit. Every nonzero ideal is generated by an element of least such j. The ideal cutting out the local quotient of length Q is consequently (tQ). On an infinitesimal point of the ambient torsion, membership in this kernel is equivalent to vanishing of the Q-th powers of the functions vanishing at the identity. This is exactly the absolute-Frobenius test just described. Stacks Project, Nakayama’s lemma.

Compare this kernel with the one for Frel ∘ ψ₀. On geometric points the two agree because ψ₀ has the same distinguished reduced points as ψ₁ and relative Frobenius adds no geometric kernel points. On local points reducing to zero, applying ψ₀ preserves and reflects the Frobenius test on the distinguished torsion. Indeed, the test applied after ψ₀ puts the Frobenius image of the point into the distinguished kernel of ψ₀. That kernel is étale, so a local point of it reducing to the identity is the identity. The converse follows by naturality. Thus the local tests agree in both directions. Translating each local factor to the identity gives equality of the distinguished kernel functors on every test scheme.

The relative Frobenius is finite and surjective. It commutes with the coefficient action, carries level sections to their twists, and pulls back the twisted polarization line bundle to its Q-th tensor power. The polarized-isogeny theorem gives a complementary map whose composites are multiplication by Q. Thus composing with ψ₀ changes the multiplier from Qr to Qr+1, the multiplier of ψ₁. Applying the full-kernel lemma identifies the two quotient maps as claimed. Representability and the same intertwining equality give the twist both for the base point and for the image of each source point. The marked-target comparison applies these equalities to the full level marking and the fibre coordinates.
