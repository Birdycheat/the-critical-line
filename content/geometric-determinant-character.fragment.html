Where does the prescribed determinant come from?

The rank-extraction theorem needs a scalar ψ(g) before it produces a matrix r(g). We obtain this scalar from the automorphic representation. Let U be an irreducible automorphic representation of the definite unitary group of rank m ≥ 1 over the CM field E. Fix a prime ℓ and a field identification ι from the algebraic closure of ℚℓ to ℂ. Let Γ be the absolute Galois group of E.

Determinant character theorem. There is a continuous character ψ of Γ with values in the multiplicative group of the algebraic closure of ℚℓ. At every split finite place w where U has spherical Satake parameters, its local parameter is the product of the normalized Satake entries. Away from ℓ this is its value, read through ι, at geometric Frobenius. At w above ℓ the restriction to a decomposition group is crystalline, and the product is the Weil eigenvalue at degree −1.

From the centre to a character. For an idèle z of E, let c be CM conjugation and set ζ(z) = (z/c(z))I. This scalar matrix is unitary because its scalar times its conjugate is one. It commutes with the adelic group. To see that its right translation acts by one scalar on all of U, choose a nonzero form f in U. Its translates under the compact group at infinity span a finite-dimensional space S. A compact open subgroup K at the finite places fixing f fixes S because the finite and infinite translations commute.

Consider the maps from S to U that intertwine the actions at infinity and have K-fixed image. This vector space contains the inclusion. It is finite-dimensional because evaluation at representatives of the finitely many rational classes modulo K and the group at infinity embeds it into one copy of the dual of S per class. An intertwiner is determined there by rational invariance, its finite level and its transformation law at infinity. Composing such maps with translation by ζ(z) preserves their space by centrality. Over ℂ this operator has a nonzero eigenmap. A nonzero value of the eigenmap is an eigenvector in U. Its eigenspace is invariant under every adelic translation, again by centrality, so irreducibility makes that eigenspace all of U. The finite class number and evaluation at representatives.

Call this scalar χ(z). Translation respects products and is invertible, so χ is a multiplicative character. At a point g where f(g) ≠ 0, it equals f(gζ(z))/f(g), hence is continuous. For a principal idèle ζ(z) is rational and central, so left rational invariance gives χ(z) = 1. Thus χ is a Hecke character. It is also one on every idèle fixed by c.

The exponent at infinity. At a pair of conjugate complex embeddings τ, cτ, the last property makes the archimedean character trivial on ℝ×. It factors through the circle via z ↦ (z/|z|)², a surjection with kernel ℝ×. A continuous character of the circle has the form u ↦ un for an integer n. Indeed its image is compact in ℂ×, so has modulus one. Lifting its argument along ℝ → S¹ gives a continuous additive function of the angle, and periodicity makes its slope integral. The exponents of χ at τ and cτ are therefore nτ and −nτ. If the weight of U at τ is λ in the right-translation convention, the scalar uI acts on its highest-weight vector by u∑λj. Evaluating the same central action gives nτ = ∑j λτ,j. The action of the diagonal circle at the chosen embedding.

The value at a split spherical place. An idèle supported at w with value u has conjugate supported at the other place over the same real-subfield prime. Thus ζ sends it to the local scalar uI in the identification with GLm(Ew). Let B be the multiset of unnormalized Satake entries. On a spherical vector, scalar units act trivially. For a uniformizer ϖ the spherical Hecke operator indexed by m is translation by ϖI alone, since its central double coset is a single coset. Its Satake eigenvalue is the mth elementary symmetric function, ∏β∈Bβ. The local irreducible representation maps nontrivially into U by the spherical-component hypothesis. The kernel is invariant, so the map is injective and the spherical vector still detects this scalar in U. Consequently χ is unramified at w and χw(ϖ) = ∏β.

Write q for the residue-field size and t = m(m−1)/2. Normalization multiplies each of the m entries β by q(m−1)/2, so their product gains qt. The idèle norm has local value |ϖ|w = q−1. The Hecke character with the required product is therefore

ξ = χ |·|𝔸E−t,   Dw = ξw(ϖ) = qt ∏β∈B β.

Passing to the Galois group. At a complex place the idèle norm is the square of the complex modulus. The twist therefore subtracts t from the exponent at each embedding. Put n′τ = nτ − t. Form the ℓ-adic character of the idèles by dividing ξ by its archimedean algebraic powers, applying ι−1 to the remaining values, and restoring the same powers at the embeddings into the algebraic closure of ℚℓ. The quotient after removing the infinite powers is locally constant. The restored powers are continuous in the ℓ-adic topology. On principal idèles the removed and restored powers cancel under ι. The corrected character is thus continuous, trivial on principal idèles and on the connected component at infinity. Global Artin reciprocity lets it descend to the abelianized Galois group. Composing with the abelianization of Γ gives ψ. We take reciprocity to send a local uniformizer to geometric Frobenius in the unramified quotient. J. S. Milne, Class Field Theory, Chapter V, §5, Theorems 5.3 and 5.5 and the description of the absolute reciprocity kernel.

At w away from ℓ the corrected character agrees on local idèles with ι−1ξw. Its triviality on units gives trivial inertia. If F acts on the residue field by the qth power, then ι(ψ(F−1)) = Dw. This fixes the sign needed for extraction. The monic degree-m Satake polynomial has constant term (−1)mDw. Once the extracted representation has this polynomial on the dense set of inverse Frobenius elements with simple spectrum, its determinant equals ψ there and hence throughout Γ by continuity. In rank one t = 0, so a single Satake entry β gives ψ(F−1) = ι−1(β).

The comparison above ℓ. Restrict ψ to a decomposition group at a split spherical place w above ℓ. Here the algebraic powers at w enter its action on units. The rank-one comparison theorem identifies its de Rham filtration jump at an inducing ℓ-adic embedding σ with −n′ισ = t − nισ. Since the smooth local character ξw is unramified, the Galois character is crystalline. In the associated Weil representation local reciprocity gives a uniformizer to the power −d modulo units at any Weil element h of degree d. Its characteristic polynomial is consequently

det(X I − WD(ψ)(h)) = X − Dw−d.

At degree −1 this recovers Dw. The equality det r = ψ persists upon restriction to this decomposition group. The determinant is the top exterior power. The Weil–Deligne construction preserves tensor products and the permutation of tensor factors, so it preserves the alternating summand defining this exterior power. This gives the same product of Weil eigenvalues for r. At a place where the Satake parameters also satisfy the multiplicative genericity hypothesis and the geometric annihilation relation holds, the local multiplicity test combines this determinant with that relation to determine the full polynomial. The local input here is the rank-one de Rham and Weil comparison for algebraic characters, together with the crystalline criterion on units. B.
