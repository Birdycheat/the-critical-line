How can lowering the smaller parameter preserve the occurrence?

Keep the ambient discrete series π of G = U(p,q) fixed. Suppose the smaller discrete series σ of H = U(p−1,q) already occurs in its restriction. We want to replace μ by μ − ej. Assume the separated chamber hypotheses at both endpoints, including the opposite-sign gaps of at least 200(n+1)³, where n is the size of the defining matrices of H. Assume also that the midpoint μj−½ differs from every ambient entry λs. The first task is to find a vector in the desired summand of the smaller tensor.

Let J be the given nonzero bounded intertwiner from σ into π. By unitarity its adjoint intertwines in the reverse direction. Put P = J*/‖J‖, so ‖P‖ = 1. Write z for the fixed positive coordinate and Eab for the ambient complexified matrix-unit derivatives. On a smooth ambient vector η form the column Φ whose ath entry is P Eazη. Conjugating the matrix unit by the embedded h−1 changes its row coordinate while fixing its column z. Thus

Φ(π(h)η)a = ∑b (h−1)b,a σ(h) Φ(η)b.

This is the action on σ tensored with the dual defining representation of H. By regular tensor translation, the lowered parameter gives a smooth summand with projector pj and map rj into its Hilbert model σ′. For q > p−1, apply the translation theorem to the negated smaller Hermitian form. Both smaller signs occur because p ≥ q ≥ 1 and p+q ≥ 3. Negating the form gives the same matrix group, compact subgroup, diagonal parameters, smooth action and matrix-unit derivatives. It exchanges the two sign blocks, preserving the opposite-sign gaps. The dual tensor form and its scalar on each summand both change sign, so the same maps and projections give the decomposition for the original form.

Take the spectral variable w = −μj + (n−1)/2. At w the smaller quantum determinant is zero. Its comatrix CH(w) acts on smooth columns by djpj, where dj = ∏b≠j(μb−μj) is nonzero by regularity. In the ambient determinant at w+1, put the distinguished coordinate last and expand the last row, then the remaining last column. The diagonal term contains the smaller determinant at w on the left. Applying P brings the smaller-block derivatives through P, so this diagonal term vanishes. Each other term has the signed smaller cofactor followed by EazEzc, in that order. The two expansion signs give a common minus. On every smooth η the resulting identity is

cG Pη = −∑c [CH(w) Φ(Ezcη)]c,   cG = ∏s(λs−μj+½).

The half-unit is the difference between the two ranks’ central-character shifts after increasing the spectral variable by one. The midpoint assumption makes cG nonzero. If pjΦ vanished on every smooth vector, every summand on the right would vanish since Ezcη is smooth. Then P would vanish on a dense subspace, contradicting ‖P‖ = 1. We have obtained some smooth η₀ such that pjΦ(η₀) ≠ 0.

A smaller step in U(2,1). Put T = 6,000. Take λ = (4T,2T,0) of signs (+,+,−) and μ = (3T+½,−T+½) of signs (+,−). The merged sequence is G+, H+, G+, G−, H−, so the starting pair satisfies the discrete branching criterion. Lower the last μ to −T−½. The ambient opposite-sign gap is at least 2T and the smaller gap increases from 4T to 4T+1, all above 5,400. The midpoint is −T. Here w = T, the smaller projector denominator is 4T, and the ambient scalar is (5T)(3T)(T) = 15T³. Division by 4T normalizes the projector. The nonzero scalar 15T³ forces a transverse vector into its image. He, Definition 1 and Theorem 0.1 on discrete branching.

Why can the projected vector still be tested inside the ambient representation?

The formula for pjΦ uses operators in the smaller group. Every smaller-block derivative commutes past the intertwiner in the sense E′abP = P Eab on smooth vectors. Pull each cofactor back to the ambient generators in that block. Denote the resulting operator in row l, column a by Cblockla(w). Then

(pjΦ(η))l = P Rlη,   Rl = dj−1∑a Cblockla(w) Eaz.

Each cofactor is an ordered minor of size n−1. A factor is a single derivative plus a scalar, and one further derivative stands on the right. Consequently each Rl is a linear combination of words of length at most n. We use this bound when integrating an ambient coefficient with Rl applied to its moving vector.

Write ρ(h) = ‖h‖F² and let φ(h) be the complex conjugate of the σ′-coefficient of a unit vector v in its lowest compact type. The lowest-type decay estimate at the shifted parameter gives |φ(h)| ≤ Cρ(h)−(n²+1+n). For fixed smooth η, conjugating an ambient generator across the translate π(h) produces products of an entry of the embedded h and one of its inverse. Both matrices have squared Frobenius norm ρ(h)+1. Each pair costs at most ρ(h)+1 ≤ 2ρ(h). Repeating for at most n factors gives ‖Rlπ(h)η‖ ≤ Cη,lρ(h)n. Thus for any ambient test vector b,

|φ(h) ⟨b, Rlπ(h)η⟩| ≤ C′ ‖b‖ ρ(h)−(n²+1).

The last power is Haar integrable. Indeed the Haar volume of ‖h‖F ≤ B is at most a constant times B2n² for B ≥ 1. To see this, choose a small closed ball O about the identity in the real vector space of complex n by n matrices, lying entirely among invertible matrices. Each hO has the same positive Lebesgue volume since |det h| = 1. Their union for ‖h‖F ≤ B lies in an ambient ball of radius C B. For any matrix in that union, choose one h₀ whose translate contains it. All other such h lie in h₀(OO−1 ∩ H), a translate of a compact set of bounded Haar measure. Integrating this overlap bound proves the volume estimate. On the shell 2k ≤ ‖h‖F < 2k+1, the integral of the displayed power is therefore bounded by a constant times 2−2k.

Suppose all ordinary ambient coefficients integrate to zero against φ. For smooth b, move the formal adjoint of Rl onto b. The adjoint reverses each word and replaces Eab by its signed transpose generator, so it preserves smoothness. The resulting integral is one of those ordinary coefficients. For general b, approximate by smooth vectors. The integrated majorant above bounds the change by a constant times the norm of the difference. This proves that the word-inserted integral also vanishes for every b.

How does the surviving coordinate give a copy of the lowered representation?

Write x = Φ(η₀) and choose a coordinate l with (pjx)l ≠ 0. Put that coordinate in a column y supported only at l. The invariant form on a tensor summand gives a scalar cj satisfying

cj ⟨rjy, rjx⟩ = [y, pjx] = ε′l ‖(pjx)l‖² ≠ 0.

Testing one coordinate prevents cancellation between the positive and negative coordinates of the tensor form. In particular rjx and rjy are both nonzero. The translates of either span a dense subspace because σ′ is irreducible. Translate x and y independently so that their images a and b in the σ′ model satisfy ⟨v,a⟩ ≠ 0 and ⟨b,v⟩ ≠ 0 for the same lowest-type unit vector v. Denote the translated column of y by y₂. Equivariance of Φ writes the translated x as Φ(η₁) for a smooth ambient vector η₁.

Integrate the signed tensor coefficient [y₂, pjΦ(π(h)η₁)] against φ for a fixed Haar measure. In the shifted Hilbert model this becomes the integral on the left below. Schur orthogonality, with κ′ the positive reciprocal formal degree for this measure and the inner product linear in its second argument, evaluates it exactly. Duflo and Moore, orthogonality of square-integrable representations.

cj ∫H φ(h) ⟨b, σ′(h)a⟩ dν = cj κ′ ⟨v,a⟩ ⟨b,v⟩ ≠ 0.

If σ′ were absent from the restriction, the integral construction of an intertwiner would force every ordinary ambient coefficient against φ to integrate to zero. Expanding the very same signed tensor coefficient by coordinates gives a finite signed sum of the word-inserted integrals above, now with tests P* (y₂)l. Each would vanish. This contradicts the displayed Schur value and proves occurrence for μ − ej. Unitary equivalence carries the resulting intertwiner to every model of the prescribed signed parameters, as required in the descent.
