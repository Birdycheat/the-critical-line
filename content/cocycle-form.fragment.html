How does a group cocycle become a differential form?

Let Γ = GLN(𝒪L′) act on the cone of positive Hermitian matrices at the archimedean places. An m-cocycle c assigns a real number to m + 1 group elements. It is invariant when all are left-multiplied by the same element, and its alternating sum over the faces of any tuple of length m + 2 is zero. We want a smooth m-form whose integral on an arithmetic cycle recovers the value of c on that cycle. A partition of unity lets us trade one group argument at a time for a differential.

Write Aγ for the linear action of γ on Hermitian matrices. From a smooth function φ on the cone form the weights ψγ(h) = φ(Aγ⁻¹h). Only finitely many terms occur near any point, and they sum to 1. For a cochain β whose values are forms, let K insert a new first argument and average with these weights. Write δ for the alternating deletion of group arguments and d for exterior differentiation. The construction is

(Kβ)(g₀,…) = ∑γ ψγ β(γ,g₀,…),   η₀ = c,   ηj+1 = d(Kηj).

In Kδβ, deleting the newly inserted γ gives β times ∑ψγ = β. Every other deletion cancels the corresponding term of δKβ. Thus δK + Kδ = 1 in the degrees used here. Starting with δη₀ = 0, induction gives δηj+1 = dδKηj = dηj = 0. Indeed η₀ is constant in the cone variable, and every later ηj is an exterior derivative. After m steps only one group argument remains. The equation δηm = 0 now says its values at any two group elements coincide. This leaves a smooth closed form on the cone. Equivariance of the weights gives arithmetic invariance. To compare periods, fix the identity matrix I. On a homogeneous tuple (g₀,…,gm), let Wj integrate ηj(g₀,…,gm−j) over the simplex with the last j + 1 vertices Agₘ₋ⱼI, …, AgₘI. Thus W₀ = c. A comparison cochain Bj integrates Kηj in the same way on an input with one fewer group element. In δBj, deleting an early argument invokes δKηj = ηj. The other deletions give the oriented faces of the next simplex. Stokes replaces their sum with the integral of d(Kηj) = ηj+1. Including the orientation sign gives

δBj = Wj + (−1)m−1−jWj+1,   0 ≤ j < m.

Substitute this identity successively for j = 0,…,m−1. The result is c = δb + sWm for an invariant cochain b. The sign starts at 1 and is multiplied at step j by (−1)m−j. Choose ω = sηm. Evaluating δb on a bar chain z gives b evaluated on ∂z. Consequently the periods of ω and c agree on every cycle. Check the sign in degree one. Let ℤ act on the positive real line by h ↦ 2nh and take c(a,b) = b − a. In the coordinate t = log₂ h, choose a smooth bump θ supported on [−1,1] and positive on (−1,1). Normalize its integer translates to weights ψn(t) = θ(t − n)/∑p θ(t − p). Their weighted mean A(t) = ∑n nψn(t) satisfies A(t + 1) = A(t) + 1. Hence (Kc)(b) = b − A and η₁ = −dA. The sign in the comparison is −1, giving ω = dA. The group element 2 sends h = 1 to h = 4. Their images in the quotient coincide, so the interval from 1 to 4 represents the cycle [2]. Its integral is A(2) − A(0) = 2. This verifies the cocycle construction and its orientation in one dimension.

The arithmetic partition has additional properties. Its generator is unchanged under common positive dilation, so ω already has scalar invariance whenever the scalar moduli agree at all places. Its translated supports have finite overlap, and each weight derivative in the moving frame has norm at most a constant times heighta. At step j + 1 the product rule leaves dψγ wedged with ηj, since dηj = 0. At a point of overlap, translate all active labels on the left by the inverse of one of them. Each resulting label belongs to a fixed finite set. This bounds both the number of terms and the values of c on the relevant tuples, using its invariance. The form therefore grows at most like heightam, with a constant allowed to depend on the cocycle. The construction from the region of bounded lattice minima gives a = 1 for every matrix size N ≥ 1 over the fixed totally complex field. The comparison uses the weaker statement that one a works in every size. For its Stokes input, the source proves the formula on affine simplices by integrating in one coordinate and summing the endpoint faces with alternating signs.
