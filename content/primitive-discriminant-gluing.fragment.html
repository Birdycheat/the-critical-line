What do the two projections of an integral lattice remember?

Fix a full integral lattice L in a totally definite Hermitian space V over E. Write R = 𝒪E. A rational subspace U and its perpendicular W give a direct sum V = U ⊕ W. Write p and q for the two orthogonal projections. Set P = L ∩ U and Q = L ∩ W. Clearing denominators on a rational basis shows that each intersection spans its subspace. They are primitive: if a nonzero element of R times an element of L lies in P, the element itself lies in U, hence in P. The same holds for Q.

Although the ambient space splits, the lattice need only contain P ⊕ Q. A vector in L can have fractional components on both sides. The finite quotient C = L/(P ⊕ Q) records these pairs of components. Each component determines the other modulo its intersection lattice:

pL/P ≃ C ≃ qL/Q.

For the first isomorphism, send the class of x to the class of px. It is surjective by definition. If px belongs to P, then qx = x − px belongs to L ∩ W = Q. This proves injectivity. Exchanging the projections proves the second isomorphism. In particular the number of paired residue classes is counted only once.

For any lattice A denote its Hermitian dual in its own space by A∨. We have P∨ = p(L∨) and Q∨ = q(L∨). To see the first identity, test a vector u ∈ U against p(L∨). Orthogonality gives h(u,pz) = h(u,z) for every z ∈ L∨. This is integral for all z exactly when u belongs to the double dual of L, which is L itself. Thus the dual of p(L∨) is P. Taking duals once more proves the claim. The double-dual identity applies to these full lattices because they are finite projective over a Dedekind domain. For a free module it is evaluation on a basis and its dual. A finite projective module is a direct summand of a finite free one, so the evaluation identity restricts to it. The form identifies the conjugate-linear dual with the indicated Hermitian dual. The Stacks Project, Lemma 15.22.11 · Lemma 10.78.2, finite projective modules.

Why is the perpendicular discriminant at most the product of the other two?

Write n = |P∨/P|, a = |Q∨/Q| and N₀ = |L∨/L|. Let c = |C|. Integrality of L places pL between P and P∨ and places qL between Q and Q∨. The first chain shows c divides n. In the second chain Q∨/qL is an image of L∨/L, since Q∨ = q(L∨). Its order divides N₀. Multiplying the two successive indices on the perpendicular side gives

a = c · |Q∨/qL| ≤ nN₀.

Equality in a plane. Take L = R² with Gram matrix diag(1,2). For u = (1,1) and w = (−2,1), the two lines are orthogonal, and their intersections are P = Ru and Q = Rw. The squared lengths are 3 and 6. In x = (s,t), the coefficient of u is (s + 2t)/3 and the coefficient of w is (t − s)/3. In particular the vector (1,0) supplies the residue pair (1/3,−1/3), generating the common quotient R/(3). Meanwhile L∨ allows a half in the second coordinate, so q(L∨) = (1/6)Q. Consequently Q∨/qL has order |R/(2)|. Writing D = [E ∶ ℚ], the orders are n = 3D, N₀ = 2D and a = 6D. This attains the general product bound. As a second check, the index-square formula for P ⊕ Q ⊂ L reads na = N₀c². Here both sides equal 18D. This example tests the lattice lemma before the row specifies its level prime.

Why does the gluing count cost only twice the perpendicular rank plus one?

Let s be the dimension of W, and assume L∨/L can be generated by e elements over R. A finite quotient of one full lattice in an s-dimensional space by another contained in it needs at most s generators. At each prime in its support the upper lattice is free of rank s, so the images of a local basis suffice. The finite module is a direct sum of its primary parts. Pad their generating lists with zeros to length s and assemble the first entries into one element, then the second entries into another, and so on. The Chinese remainder theorem supplies scalars which select a single primary part, proving that the assembled elements generate the whole module. Local freeness over a Dedekind domain · Chinese remainder theorem.

Apply this to qL/Q. Through the common residue module, the same s generators suffice for pL/P. The quotient P∨/pL is the image of L∨/L under p, so e more generators suffice. Lifting these latter generators and adjoining the former ones generates P∨/P. On the perpendicular side the preceding rank bound applies directly to Q∨/Q. Thus the two budgets are s + e and s, even when U has much larger dimension.

Fix both intersections. Every candidate ambient lattice lies between P ⊕ Q and P∨ ⊕ Q∨, because its vectors pair integrally with P and Q. Taking the quotient by the lower lattice embeds the choices into the set of submodules of the finite module A = (P∨/P) ⊕ (Q∨/Q), of size na. In the row, the ambient discriminant module is cyclic, giving e = 1 as a sufficient bound. Each submodule of a finite module generated by g elements over R needs at most g generators, by the local lifting argument. Counting ordered tuples yields

# compatible lattices ≤ |A|2s+1 = (na)2s+1,   1 ≤ a ≤ nN₀.

Only some submodules produce an integral lattice with exactly these intersections. Counting all tuples gives an upper bound valid for every fixed pair. The genus mean multiplies it by the weighted counts of the two intersections and the number of rational subspace orbits.
