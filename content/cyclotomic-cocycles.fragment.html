How does a character on the infinite kernel extend across the cyclotomic direction?

Let G be the absolute Galois group of a number field F, χ its ℓ-adic cyclotomic character, U = ker χ and A = ℚℓ/ℤℓ with action ρ = χ−e. A character φ on U in the restriction theorem is locally constant, additive on products and satisfies φ(gug−1) = ρ(g)φ(u). It vanishes on the intersections of U with finite inertia groups and with decomposition groups over ℓ. We assume e ≠ 0. At odd ℓ the generator condition holds for the cyclotomic image of any number field. At 2 the theorem assumes χ ≡ 1 modulo 4 throughout G, as happens when F contains a primitive fourth root of unity. How do the restrictions survive passage to the unramified quotient? Let S be the places over ℓ and N the closed normal subgroup of G generated by all inertia groups away from S. The cyclotomic character χ, and hence its twist, factors through GS = G/N. In particular, all such inertia groups lie in U and z vanishes there. Inside N the zero set of z is a closed subgroup, since N acts trivially on the coefficients. It is normal in G by the cocycle rule, which gives z(gng−1) = ρ(g)z(n) for n in N. It therefore contains all of N. Now z(gn) = z(g) gives the continuous quotient cocycle.
