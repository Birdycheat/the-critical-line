Fourier coefficients of averaged theta and Eisenstein series and quadratic level-set densities

In the one-vector discrepancy bound, passing from a uniform Siegel-domain bound on the difference between an averaged theta function and an Eisenstein series to a comparison between the global count I(a) and the product of local Gram-fibre masses requires extracting the β-th Fourier coefficient of both sides and recognizing the Eisenstein coefficient as a product of continuous quadratic level-set densities. The mechanism has three interlocking parts. First, a continuous standard additive character ψ on the finite adeles 𝔸F,f is trivial on the integral subring ÔF, so for any Schwartz–Bruhat test φ the quadratic phase integral f*(φ, ·) is periodic modulo N ÔF for a common denominator N ≥ 1. Second, integrating against exp(−2πi ∑w ρw(β) xw) over a fundamental domain D of N 𝒪F in ℝd isolates the level set q(z) = β on the theta side by character orthogonality, while unfolding the Eisenstein series over cosets of N 𝒪F splits it into the product of a finite-adelic Fourier integral of f*(φ, ·) and an archimedean Fourier integral of cG(x + i). Third, because M ≥ 3 makes both Fourier transforms absolutely integrable (using the inverse-denominator decay from the adelic Weil orbit bound on the finite side and |t + i|−M decay on the archimedean side), Fourier inversion evaluates both integrals directly in terms of continuous quadratic level-set densities Df and D∞.

Proposition (Standard adelic character, theta and Eisenstein Fourier coefficients, and level-set densities). Let F be a totally real number field of degree d = [F ∶ ℚ] with real embeddings ρw ∶ F → ℝ, normalized additive Haar measure λf on 𝔸F,f satisfying λf(ÔF) = 1, symmetric nonsingular 2M × 2M matrix B over F with M ≥ 3, and positive-definite majorants Pw satisfying Pw Bw−1 Pw = Bw. Write q(u) = ½ u⊤ B u over F and 𝔸F,f, and qw(x) = ½ xw⊤ Bw xw at each infinite place w.

  1. Standard finite-adelic character and lattice periodicity. There exists a continuous additive character ψ ∶ 𝔸F,f → ℂ satisfying ψ(b) = exp(−2πi TrF/ℚ(b)) for every b ∈ F. Every such character obeys |ψ(x)| = 1 for all x ∈ 𝔸F,f, ψ(x) = 1 for all x ∈ ÔF, and ψ(b x) = 1 for all x ∈ ÔF if and only if TrF/ℚ(b 𝒪F) ⊆ ℤ. For any Schwartz–Bruhat test φ on 𝔸F,f2M, there exists an integer N ≥ 1 such that UN x φ = φ and f*(φ, y + N x) = f*(φ, y) for all x ∈ ÔF and y ∈ 𝔸F,f.
  2. Fundamental domain volume and character orthogonality. For every integer N ≥ 1, the embedded lattice ΛN = {(ρw(N r))w ∶ r ∈ 𝒪F} ⊂ ℝd is countable and admits a measurable fundamental domain D ⊂ ℝd. Every fundamental domain D of ΛN has Lebesgue measure vol(D) = Nd |disc F|1/2 and satisfies ∫D exp(2πi ∑w ρw(β) xw) dx = 0 for every nonzero β ∈ F with N β ∈ 𝒪F.
  3. Averaged theta Fourier coefficient. Let (Ω, Pr) be a probability space of orthogonal pairs (gf, g∞) preserving B and satisfying the one-vector ball-count hypothesis, let a ≥ 1, let φa be the indicator of a−1 ÔF2M, and let β ∈ F× and N ≥ 1 satisfy N β ∈ 𝒪F and N q(u) ∈ ÔF whenever a u ∈ ÔF2M. Then the weighted average on D is integrable and equals
    ∫D (∫Ω θ(φa; x + i) dPr) exp(−2πi ∑w ρw(β) xw) dx = vol(D) · Iβ,
    where Iβ = ∫Ω ∑q(z) = β φa(gf z) exp(−π ∑w (g∞,w zw)⊤ Pw (g∞,w zw)) dPr is finite and nonnegative.
  4. Eisenstein Fourier coefficient and quadratic level-set densities. For any Schwartz–Bruhat φ with f*(φ, ·) integrable on (𝔸F,f, λf) and N ÔF-periodic, any β ∈ F× with N β ∈ 𝒪F, and any fundamental domain D of ΛN, the Eisenstein integrand is integrable on D and unfolds as
    ∫D E(φ; x + i) exp(−2πi ∑w ρw(β) xw) dx = (Nd ∫𝔸F,f f*(φ, y) ψ(−β y) dλf(y)) · (∫ℝd cG(x + i) exp(−2πi ∑w ρw(β) xw) dx).
    For φ = φa, both f*(φa, ·) and x ↦ cG(x + i) are integrable, and there exist continuous nonnegative densities Df ∶ 𝔸F,f → ℝ and D∞ ∶ ℝd → ℝ disintegrating λf⊗2M|a−1 ÔF2M along q and exp(−π ∑w xw⊤ Pw xw) dx along (qw)w respectively, such that for every z ∈ 𝔸F,f and every z∞ ∈ ℝd,
    ∫𝔸F,f f*(φa, y) ψ(−z y) dλf(y) = |disc F|−(M − 1) |NF/ℚ(det B)|−1/2 Df(z),   ∫ℝd cG(x + i) exp(−2πi ∑w z∞,w xw) dx = |NF/ℚ(det B)|1/2 D∞(z∞).

Proof. Because F is dense in 𝔸F,f and ÔF is open, every x ∈ 𝔸F,f admits b ∈ F with x − b ∈ ÔF. If b, c ∈ F both satisfy this condition, then b − c ∈ F ∩ ÔF = 𝒪F, so TrF/ℚ(b − c) ∈ ℤ and exp(−2πi TrF/ℚ(b)) = exp(−2πi TrF/ℚ(c)). Defining ψ(x) to be this common value yields a locally constant (hence continuous) additive character agreeing with exp(−2πi TrF/ℚ(b)) on F. Conversely, for any continuous standard character ψ, the closed set {y ∶ ψ(y) = 1} contains 𝒪F and hence its closure ÔF; decomposing x = b + (x − b) then gives |ψ(x)| = 1, and closing {y ∶ ψ(b y) = 1} identifies the annihilator of b ÔF with {b ∈ F ∶ TrF/ℚ(b 𝒪F) ⊆ ℤ}. For a Schwartz–Bruhat test φ, the continuous map q sends the compact support of φ to a compact subset of 𝔸F,f, so some integer N ≥ 1 satisfies N q(supp φ) ⊆ ÔF; then ψ(N x q(u)) = 1 for all x ∈ ÔF and u ∈ supp φ, proving UN x φ = φ and N ÔF-periodicity of f*(φ, ·).

Choosing a ℤ-basis b1, …, bd of 𝒪F gives an embedding matrix M0 = (ρw(bj)) with (det M0)2 = disc F ≠ 0. Scaling by N yields a real basis of ℝd generating ΛN whose fundamental parallelepiped has volume |det(N M0)| = Nd |disc F|1/2; every fundamental domain D of ΛN has the same measure. For nonzero β ∈ F with N β ∈ 𝒪F, the character eβ(x) = exp(2πi ∑w ρw(β) xw) is ΛN-periodic (since ∑w ρw(N β s) = TrF/ℚ(N β s) ∈ ℤ for every s ∈ 𝒪F). Picking an infinite place w0 and setting x0 to have coordinate (2 ρw0(β))−1 at w0 and 0 elsewhere gives eβ(x0) = −1; translation invariance of Lebesgue measure and periodicity over x0 + D force ∫D eβ = −∫D eβ, hence ∫D eβ = 0.

For the theta Fourier coefficient, each summand at τ = x + i factors as eq(z)(x) times the nonnegative term at τ = i. The ball-count bound guarantees finite expected total norm at τ = i, permitting Fubini exchange of ∑z, ∫Ω, and ∫D. Every z with q(z) = β yields integrand 1 on D and contributes vol(D); every z with q(z) ≠ β either vanishes identically on Ω or satisfies N q(z) ∈ 𝒪F, so N(q(z) − β) ∈ 𝒪F ∖ {0} and character orthogonality on D kills its integral. On the Eisenstein side, the constant term φ(0) integrates to 0 against e−β on D, while the uniform bound on ∑b ∈ F |f*(φ, b) cG(x + b + i)| justifies swapping ∫D and ∑b ∈ F. Grouping b ∈ F into cosets q + h of H = N 𝒪F uses H-periodicity of f*(φ, ·) and unfolds the inner sum over h ∈ ΛN from D to all of ℝd, pulling out e−β(−q) = ψ(−β q) times ∫ℝd cG(x + i) e−β(x) dx. The remaining coset sum ∑q ∈ F/H f*(φ, q) ψ(−β q) is constant on each disjoint open translate q + N ÔF covering 𝔸F,f; since λf(N ÔF) = N−d, summing over cosets equals Nd ∫𝔸F,f f*(φ, y) ψ(−β y) dλf(y).

Finally, at infinity, the pushforward measure m∞ of exp(−π ∑w xw⊤ Pw xw) dx along (qw)w has Fourier transform t ↦ |NF/ℚ(det B)|−1/2 cG(t + i). Because |cG(t + i)| ≤ ∏w |tw + i|−M and M ≥ 3, this transform lies in L1(ℝd), so Euclidean Fourier inversion gives the continuous nonnegative density D∞ and the archimedean identity. On the finite adeles, f*(φa, ·) is N ÔF-periodic and satisfies |f*(φa, b)| ≤ C den(b)−M from the finite-orbit decay bound; partitioning 𝔸F,f into translates of N ÔF indexed by F / N 𝒪F and summing den(b)−M over representatives proves f*(φa, ·) ∈ L1(𝔸F,f, λf). Writing f*(φa, y) = |disc F|−M |NF/ℚ(det B)|−1/2 ∫ ψ(u y) dmf(u) for the pushforward mf of λf⊗2M|a−1 ÔF2M along q, self-dual Fourier inversion with respect to |disc F|−1/2 λf yields a continuous nonnegative density D relative to |disc F|−1/2 λf; setting Df = |disc F|−1/2 D (the density relative to λf) absorbs one factor of |disc F|−1/2 from the density and another from the self-dual measure, leaving the exact prefactor |disc F|−(M − 1) |NF/ℚ(det B)|−1/2. ∎

Discriminant-exponent bookkeeping and an explicit six-dimensional Gaussian inversion check

Tracking discriminant and determinant factors on division by domain volume. Multiplying the two identities in the level-set density equation and multiplying by Nd gives the Eisenstein Fourier coefficient as Nd |disc F|−(M − 1) Df(β) D∞(ρ(β)), where |NF/ℚ(det B)|−1/2 and |NF/ℚ(det B)|1/2 cancel identically. Dividing both the theta and Eisenstein coefficients by vol(D) = Nd |disc F|1/2 cancels Nd and leaves |disc F|−(2M − 1)/2 Df(β) D∞(ρ(β)), matching the exact quotient of the 2M-dimensional source self-dual normalization |disc F|−M and the one-dimensional target self-dual normalization |disc F|−1/2.

Worked check (six-dimensional archimedean level-set density). Take F = ℚ (d = 1, disc ℚ = 1), M = 3 (dimension 2M = 6), and B = P = 2 I6, so P B−1 P = B, |det B|1/2 = 8, and q(x) = ½ x⊤ B x = ‖x‖2. In polar coordinates on ℝ6, the unit 5-sphere has area 2π3/Γ(3) = π3; substituting y = r2 (so r5 dr = ½ y2 dy) gives ∫q(x) ∈ U exp(−2π ‖x‖2) dx = ∫U ∩ (0, ∞) (π3/2) y2 exp(−2π y) dy, hence D∞(z) = (π3/2) z2 exp(−2π z) for z > 0 and 0 for z ≤ 0. Independently, the six-dimensional Gaussian integral evaluates to ∫ℝ6 exp(−2π(1 − i t) ‖x‖2) dx = (2(1 − i t))−3 = (1/8)(1 − i t)−3, so cG(t + i) = 8 · (1/8)(1 − i t)−3 = (1 − i t)−3. For z > 0, closing ∫−∞∞ (1 − i t)−3 exp(−2πi z t) dt clockwise in the lower half-plane picks up (−2πi) times the residue at t = −i of (−i)(t + i)−3 exp(−2πi z t), namely (−2πi)(−i · ½ · (−4π2 z2) exp(−2π z)) = 4π3 z2 exp(−2π z), matching |det B|1/2 D∞(z) = 8 · (π3/2) z2 exp(−2π z) identically, while for z < 0 closing in the upper half-plane yields 0.

Coordinate Haar measures, local Gram-fibre positivity, lattice stability, and Euler-product factorization

Before comparing the global theta average with an Euler product in the one-vector mass normalization, one must build the compatible local coordinate Haar measures on tuples and Hermitian matrices, verify that every local Gram-fibre measure is locally finite and strictly positive on regular open sets, and establish that an invariant measure supported on a regular Gram fibre factors as a global scalar times a convergent Euler product of those local fibre measures. The algebra rests on the degree-two CM involution σ: its fixed elements in any local algebra R ⊗F E are recovered continuously by half-trace, identifying Hermitian matrices homeomorphically with diagonal and strictly upper-triangular coordinates. Local positivity is proved by transporting any putative null open set across a whole neighborhood of regular fibres via a continuous congruence section and contradicting the positivity of ambient Haar measure. Finally, inserting one finite place at a time into the standard-lattice indicator yields a multiplicative renormalization recursion whose convergence supplies a single global scalar c valid on every restricted measurable box.

Proposition (Scalar Hermitian identification, coordinate Haar measures, regular fibre positivity, lattice stability, and Euler-product factorization). Let E/F be a CM extension with conjugation σ, let b be an F-basis of E of length d0, and let J be a nondegenerate M × M Hermitian matrix over E. Equip A∞ = F ⊗ℚ ℝ and every finite completion Fv with standard additive Haar measures λ∞ and λv (where λv(𝒪v) = 1), and let Λv denote the standard coordinate lattice of 𝒪v-linear combinations of b in (Fv ⊗F E)n × M.

  1. One-by-one Hermitian scalars and coordinate Haar measures. Every 1 × 1 Hermitian matrix T over E has the form T = (β) for a unique scalar β ∈ F (with β ≠ 0 whenever det T ≠ 0), and its base change to any F-algebra R equals the scalar matrix (β ⊗ 1). For every locally compact, second-countable, Hausdorff topological F-algebra R with additive Haar measure λ, pulling back product Haar measure along the b-coordinates defines additive Haar measures on R ⊗F E and (R ⊗F E)n × M, while the map sending an n × n Hermitian matrix S over R ⊗F E to ((½ Tr(R ⊗F E)/R(Si,i))i, (Sa,b)a < b) is an additive topological homeomorphism onto Rn × (R ⊗F E)n(n − 1)/2 whose pullback defines an additive Haar measure on Hermn(R ⊗F E).
  2. Local finiteness and strict open-set positivity of Gram fibres. Over a local base ring R (either A∞ or Fv), if a family of Borel measures mS disintegrates an additive Haar measure μ on (R ⊗F E)n × M along the Gram map over an additive Haar measure ν on Hermn(R ⊗F E), then every mS is locally finite, and for every open set U containing a point X0 with Gram matrix S and invertible determinant, mS(U) > 0.
  3. Almost-everywhere rational stability of standard lattices. For every invertible n × n matrix g over E, every h ∈ GLM(E), and the rank-increasing block embedding X ↦ diag(1, X), right multiplication by g, left multiplication by h, and block embedding each preserve membership in Λv for all but finitely many finite places v.
  4. Euler-product factorization and restricted-box integration. Suppose n = 0 or n + 2 ≤ M, let T be an invertible n × n Hermitian matrix over E, and let I be a locally finite U(J, 𝔸F)-invariant Borel measure on (𝔸F ⊗F E)n × M supported on the T-Gram fibre. Then there exists a scalar c ≥ 0 such that I factors as an Euler product of m∞(T) and (mv(T))v with scalar c, satisfying ∑v |mv(T)(Λv) − 1| < ∞ and the place-insertion renormalization law
    c(S) = c(S ∪ {v}) · mv(T)(Λv) = c · ∏w ∉ S mw(T)(Λw)   (S ⊇ S0, v ∉ S).
    Furthermore, for any Euler-product measure I with scalar c, any measurable nonnegative archimedean test φ∞, and any measurable finite-place sets Av of finite mv-measure agreeing with Λv at all but finitely many places v, the restricted box {X ∶ ∀ v, Xv ∈ Av} is measurable, the infinite product ∏v mv(Av) converges, and
    ∫{∀ v, Xv ∈ Av} φ∞(X∞) dI(X) = c · (∫ φ∞ dm∞) · ∏v mv(Av).

Proof. Because every infinite place of the CM field E is complex whereas σ induces complex conjugation at every embedding, σ cannot be the identity; pick y ∈ E with σ(y) ≠ y. If x ∈ E satisfies σ(x) = x and x ∉ F, then {1, x} would be an F-basis of the degree-two extension E/F fixed pointwise by σ, contradicting σ(y) ≠ y. Hence the fixed field of σ is F, which identifies every 1 × 1 Hermitian matrix over E with a scalar β ∈ F. Likewise, setting δ = y − σ(y) ≠ 0 gives σ(δ) = −δ, so {1, δ} is an F-basis of E and {1 ⊗ 1, 1 ⊗ δ} is an R-basis of R ⊗F E. Any element r0 ⊗ 1 + r1 ⊗ δ fixed by σ satisfies r1 = −r1, hence r1 = 0 (as 2 is invertible in F), and half-trace recovers r0 continuously. Thus diagonal half-traces and strictly upper-triangular entries establish an additive homeomorphism Hermn(R ⊗F E) ≃t Rn × (R ⊗F E)n(n − 1)/2, and pullback of additive Haar measure along an additive homeomorphism is again an additive Haar measure.

For local finiteness and positivity of a Gram-fibre measure mS, local finiteness holds directly from the continuous Gram disintegration over the open regular locus {det S ∈ (R ⊗F E)×}. Suppose for contradiction that an open set U contains a regular point X0 of Gram matrix S yet mS(U) = 0. Choose a continuous local congruence section gs on an open neighborhood B1 of S such that gs(S) = 1 and right multiplication by gs(S′) transforms Gram matrix S to S′. Define the continuous map T0(X) = X gs(gram(X))−1 on gram−1(B1) and set W = gram−1(B1) ∩ T0−1(U), an open neighborhood of X0. For every S′ ∈ B1, GLn-equivariance expresses mS′(W) as a scalar multiple of the mS-measure of the preimage of W under right multiplication by gs(S′), which lies inside U away from the mS-null complement of the regular S-fibre and therefore has measure 0; for S′ ∉ B1, W misses the S′-fibre altogether. Disintegration therefore yields μ(W ∩ {det gram invertible}) = 0, contradicting the strict positivity of additive Haar measure μ on the nonempty open set containing X0.

For almost-everywhere lattice stability, every element e ∈ E has structure-constant matrix for multiplication in the F-basis b whose entries lie in F and are therefore v-integral for almost all finite places v. Applying this simultaneously to all entries of a rational invertible matrix and its inverse shows both the action and its inverse map Λv into Λv at almost all v; for block extension, it suffices that the basis coordinates of 1 ∈ E be v-integral, which again holds cofinally.

To assemble the Euler-product factorization when n = 0 or n + 2 ≤ M, first note that if any local fibre measure m∞(T) or mv(T) vanishes, open-set positivity implies no local tuple has Gram matrix T at that place, so the global T-fibre is empty, I = 0, and c = 0 works. Otherwise every local fibre measure is σ-finite and nonzero, hence admits a measurable test set of finite positive measure (A at infinity and Bv at each finite place v). Orbital-measure uniqueness supplies a scalar c(S) for every finite place set S on pure tests equal to 1Λw off S. Comparing S with S ∪ {v} (v ∉ S) by inserting the indicator 1Λv at v and evaluating on A and (Bw)w ∈ S yields c(S) · a Q = c(S ∪ {v}) mv(T)(Λv) · a Q with 0 < a Q < ∞, cancelling to c(S) = c(S ∪ {v}) mv(T)(Λv). Together with absolute convergence of ∑v |mv(T)(Λv) − 1|, infinite-product renormalization yields a single constant c and finite set S0 such that c(S) = c ∏w ∉ S mw(T)(Λw) for all S ⊇ S0. Given any measurable box (Av) agreeing with Λv outside a finite set, choosing S to contain both S0 and that exceptional set writes the box as the finite intersection of the off-S integral condition with the preimages of Av for v ∈ S (proving measurability), merges ∏v ∈ S mv(Av) with the convergent tail ∏v ∉ S mv(Λv), and gives the restricted-box formula directly. ∎

Trace coordinates on binary Hermitian matrices and renormalization consistency at the prime 3

Worked check (trace coordinates and place-insertion renormalization). Over the CM field E = ℚ(i) with F = ℚ and skew element δ = i, an arbitrary 2 × 2 Hermitian matrix S over R ⊗ℚ ℚ(i) has diagonal entries r1 ⊗ 1, r2 ⊗ 1 and upper-right entry z ∈ R ⊗ℚ ℚ(i) (determining the lower-left entry as σ(z)). Since Tr(R ⊗ E)/R(r ⊗ 1) = 2r, half-trace extracts (r1, r2) continuously and pairs them with z to give R2 × (R ⊗ℚ ℚ(i)), of real dimension 2 + 2 = 4 when R = ℝ. To check the renormalization step in the rank-three example (E = ℚ(i), J = I3, β = 1), recall that at the inert prime 3 the standard-lattice mass is m3(1)(Λ3) = 252/35 = 28/27. Inserting v = 3 into S = ∅ gives c(∅) = (28/27) c({3}), so dividing by the tail product gives c(∅) / ∏p mp(1)(Λp) = c({3}) / ∏p ≠ 3 mp(1)(Λp) = c, confirming independence of the finite cutoff set S.

Cocompactness and Euler-product factorization in the anisotropic binary case

In dimension M = 2, the higher-rank exponent gap of the scalar-normalization comparison vanishes and the automatic rank condition n + 2 ≤ M of the general Euler-product factorization no longer holds for n = 1. However, when the binary Hermitian form J is anisotropic over E, the adelic unitary quotient U(J, F) ∖ U(J, 𝔸F) is compact rather than merely of finite volume. Cocompactness immediately forces every normalized theta measure to be locally finite via a uniform finite counting bound on compact sets, and combining that local finiteness with the quadratic-tail binary local mass bound |mv(β)(Λv) − 1| ≤ C1 / qv2 recovers the exact Euler-product factorization up to a scalar c ≥ 0 needed by the anisotropic binary normalization argument.

Proposition (Anisotropic binary cocompactness, local finiteness of theta measures, and scalar-factorized Euler product). Let E/F be a CM extension with conjugation σ, let J be an anisotropic nondegenerate 2 × 2 Hermitian matrix over E (meaning x* J x = 0 implies x = 0 for every x ∈ E2), let b be an F-basis of E, and let μ be a Haar measure on U(J, 𝔸F) with fundamental domain 𝓕 for U(J, F).

  1. Cocompactness and finite positive covolume. There exists a compact subset C ⊂ U(J, 𝔸F) such that every h ∈ U(J, 𝔸F) factors as h = γ c with γ ∈ U(J, F) and c ∈ C; moreover, 0 < μ(𝓕) < ∞.
  2. Local finiteness of cocompact theta measures. More generally, for any nondegenerate M × M Hermitian form J over E whose adelic quotient is covered by a compact set C with 0 < μ(𝓕) < ∞, every subset S ⊆ En × M defines a locally finite normalized theta measure θS = μ(𝓕)−1 ∑X ∈ S (μ|𝓕)*(h ↦ h−1 X) on (𝔸F ⊗F E)n × M.
  3. Anisotropic binary Euler-product factorization up to a scalar. Assume the binary local mass bound holds: there exists C1 ≥ 0 such that for all but finitely many finite places v and every local unit s ∈ 𝒪v×, |mv(s)(Λv) − 1| ≤ C1 / qv2 (where qv = N(v)). Then for every nonzero β ∈ F×, there exists a scalar c ≥ 0 such that the one-vector theta measure on the β-sphere Sβ = {X ∈ E2 ∶ X* J X = β} satisfies
    θSβ = c · (m∞(β) ⊗ ∏v mv(β))   as an Euler-product measure on (𝔸F ⊗F E)2.

Proof. Because J is anisotropic on E2, the empty (rank-zero) isotropic frame is a maximal Witt frame for (σ, J). Applying unitary Siegel-set reduction to this rank-zero Witt frame yields a compact subset C ⊂ U(J, 𝔸F) covering U(J, F) ∖ U(J, 𝔸F), while nonvanishing of Haar measure on a fundamental domain and the general finite-volume theorem for adelic unitary groups give 0 < μ(𝓕) < ∞.

To prove local finiteness of θS whenever a compact cover C exists, fix any point X0 ∈ (𝔸F ⊗F E)n × M and pick a compact neighborhood K of X0. Because the group action of U(J, 𝔸F) on (𝔸F ⊗F E)n × M is continuous, the image K′ = C · K is compact. The diagonally embedded rational tuples En × M form a discrete (hence closed) additive subgroup of (𝔸F ⊗F E)n × M, so the intersection P = {X ∈ En × M ∶ X ∈ K′} is finite. Now take any h ∈ U(J, 𝔸F) and write h = γ c with γ ∈ U(J, F) and c ∈ C. Whenever X ∈ S satisfies h−1 X = c−1 (γ−1 X) ∈ K, applying c places the rational tuple γ−1 X inside C · K = K′, hence in P. Since X ↦ γ−1 X is injective on En × M, the fibre count ∑X ∈ S 1K(h−1 X) is at most |P| for every h ∈ U(J, 𝔸F). Integrating this uniform constant over 𝓕 and dividing by 0 < μ(𝓕) < ∞ yields θS(K) ≤ |P| < ∞, proving local finiteness.

Finally, given β ∈ F× and the 1 × 1 Hermitian matrix T = (β), the scalar identification and coordinate-Haar results from the local Gram-fibre construction show that each finite local measure mv(T) is locally finite, so mv(T)(Λv) < ∞ for every compact lattice Λv. Because β ∈ F× is a local unit at all but finitely many finite places v, the binary local mass bound gives |mv(T)(Λv) − 1| ≤ C1 / qv2 cofinally. Since ∑v qv−2 converges over all finite places v of the number field F, the deviations |mv(T)(Λv) − 1| are summable. The normalized theta measure θSβ is locally finite (by part 2), U(J, 𝔸F)-invariant, and supported on the T-fibre; repeating the place-insertion renormalization proof from the Euler-product factorization directly from this summability produces a scalar c ≥ 0 satisfying the stated Euler-product identity. ∎

Uniform compact-set counting bound in the definite binary plane

Worked check (definite binary unitary quotient and quadratic prime tail). Take E = ℚ(i), F = ℚ, and J = I2, which is positive definite and hence anisotropic over ℚ(i) since |z1|2 + |z2|2 = 0 forces z1 = z2 = 0. Every lattice in the genus of ℤ[i]2 has real covolume 1 in ℝ4; since the 4-ball of radius √2 has volume 2π2 > 16 = 24, Minkowski's theorem supplies a nonzero lattice vector of squared norm strictly below 2, hence equal to 1, splitting the lattice isometrically as I2. Thus the compact subgroup C = U(2) × ∏p U(I2, ℤp ⊗ℤ ℤ[i]) already covers U(I2, ℚ) ∖ U(I2, 𝔸ℚ).
