Why mark a maximal lattice before counting smaller ones?

Fix a totally positive definite Hermitian space of rank r ≥ 1 over the CM field E of degree D over ℚ. Assume E is unramified at finite places over its real subfield. For an integral full lattice L let L∨ be the lattice of vectors pairing integrally with all of L. Write n = |L∨/L|. The quantity to bound is the sum of 1/|Aut(L)| over isometry classes with this fixed order. Positive definiteness at infinity makes each stabilizer finite. A change of coordinates may identify many of the smaller lattices, so we keep these weights throughout.

Every integral lattice L is contained in a maximal integral lattice A. Indeed any integral enlargement lies between L and L∨, whose quotient is finite. If i = [A:L] is the cardinality of the quotient, duality gives [L∨:A∨] = i. To check the index identity locally, diagonalize the inclusion of the two lattices over the discrete valuation ring. In the dual bases the corresponding inclusion has the conjugate elementary divisors. Conjugate prime ideals have equal norms, so multiplication over the finite places gives the same global index. The chain L ⊆ A ⊆ A∨ ⊆ L∨ gives

|L∨/L| = [A:L]² |A∨/A| = i² n₀.

The local classification of maximal integral Hermitian lattices applies here. In this fixed space they are all in one genus, meaning that at every finite place there is a local isometry between any two. The places with a nontrivial dual quotient are determined by the determinant of the ambient form. At other places the maximal lattices are unimodular. At a bad place the classification compares two maximal lattices by extending each to a unimodular lattice in one extra dimension and then aligning and removing the added line by an integral isometry. In particular n₀ is common to the maximal lattices. If any L with order n exists, its index in every maximal lattice containing it is therefore the same positive integer i.

Mark the pair (L,A). For a fixed L, its finite automorphism group permutes its maximal enlargements. An orbit of b marks contributes b/|Aut(L)| to the pair mass, by orbit–stabilizer. Since there is at least one mark, forgetting A gives the original mass as a lower bound on the pair mass. Counting pairs in the other order gives an upper bound. Over each maximal representative A, there are at most as many marks as sublattices of additive index i, each counted with weight 1/|Aut(A)| before passing to orbits.

How many sublattices have that index? We claim that a projective rank-r module over the integers of E has at most irDΩ(i) submodules of index i, where Ω counts rational prime factors with multiplicity. Index one has just the module itself. For i > 1 fix a rational prime p dividing it. The p-primary part of each quotient of cardinality i has a simple quotient over some prime ideal 𝔭 above p. This puts the submodule inside an intermediate kernel with quotient the residue field of size q = pf. Modulo 𝔭 the original module has dimension r because it is locally free. There are (qr−1)/(q−1) possible kernels, one for each nonzero functional up to scalar. This is at most qr.

Each intermediate kernel is again a torsion-free rank-r lattice over a Dedekind domain and hence projective. Apply induction inside it at index i/q. For any given 𝔭 the product of the two bounds is at most irDΩ(i)−1, since f ≥ 1. There are at most D primes above p. Adding the bounds proves the claim even when different intermediate kernels count the same submodule. Since 2Ω(i) ≤ i, the number is at most ir+log₂D.

Count and mass in a split example. In ℤ[i]² with the standard form, consider sublattices of index 5. There are two primes above 5. At each one the possible kernels are the six lines of 𝔽₅², giving twelve sublattices. Each has discriminant order 25 by the index square. The automorphisms of the standard lattice permute the two coordinate lines and may multiply each by one of the four Gaussian units, giving 2·4² = 32 automorphisms. The pairs lying over this one maximal class thus have mass 12/32 = 3/8. The extension ℚ(i)/ℚ ramifies at 2. Here the example tests the marking and index identities at the split prime 5.

The genus mass at a fixed discriminant order

Let cE,r be the genus mass of the standard orthonormal lattice of rank r over E. The standard lattice may lie in another Hermitian space. The maximal-lattice mass formula has the same constant cE,r for all totally positive definite spaces of this rank. The distinction between spaces enters through the factors at bad places. More precisely, a bad prime ideal of E is inert over a place of the real field of residue size q. The maximal lattice has a one-dimensional residue defect there, so the place contributes q² to n₀ and

λq = (qr − (−1)r)/(q + 1)

to its genus mass relative to cE,r. At unimodular places the factor is 1. In the local density formula the exceptional Jordan component has length one. Its isometries are the norm-one elements in the quadratic residue field, a group of size q+1. This gives the denominator. The last factor in the standard rank-r unitary density is 1−(−1)r/qr, which gives the numerator after scaling by qr. The global mass formula and this local classification are the inputs in this comparison. W. T. Gan, J. P. Hanke and J.-K. Yu, On an exact mass formula of Shimura, Propositions 4.4 and 4.5, p. 110.

For q ≥ 2 the local factor is positive and at most qr, since even qr+1 is at most (q+1)qr. Multiplying gives a maximal genus mass at most cE,r n₀r/2. Put λ = log₂D. The marked-pair count now bounds the desired mass by cE,r ir+λ n₀r/2. Because n₀ ≥ 1 and λ ≥ 0, we may add λ/2 to its exponent and use n = i²n₀. We obtain

Σ[L], |L∨/L|=n 1/|Aut(L)| ≤ cE,r n(r+log₂D)/2.

An empty set of lattices contributes zero. In the primitive-sublattice count the formula is used twice. For a fixed j-dimensional subspace U the discriminant order of its intersection lattice is exactly n. For the perpendicular space of dimension s the order lies among the integers from 1 to X = nN₀, where N₀ is the order of the ambient discriminant module. For the row at a completely split rational prime T, we have N₀ = t² with t = Tf and [E:ℚ] = 2f. The second application therefore costs at most X times the rank-s bound evaluated at X. The other factors in the row estimate count subspaces and gluings.
