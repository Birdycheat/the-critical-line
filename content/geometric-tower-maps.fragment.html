Which maps of abelian schemes give the transitions in the tower?

Fix the standard base over the CM field E and the auxiliary rational prime p. For each integer N ≥ 3 prime to p, choose a moduli scheme XN over E with universal abelian scheme aN ∶ AN → XN. A point of XN specifies an abelian variety with an action of the order in the unitary datum, a polarization compatible with its involution, and a level marking. In a family the polarization is recorded by a symmetric line bundle on the fibre square, trivial on the zero axes and relatively ample on the diagonal. The action on tangent spaces has the prescribed labelled multiplicities. The marking αN sends the fixed lattice Λ into torsion sections. At each geometric point it extends to compatible torsion coordinates respecting the action and the Weil pairings. In particular it identifies Λ/NΛ with the N-torsion there.

Existence of the generic model. The Harris–Taylor integral moduli theorem used here supplies a representing scheme with its universal object at each such level over a Noetherian base satisfying the integral datum. A Noetherian ℚ-algebra with the standard labels qualifies since all nonzero integers are units. In characteristic zero the comparison of moduli problems keeps the abelian scheme, its action, line bundle and sections. The full Tate-coordinate theorem supplies the required coordinates on every geometric fibre. Conversely, the polarization kernel condition and the prime-to-p torsion coordinates recover the integral moduli conditions. Both descriptions use the same Cartesian maps preserving the structures. Thus a family over an E-scheme S has a unique classifying morphism S → XN, along which its structured abelian scheme is the pullback of the universal one. Representability of the integral problem and the two torsion comparisons are inputs to this deduction. The classical construction at the unitary levels is given in M. Harris and R. Taylor, The Geometry and Cohomology of Some Simple Shimura Varieties, Chapter III.

The pair to construct. Write KN for the principal subgroup of level N and Λ̂ for the adelic lattice away from p. An adelic element h gives a transition from N′ to N when hΛ̂ ⊆ Λ̂ and hKN′h−1 ⊆ KN. Realizing this action on the moduli data requires a base map πh ∶ XN′ → XN over E and a map of total spaces Φh ∶ AN′ → AN. To specify the second map, pull back the target abelian scheme to XN′. We seek an isogeny ψh from AN′ to πh∗AN. Composing it with the projection to AN gives Φh and proves the equality

aN ∘ Φh = πh ∘ aN′.

The isogeny commutes with the order action. It pulls back the target polarization bundle to a positive tensor power of the source bundle, whose exponent is prime to p and agrees with the similitude multiplier of h up to an integral adelic unit away from p. The torsion condition uses geometric coordinates extending the source and target markings. For every positive m prime to p, the source point labelled x maps to the target point labelled y precisely when hx ≡ y modulo mΛ̂. This specifies the action even when the source and target levels differ.

Building the isogeny at a deeper level. Clear denominators in the inverse action of h by an integer k > 0 prime to p. The lattice construction supplies compatible additive maps Fm on Λ with hFm(x) ≡ kx modulo mΛ̂, together with the order and pairing compatibilities. Work first at level Nk. The subgroup of sections by which to quotient has elements αNk(N FNk(x)) for x ∈ Λ. It is a subgroup by additivity, and multiplication by k sends every one of its elements to zero because αNk kills NkΛ. The Hecke quotient theorem for a locally Noetherian base gives the quotient ψ ∶ A → A′ with its descended polarization and level-N marking. Multiplication by k on A is constant on the fibres of ψ and descends to a map χ ∶ A′ → A. Thus χ ∘ ψ = [k]. Composing with ψ on the left and using that ψ is a surjective quotient gives ψ ∘ χ = [k] on A′. In characteristic zero such a pair gives a finite étale surjective isogeny. The universal property of XN now classifies the quotient family.

The quotient is first available with this additional marking. To use it over the original source, one must show that changing the extra marking leaves both maps unchanged. The descent argument below proves this on the overlap of two lifts. It produces the pair (πh, Φh) at the required level.

Uniqueness and composition when the maps exist. Two transitions for the same h agree on geometric points by the marked-isogeny criterion applied with the identity change of marking. Reducedness of the source and separatedness of the target give equality of the base maps. The same argument on the smooth total spaces of the abelian schemes gives equality of Φ. In particular an element of KN at unchanged level induces the identity. If the first step has label h and the second h′, pull the second isogeny back along the first base map and compose. A possible change u between the intermediate torsion coordinates belongs to the middle level subgroup. Admissibility of h′ puts h′u−1(h′)−1 in the final level subgroup, where it can be absorbed in the target coordinates. Thus the composite acts on the marked lattice by h′h. Uniqueness gives

πh′h = πh′ ∘ πh,   Φh′h = Φh′ ∘ Φh.

For a simple case, take p = 5, source level 12, target level 3 and h = 1. If a lattice vector gives a point P of order 12 in the original marking, it gives 4P of order 3 in the new marking. The abelian variety over this geometric point is the same. The map of moduli schemes forgets the finer marking. This is the transition that yields Cartesian squares when one passes to the fibre powers.
