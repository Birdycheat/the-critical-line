How does one trace approximation give a map of lattices?

Fix a precision ℓn. Write R = ℤℓ, F = ℚℓ and Ω for an algebraic closure of F. Let r be the continuous semisimple representation of the compact group Γ, of dimension a. Its matrix lattice is M = ∑g Rr(g). The translated trace test has a fixed loss of c digits. Choose k ≥ 0 such that the traces of ℓkM are integral and put N = n + k. The invariant lattice already constructed permits k = 0.

Assume that the character of r can be approximated uniformly on Γ to arbitrary precision by finite linear combinations of characters of continuous representations of dimension a. At this precision choose a single list ri and weights wi ∈ Ω that work for every g. Each representation has a finite field of definition Ei containing a prescribed finite extension L0 of ℚℓ. For the estimate, take a finite extension E′ containing all entries of these representations and of r, and all the weights. The required error is

δ(g) = tr r(g) − ∑i wi tr ri(g) ∈ ℓN + c𝒪E′.

If E′ has ramification index e, this ideal is the e(N + c)-th power of its maximal ideal. The weights themselves may have denominators.

Lemma. For f = ∑h fh[h] in the group ring R[Γ], write T(f) for the tuple with components ∑h fh ri(h). Define r(f) by the same sum for the target. If T(f) = 0, then r(f) ∈ ℓNM.

Indeed, multiply the i-th zero matrix on the right by ri(g) and take its trace. The products have arguments hg. After weighting and summing these zero traces we obtain

tr(r(f) r(g)) = ∑h fh δ(hg) ∈ ℓN + c𝒪E′.

Every coefficient fh is integral. The left side lies in the field of definition of r and satisfies the same valuation bound there. Applying the trace test proves the lemma.

To turn this observation into a full lattice, choose a lattice Di in each matrix space Mata(Ei) containing the identity and stable under left multiplication by the group. For example, take the sum of the left translates of Mata(𝒪Eᵢ). Compactness bounds their entries, so the sum lies in one finitely generated lattice. Write D for their product and A for the image of T. Thicken the tuple span to Λ = A + ℓtD. It spans the entire product over F, and it is finite free over the discrete valuation ring R. Stacks Project, Lemma 15.22.11.

Why can we still evaluate an element of Λ in the target? The submodules {x ∈ D | ℓjx ∈ A} increase with j. Since D is Noetherian, they stabilize at some u. If ℓtx ∈ A and t ≥ N + u, then ℓux ∈ A, hence ℓtx ∈ ℓNA. Thus whenever T(f) ∈ ℓtD, we can subtract ℓNf′ to obtain an exact tuple relation. The lemma gives r(f) ∈ ℓNM. Consequently T(f) + y ↦ r(f) modulo ℓNM is independent of the chosen decomposition, for y ∈ ℓtD.

Choose such a decomposition for each basis element of Λ and send it to the actual matrix r(f). Extending linearly gives Π from Λ to M. On D the functional λ(x) = ∑i wi tr xi is bounded since it has finitely many values on a basis to bound. Increase t until λ(ℓtD) ⊆ ℓn𝒪E′. For each chosen basis element, tr(Πx) − λ(x) is now a sum of the errors δ(h) with integral coefficients, minus λ(y). It belongs to ℓn𝒪E′, as it therefore does for every x ∈ Λ. The depth t can depend on the approximation and on its weights.

Which maps are compatible when the precision increases?

The family chosen at precision ℓn + 1 may use entirely new representations. Keep all earlier coordinates. If Sn denotes the finite support of the current approximation, use the union Un = ⋃j ≤ n Sj for the tuple, with weight zero on coordinates outside Sn. Use the same field and lattice whenever a representation reappears. The previous proof allows every depth above a threshold, so choose the depths tn nondecreasing. Let Λn, Πn and Tn denote the resulting lattices, evaluation maps and tuples.

Tower lemma. Forgetting the new coordinates defines an integral map fn from Λn + 1 to Λn. It commutes exactly with the action of Γ. Both maps from Λn + 1 to M give the same answer modulo ℓnM. At each level Πn induces an equivariant surjection after reduction modulo ℓn. Its underlying module map has a right inverse obtained by choosing lifts.

Indeed, restriction sends Tn + 1(b) to Tn(b) for each group-ring element b. It sends the error lattice into the preceding error lattice because the depth increases. The action at both levels is left multiplication, component by component. If σn denotes this action and m(g) sends X to r(g)X on M, we obtain

fn σn + 1(g) = σn(g) fn,
Πn fn ≡ Πn + 1,   Πn σn(g) ≡ m(g) Πn   (mod ℓn).

For the first congruence both sides evaluate the same b. For the second, left multiplication replaces b by [g]b, whose target is r(g)r(b). To construct a section modulo ℓn, express each basis element of M as r(b) and send it to Tn(b). The composite agrees with the identity modulo ℓn. Similarly, Πn takes the identity tuple to the identity matrix modulo ℓn.

A transition of index ℓ. Take the trivial group and the trivial representation of rank one, both for the target and for a single auxiliary with weight one. Take L0 equal to the unramified quadratic extension of F and write its ring of integers as R ⊕ Rω. With depth n, the target is M = R and the layer is Λn = R ⊕ ℓnRω. Choose Πn(x + ℓnyω) = x. Its section sends x to x, its value at 1 is 1, and its difference from the weighted trace is −ℓnyω. All actions are trivial. The transition is inclusion and commutes with these projections even before reduction. Its image has index ℓ because its second coordinate acquires an extra factor of ℓ. Thus the surjectivity asserted by the lemma belongs to the maps to M/ℓnM. This example checks the lattice maps independently of any local period comparison.

What are the local representations in a rationalized layer?

Fix a finite extension K of F and a continuous homomorphism from its absolute Galois group GK to Γ. Restrict each action along this homomorphism. The lattices, identity elements, trace functionals and transition maps all stay the same because their identities hold for every element of Γ, including the image of every element of GK. In particular one uses the lattice already built from the global semisimple representation. The restriction itself can be reducible and need not be semisimple.

Column decomposition. Write Vi = Eia with the restricted action of ri. Tensoring Λn with F gives the whole product of matrix spaces over its finite list of fields, since Λn contains the full scaled lattice ℓtₙD. Left multiplication acts on each of the a columns by the same ri. Reading the columns is therefore an equivariant isomorphism

Λn ⊗R F ≃ ⨁i ∈ Uₙ (ResEᵢ/F Vi)⊕a.

Here restriction of scalars means viewing the same vectors as an F-space. For a = 2 one matrix has two columns and contributes two copies, even when its coordinate has approximation weight zero. The layer has dimension a2 ∑i [Ei : F]. The scalars wi enter the trace functional alone.

For the application, require L0 to contain the images of all embeddings of K in Ω. Fix for each such embedding τ a finite multiset of integers Jτ. Assume every auxiliary restriction is semistable and has these labelled filtration multiplicities in each finite matrix model over a coefficient field containing L0. Write H for a common bound on |h| for h ∈ Jτ. The semistable period invariants commute with finite direct sums, and forgetting the coefficient-field structure preserves the dimension equality that defines semistability. The same argument applies to crystalline invariants when every auxiliary is crystalline. Consequently each rationalized layer is semistable, and it is crystalline under the stronger assumption.

The factors of K ⊗F Ei are indexed by the embeddings of K because Ei contains L0. In each factor of the de Rham period space the filtration starts with the whole space at −H and vanishes at H + 1. Indeed all graded pieces outside that interval vanish, and the filtration is finite and exhaustive. Reading each column proves the same two bounds on every rationalized layer. These bounds are uniform even when the number of columns or the degrees of their fields vary.

What the period comparison must supply. The bounds allow one to ask for an integral Breuil–Kisin frame of uniformly bounded height for each layer, with de Rham compatibility and the crystalline condition when applicable. To pass to the target one needs a further theorem. It constructs a limiting frame for the fixed representation on M, with a single bound on the loss of precision and, for each precision n, a comparison with some layer of index m ≥ n. The adapted basis at a single level explains how the bound on the filtration enters the period calculation. Constructing the Breuil–Kisin frames and their cocycles, producing the comparison maps, and preserving the period conditions in the limit require further arguments.

Once that theorem gives de Rham periods for M ⊗R F, they pass to the target representation. Indeed, take a finite field of definition L containing L0. The restricted matrices have entries in L. By the common coefficient field lemma, one finite extension L′ of L contains the entries of every matrix in M. Choose an L-linear projection p from L′ to L fixing 1. For v in an F-basis of La, the map X ↦ p(Xv) applies p coordinate by coordinate. It is equivariant because the local action has entries in L, and takes the identity matrix to v. Together these maps give a surjection from a finite sum of copies onto the target. De Rham and crystalline admissibility pass to quotients. Brinon and Conrad, CMI Summer School Notes on p-adic Hodge Theory, Theorem 5.2.1(2).

The exact multiset at each embedding still requires the closure argument for fixed weights. It compares powers of the Hodge–Tate operators through the period maps and the weighted traces. Degree zero normalizes the total weight in the limit. For a nonzero period value z of infinite multiplicative order, the remaining comparisons identify the power sums of the numbers zh. Newton’s identities recover their multiset in characteristic zero, and distinct integers give distinct powers of z. With the period theorems in place, the target is de Rham with precisely the prescribed multiplicities. It is also crystalline if every auxiliary restriction is crystalline.
