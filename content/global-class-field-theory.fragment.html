Global Artin reciprocity and kernel identification with idèle-class norms

Why do the local Artin norm residue symbols across all finite and infinite places of a number field K multiply to 1 on every principal idèle diag(a), and why is the kernel of the resulting global homomorphism on the idèle class group CK equal to the idèle-class norm subgroup NL/K(CL)? For a finite abelian extension L/K, the local norm residue symbols assemble into a continuous surjective global idèle symbol θL/K : 𝕀K → Gal(L/K), normalized by arithmetic Frobenius at unramified finite places. Over ℚ, direct evaluation of the local symbols at ∞, p, and every distinct prime q ≠ p shows that θL/ℚ(diag(a)) fixes every root of unity, establishing the product formula for all cyclotomic extensions of ℚ. For an arbitrary finite abelian extension L/K inside an algebraic closure ℚ̄, the cyclic class-field axiom endows the inductive limit of idèle class groups with an abstract class-formation reciprocity map CK → Gal(L/K)ab whose kernel is NL/K(CL). Because the abstract map and the local-product symbol agree on single-place idèles and both vanish on the open subgroup NL/K(𝕀L), density of single-place products forces the two maps to coincide everywhere on 𝕀K. That single identity delivers both the global product formula θL/K(diag(a)) = 1 and the exact kernel NL/K(CL), inducing the Artin reciprocity isomorphism CK/NL/K(CL) ≅ Gal(L/K) used in the Hilbert class field identification.

1. Global product formula and the Artin reciprocity isomorphism. Let K and L be number fields with L/K a finite abelian Galois extension, 𝕀K the idèle group of K, diag : K× ↪ 𝕀K the diagonal embedding, and CK = 𝕀K/diag(K×) the idèle class group (with the quotient topology). Write θL/K : 𝕀K → Gal(L/K) for the global idèle symbol formed as the product of the local norm residue symbols over all finite and infinite places of K, with arithmetic Frobenius normalization at unramified finite places.

Proposition (Global Artin reciprocity and idèle-class norm index).

(i) For every a ∈ K×, the global idèle symbol vanishes on diag(a), and its kernel in 𝕀K is the product of principal idèles and idèle norms:

θL/K(diag(a)) = 1   (∀ a ∈ K×),   ker(θL/K) = diag(K×) · NL/K(𝕀L).

(ii) Consequently θL/K descends to a continuous surjective homomorphism θ̄L/K : CK → Gal(L/K) with ker(θ̄L/K) = NL/K(CL), inducing a group isomorphism and the abelian norm-index equality

CK / NL/K(CL) ≅ Gal(L/K),   [CK : NL/K(CL)] = [L : K].

Proof. First suppose K ⊆ L are finite subfields of ℚ̄ with L/K abelian Galois. Let f : 𝕀K → Gal(L/K)ab be the composite of the quotient map 𝕀K ↠ CK with the abstract class-formation reciprocity homomorphism CK → Gal(L/K)ab (built from the Tate cohomology isomorphism Ĥ0(Gal(L/K), CL) ≅ Gal(L/K)ab supplied by the class-field axiom of cyclic Tate vanishing), and let g : 𝕀K → Gal(L/K)ab be the composite of θL/K with the abelianization map Gal(L/K) → Gal(L/K)ab. Both f and g agree on every single-place embedding ιv(a) at each finite place v and each infinite place w (normalized via the rational cyclotomic product formula below), so they agree on the subgroup Hloc ⊆ 𝕀K generated by all single-place idèles. Moreover, both f and g vanish on the idèle norm subgroup NL/K(𝕀L): for f because the abstract symbol vanishes on NL/K(CL), and for g because each local symbol vanishes on local norms. Since NL/K(𝕀L) is open in 𝕀K (detailed below) and Hloc is dense in 𝕀K, every idèle x ∈ 𝕀K factors as x = h · NL/K(y) with h ∈ Hloc and y ∈ 𝕀L. Evaluating gives f(x) = f(h) = g(h) = g(x), so f = g on all of 𝕀K.

Because Gal(L/K) is abelian, the abelianization map Gal(L/K) → Gal(L/K)ab is injective. For every a ∈ K×, the class [diag(a)] = 1 in CK gives g(diag(a)) = f(diag(a)) = 1, hence θL/K(diag(a)) = 1. Thus θL/K descends to a homomorphism θ̄L/K : CK → Gal(L/K) whose composition with abelianization equals the abstract reciprocity map. By the abstract Tate Ĥ0 reciprocity isomorphism, the kernel of the abstract map is precisely NL/K(CL); injectivity of abelianization therefore yields ker(θ̄L/K) = NL/K(CL), and lifting to 𝕀K gives ker(θL/K) = diag(K×) · NL/K(𝕀L) (if [x] = [NL/K(y)], then NL/K(y)−1x ∈ diag(K×)).

For arbitrary number fields K and L with L/K finite abelian, choosing an embedding L ↪ ℚ̄ identifies K ≅ K′ ⊆ L′ ≅ L with finite subfields of ℚ̄, and functoriality of θ and idèle norms transports both θL/K(diag(a)) = 1 and ker(θL/K) ⊆ diag(K×) · NL/K(𝕀L) back to L/K (the reverse inclusion holds because diag(K×) and NL/K(𝕀L) both lie in ker(θL/K)). Quotienting by diag(K×) defines the continuous homomorphism θ̄L/K : CK → Gal(L/K) (continuity follows from continuity of θL/K and the quotient topology on CK) with ker(θ̄L/K) = NL/K(CL). Surjectivity of θL/K implies surjectivity of θ̄L/K, so the first isomorphism theorem gives CK/NL/K(CL) ≅ Gal(L/K). Taking cardinalities yields [CK : NL/K(CL)] = |Gal(L/K)| = [L : K].

Rational cyclotomic product formula, openness of the idèle norm subgroup, transfer from ℚ̄, and verification on ℚ(i)/ℚ

To establish the product formula for a cyclotomic extension L/ℚ, fix a prime p, an integer n ≥ 0, and a root of unity ζ ∈ L with ζpn = 1. Under the arithmetic Frobenius normalization, the local symbol at ∞ sends −1 to complex conjugation ζ ↦ ζ−1 and fixes positive rationals; at the ramified place v0 = (p), the local symbol maps p to the identity on ζ and maps a p-adic unit u ∈ ℤp× to ζ ↦ ζu−1 mod pn; and at every distinct prime q ≠ p, the unramified local symbol sends the uniformizer q to arithmetic Frobenius ζ ↦ ζq and kills q-adic units. Evaluating the global product on generators of ℚ×: on diag(−1), the factors at ∞ and p each invert ζ while all other primes act trivially, so their product fixes ζ; on diag(p), only p acts (trivially); and on diag(q) for q ≠ p, the symbol at q raises ζ to the q-th power while the symbol at p raises the result to the (q−1 mod pn)-th power, again fixing ζ. Because −1 and all rational primes generate ℚ×, the stabilizer subgroup of ζ under a ↦ θL/ℚ(diag(a)) is all of ℚ×. Multiplicative induction over coprime factors extends the fixed-point property to every m-th root of unity ζ (m ≠ 0), and because a cyclotomic extension is generated by roots of unity, θL/ℚ(diag(a)) = 1.

Openness of NL/K(𝕀L) inside 𝕀K follows from the restricted product topology: at each infinite place w, im Nw is open in (Kw)×; at each finite place v, im Nv ∩ 𝒪Kv× is open in (Kv)×; and at all but finitely many finite places v (the unramified ones, as in local-to-idelic assembly), local integer units surject onto 𝒪Kv×, so im Nv ∩ 𝒪Kv× = 𝒪Kv×. The resulting basic open neighborhood of 1 lies inside NL/K(𝕀L) by local-to-idelic assembly. For the embedding transfer K ≅ K′ ⊆ L′ ≅ L in ℚ̄, bijectivity of K → K′ gives [K′ : K] = 1, so the idèle norm satisfies NK′/K(ιK′/K(x)) = x; compatibility of θ with restriction of automorphisms and norms immediately yields θL/K(diag(a)) = 1 and maps diag(K′×) · NL′/K′(𝕀L′) into diag(K×) · NL/K(𝕀L).

As a concrete check on L = ℚ(i) over ℚ (where i22 = 1, p = 2, n = 2): on diag(−1), θ∞(−1)(i) = −i and θ2(−1)(i) = i(−1)−1 mod 4 = i3 = −i, while odd primes act trivially, so the product sends i ↦ −(−i) = i. On diag(3), θ∞(3) = 1, the inert prime 3 gives arithmetic Frobenius θ3(3)(i) = i3 = −i, and the ramified prime 2 gives θ2(3)(i) = i3−1 mod 4 = i3 = −i, whose product again fixes i. On the split prime diag(5), θ5(5)(i) = i5 = i and θ2(5)(i) = i5−1 mod 4 = i, so every local factor is already trivial.

Global existence theorem for abelian extensions from Kummer theory and norm limitation

Given an arbitrary open finite-index subgroup N ⊆ CK of the idèle class group of a number field K, how does one construct a finite abelian extension L/K whose idèle-class norm subgroup is precisely N, even when K contains no roots of unity beyond ±1? The proof combines three mechanisms. First, norm limitation shows that the idèle-class norm subgroup of any finite Galois extension M/K coincides with that of its maximal abelian subextension Mab, so any finite extension L/K (even non-Galois) whose norm group lands inside N can be replaced by a finite abelian extension E/K with NE/K(CE) ⊆ N; Galois theory via global Artin reciprocity then cuts out a subextension of E with norm group equal to N. Second, when K contains primitive n-th roots of unity and CKn ⊆ N, openness of N places a sufficiently large S-unit Kummer norm group inside N, realizing N as the norm group of a subextension of K(𝒪K,S× 1/n). Third, for a general open subgroup N of index m, adjoining primitive m-th roots of unity K′ = K(μm) pulls N back to an open subgroup N′ = NK′/K−1(N) containing CK′m; Kummer realization over K′ yields L′/K′ with NL′/K(CL′) ⊆ N, and norm limitation descends to the desired abelian extension L/K. Together with reciprocity on abelian composita, this establishes the order-reversing bijection between finite abelian subfields of an algebraic closure Ω and open finite-index subgroups of CK used in selecting the Hilbert class field.

1. Norm limitation, Kummer realization, and the global existence correspondence. Let K be a number field with fixed algebraic closure Ω. For any finite Galois extension M/K, write Mab ⊆ M for the maximal abelian subextension over K, namely the fixed field of the commutator subgroup [Gal(M/K), Gal(M/K)].

Proposition (Norm limitation, Kummer realization, and global existence theorem).

(i) Norm limitation. For every finite Galois extension M/K,

NM/K(CM) = NMab/K(CMab),   [CK : NM/K(CM)] = |Gal(M/K)ab|.

In particular, for every finite extension L/K (not necessarily Galois), there exists a finite abelian Galois extension E/K such that NE/K(CE) ⊆ NL/K(CL).

(ii) Kummer realization. If n ≥ 1, K contains a primitive n-th root of unity, and N ⊆ CK is an open subgroup with cn ∈ N for all c ∈ CK, then there exists a finite abelian Galois extension L/K of exponent dividing n (contained in an S-unit Kummer extension K(𝒪K,S× 1/n)) such that NL/K(CL) = N.

(iii) Global existence and order-reversing correspondence. For every finite abelian extension L/K, the norm subgroup NL/K(CL) is open of finite index in CK. Conversely, every open subgroup N ⊆ CK of finite index equals NL/K(CL) for a unique finite abelian intermediate field L ⊆ Ω over K, and the assignment L ↦ NL/K(CL) is an inclusion-reversing order isomorphism:

L1 ⊆ L2   ⟺   NL2/K(CL2) ⊆ NL1/K(CL1).

Proof. For (i), transitivity of the idèle-class norm in the tower K ⊆ Mab ⊆ M gives NM/K(CM) ⊆ NMab/K(CMab). Since Mab/K is abelian with Galois group Gal(M/K)ab, the abelian norm-index equality gives [CK : NMab/K(CMab)] = [Mab : K] = |Gal(M/K)ab| > 0. On the other hand, in the abstract class formation on ℚ̄ (transferred back to M/K via an embedding into ℚ̄), the kernel of the abstract reciprocity map CK → Gal(M/K)ab lies inside NM/K(CM), so [CK : NM/K(CM)] divides |Gal(M/K)ab|. Because NM/K(CM) ⊆ NMab/K(CMab), the reverse divisibility [CK : NMab/K(CMab)] ∣ [CK : NM/K(CM)] holds as well. Antisymmetry of divisibility forces equality of finite indices, so the relative index [NMab/K(CMab) : NM/K(CM)] equals 1, proving NM/K(CM) = NMab/K(CMab). For an arbitrary finite extension L/K, let M be the normal closure of L/K inside an algebraic closure of L and set E = Mab; then E/K is finite abelian and NE/K(CE) = NM/K(CM) ⊆ NL/K(CL).

For (ii), suppose K contains a primitive n-th root of unity and N ⊆ CK is open with CKn ⊆ N. Choosing a sufficiently large finite set S of finite places of K (containing all places dividing n, the support of a ray congruence subgroup U(𝔪) inside the preimage of N, and a finite set S0 whose S0-idèles generate CK modulo diag(K×); detailed below) ensures that the image in CK of the n-th power idèle subgroup P(n, S) lies inside N. By Kummer norm calculation, the S-unit Kummer extension M = K(𝒪K,S× 1/n) is finite abelian of exponent dividing n with norm group NM/K(CM) = [P(n, S)] ⊆ N. Taking the fixed field L = Mθ̄M/K(N) inside M gives a finite abelian extension L/K whose exponent divides n and whose norm group is precisely N (since c ∈ NL/K(CL) if and only if θ̄M/K(c) fixes L, i.e., θ̄M/K(c) ∈ θ̄M/K(N), which holds if and only if c ∈ N · ker(θ̄M/K) = N · NM/K(CM) = N).

For (iii), let N ⊆ CK be an open subgroup of finite index m = [CK : N] ≥ 1. Let K′ = K(μm) be the m-th cyclotomic extension of K, which contains a primitive m-th root of unity, and set N′ = NK′/K−1(N) ⊆ CK′. By continuity of NK′/K, N′ is open in CK′, and for every c ∈ CK′ we have NK′/K(cm) = NK′/K(c)m ∈ N because CK/N has order m; thus cm ∈ N′. Applying (ii) over K′ produces a finite abelian extension L′/K′ with NL′/K′(CL′) = N′. By norm transitivity, NL′/K(CL′) = NK′/K(N′) ⊆ N. Applying (i) to the finite extension L′/K yields a finite abelian extension E/K with NE/K(CE) ⊆ NL′/K(CL′) ⊆ N, and taking the fixed field L0 = Eθ̄E/K(N) realizes NL0/K(CL0) = N. Embedding L0 ↪ Ω via an algebraically closed lift produces a finite abelian intermediate field L ⊆ Ω with NL/K(CL) = N. Conversely, for any finite abelian L/K, NL/K(CL) = ker(θ̄L/K) is open (preimage of {1} under continuous θ̄L/K into discrete Gal(L/K)) and of finite index [L : K]. Finally, for two finite abelian subfields L1, L2 ⊆ Ω, if L1 ⊆ L2 then norm transitivity gives NL2/K(CL2) ⊆ NL1/K(CL1). Conversely, inside the finite abelian compositum M = L1 ⊔ L2, each norm subgroup equals θ̄M/K−1(Gal(M/Li)); surjectivity of θ̄M/K turns NL2/K(CL2) ⊆ NL1/K(CL1) into Gal(M/L2) ⊆ Gal(M/L1), whence L1 ⊆ L2 by Galois theory. Antisymmetry yields uniqueness of L and the order-reversing isomorphism.

Ray class fields, prime splitting laws, and Hilbert unramified reciprocity

How do the idèlic reciprocity and existence theorems specialize to classical ideal ray class groups, prime splitting criteria, and the Hilbert class field? Given a modulus 𝔪 = (𝔪0, 𝔪∞) of a number field K, weak approximation allows every idèle class to be represented by an idèle congruent to 1 modulo 𝔪, identifying the idèlic quotient CK/[U(𝔪)] with the finite ideal ray class group Cl𝔪(K) = I(𝔪)/P𝔪. By the global existence theorem, [U(𝔪)] cuts out a unique finite abelian ray class field K(𝔪) ⊆ Ω whose Galois group identifies via global Artin reciprocity with Cl𝔪(K), sending each prime ideal class [𝔭] (for 𝔭 ∤ 𝔪0) to arithmetic Frobenius Frob𝔭. At every finite prime 𝔭 ∤ 𝔪0, local units lie in U(𝔪) and hence in the kernel of the local Artin symbol, which forces 𝔭 to be unramified in K(𝔪) and makes complete splitting equivalent to Frob𝔭 = 1, i.e., [𝔭] = 1 in Cl𝔪(K). For the trivial modulus 𝔪 = (1, ∅), U(1) consists of all finite-unit idèles with unrestricted real components, Cl1(K) is the ordinary ideal class group Cl(𝒪K), and every abelian extension unramified at all finite and infinite places has trivial idèle symbol on U(1), placing it inside the Hilbert class field H = K(1) used throughout the Artin character comparison.

1. Ideal ray class isomorphism, ray class fields, splitting law, and everywhere-unramified extensions. Let K be a number field with fixed algebraic closure Ω. A modulus 𝔪 = (𝔪0, 𝔪∞) consists of a nonzero integral ideal 𝔪0 ⊆ 𝒪K and a set 𝔪∞ of real infinite places of K. Write U(𝔪) ⊆ 𝕀K for its congruence subgroup (idèles x with xv ∈ 𝒪Kv× at all finite v, xv ≡ 1 mod 𝔭vordv(𝔪0) when v ∣ 𝔪0, and xw > 0 at each real place w ∈ 𝔪∞), I(𝔪) for the group of fractional ideals of 𝒪K coprime to 𝔪0, and P𝔪 ⊆ I(𝔪) for the subgroup of principal ideals (a) generated by elements a ∈ K× satisfying the congruence and positivity conditions of 𝔪 at v ∣ 𝔪0 and w ∈ 𝔪∞. Define the ideal ray class group Cl𝔪(K) = I(𝔪)/P𝔪 and let K(𝔪) ⊆ Ω be the unique finite abelian field with NK(𝔪)/K(CK(𝔪)) = [U(𝔪)]. Say that a finite extension L/K is everywhere unramified if every finite prime w of 𝒪L over v has ramification index e(w/v) = 1 and every infinite place of L over a real place of K is real.

Proposition (Ray class Artin isomorphism, splitting criterion, and everywhere-unramified vanishing).

(i) Idèlic-to-ideal isomorphism and ray Artin map. The fractional-ideal map induces a canonical group isomorphism CK/[U(𝔪)] ≅ Cl𝔪(K) (showing in particular that Cl𝔪(K) is finite), and composing with global Artin reciprocity yields an isomorphism

CK/[U(𝔪)] ≅ Cl𝔪(K) ≅ Gal(K(𝔪)/K),   [𝔭] ↦ Frob𝔭   (𝔭 ∤ 𝔪0).

(ii) Unramifiedness, splitting law, and exhaustiveness. Every finite prime 𝔭 ∤ 𝔪0 is unramified in K(𝔪)/K, and

𝔭 splits completely in K(𝔪)   ⟺   Frob𝔭 = 1   ⟺   [𝔭] = 1 in Cl𝔪(K).

Moreover, every finite abelian intermediate field L ⊆ Ω over K is contained in K(𝔪) for some modulus 𝔪.

(iii) Everywhere-unramified extensions and the Hilbert class field. If L/K is a finite abelian extension that is everywhere unramified, then θL/K(x) = 1 for every idèle x ∈ 𝕀K whose finite components are local integer units (with arbitrary infinite components). Consequently [U(1)] ⊆ NL/K(CL), so L ⊆ K(1) = H for the trivial modulus 𝔪 = (1, ∅).

Proof. For (i), let 𝕀K(𝔪) ⊆ 𝕀K be the subgroup of idèles satisfying the congruence conditions of 𝔪 at all v ∣ 𝔪0 and positivity at all w ∈ 𝔪∞ (unrestricted at v ∤ 𝔪0). Weak approximation on the finitely many places dividing 𝔪0 and the infinite places shows that for every x ∈ 𝕀K there exists a ∈ K× with diag(a)x ∈ 𝕀K(𝔪), so the natural projection 𝕀K(𝔪) → 𝕀K/(diag(K×) · U(𝔪)) ≅ CK/[U(𝔪)] is surjective with kernel 𝕀K(𝔪) ∩ (diag(K×) · U(𝔪)). On the other hand, sending x ∈ 𝕀K(𝔪) to its fractional ideal ∏v 𝔭vordv(x) lands in I(𝔪) and surjects onto I(𝔪) (each prime 𝔭 ∤ 𝔪0 is realized by a single-place uniformizer idèle ι𝔭(π𝔭) ∈ 𝕀K(𝔪)). Its composite 𝕀K(𝔪) ↠ Cl𝔪(K) has the exact same kernel 𝕀K(𝔪) ∩ (diag(K×) · U(𝔪)) (detailed below), giving CK/[U(𝔪)] ≅ Cl𝔪(K). Composing the inverse of this isomorphism with the reciprocity isomorphism CK/[U(𝔪)] = CK/NK(𝔪)/K(CK(𝔪)) ≅ Gal(K(𝔪)/K) produces the Artin isomorphism Cl𝔪(K) ≅ Gal(K(𝔪)/K).

For (ii), fix a finite prime v = 𝔭 with 𝔭 ∤ 𝔪0. For every local unit u ∈ 𝒪Kv×, the single-place idèle ιv(u) lies in U(𝔪), so [ιv(u)] ∈ NK(𝔪)/K(CK(𝔪)) = ker(θ̄K(𝔪)/K); hence the local symbol θv vanishes on 𝒪Kv×. By the local unramifiedness criterion (detailed below), 𝒪Kv× ⊆ ker(θv) forces e(w/v) = 1 for every prime w of K(𝔪) above v, so 𝔭 is unramified in K(𝔪)/K. Furthermore, 𝔭 splits completely in a finite abelian extension L/K if and only if every local degree [Lw : Kv] = 1, which by surjectivity of the local norm residue symbol holds if and only if θv = 1 on all of Kv×. When 𝒪Kv× ⊆ ker(θv), every x ∈ Kv× factors as πvordv(x)u with u ∈ 𝒪Kv×, so θv = 1 on Kv× if and only if θv(πv) = Frob𝔭 = 1. Under the Artin isomorphism Cl𝔪(K) ≅ Gal(K(𝔪)/K), [𝔭] maps to Frob𝔭 (via ιv(πv)), so Frob𝔭 = 1 if and only if [𝔭] = 1 in Cl𝔪(K). Finally, for any finite abelian subfield L ⊆ Ω, NL/K(CL) is open by global existence and therefore contains [U(𝔪)] = NK(𝔪)/K(CK(𝔪)) for some modulus 𝔪; inclusion reversal gives L ⊆ K(𝔪).

For (iii), suppose L/K is finite abelian and everywhere unramified. At every finite place v, e(w/v) = 1 implies 𝒪Kv× ⊆ ker(θv). At every infinite place v, the infinite local symbol θv is trivial if and only if v is unramified in L (trivially when v is complex; when v is real, θv(−1) is the complex conjugation automorphism attached to a place w ∣ v of L, which equals 1 if and only if w is real). Consequently, for any idèle x ∈ 𝕀K whose finite components all lie in 𝒪Kv× (with arbitrary infinite components), every finite and infinite local factor θv(xv) equals 1, so θL/K(x) = 1. Since the trivial modulus 𝔪 = (1, ∅) has U(1) equal to this exact subgroup of finite-unit idèles, [U(1)] ⊆ ker(θ̄L/K) = NL/K(CL), and inclusion reversal in the existence correspondence yields L ⊆ K(1) = H.

Kernel matching for ideal ray classes, the local unramifiedness lemma, and the Hilbert class field of ℚ(√−5)

To check ker(𝕀K(𝔪) ↠ Cl𝔪(K)) = 𝕀K(𝔪) ∩ (diag(K×) · U(𝔪)): if x ∈ 𝕀K(𝔪) has fractional ideal (a) with a ∈ K𝔪,1 (i.e., diag(a) ∈ 𝕀K(𝔪)), then u = diag(a)−1x lies in 𝕀K(𝔪) and has trivial fractional ideal (so uv ∈ 𝒪Kv× for every finite v), hence u ∈ U(𝔪) and x = diag(a)u. Conversely, if x ∈ 𝕀K(𝔪) writes x = diag(a)u with u ∈ U(𝔪) ⊆ 𝕀K(𝔪), then diag(a) = xu−1 ∈ 𝕀K(𝔪) gives a ∈ K𝔪,1, and since u has trivial fractional ideal, the ideal of x equals (a) ∈ P𝔪.

For the local unramifiedness lemma in (ii), let M/k be a finite abelian extension of nonarchimedean local fields with local norm group N = NM/k(M×) satisfying 𝒪k× ⊆ N. Choose uniformizers π ∈ k× and ϖ ∈ M×, and write e and f for the ramification index and residue degree (so ef = [M : k]). Because ordk(NM/k(ϖ)) = f, the ratio π−fNM/k(ϖ) has valuation 0 and therefore belongs to 𝒪k× ⊆ N, forcing πf ∈ N. Meanwhile every x ∈ k× satisfies π−ordk(x)x ∈ 𝒪k× ⊆ N, so the coset of π generates k×/N. By local reciprocity, [M : k] = |k×/N| = ord(π mod N) ≤ f; since ef = [M : k] with e, f ≥ 1, this forces e = 1.

Returning to the field K = ℚ(√−5) from the split-prime example above, take the trivial modulus 𝔪 = (1, ∅). Then Cl1(K) = Cl(𝒪K) ≅ ℤ/2ℤ is generated by the non-principal prime 𝔭 = (2, 1 + √−5), and the Hilbert class field is H = K(1) = ℚ(√−5, i) = ℚ(√−5, √5) of degree 2 over K. Because ℚ(i) and ℚ(√5) have coprime discriminants −4 and 5, H/K is unramified at 2 (via ℚ(√5)) and at 5 (via ℚ(i)) as well as all other finite primes, and all infinite places of K are complex, so H/K is everywhere unramified. By the splitting law (ii), since [𝔭] ≠ 1 in Cl(𝒪K), Frob𝔭 ≠ 1 and 𝔭 remains inert in H (matching irreducibility of x2 − x − 1 modulo 2 in ℚ(√5)). By contrast, 29 = 32 + 5 · 22 factors in 𝒪K into the two principal primes (3 ± 2√−5), whose trivial classes satisfy Frob = 1 and split completely in H (indeed 122 ≡ −1 and 112 ≡ 5 modulo 29).
