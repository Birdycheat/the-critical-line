Where does the strict half-plane come from?

At the identity, let hγ be the positive height of a representative of a rational Siegel coset. The spherical section to be summed has magnitude hγT, with T = Re s + a/2. Arithmetic reduction assigns to each coset a tuple j of a nonnegative integers. For a base B > 1 and a constant C, its height is at most C B−∑j. For any fixed tuple the number of cosets in any finite sample with this label is at most C Ba∑j. Multiplying those bounds gives a bound for the whole label.

∑γ with label j hγT ≤ C1+T (Ba−T)∑j.

For T > a, summing over the tuple gives a product of a geometric series with common ratio Ba−T < 1. This bounds every finite subsum, establishing summability of the nonnegative spherical majorant. Iwasawa decomposition and the bound for the section on the compact subgroup transfer convergence to the flat section and its translates. The terms vary holomorphically in s through powers of positive heights. Choose a small vertical strip around the point, with both edges inside the half-plane. Each term is bounded in absolute value throughout the strip by the sum of its absolute values at the two endpoint real parts, since a positive height raised to a real exponent is monotone in that exponent. Both endpoint majorants are summable. The holomorphic terms therefore converge locally uniformly.
