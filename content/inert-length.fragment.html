How the length of one matrix determines the two levels

Length counts independent denominators in the lattice generated by the standard rows and the rows of the given matrix. Fix the signed outer valuation a, a middle unitary block g of size m and length h, and one unipotent representative with row r, paired column b and upper corner c. In three blocks their product has the form

G = diag(ϖa, g, ϖ−a) · [(1, r, c), (0, I, b), (0, 0, 1)],

where the three parenthesized triples are block rows. The middle lattice supplies integral matrices U,V,C with gC integral, (U + Vg)C = I and v(det C) = h. We use the additive valuation v(ϖ) = 1. For any scalar or row x, let d(x) be the least integer k ≥ 0 for which ϖkx is integral in every coordinate. This includes d(0) = 0. Put

α = max(a,0),   R = d(rC),   S = d(c − r · (CVg)b),   t = max(0,R − α).

The length of G is |a| + h + max(2t, S − 2α). Thus a budget of j above |a| + h allows R ≤ α + ⌊j/2⌋ and S ≤ 2α + j, simultaneously. For a ≥ 0 the allowable levels are e = a + ⌊j/2⌋ and f = 2a + j. For a < 0, the base length still includes −a but the two levels are just ⌊j/2⌋ and j. Derive the length by eliminating rows

In the sum of the two row lattices, combine the middle rows of the identity using U and the middle rows of G using V. These mixed rows form the block (0, C−1, Vgb). Multiplying this block on the left by C and subtracting the standard middle rows leaves pure last coordinates (CVg)b. Likewise, subtracting gC times the mixed rows from the middle rows of G leaves (gCU)b. The original last rows also give the scalars 1 and ϖ−a. The pure last coordinates just listed span the fractional ideal generated by y = ϖ−α−t. Indeed the row and column identity says that a power of ϖ clears rC exactly when it clears both columns (CVg)b and (gCU)b. The largest denominator among those columns, 1 and ϖ−a thus needs α + t powers, and some generator attains that value. Build a triangular block matrix P with these mixed middle rows and last row (0,0,y). For a ≥ 0 keep the first standard row in P and subtract ϖa times it from the first row of G. For a < 0 keep the first row of G and subtract the first standard row from ϖ−a times the retained row. Either way the extra row is (0,ϖαr,ϖαc). The pivot in the first column of P is 1 in the first case and ϖa in the second. The other diagonal blocks are C−1 and y, so −v(det P) = max(−a,0) + h + α + t = |a| + h + t. In the basis of rows of P, the coordinates of the extra row are

ρ = (0, ϖαrC, ϖα(c − r · (CVg)b)/y).

The middle term already contributes ϖαr · (CVg)b in the last column, explaining the subtraction. The largest denominator exponent in ρ is t′ = max(t, S − 2α − t). If t′ is zero, the extra row already belongs to the lattice of P. Otherwise replace a row of P whose coefficient in ρ has valuation −t′ with the extra row. The new determinant is the old determinant times that coefficient. The old row is recovered by dividing by this same coefficient, since its inverse and the ratios of every other coefficient to it are integral. This gives a basis of the full enlarged lattice. Its length is |a| + h + t + t′, the formula above. Equal entry valuations, different lengths. Work over ℚ₃(i), with i² = −1 and conjugation σ(i) = −i. The residue field is 𝔽₉. Take a = 0, g = diag(3,1/3), C = diag(1,3), U = diag(1,0), V = diag(0,1), r = (1,1/3) and b = (−1/3,−1). The middle matrix preserves the anti-diagonal Hermitian form and h = 1. The two corners c₀ = −1/3 and c₁ = (−1 + i)/3 both have valuation −1 and trace −2/3 = r · b, as unitarity requires. The resulting matrices differ in that one entry alone. In both cases rC = (1,1) and r · (CVg)b = −1/3. The corrected corners are 0 and i/3. The formula gives lengths 1 and 2.

One can also count directly modulo the standard lattice. The third row of G is (0,0,1/3,−1/3). After subtracting this row and the first two standard basis vectors from the first row of G, the only remaining entry is c + 1/3 in the last column. The other rows of G are integral. Thus the first matrix adds one residue-field direction. The second adds that same direction and the independent vector (0,0,0,i/3). The respective quotients have 9 and 81 elements.
