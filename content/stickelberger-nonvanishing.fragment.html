The class-group argument needs one depth at which every allowed character leaves a nonzero coefficient. Fix a prime ℓ, an integer f ≥ 1 coprime to ℓ and an odd integer c > 1 coprime to fℓ. In the selected cyclotomic tower f > 1 and c = 2fℓ + 1. At level n put M = fℓn. For a residue s modulo M, let ⟨s⟩ be its representative between 0 and M−1. Each unit supplies a basis element [s] of the group ring. The integral relation uses just one unit from each pair {s,−s}. For odd ℓ let W be the group of (ℓ−1)st roots of unity in ℤℓ. For ℓ = 2 take W = {1,−1}. At depth d ≤ n, the map πd reduces a unit modulo ℓd and then takes its coset modulo the image of W. Convert the coefficients into a difference. Extend χ by zero on nonunits, calling the extension Ψ. Sum g(s)Ψ(s) over all residues s above y modulo ℓd and call the result α(y). Choose one ℓ-adic root r from each pair {r,−r} in W, forming a set R. On units, both g and χ change sign under negation, so their product has the same value at both representatives. The lower-half sum defining Θ can therefore use the representatives selected by R in each quotient fibre. Reduction is injective on W at depth d ≥ 2, and on nonunit fibres α vanishes. Thus vanishing of the twisted image would imply ∑r∈R α(r−1y) = 0 for every y, with r reduced modulo ℓd. This argument chooses representatives directly, which is essential in characteristic two. The function Ψ has additive period fℓ. Adding fℓ preserves the prime divisors shared with M. On a unit s it amounts to multiplying by 1 + fℓ/s, a unit of ℓ-power order. Its character value is 1 because in characteristic ℓ the only ℓ-power root of unity in a field is 1. Set B = cfℓ. Subtracting c from s lowers the residue ⟨c−1s⟩ by one, with wraparound at zero. For these same parameters the general argument gives d = 7. We will then calculate the first working depth directly. There is just one direction, which we may choose as 1, and B = 78 < 128. If all projected coefficients vanished, α would vanish everywhere. At y = 1 the difference equation instead gives α(1)−α(51) = P(1) = 1. This works for every input level n ≥ 7. As a numerical check at n = 7 and χ trivial, the units above 1 are 1 and 257 with coefficients 5 and 0. The units above 51 are 179 and 307 with coefficients 0 and 4. The two fibre sums are therefore 1 and 0 in 𝔽₂. The finite fibre calculation lets you compare cancellations at different levels.

For this example the first depth that works at all later levels is actually 3. Every character in characteristic two is trivial here: the unit group modulo 3·2n has order a power of two, and in a field of characteristic two an element of 2-power order equals 1. At M = 24 the lower-half representatives 1, 5, 7, 11 have coefficients 1, 3, 4, 6. The two cosets modulo 8 are {±1} and {±3}. Their respective sums are 1 + 4 = 5 and 3 + 6 = 9, both odd.

Why does this persist when the modulus doubles? Write gM for the coefficient at modulus M. The two lifts of any unit s have preimages under multiplication by 13 of the form t and t + M. The identity ⌊x⌋ + ⌊x + 13/2⌋ = ⌊2x⌋ + 6, with x = 13t/(2M), gives g2M(s) + g2M(s + M) = gM(s). Thus the sum on each individual fibre y modulo 8 is preserved exactly. The projected sum instead chooses the lower-half member of each pair {s, −s} above {y, −y}. Replacing it by the member above y preserves its parity because gM(−s) = −gM(s). Hence the projected sums stay odd for every n ≥ 3. The calculation modulo 12 already excluded depth 2.
