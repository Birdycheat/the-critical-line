Why does the class product give a derivative at one?

Let the imaginary quadratic host field have fundamental discriminant −d, with d > 4, class number h, and w roots of unity. Write χ for its primitive quadratic character of conductor d. For each ideal class choose its point z in the upper half-plane and rescale the period lattice to Weierstrass discriminant one. Write Az for the area of the resulting fundamental parallelogram. The class mean used in the period calculation is

(1/h) ∑classes log(Az/π) = γ + log(2π/√d) − L′(1,χ)/L(1,χ).

Fix the scale of the lattice. Write it as a(ℤ + zℤ), with y = Im z. Its area is |a|²y. The discriminant on ℤ + zℤ is (2π)¹²η(z)²⁴, and scaling the periods by a multiplies it by a−12. Setting the discriminant to one gives |a|² = 4π²|η(z)|⁴. Thus, for F(z) = √y |η(z)|², we get Az/π = 4π F(z)². The choice of twelfth root disappears upon taking absolute values. Barquero-Sanchez et al., (3.2) and the discriminant formula after (6.1)

Take the logarithm of the class product. Put S = ∑1≤r≤d χ(r) log Γ(r/d). The Chowla–Selberg formula at conductor one gives (2/h) ∑ log F(z) = −log(4π√d) + wS/(2h). The evaluation of the Dirichlet logarithmic derivative at zero gives L′(0,χ)/L(0,χ) = −log d + wS/(2h). Substituting the second identity in the first cancels the gamma sum. Adding log(4π) from the area gives the following equality. Barquero-Sanchez et al., Theorem 5.1 and (5.13)

(1/h) ∑classes log(Az/π) = ½ log d + L′(0,χ)/L(0,χ).

Reflect from zero to one. Write l(s) = L′(s,χ)/L(s,χ). Here L(0,χ) = 2h/w and L(1,χ) ≠ 0. Since χ is real and odd, its completed function is Λ(s) = (d/π)(s+1)/2 Γ((s+1)/2)L(s,χ). The functional equation identifies Λ(s) with a constant times Λ(1−s). The change of variable reverses the sign of the logarithmic derivative. At zero this gives l(0) + l(1) = −log(d/π) − (ψ(½)+ψ(1))/2, where ψ = Γ′/Γ. Inserting ψ(1) = −γ and ψ(½) = −γ − 2 log 2 gives l(0) = γ + log(2π/d) − l(1). The remaining ½ log d in the area mean changes the denominator to √d.
