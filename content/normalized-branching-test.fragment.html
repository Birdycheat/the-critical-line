How do we find a smooth vector that sees the occurrence?

At the start of a single lowering, an occurrence gives a bounded nonzero H-intertwiner j from the smaller discrete series σ into the ambient representation π. The distant occurrence provides such a map for the first pair of parameters. At a later move the preceding lowering provides one for the current pair. In each case the only datum carried into this argument is an intertwiner. We now choose a fresh smooth ambient vector whose coefficient average detects a copy of σ.

Write ρ for π restricted to H, and use inner products linear in the second argument. Fix Haar measure ν on H and let κ = 1/dσ for this measure. For a unit vector w in the lowest compact type of σ put φ(h) = ⟨w,σ(h)w⟩. Let n be the dimension of the smaller Hermitian space and put N = n+1. The gap for this step is 200N³. Here is why this suffices for absolute integrability.

For a noncompact group let q₀ be the smaller signature index, t the Euclidean norm of the logarithms of the singular values of h, and F its Frobenius norm. For t ≥ 1 the lowest-type coefficient estimate gives |φ(h)| ≤ exp(−25N³t/√(2q₀)). The coefficient of t in this exponent has magnitude at least 12N². For t ≤ 1, unitarity gives |φ| ≤ 1. Since F ≤ √n exp(t), both bounds imply |φ| ≤ C F−12N² for a fixed C. The shell count on the smaller group bounds the volume up to radius F by a constant times F4n² for F ≥ 1. The dyadic sum converges since 12N² > 4n². For a compact group the unit bound and finite Haar measure suffice.

Define the bounded averaging operator Ra = ∫Hφ(h)ρ(h)a dν. Since ρ is unitary, ‖R‖ ≤ ∫|φ| dν.

Put b₀ = jw. The kernel of j is closed and invariant. Irreducibility and j ≠ 0 give b₀ ≠ 0. The functional L(a) = ⟨b₀,Ra⟩ already detects this vector. To evaluate it, use ρ(h)jw = jσ(h)w, move j* to the first slot, and apply mixed Schur orthogonality for the unit vector w. This gives

L(b₀) = ∫ φ(h) ⟨j*b₀,σ(h)w⟩ dν = κ ‖b₀‖² > 0.

Choose any unit vector v₀ in the lowest compact type of π. Such vectors are smooth. The closed span of their translates, even starting with this one vector, is the whole ambient Hilbert space by irreducibility of π on G. If L vanished on every π(g)v₀, continuity would force L to vanish on b₀. Hence some ambient translate η is a smooth unit vector with L(η) ≠ 0, and therefore Rη ≠ 0. This step uses translates by the full group G. M. Duflo and C. C. Moore, Schur orthogonality for square-integrable representations.

Which normalization identifies the projected coefficient?

Fix this η. Since φ(h−1) is the complex conjugate of φ(h), inversion invariance of ν gives R* = R. Orthogonality of the unit-vector coefficient also gives φ ∗ φ = κφ. Integrating twice is justified by ∫|φ| < ∞, so R² = κR. Put ξ₀ = Rη and r = ‖ξ₀‖ > 0. We obtain ‖ξ₀‖² = ⟨η,R²η⟩ = κ⟨η,ξ₀⟩. In particular the last pairing is real and positive.

The integrated intertwiner construction applied to η gives a bounded H-map J from σ into ρ, with Jw = ξ₀. It begins on the dense subspace on which h ↦ ⟨σ(h)w,y⟩ is integrable and sends y to its integral against ρ(h)η. Boundedness permits extension to the Hilbert space. Since the actions are unitary, J*J commutes with σ. Schur’s lemma makes it scalar, and evaluating it on the unit vector w gives that scalar as r². Thus i = J/r is an isometry. Now iw = ξ₀/r and ⟨η,iw⟩ = r/κ. For every ambient vector a, self-adjointness gives the required identity

∫H φ(h) ⟨η,ρ(h)a⟩ dν = ⟨ξ₀,a⟩ = κ ⟨η,iw⟩ ⟨iw,a⟩.

A test in a representation with two copies. Let H be the three cube roots of unity, with counting measure of total mass 3. Let σ(h) = h on ℂ and ρ(h) = diag(h,h,h) on ℂ³. Here w = 1, κ = 3, and R acts as diag(3,3,0), since the sum of a nontrivial character vanishes. Take η = (1,2,2)/3. Then ξ₀ = (1,2,0), r = √5 and the chosen copy sends 1 to (1,2,0)/√5. Both sides of the displayed identity equal a₁ + 2a₂. For instance the original map j can send 1 to (1,0,0). The test then chooses a different copy in the same two-dimensional character space. Taking (0,0,1) as the test would give zero for every a, even though the original copy still exists. This is why the construction chooses η by testing its translates. The same distinction matters when the ambient weight is lowered.
