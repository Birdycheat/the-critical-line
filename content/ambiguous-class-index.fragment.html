Intersect the kernels of all characters in E inside U, and call the intersection N. It is open since E is finite and U is open. Since G is profinite and N is normal by conjugation invariance, G/N is a finite group. Every commutator gug−1u−1 belongs to N, so U/N is central in G/N. The quotient G/U is cyclic. Fixed classes and coinvariants in two dimensions

Let C = 𝔽₃² and τ(x,y) = (x + y, y), an automorphism of order three. Its fixed vectors form the first coordinate line. Since (1 − τ)(x,y) = (−y,0), this entire line maps to zero in Q. The second coordinate induces Q ≃ 𝔽₃. Both groups have three elements, but the projection kills every fixed vector. The three invariant characters are exactly (x,y) ↦ by, one for each b ∈ 𝔽₃.

For an arithmetic check of the two indices, take ℓ = 3, f = 2 and n = 2. The units mod 18 form a group of order six. The powers of γ = 7 are 1, 7, 13, so their subgroup has index two and the relative class action uses 13. The field ℚ(μ₁₈) = ℚ(μ₉) has absolute discriminant 3⁹. Its Minkowski bound is (4/π)³(6!/6⁶)√(3⁹) < 4.47. The prime 2 has order six mod 9, so the prime above it has norm 64. The sole prime above 3 is generated by 1 − ζ₉. Every class thus has a principal representative of norm at most four. Here the class-group index |Q| is one. Over the base ℚ(μ₃), Kummer theory also gives BF = 9. Its Eisenstein integers are principal, so every cube class unramified away from 3 has a representative which is a unit times a power of 1 − ζ₃. Conversely these representatives are units away from 3, where the cube-root polynomial has separable reduction. The six roots of unity supply three classes modulo cubes and the exponent at the sole prime above 3 supplies three more choices, giving 3 · 3.
