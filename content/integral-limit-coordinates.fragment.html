How can an integral period be written in power-series coordinates?

In the last congruence the entries on the right of the matrix e(Rn) lie in A. We need coordinate maps from A to 𝔖 that commute with multiplication by every coefficient in 𝔖. We will construct them by successively lifting finite expansions. Write x = e(u) = [b], the Teichmüller lift of a nonzero element of positive valuation in the tilt. Put I = (ℓ, u) in 𝔖 and write IA = (ℓ, x) in A. Choose a basis of the vector space A/IA over 𝔖/I = k and lift its elements to bλ ∈ A. We may choose 1 as one of the lifts, since its residue is nonzero: reducing first modulo ℓ and then modulo b sends 1 to 1 in the nonzero quotient of the tilt valuation ring.

The coefficient pair ℓ, u is a regular sequence in 𝔖. The corresponding pair ℓ, x is regular in A, since Witt vectors have no ℓ-torsion and multiplication by b is injective in the tilt valuation ring A/ℓA. For a regular pair the associated graded ring is the polynomial ring in its two initial forms over the residue quotient. Thus in degree j the monomials ℓixj−i, with 0 ≤ i ≤ j, have independent coefficients in A/IA. On the coefficient side the matching monomials ℓiuj−i have coefficients in k. Stacks Project, Lemma 10.69.2.

Given z ∈ A, express its residue in the chosen basis and subtract that finite sum of lifts. The error belongs to IA. If the error already belongs to IjA, express its degree-j class using the preceding monomials. Each coefficient is a finite linear combination of the residual basis elements. Lifting these coefficients gives a polynomial correction of total degree j for each of finitely many bλ. Subtract the corrections. The new error belongs to Ij+1A. At any fixed precision, only finitely many basis elements have appeared.

The corrections for each λ converge in 𝔖. Indeed reduction modulo Im retains only the coefficients of degrees i < m, to precision ℓm−i. The resulting coefficient cλ(z) is unique: a nonzero family giving a zero expansion would have a first total degree with a nonzero term. In that degree the independence of the monomials and of the residual basis would force all its coefficients to vanish. Here uniqueness is among families with only finitely many nonzero residues modulo each Im.

z = ∑λ e(cλ(z))bλ,  cλ(e(s)z) = s cλ(z).

The sum recovers z at every precision by construction. To check separatedness and completeness on the period side, observe that (IA)2m ⊆ (ℓm, xm) ⊆ (IA)m. After reduction modulo ℓm, multiplication by [b]m multiplies Witt coordinate j by bmℓj for 0 ≤ j < m. These congruences give exactly the product topology of the complete tilt valuation ring on the Witt coordinates. These bounds apply to the Witt coordinates of a difference. Witt subtraction is polynomial in each finite set of coordinates, which gives the same neighbourhoods for ordinary coordinate differences. More explicitly, the subtraction polynomials vanish when the two lists agree, so sufficiently close integral inputs give a small difference in each prescribed coordinate. Conversely addition of an element with small Witt coordinates changes those prescribed coordinates by small amounts. Completeness of each coordinate ring now gives completeness of the product. Elements agreeing at every precision are equal. Uniqueness of the expansion gives additivity and the stated 𝔖-linearity by adding expansions or multiplying them by a single coefficient.

The period equation uses J = (ℓ, E). If e₀ ≥ 1 is the degree of the Eisenstein polynomial, then E ≡ ue₀ modulo ℓ. Hence J = (ℓ, ue₀) and Ie₀ ⊆ J ⊆ I. Taking powers shows that the two topologies agree. The same expansion is therefore finite modulo every Jm. The coordinates of one fixed period will determine how much precision is lost.
