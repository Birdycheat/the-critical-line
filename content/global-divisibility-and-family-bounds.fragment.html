Realizing the uniform divisibility bridge and twisted-place gain bounds

The uniform divisibility input used in the family assembly compares the dual lattice slope, the map height, and the signed finite-place sum against a main term and two explicit error budgets. Before that inequality can be applied uniformly across all sufficiently large scales x and all family indices, three structural steps must be closed: first, translating numerical divisibility records into the exact record-level error envelope; second, pulling the resulting twelve-threshold family bound back through the row-face, tied-column, and square-integrable kernel interfaces without losing uniformity in the constants; and third, bounding the finite-place Gram valuations and lift indices at twisted places so that definite division inputs require no external norm assumption.

Theorem (Uniform divisibility bridge and twisted-place gain bounds). Let F be a family of global parameter records at scale x > 1 with exponent ε ∈ (0, 1), signed finite-place sum F0, main term Mu, error budgets (B0, Nc), and logarithmic scale La ≥ 0.

  1. Record-level domination and pointwise divisibility. Suppose a numerical divisibility datum ties to the record at word u with constants (C0, ce). Whenever the target constants satisfy C ≥ max(50 C0 + 10, 60 ce, 0) and Cabs ≥ 1, the numerical divisibility upper bound is dominated by the record-level envelope
    (50 C0 + 10) |Ttop| a La + 60 ce m xε (B0 + Nc) ≤ C |Ttop| a La + C m (B0 + Nc) xCabs ε.
    Combining pointwise word-level divisibility facts with the third complement bound, the frame-number cap with exponent CH, admissibility, and rationality yields the pointwise divisibility estimate at scale x.
  2. Uniform family pullback across row, face, tied-column, and kernel bridges. Given family-wide row data, third-complement bounds, admissibility, rationality, and nonnegativity of La with constants (C1, C2, C3), setting
    C = max(max(C1, C3), max(C2, 0))
    and taking the maximum of the twelve component thresholds yields the uniform divisibility bound for all sufficiently large x and every family index. By monotonicity of word facts and complement bounds, this uniform divisibility bridge pulls back successively through row-face packages, tied column records, split-model to place-model column transport, compatible square-integrable column kernels, and closed family classes.
  3. Extended Gram-valuation and twisted-place gain sum. Let K be a totally complex number field, G a finite group, s > 0 an integer scaling parameter, D > 0 the common lift rank, and Tw a finite set of twisted places of K. At every finite place y, the Gram matrix valuation satisfies vy(Gm) ≤ D · vy(|G| s); at every y ∈ Tw and layer j, the lift index obeys jy ≤ (ey J0) D + ηy, where ηy = ηyJW + D (vy(DG) + vy(s)).     ∑y ∈ Tw ly ηy / D ≤ bd(j) + log dg + log s.
    This upgrades definite division inputs without an explicit norm assumption into definite division clauses and realizes concrete column-chain divisibility packages with size budget C xε (B0 + Nc).
Proof

Step 1 (Record domination and pointwise assembly). Because x > 1, ε > 0, and Cabs ≥ 1, we have ε ≤ Cabs ε and therefore xε ≤ xCabs ε. Nonnegativity of |Ttop|, a ≥ 1, La, m, and B0 + Nc then allows replacing 50 C0 + 10 and 60 ce by C and xε by xCabs ε, yielding the record-level domination inequality. Step 3 (Extended valuations and twisted-place gain sum). At any finite place y of K, the Gram determinant on the common rank-D lattice satisfies vy(Gm) ≤ D · vy(|G| s) directly from the underlying lattice-block estimate once the quotient rank is identified with D. At a twisted place y ∈ Tw and layer j, spectral-weight monotonicity bounds the local exponent by ey J0, and multiplying by D ≥ 0 in the extended lift-index inequality jy ≤ ηyJW + D (Jy + vy(DG) + vy(s)) yields jy ≤ (ey J0) D + ηy. Dividing ly ηy by D > 0 and summing over y ∈ Tw splits the sum into three nonnegative pieces:

(∑y ∈ Tw ly ηyJW) / D + ∑y ∈ Tw ly vy(DG) + ∑y ∈ Tw ly vy(s).

The first term is at most bd(j) by hypothesis; since DG divides dg > 0, we have vy(DG) ≤ vy(dg) at every place y, and the product-formula bound ∑y ∈ Tw ly vy(n) ≤ log n for positive integers n bounds the second and third terms by log dg and log s, proving the twisted-place gain bound. Ideal-class principalization and constructed-point family closure

The period-metric calculation connects the averaged column metric to CM period ratios once the fractional dual lattices become principal over a common algebraic number field with unit discriminant. This final section constructs that trivializing number field explicitly via finiteness of the ideal class group and root adjunction, and supplies the threshold-conjunction algebra that transfers all class-zero and class-two pointwise estimates on constructed points into closed properties of the working families, alongside the arithmetic-progression prime-counting interface.

Theorem (Ideal-class power principalization and constructed-point family closure). Let E be a CM number field with subfield K, ring of integers 𝓞E, base fractional ideal 𝔞0, a base number field M0 ⊂ ℂ, and algebraic curve invariants (g2, g3).

  1. Galois-conjugate CM metric identity and dual-lattice characterization.     exp(−logmet(Λ, ρ, a)) = area(Λρ) / (π |ρ(a)|2).
    Given a finite E / K-trace-dual family {ak, a'k} satisfying u = ∑k TrE / K(u · a'k̄) ak for all u ∈ E, linear independence of distinct embeddings gives ∑k τ(a'k̄) σ(ak) = δτ, σ; combined with unimodularity of the half-different, an element x ∈ M belongs to the dual fractional lattice ℓτ(M) if and only if x τ(ȳ) is integral over ℤ for every y ∈ 𝔞0.
