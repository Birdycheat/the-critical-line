How many flags can have the same Gram data?

Fix a row in the common frame and one Gram class Z. To count generic flags in this row, keep the part of a flag visible in the definite orthogonal space. It is a sublattice P of a fixed lattice L₀ of ambient dimension k. Its rank is k − 1. The frame determinant formula gives a bound G depending on the prescribed data. Write B for the fixed-witness bound on the number of flag classes supported by any one such P. Each contributing flag has a witness P. Summing over possible witnesses gives an upper bound even when two witnesses count the same class. The difficulty is that P can have large index in the integral points of its own span. Set M = L₀ ∩ spanK(P), where K is the CM field. This saturation has the same field span and rank as P. It is primitive in L₀, meaning it contains every point of L₀ in that span. With the normalized determinant (the square root of the order of the dual quotient in the span), write d = det(M) and j = #(M/P). The integral, positive definite lattice and the standing CM norm hypotheses give an integer d ≥ 1. The index formula gives det(P) = j d. Hence the determinant budget G leaves room for indices only up to G/d. Passing to the saturation · Integral determinant and finite index

For a fixed saturation let α = k + log₂[K ∶ ℚ]. The projective-module counting bound supplies at most jα submodules of index j. For x ≥ 1 there are at most ⌊x⌋ indices through x, each contributing at most xα. This accounts for the extra power in the estimate. If Cprim(L₀,n,d) counts primitive sublattices of rank n and determinant d, the total number of eligible witnesses satisfies

#{P ⊆ L₀ ∣ rank P = n, det(P) ≤ G} ≤ ∑1 ≤ d ≤ ⌊G⌋ Cprim(L₀,n,d) (G/d)α + 1.

For G < 1 there are no witnesses. For the single generic flag take n = k − 1 and multiply by B. The row weights are nonnegative, so summing first over rows or first over d yields the same upper bound. The remaining arithmetic estimate concerns the mean of the primitive counts. Count the eight rank-one sublattices of determinant at most two by hand

Take the standard Hermitian lattice ℤ[i]². A nonzero rank-one submodule has a generator (a,b), unique up to the four units, because ℤ[i] is a principal ideal domain. The Gram entry is h = |a|² + |b|². Its dual in its own line is obtained by dividing the generator by h. The quotient has h² elements, hence the normalized determinant is h. At h = 1 the two coordinate axes are the only possibilities. At h = 2 both coordinates can be units, giving the four primitive lines generated by (1,u), u ∈ {1,−1,i,−i}. Otherwise one coordinate vanishes and the other is a unit times 1 + i. These last two submodules have index two in the coordinate axes. This lists 2 + 4 + 2 = 8 submodules. In this concrete model the primitive counts are 2 and 4, and α + 1 = 4. The general counting expression gives 2·2⁴ + 4·1⁴ = 36. Its slack comes from bounding every index by the same power.

Why a pair costs two flag counts. A loop term carries two flags and a comparison isometry. For fixed Gram classes, positive corank s and a determinant cutoff, the two flags admit a common witness P of rank k − s. Under the level and admissibility hypotheses, moving each flag by its allowed integral unipotent transformation carries the comparison isometry to the one for the new pair, by uniqueness on the orthogonal quotient. Thus the two flag classes determine the triple class. If each flag set at P has at most J classes, the ordered pair has at most J² choices. Multiply the displayed witness bound by J² and average the primitive counts in the relevant genus.
