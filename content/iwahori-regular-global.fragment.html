Clearing denominators without losing regular support

Write r for the auxiliary prime and W for its chosen half of the places above r. In the odd-rank compact unitary setting of the spectral trace test, take an admissible multiple N of r whose principal subgroup K₀ stabilizes the original level data with trivial type character. Let K be the larger subgroup carrying that character at w₀, and give K₀ volume one in convolution. For w ∈ W, let Iw be the Iwahori factor of K, prw the local projection and tw the diagonal with entries πw−i for 0 ≤ i < n, where πw is a uniformizer. We seek a test whose support preserves the away-from-p adelic lattice Λ under inverse action and has separable characteristic polynomial at every chosen place.

Multiply the local lifts of tw to obtain τ. Its global type function Tτ on KτK is the inverse type character at w₀ divided by [K ∶ K₀], extended by zero. Put e = T₁, the projector onto the type. The independent local factors are trivial at w₀. Multiplying their left convolution inverses in reverse order gives v ∗ Tτ = e.

Choose a positive integer c prime to p so that ckΛ ⊆ Λ for every k ∈ K. Such a common denominator exists because only finitely many local lattice coordinates can fail to be integral, and on the compact level each of those coordinates has bounded denominator. The inverse τ⁻¹ lifts to an element trivial away from r. A single power rM clears the denominators of its finitely many matrix entries over r. Write [b] for the central scalar acting by a positive integer b on the lattice space. Translate by D = [c][rM][c], whose local scalar is d = c²rM. Set u(g) = Tτ(Dg) and u′(g) = v(D⁻¹g). Translation by a central element passes to either convolution factor by a change of variable. Hence e ∗ u = u ∗ e = u, and the two translations cancel in u′ ∗ u = e. If u(g) ≠ 0, write Dg = k₁τk₂ with both kᵢ ∈ K. Each of the following three factors carries Λ into Λ.

g⁻¹ = (k₂⁻¹[c]) (τ⁻¹[rM]) (k₁⁻¹[c]).

For each m ≥ 1 put fm = u∗m ∗ e and define

Am = {g ∣ dm prw(g) ∈ Iw twm Iw for every w ∈ W}.

Then fm(g) ≠ 0 implies both g⁻¹Λ ⊆ Λ and g ∈ Am. For m = 1 this follows from Dg ∈ KτK. At each convolution step a nonzero summand gives a product of support elements. Their inverses preserve Λ in reverse order, and the local double-coset product puts the product in the next cell. The root valuations in the cell of twm are −mi. Removing the scalar subtracts m ordw(d) from each valuation, preserving their distinctness.

Transporting regular support to the orbital terms

Fix any m ≥ 1. Each observed Hecke function ψj from the level construction satisfies prw(b) ∈ Iw whenever ψj(b) ≠ 0 and w ∈ W. Consequently bAm ⊆ Am, since the scalar dm commutes with prw(b) and the cell is stable under left multiplication by Iw. If a convolution (ψj ∗ h)(g) is nonzero, some term ψj(b)h(b⁻¹g) is nonzero. Induction on words gives supp(s(ψ)fm) ⊆ Am for every finite linear combination s of words. It is this local support condition that passes to arbitrary word multipliers. The inverse-lattice inclusion was established on the support of fm itself.

To read this condition in the trace formula, let j be the frame map from unitary similitude adeles onto the group away from p. Write Mw for the w-component of an adele y, and aw for the local change of frame. Specializing the matrix of j(y) gives awMwaw⁻¹. The local realization Pw undoes this change, so Pw(j(y)) = Mw. For a rational element with algebra component γ, a trivialization with local matrix X therefore represents its orbital element by Xιw(γ)X⁻¹, where ιw is the local algebra embedding. Similarity preserves the characteristic polynomial even when the Hecke function takes different values on conjugate elements. Thus any one w₁ ∈ W supplies the separability test on every nonzero value.

At the rational prime r, regularity also requires a commutative centralizer. Let w̄ be conjugate to w and cw ∶ Ew̄ → Ew the induced isomorphism of completions of the CM field E. The unitary similitude relation, with nonzero local multiplier νw, gives

cw(Mw̄)ᵗ Mw = νw I.

After transport to Ew, the roots at w̄ are therefore νw/λ as λ runs through the roots at w. This is injective on nonzero roots. Since W contains one place from each pair, every component above r has simple spectrum. In an eigenbasis its commuting matrices are diagonal, so their commutators vanish in every local factor. The faithful product of these factors over r then gives a commutative rational-place centralizer. For instance, with local multiplier 1 the split pair diag(1, π⁻¹, π⁻²) and diag(1, π, π²) has simple spectrum on both sides.

The original cuspidal type at w₀ still kills the constant terms of all slices after left convolution. Together with separability at w₁ this gives the vanishing for reducible marked pairs and for definite orbital terms outside the regular irreducible locus.

Choosing one power for the nonzero definite trace

Fix an observed family (ψj, θj). Its deck or spherical tests and the transport of the original automorphic vector supply a nonzero common eigenvector of eigenvalues θ in the image of e. Use the weight comparison to take the coefficient representation of the prescribed weight. Let M be the finite direct sum of the resulting spaces of forms over the definite Hermitian classes and T the Hecke action. On M₀ = im T(e) the observed operators commute. They commute there with B = T(u)|M₀ because the observations, after convolution with e, are linear combinations of type functions supported on double cosets with representatives trivial at w₀ and W. These type functions convolve on factors separate from the factors defining τ, and central translation preserves their convolution identity. The relation u′ ∗ u = e makes B injective.

Apply the polynomial projector construction on M₀. It gives a polynomial A in finitely many observations whose action projects onto the joint generalized θ-eigenspace L, with A(θ) = 1. The common eigenvector belongs to L, so dim L > 0. The restriction of B to L is invertible. If every positive power trace vanished, taking the trace of its minimal-polynomial identity would give zero for its nonzero constant coefficient times dim L. Fix one m ≥ 1 with tr(Bm|L) ≠ 0. For a word polynomial s put Fs = (As)(ψ)fm. If Tq denotes the action in the summand for the class q, then

∑q tr(Tq(Fs)) = s(θ) tr(Bm|L).

Indeed, convolution acts by composition. The trailing e and the projector A reduce the trace to L. There s(ψ) − s(θ)I acts nilpotently and commutes with B. Its product with Bm is nilpotent and contributes zero trace. Thus the same A and m work for every s with s(θ) ≠ 0. Regular support holds for every positive power, while even the invertible restriction diag(1, −1) has zero trace in each odd power. The worked spectral example gives trace six on choosing the second power and s(X) = X + 1.
