How many lists of minors have a given size?

Fix a diagonal word u and an intersection defect j with 1 ≤ j ≤ r. Each surviving term supplies an ordered list δ = (δ₁, …, δj) of leading determinants. Two terms can supply exactly the same list. The fixed-list estimate has already paid for the terms with that list. We now count each distinct list once. Vanishing intermediate determinants retain their positions.

Both frames belong to the same self-dual Hermitian lattice. Pairing a vector in its dual with a lattice vector gives an element of the ring of integers 𝒪E. Every matrix entry therefore lies in this ring, and its determinant does too by the permutation formula. This puts each δℓ in a set whose spacing we can control.

Separation of algebraic integers. Let τ range over one embedding from each of the f conjugate pairs of the CM field E. For distinct x, y in 𝒪E, the product ∏τ|τ(x) − τ(y)|² equals |NmE/ℚ(x − y)| ≥ 1. Indeed the norm multiplies all conjugates, and each conjugate pair contributes a squared modulus. Multiplication by the nonzero algebraic integer x − y on the integral lattice 𝒪E has nonzero integral determinant. This also covers units, whose absolute norm equals one.

Divide each complex plane into half-open squares of side ½. Two points in the same square differ by less than 1 in modulus. If two algebraic integers occupied the same square at every place, their product of squared differences would be less than 1. Thus a choice of one square at each place contains at most one algebraic integer.

Counting at a fixed size

Let d be the root discriminant of E and H₁ the positive height scale of the fixed-list estimate. For a minor of order ℓ set eℓ = ∑a=1ℓ(r + 1 − a), ρℓ = d2eℓ(2H₁)2r and Rℓ = max(1, ρℓ). At place τ the size of a list is στ = max(1, max1≤ℓ≤j |τ(δℓ)|/ρℓ). Its global size is S = ∏τστ. Different places can attain their maxima at different minors.

Record the integer aτ for which 2aτ ≤ στ < 2aτ+1. Write A = ∑τaτ. Fixing these integers places τ(δℓ) in a disk of radius 2aτ+1Rℓ. Each real or imaginary coordinate in that disk meets at most 8·2aτRℓ + 2 ≤ 10·2aτRℓ half-unit intervals. Multiply the choices for two coordinates, for all places, and then for the j positions. By separation, the resulting bound on the number of distinct lists with this profile is Bj22jA, where Bj = ∏ℓ=1j(10Rℓ)2f. Counting every possible combination of entries gives an upper bound even when only a few combinations are realized by flags.

Shell bound. Let Nj(h) count the realized lists with 2h ≤ S < 2h+1. Multiplying the local inequalities gives 2A ≤ S < 2A+f, so A ≤ h ≤ A + f − 1. For an upper bound it suffices to include every profile with A ≤ h. There are exactly binom(h + f, f) nonnegative profiles with this property. To see this, put the unused h − A in a final slot and choose the positions of f separators among h + f symbols. This gives

Nj(h) ≤ binom(h + f, f) · Bj · 22jh   (h ≥ 0).

A list may have zero entries. In this one-place experiment, fix a single nonzero entry x in the Gaussian integers and take unit radius, so S = max(1, |x|). The shell 2 ≤ S < 4 contains 36 possible entries. For |x| < 4, the vertical lines with real part 0, ±1, ±2 and ±3 contain respectively 7, 14, 14 and 10 points in total. The smaller disk |x| < 2 contains 9, including the origin. The origin cancels in the difference 45 − 9 = 36. Any realization condition on the minor can only reduce this count.

Why the remaining power pays for every list

Each list in a profile of total A has weight S−3r ≤ 2−3rA. Put κ = 3r − 2j ≥ r ≥ 1. The whole profile contributes at most Bj2−κA. Summing independently over the f nonnegative place indices gives one geometric series per place. In particular, every finite set T of distinct realized lists satisfies

∑δ∈T S(δ)−3r ≤ Bj(1 − 2−κ)−f ≤ 2j ∏ℓ=1j(64Rℓ)2f.

The last inequality follows from (1 − 2−κ)−1 ≤ 2 and j ≥ 1. The bound is independent of T, so taking the supremum over finite subsets also bounds the full nonnegative sum. Under the standing scale assumptions b = exp(L), L > 1, log d ≤ L/Cl, Cl ≥ 1, r ≤ m and H₁ = bCHm, the arithmetic scale comparison bounds the right-hand side by bCc²m²fr with Cc = 21 + 4|CH|. To check the scale, use eℓ ≤ r² and log 2 < L. They give log Rℓ/L ≤ 2r² + 2r(1 + |CH|m). After taking logarithms to base b, the bound for the weighted sum costs at most

j + 2fj[6 + 2r² + 2r(1 + |CH|m)] ≤ (21 + 4|CH|)m²fr.

Here j ≤ r ≤ m and f ≥ 1. Since Cc ≥ 1, this fits inside the stated allowance Cc²m²fr.

At fixed j, the amplitude times the weight of its representatives was bounded by a factor independent of δ times S(δ)−3r. Multiplying that factor by the uniform sum now eliminates the list from the estimate. The sum over the two positive ranks can then be taken with the number of columns in this word’s source space still explicit.
