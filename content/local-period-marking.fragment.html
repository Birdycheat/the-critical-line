Which lattice does the coordinate at p specify?

The integral gluing argument starts from a lattice at p. To construct it, fix the linked unitary datum with one distinguished embedding (a = 1). The multiplicity m = 1 means that the order is the image of the integers of the CM field acting by scalars. Indeed, the split local algebra has matrix size one, so the order is commutative. The centrality condition identifies each element with a scalar in the CM field after extension to ℚ. Each scalar is integral over ℤ because the order is finite over ℤ. Fix a rational polarised form class with symmetric parameter β. Write V for its rational period space and Bβ for its alternating form. Choose one place w from each conjugate pair above p. Let U = ∏w Fwn and Λ = ∏w 𝒪wn, where 𝒪w is the valuation ring of the completion Fw.

There are two coordinate maps T, T′ from V to U, obtained from the chosen embeddings and their CM conjugates. Write ⟨u,v⟩ for the sum of the local traces of their dot products. The maps are chosen so that

Bβ(x,y) = ⟨T′y,Tx⟩ − ⟨T′x,Ty⟩.

The rational matrix description identifies V with Fn. Over ℚp, splitting the field trace into the two conjugate sets of places gives the two terms in the displayed identity. At each place an invertible change of basis identifies the first matrix action with its standard action. In the second block the dual change of basis incorporates the invertible matrix of the form. The combined map (T,T′) thus identifies V ⊗ ℚp with U × U. Rational vectors are dense there by weak approximation.

Local lattice and its polarisation. Write bp = (c,A), where c ∈ ℚp× and A is a tuple of invertible matrices over the completions. Put P = AΛ. Its trace dual P∨ consists of the v for which ⟨v,P⟩ ⊂ ℤp. The dual of 𝒪w for the local trace is its inverse different. The rational lattice specified by bp is

Mb = {x ∈ V  |  Tx ∈ P,   T′x ∈ cP∨}.

This is a full order-stable lattice over ℤ(p), the rationals integral at p. Indeed, P × cP∨ lies between two p-power multiples of the ℤp-span of any rational basis in these coordinates. Intersecting with V gives the same bounds over ℤ(p), a principal ideal domain. The two blocks are preserved by the order, which acts through conjugate integral scalars.

Choose the positive rational s = p−vp(c), where vp(p) = 1. Then Mb is its own dual for sBβ. Indeed, testing a pair (u,v) against (0,cη) and (z,0) with η ∈ P∨ and z ∈ P gives scu ∈ P and sv ∈ P∨. Here the double trace dual is P, as one sees in a ℤp-basis and its dual basis. Since sc is a unit, these are exactly the original two tests. The rational pairs from Mb are dense in the open lattice P × cP∨. Continuity of the pairing and closedness of ℤp therefore give the same duality on rational vectors. Integrality depends only on the valuation of the scale. Every positive rational s with vp(sc) = 0 therefore gives the prescribed local polarisation.

How does the scalar change the Gaussian lattice at 5?

Choose r² = −1 in ℤ5 with r ≡ 2 modulo 5 by Hensel's lemma. On ℚ(i) take T(a + bi) = a + rb and T′(a + bi) = −(a − rb)/(2r). Substitution in the trace formula gives the determinant in the basis 1, i. Fix A = 1. For c = 1 the lattice has basis 1, i over ℤ(5). For c = 5 the second test imposes a ≡ 2b modulo 5. The resulting basis 5, 2 + i has determinant 5, so the pairing divided by 5 takes value 1 on it. For c = 10 the lattice is the same because 2 is a unit at 5.

When do two local markings give the same lattice?

Let b = (c,A) and b′ = (c′,A′) be two representatives. The rational lattice determines its closure in the two local coordinates, since rational pairs are dense in each open lattice. The two closures are equal precisely when their first and second projections agree. Equality of the first projections says AΛ = A′Λ. Thus A−1A′ and its inverse have integral entries at every place. Once this common lattice P is fixed, equality of the second projections says (c′/c)P∨ = P∨. Since P∨ is a nonzero free ℤp-module, c′/c must be a unit. Conversely these two conditions preserve both projections. For the prescribed integral subgroup Kp this gives

Mb = Mb′  ⇔  b−1b′ ∈ Kp,   Kp = ℤp× × ∏w GLn(𝒪w).

A rational similitude y of the chosen form acts on the marking from the left. Write t for its multiplier and H for its matrices on the first block. The trace pairing forces the action on the second block to be t(H−1)t. Thus the first lattice becomes HP, and the second becomes tc(HP)∨. The resulting rational lattice is yMb, with representative (tc,HA). This explains how the rational group acts on the right cosets in the period quotient.

Which lattices occur?

Converse. Let M be a full order-stable ℤ(p)-lattice in V, and suppose that M is its own dual for sBβ with s a positive rational. Then M = Mb for a unique right coset bKp.

To find a representative, choose generators y₁,…,yk for M. At each w, their first coordinates span Fwn, since they span V rationally and the complete first projection is onto. Among n of these columns choose those with maximal determinant norm. They give a matrix Aw with nonzero determinant. By Cramer's rule, the coordinates of each remaining column have norm at most one, since replacing a chosen column cannot increase the determinant norm. Consequently P = AΛ is exactly the product of the local spans of the first coordinates.

Set c = s−1. The first projection test now holds on M. For the second, fix x, y ∈ M and integral local scalars α = (αw). By weak approximation, choose CM elements tending to α at the chosen places and to 0 at their conjugates. Each sufficiently close approximant ξ is integral everywhere above p. A positive integer d prime to p clears its remaining denominators, so dξ belongs to the ring of CM integers. Its scalar action lies in the prescribed order. Thus (dξ)y ∈ M, and division by the unit d in ℤ(p) gives ξy ∈ M. Evaluating sBβ on that product and x, then passing to the limit in the trace formula, gives s⟨T′x, αTy⟩ ∈ ℤp. Taking coefficients supported at one place and summing over the generators tests every vector in P. Hence T′x ∈ cP∨ and M ⊂ Mb.

Both lattices are self-dual for the same form sBβ, because sc = 1. Taking duals reverses their inclusion and yields equality. The stabilizer criterion gives the asserted uniqueness.
