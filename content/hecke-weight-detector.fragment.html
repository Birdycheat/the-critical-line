Can a specified nonzero vector be detected at the chosen embedding?

After projecting the family into an irreducible automorphic representation, we obtain functions on the adelic group. The circle calculation applies to vectors in a finite-dimensional weight representation. The next step takes a specified nonzero function to a nonzero vector there while preserving the action of the chosen unitary group.

Detection lemma. Let a group G act irreducibly on a complex vector space U, and let H be a subgroup. Suppose every element of G can be written hc, where h ∈ H and c commutes with all of H. Let V be an irreducible representation of H admitting a nonzero intertwining map into U. For each nonzero u ∈ U there exists a linear map p from U to V with

p(u) ≠ 0,   p(hv) = h p(v)   for every h ∈ H, v ∈ U.

Proof. A nonzero intertwiner from V is injective, since its kernel is an invariant subspace of an irreducible representation. Call its image a copy of V. The sum of all such copies is nonzero. Any c commuting with H carries a copy to another copy, because composing its inclusion with c is still an intertwiner. Every h acts inside each copy. The factorization of elements of G therefore makes this sum G-invariant. By irreducibility it is all of U.

Select a maximal family of copies whose sum is direct. Such a family exists by the maximal principle. The union of a chain of independent families is independent, since every linear relation uses only finitely many members. A further copy meets their sum in an H-invariant subspace. A zero intersection would let us add it, so every copy is already contained in the sum. Thus U is an algebraic direct sum of copies. Some coordinate of u is nonzero. Project onto this coordinate and invert its identification with V. All summands are H-invariant, which gives the required equivariance. M. Zorn, A remark on method in transfinite algebra, pp. 667–670.

In the adelic application H is the unitary factor at the archimedean place of the specified embedding τ. An adelic group element is a tuple of local elements. Taking its component at this place gives h, and the tuple with that component set to the identity commutes with H. Under τ this factor acts by U(a). To say that the weight κ occurs means that the representation of highest weight κ maps nontrivially into U as an H-representation. It is irreducible there: an invariant complex subspace is stable under differentiation by skew-Hermitian matrices, whose complex span is every complex matrix. Thus it is stable under their complex Lie algebra and under exponentials. Elementary row additions and invertible diagonal matrices are exponentials over ℂ and generate GLa(ℂ) by elimination. Irreducibility of the highest-weight module then applies. The algebraic highest-weight representation.

For example, in the restricted representation V ⊕ V a vector (0, w) with w ≠ 0 is killed by projection onto the first copy and detected by the second. The map in the lemma may depend on the given vector. In the lower partial-sum estimate it is chosen after a nonzero value has been transported to the slice where the first matrix is the identity.
