Which way does the Jacobian go in the real-place integral?

At one real place let G = U(p,q), with n = p + q. Fix a totally isotropic flag with positive rank jumps jᵢ. Its stabilizer has unipotent radical N and block-diagonal subgroup M. The maximal compact group K = U(p) × U(q) preserves the ordinary positive Hermitian norm as well as the signed form. Every element of G has the form umk. The compact group L = M ∩ K accounts for the ambiguity, since replacing (m,k) by (mℓ,ℓ−1k) gives the same element. These are all the repetitions because (NM) ∩ K = L. Knapp, Lie Groups Beyond an Introduction, Proposition 8.44.

For each matrix group H among G,N,M, choose the left Haar measure for which the ball ‖h−1‖HS ≤ r has volume asymptotic to the Euclidean unit-ball volume times rdim ℝ H. The Hilbert–Schmidt norm is the square root of the sum of squared absolute entries. Write these three measures as dg, du, dm, and give K its Haar probability dk. If μG is the initial Haar measure and c its gauge correction, then dg = c dμG. For every nonnegative measurable test φ the one-place formula is

∫G φ(g) dg = cIw ∫N ∫M ∫K φ(umk) δ(m) dk dm du.

To find δ, conjugate the radical by m. Put D(m) = |detℝ(Ad(m) on Lie(N))|. Conjugation multiplies the volume of an image set by D(m), so its pushforward measure assigns that set the factor D(m)−1. Under left multiplication by m₀ the coordinates change to (m₀um₀−1, m₀m, k). The change in du cancels precisely against the change in the weight when δ = D−1. The parabolic integration theorem supplies the equality of the measures up to a constant. Casselman, Representations of SL₂(ℝ), Proposition 18.5.

Let sᵢ = |det mᵢ|−1/jᵢ on the ith graded block. Conjugation on a rectangular block sends X to mbXmc−1. Taking its determinant and pairing the reflected rectangles gives the flag product δ(m) = P1(s), where the subscript indicates one place. For the real unitary radical the modulus of the complex determinant of its complexification equals the absolute real determinant. Each local size enters the global size by τᵢf = (τᵢ⁰)f ∏vsᵢᵥ. Since the flag product is a monomial in the sizes, Pf(τ⁰)∏v δ(mᵥ) = Pf(τ), as used in the marked slice.

What fixes the constant when the compact group has mass one?

Write d = dimℝ N, and use Hilbert–Schmidt volumes on K and L. Integrating m first absorbs right multiplication by ℓ ∈ L, which preserves dm and has δ(ℓ) = 1. The compact variable then ranges over the left cosets L∖K. Its probability density is vol(L)/vol(K) times Riemannian volume there. One more factor comes from the angle at which the radical directions meet the compact directions.

Use an ordinary orthonormal basis adapted to the isotropic blocks and their duals. Choose a Hilbert–Schmidt orthonormal real basis X₁,…,Xd of Lie(N). These matrices are strictly upper block triangular, so their adjoints are perpendicular to them. The skew-Hermitian matrices Yᵢ = (Xᵢ−Xᵢ∗)/√2 are unit compact directions perpendicular to Lie(L). They span that complement because dim K − dim L = d. The derivative of multiplication adds the tangent vectors. For each pair its columns Xᵢ, Yᵢ have inner product 1/√2 and hence span a parallelogram of area √(1−1/2) = 1/√2. Different pairs are perpendicular, and the block-diagonal directions of Lie(M) are perpendicular to all of them. The transverse Jacobian at the identity is therefore 2−d/2. The Hilbert–Schmidt small-ball slab normalization makes this tangent Jacobian the ratio of the corresponding local volume densities. Combining it with the quotient probability gives

cIw = 2−d/2 vol(K)/vol(L).

Put J = ∑i jᵢ and V(r) = vol U(r) for the Hilbert–Schmidt metric, with V(0) = 1. The numerator is V(p)V(q). In L, each U(jᵢ) acts on both an isotropic block and its dual. Its squared tangent norm doubles in jᵢ² real directions. The remaining U(p−J) and U(q−J) occur once. Consequently vol(L) = V(p−J)V(q−J) ∏i 2jᵢ²/2V(jᵢ). In this normalization V(r) = (2π)r(r+1)/2/∏h=0r−1 h!. Życzkowski and Sommers, Appendix A, equation (A14), metric A.

A hyperbolic plane. In an orthonormal Witt basis of U(1,1) the signed form exchanges the two basis vectors. Write u(t) = I + itE₁₂ and m(a) = diag(a,a−1) for real t and a > 0. Conjugating u(t) gives u(a²t), so at a = 2 the inverse density is 1/4. Here d = 1, K has volume (2π)², and L is the scalar circle of length 2π√2. Thus cIw = π. For a common signature and flag type at f places, multiplying the normalized formulas gives the power cIwf.
