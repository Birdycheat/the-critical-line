How does a Hecke transition lift to the integral correspondence?

Fix a tower of fibre powers over a number field E. Write ZN for its kth fibre power at principal level N. At a place w of E, keep the marked integral moduli scheme Y and the fibre power 𝒵 of its universal abelian scheme. The two markings give the comparison πN ∶ ZN → 𝒵E. In identities with integral maps we compose πN with the generic-fibre inclusion into 𝒵. Fix the residue characteristic q at w, distinct from the auxiliary prime p of the tower. The chosen prime ideal 𝔴 in the coefficient integer ring and the place w both have residue cardinality Q = qf. Put n equal to the rank in the local Hecke datum.

Here are the level and isogeny hypotheses. The site supplies an ideal 𝔞 and a level N₀ ≥ 3 prime to p and q. The subgroup K₀ consists of the elements of K(1) acting as the identity modulo (N₀)𝔞 on the completed lattice Λ. Take N, M ≥ 3 prime to p, with N = N₀c, M = Nτ and K(N) ⊆ K₀. The marking uses an element a of the coefficient integer ring satisfying (a, N)𝔞 = (N). At level M, the new choices cτ and τa satisfy the same ideal identity after multiplication by τ. Choose quotient data at these two levels.

Choose a Hecke index 0 ≤ i ≤ n and its integral adelic element h. Integrality means hΛ ⊆ Λ. The prescribed local type requires h to act trivially modulo the positive integers prime to q and modulo the ideals prime to 𝔴 containing a power of the chosen split ideal (q, ω − r). Here ω and r specify the quadratic generator and its chosen root modulo q. For some scalar α outside 𝔴, α𝔴Λ ⊆ hΛ. The index of hΛ + 𝔴Λ in Λ equals Qi, the similitude multiplier is Qi times a prime-to-p adelic integral unit, and QiΛ ⊆ hΛ. For every u ∈ K₀ assume (hu)K(M)(hu)−1 ⊆ K(N), so the translated transitions are defined. Fix the integral moduli correspondence for these isogenies and its pullback to the chosen level structure. On fibre powers denote its maps by P, R ∶ Ti → 𝒵.

Lifting theorem. Let b be a coset in K₀/K(N) and choose its representative rb. On the copy of ZM labelled by b, let s forget the finer level and let tb be the transition for hrb. There exists a morphism κb ∶ ZM → Ti with

P ∘ κb = πN ∘ s,   R ∘ κb = πN ∘ tb.

Construction. Start with the marked isogeny representing the transition for hrb on the generic moduli scheme. Make the two marked quotient objects at level N₀. Their cartesian comparison maps identify the abelian schemes over the source, so the isogeny transports to a map between the quotient objects. On the prime-to-p torsion, compatibility of the two markings gives the level condition. The multiplier condition gives the polarization multiplier Qi, and the lattice kernel condition gives the prescribed Hecke type. The transported isogeny therefore defines a point of the integral moduli correspondence over the generic source, where q is invertible.

Pull that point back to the finer integral level structure using the source quotient. This gives the first identity on the moduli schemes. On the target, both candidate maps classify the same quotient with the same level marking. Indeed the congruences for h and rb fix the marked lattice vectors modulo the level, and the torsion comparison transports their markings across the isogeny. Uniqueness in the moduli problem gives the second identity. Applying the morphisms in each of the k factors proves both equations on fibre powers, including the zeroth power. The universal integral correspondence and its pullback along the auxiliary level are the geometric inputs. The construction assigns to each translated transition a morphism into that correspondence. The invariance of the quotient comparison gives πM = πN ∘ s for the two chosen markings.

How many tower lifts give the same integral Hecke point?

Retain the lifting hypotheses. Put H = K₀ ∩ h−1K₀h. Assume Qi divides M, and that the index d = [K₀ : H] equals the number of i-dimensional subspaces of the n-dimensional residue vector space over 𝒪/𝔴. The integral correspondence supplies this same Grassmannian cardinality for the geometric fibres of its source after pullback to the tower in characteristic zero. Thus the number of possible integral lifts of a fixed tower point is known before their multiplicities are calculated. The prime ideal 𝔴 is maximal here.

Fibre theorem. Let U be the disjoint union of the copies of ZM and write κ for the map given on each copy by κb. A point of ZN ×𝒵 Ti specifies a tower point z and an integral Hecke point ζ with πN(z) = P(ζ). Over any algebraically closed field the number of tower lifts of this pair is

#(s, κ)−1(z, ζ) = m = [H : K(M)] > 0,   d m = [K₀ : K(M)].

Proof. Admissibility for the identity representative gives K(M) ⊆ H. The principal subgroup K(M) is normal in K₀ because conjugating a level congruence by a lattice automorphism preserves it. By the free transitive action on the forgetful fibre, choose a lift z₀ above z and label all lifts in one copy by uK(M), with u ∈ K(N). Across the copies each lift has a label x = rbu modulo K(M). These labels exhaust K₀/K(M) once.

Suppose two lifts give the same ζ. Projecting from the fibre power to the moduli scheme gives the same marked isogeny point. For a lattice vector v, the corresponding marked torsion point belongs to the isogeny kernel for label x precisely when hxv belongs to MΛ. The two labels therefore give identical kernel tests for every lattice vector. The hypothesis Qi ∣ M ensures MΛ ⊆ hxΛ for each label, since x preserves Λ. To see what these kernel tests determine, write gj = hxj. The lattice completion lemma represents every element of Λ modulo MΛ by an ordinary lattice vector, since M is prime to p. Each gj satisfies gjΛ ⊆ Λ. The tests therefore agree on all of Λ. Moreover each preimage gj−1(MΛ) is contained in Λ by the divisibility just proved. The two preimages coincide. Cancelling the nonzero scalar M gives g1−1Λ = g2−1Λ.

Thus g1g2−1 is a lattice automorphism. It also acts trivially modulo the level ideal. Indeed h acts as the identity modulo both parts of the level. The level congruence theorem combines them into the congruence modulo (N₀)𝔞, which also holds for x1x2−1. Since the conjugate is now known to preserve Λ, reduce the equality (g1g2−1)h = h(x1x2−1) modulo the level ideal. It gives g1g2−1 ∈ K₀. Equivalently, x1x2−1 ∈ H.

Fix one ζ. If any label maps to it, every other such label lies in the same right coset Hx. This gives an upper bound of [H : K(M)] labels there, including the case of an empty fibre. There are d possible ζ. Counting the whole source by its labels gives [K₀ : K(M)] = d m by multiplication of subgroup indices. A single fibre below its upper bound would make the sum smaller than d m. Every fibre therefore attains the bound. In particular, every ζ occurs. This recovers the total from the tower degree theorem.

For a finite example of the count, let K₀ be the permutations fixing 4 in the symmetric group on four letters, take h = (3 4), K(N) the three even permutations in K₀, and K(M) the identity subgroup. The intersection H fixes 3 and 4, hence has two elements. The two copies carry three labels apiece. Grouping these six labels by the right cosets of H gives three fibres with two lifts each.

Finally, (s, κ) is finite étale. Its source is finite étale over ZN by the tower degree theorem. Its target is also finite étale there by the integral correspondence theorem after base change, since q is a unit on the generic tower. A morphism between schemes finite étale over a common base is finite and étale. The restriction to the image of the generic tower uses exactly this positive degree m to lift a compatible pair, then preserves the same multiplicity on the restricted correspondence. The calculation gives the comparison on the generic tower. The integral relation at residue points also needs the separate special-fibre point identity.
