How can a prime avoid every prescribed place and still split the quadratic field?

In the geometric construction ℓ is already fixed. A second rational prime p serves a different purpose. The geometric group is described away from p, so we must be able to remove its components without affecting the places reserved for a test. We also need a square root of −d in ℚp, where ℚ(√(−d)) is the imaginary quadratic subfield of our CM field. The list of primes to avoid is only known after choosing an automorphic form and a point at which it has nonzero value.

Auxiliary prime lemma. For any positive integer d and nonnegative integer B, there is a prime p greater than both B and 2d for which X² + d has a root in the ring ℤp of p-adic integers.

Put c = (max{B, 2d})! and take any prime divisor of d · c² + 1. Every prime up to max{B, 2d} divides c and hence leaves remainder 1 when it divides into d · c² + 1. Our prime p is larger than all of them. In particular it is odd and divides neither d nor c. The residue m = dc satisfies

m² + d = d(d · c² + 1) ≡ 0 (mod p),   2m ≢ 0 (mod p).

The derivative at this root is a unit. The simple-root form of Hensel’s lemma lifts the root to ℤp. One can see each new digit directly. If a² + d is divisible by pk with k ≥ 1, substitute a + pku. After division by pk, the congruence for the next digit is (a² + d)/pk + 2au ≡ 0 modulo p. The coefficient 2a stays invertible, so this determines a unique digit. The compatible residues define a root in the complete ring. For d = 1 and B = 3, we get c = 6 and p = 37. Starting at 6 gives 1 + 12u ≡ 0, so u = 3. The next lift is 117, and 117² + 1 = 10 · 37².

Where the finite exclusions come from. The integral arithmetic datum provides a positive integer e such that a prime at which −d is a square is good for the datum whenever it does not divide e. Fix a nonzero value of the chosen form at a finite adelic point g₀. By the restricted-product definition of the finite adèles, the matrices of g₀ are integral invertible outside finitely many places. Collect the rational primes under those exceptions, the level exceptions of the form and all the requested test places. Take B at least their maximum and at least e. The lemma supplies a good p outside that list, at which every component of g₀ is integral invertible. Thus the nonzero evaluation is still available when the stabilizer construction removes the p-component. The arithmetic datum and its integer e are inputs to this application.
