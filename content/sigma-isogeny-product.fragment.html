The sigma product for an isogeny

Write F(z) = exp(−s₂z²/2) σ(z) for the normalized sigma function of the lattice Λ. Let α ≠ 0 satisfy αΛ ⊆ Λ, and let S contain one representative of each nonzero class of α−1Λ/Λ. The size of the full kernel is d = 1 + |S| = |α|². Indeed multiplication by α identifies this quotient with Λ/αΛ, and multiplies the area of a fundamental parallelogram by |α|². For z outside Λ the multiplication formula is

F(αz)² = α² F(z)2d ∏P∈S(℘(z) − ℘(P)).

To prove it, set BP(z) = −F(z + P)F(z − P)/F(P)². The sigma addition formula gives BP(z) = F(z)²(℘(z) − ℘(P)). The exponential factors match because (z + P)² + (z − P)² = 2z² + 2P². The entire function on the right is therefore represented by α²F(z)² ∏PBP(z). Weierstrass sigma addition formula.

The two entire functions vanish to order two on each kernel class. At zero this comes from the squared sigma factors. On a nonzero class Q, one zero comes from the unique choice P ≡ Q and one from P ≡ −Q. When Q = −Q in the quotient, both zeros occur in the same factor. There are no other zeros, since sigma has simple zeros precisely on Λ. Thus the quotient extends holomorphically through every kernel point. Zeros and quasi-periods of sigma.

Under a period shift ω, the exponential multiplier of F(z) is exp(πh(z,ω) + πh(ω,ω)/2), up to sign, with h(z,ω) = z conj(ω)/area(Λ). Each BP has the squared multiplier because the shifts by ±P cancel in the exponent. The same multiplier occurs a total of d times in the product, exactly the power on the left since h(αz,αω) = |α|²h(z,ω). The quotient is periodic, hence bounded on the plane by its maximum on a closed fundamental parallelogram. Liouville’s theorem makes it constant. Finally F(z)/z → 1 and each BP(0) = 1 by oddness. Both sides have leading term α²z², so the constant is one. Liouville’s theorem.

For α = p, the formula has p² − 1 factors. In the local curve parameter T, each ℘ difference contributes a denominator T², whereas the power of normalized sigma starts in degree 2p². Their difference leaves the initial degree two in the formal multiplication formula. The integral unit and the bound on the first surviving coefficient are established in the next argument.
