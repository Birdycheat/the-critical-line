How a residue field reads a determinant

We can now construct the functional Φ used above. Its scalar part takes one input from each of the e pairs in the cyclic word and one from the gluing element. Let k be the finite residue field, let ζ have order q in k×, put r = |k×|/q and fix the additive index v on k× with values in ℤ/q. Choose an integer logarithm L additive modulo qr and normalized by L(ζ) ≡ r modulo qr. To see that the normalization is possible, write ζ = hd for a generator h. Its order gives gcd(d, qr) = r. A Bézout coefficient b satisfies bd ≡ r modulo qr, so multiplying the ordinary discrete logarithm by b gives a suitable L. Normalizing a logarithm at the chosen primitive root.

u(a,b) = [L(a) + L(b) − L(ab)] / (qr).

This integer is symmetric in its arguments. Compute the sum of the carries for a product of three elements by putting the parentheses on either side. The intermediate logarithm cancels, giving u(a,b) + u(ab,c) = u(b,c) + u(a,bc). Reduce modulo q. On a word of length 2e + 1 take the product of u over its first e consecutive pairs, times v of the last entry. This is a cocycle, a functional vanishing on boundaries. To check it, expand the alternating bar faces. The three faces involving the first pair reduce by the displayed carry identity to the first carry times the boundary test on the remaining word. For a weight x let χ(j) = ζxj. Sum one carry u(χ(j),χ(1)) over j modulo q. The sums of L(χ(j)) and L(χ(j + 1)) agree by a cyclic shift. The result is L(χ(1))/r ≡ x modulo q. Inserting g in the cyclic word gives the prism. In this abelian group its terms run over the 2e + 1 positions with alternating signs. Insertion immediately before or after the first entry of any pair gives opposite terms since u is symmetric. Only the last insertion survives. Each pair now sums independently, so the scalar prism reads xev(g). Cancellation of insertions and the product of carries · Telescoping one carry and taking the product.

From one scalar to a matrix

To transfer this scalar test to an invertible matrix on a finite k-vector space V, sum over the lines in V. Choose a scale λ on nonzero vectors with λ(av) = aλ(v). For a line represented by v and a matrix g, record λ(v)/λ(g−1v) and move to the line of g−1v. The scale ratio for two successive matrices is the product of their ratios along this path. Thus merging adjacent entries of a word gives the same result before or after transfer. Dropping the first entry permutes the starting lines. The transfer therefore commutes with the boundary. Changing λ introduces ratios at consecutive endpoints whose change on a cycle is a boundary, so the scalar cocycle gives the same answer. In a one-dimensional space there is just one line, and a scalar matrix a produces the ratio a. For a direct sum V ⊕ W, project a line with nonzero first component to its line in V. Fixing a representative of that line leaves exactly |W| choices of its second component. Every remaining line lies in W and contributes once. With the scale chosen from the projected component, the ratios are those of the corresponding block. Since q divides |k| − 1, both multiplicities are 1 modulo q. For k = 𝔽₁₁ and two one-dimensional summands the twelve lines split into the eleven [1 : w] and the single [0 : 1]. For fixed x, composing two gluing maps adds their prism values. The prism identity gives this equality up to a boundary, which Φ kills. The resulting homomorphism from GL(V) is invariant under a change of basis, and direct sums add their values. For q > 1, conjugating an elementary shear T(c) by a diagonal entry ζ replaces it by T(ζc). Conjugation leaves the value unchanged, so T((ζ − 1)c) has value zero. As ζ − 1 is invertible, every shear has value zero. Elimination reduces the calculation to diagonal matrices. On a diagonal the scalar values add to xev of the product of its entries.
