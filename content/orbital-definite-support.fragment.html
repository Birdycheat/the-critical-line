Which rational elements can contribute on the definite side?

Fix a definite Hermitian form and one of its rational similitudes. Let γ denote its algebra component in the degree-n central simple algebra C over its centre F, and put m = minpolyF(γ). After choosing a finite adelic identification of the form away from p, write δp and δp for the corresponding group elements away from and at p. The test is f ⊗ 1Kp, with f bi-invariant at the prescribed compact open level and supported on finitely many level double cosets. Its ordinary orbital term can be nonzero only under the following condition.

O(δp,δp)(f ⊗ 1Kp) ≠ 0   ⇒   deg m = n,   m separable and irreducible over F.

Two hypotheses on f enter. In a matrix realization over a field k1, every point where f is nonzero must have separable characteristic polynomial. In a split realization of the away-from-p group as GLn(k) × H, with product level over a nonarchimedean local field k, every slice at a fixed element of H must satisfy the zero constant-term condition. These realizations may coincide. Conjugating the away-from-p identification conjugates the matrix of γ in each realization, so its characteristic polynomial is independent of that identification.

A repeated root at the place of regular support. If the matrix of γ over k1 has a repeated root over its algebraic closure, so does every conjugate of δp there. The support condition gives value zero for f on every such conjugate. Multiplying by the indicator at p leaves every summand zero.

Distinct roots at the place of regular support. Write χ1 for its characteristic polynomial. For either realization over a field L, the map C ⊗F L → Matn(L) is injective because its domain is simple and the map preserves the identity. Both algebras have dimension n² over L, so it is an isomorphism. The independent powers 1, γ, …, γdeg m−1 remain independent after field extension. Thus the matrix over L has minimal polynomial mL, the image of m over L. It divides the degree-n characteristic polynomial by Cayley–Hamilton. Over an algebraic closure of k1 every root of χ1 is an eigenvalue and hence a root of mk1. The n distinct roots force equality of these two monic polynomials. Both polynomials are monic, so the same degree comparison at k gives

χ1 = mk1,   deg m = n,   χk = mk.

In particular m is separable over F. If the condition in the displayed implication still fails, m factors over F into two monic factors of positive degree. Their images give a factorization at k, where separability persists. The vanishing of all the slice orbital sums then kills the orbital factor of f away from p. The two centralizers in the product. Over a splitting field of χ1, a matrix commuting with γ preserves each of its n eigenlines, so its centralizer consists of diagonal matrices in an eigenbasis. Taking the kernel of the commutator x ↦ xγ − γx commutes with field extension. Hence the centralizer in C has dimension n and equals its n-dimensional subalgebra F[γ]. Tensoring this kernel over ℚ with the finite adeles away from p is exact. The away-from-p algebra centralizer is therefore still commutative, and so is its subgroup of similitudes, whose additional multiplier is scalar. At p this formula uses the unramified extension of degree one. The local group identifies with a scalar factor and matrix factors over the completions indexed by the local datum. In each matrix factor the characteristic polynomial is again the image of m. Its distinct eigenlines give a commutative centralizer there, and the scalar component commutes as well.

These two facts justify regrouping the product orbital sum with the centralizer volumes. At each fixed conjugate z of δp, the remaining slice is the constant 1Kp(z) times the away-from-p orbital sum already shown to vanish. Thus the whole product vanishes in the second case also.
