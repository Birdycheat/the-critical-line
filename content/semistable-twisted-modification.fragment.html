How can one change both a basis and its inverse at the same filtration depth?

Keep the period matrix U at logarithm zero and its Galois formula. To correct the lattice we first work in the de Rham field BdR, whose valuation ring is BdR+. In this paragraph Filr also denotes the fractional filtration on that field when r is negative. Embed the chosen logarithm in the de Rham field and call its image y. Its displacement is c(g)t ∈ Fil1. The fixed-class theorem for the de Rham filtration says that a class fixed modulo a higher step in a bounded range of filtration degrees is represented there by a constant in K. To see this, start in a step Fila containing the representative and read its image in each successive graded piece up to the required depth. The piece of degree i is ℂℓ(i). Its fixed vectors vanish for i ≠ 0. In degree zero the fixed vectors are K, and subtracting that scalar moves the representative into Fil1. Induction gives the assertion. Brinon and Conrad, CMI Summer School Notes on p-adic Hodge Theory, Theorem 2.2.7 and Proposition 4.4.8.

Apply this argument to y modulo Fil1, starting from a lower step containing y. Subtract the resulting constant. This gives λ ∈ Fil1 with the same displacement.

We need to approximate λ itself. At precision r, let (j, s) be the scalar witnesses for its displacement. Since s ∈ E S, the difference s − ℓjλ belongs to Fil1 after evaluation. Its class modulo Filr is fixed, by the scalar congruence. For r > 1 the fixed-class theorem now gives a constant in K ∩ Fil1, which is zero. For r ≤ 1 membership already follows. Thus s approximates ℓjλ itself to depth r. This step uses the vanishing of the constant class in positive filtration degree.

Set Λ₀ = exp(−λM/ℓk). Its inverse is the exponential with the opposite sign. In the action on UΛ₀ the exponent c(g)tM/ℓk from U cancels the change from λ to g(λ). Thus the columns of Q₀ = UΛ₀ are invariant de Rham tensors. Moreover, replacing λ by s/ℓj in both exponential polynomials gives two matrices approximating Λ₀ and its inverse. After the common factorial clearing they lie in S and satisfy

ΛΛ′ = ℓ2aI,   Λ = ℓaΛ₀ + D,   D ∈ Matd(Filr).

The product identity holds before evaluation because both exponentials are polynomials in the same nilpotent matrix. The congruence is read after evaluation in the positive de Rham ring. In that ring ℓ is invertible. Hence Λ′Λ = ℓ2aI as well. Multiplying this identity out gives the corresponding error for the inverse.

Λ′ − ℓaΛ₀−1 = −ℓ−aΛ′DΛ₀−1 ∈ Matd(Filr).

We need an untwisted change of lattice for the invariant matrix Q₀. The Hodge-adapted basis gives invariant columns in the filtration degrees hi, with inverse rows in degrees −hi. The change from Q₀ to those columns has entries fixed by GK, hence in K by the de Rham fixed-field theorem. One nonzero scalar in ℤℓ clears their finitely many denominators. Its inverse lies in Fil0 of the de Rham field, so clearing the denominators preserves both filtration bounds. Brinon and Conrad, CMI Summer School Notes on p-adic Hodge Theory, Theorem 4.4.13.

Now take N above the required threshold and large enough to bound the absolute filtration jumps and the pole orders of Q₀. Lift the integral coefficients of the adapted columns to depth 2N. The two filtration estimates applied to Q₀ give integral matrices X₀, X₀′ with product ℓbE2NI. The evaluated products Q₀X₀ and X₀′Q₀−1 have entries in FilN. It remains to pass from this invariant basis to the period matrix at logarithm zero.

Choose B ≥ 0 bounding the pole orders of both U and U−1, then choose the exponential approximations above at depth r = N + B. Set X = ΛX₀ and X′ = X₀′Λ′. The error in UX is U D X₀, of filtration degree at least −B + (N + B) = N. The other error is X₀′(Λ′ − ℓaΛ₀−1)U−1 and has the same bound. The main terms are ℓa times the two untwisted products. Thus

XX′ = ℓb+2aE2NI,   UX, X′U−1 ∈ Matd(FilN).

This lets us first prescribe the pole bound needed for integral crystalline lifts, then obtain the two filtration inequalities for the very same N. The passage to the coefficient disc uses both.

  • A logarithm in the first filtration step with the required displacement
  • Eliminating the constant ambiguity in each scalar approximation
  • Finite exponentials, addition, inverse and congruence
  • Simultaneous cleared approximation of the two exponential matrices
  • Galois-invariant de Rham columns and approximation to arbitrary order
  • Invariant coordinate change over the ground field
  • Clearing the ground-field coefficients and matching the adapted basis
  • Opposite filtration degrees and choice of the common height
  • The untwisted modification with integral lifts and column exponents
  • Both filtration inequalities for the untwisted modification
  • Approximation at the height plus the pole bound
