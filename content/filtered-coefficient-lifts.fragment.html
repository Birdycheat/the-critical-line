How can a power series represent the uniformizer to a prescribed depth?

The lattice calculation starts with coefficients in the ring of integers of a local field K. It needs coefficients in a power-series ring that can be evaluated in several different rings. Write W for the Witt ring of the residue field of K, choose a uniformizer π of K, and let P be its monic Eisenstein polynomial over W, of degree e. Put S = W[[u]] and E = P(u). In the quotient S/(E), the class of u is π. The first question is how to keep this agreement to an arbitrary depth n.

Finite-order lift. There are an integer m ≥ 0 and an element q of S such that

q ≡ ℓmu mod E,   Pm(q) ≡ 0 mod En,   Pm(T) = ℓmeP(T/ℓm).

To construct q, invert ℓ. Separability gives a Bézout identity aP + bP′ = ℓc over W after clearing denominators. Thus a root modulo E that reduces to u has invertible derivative there. Starting at u, correct it one power of E at a time up to depth n.

Why does one correction gain a power of E?

If y ≡ u modulo E and P(y) is divisible by Er, r ≥ 1, set y′ = y − b(u)P(y)/ℓc. Taylor's linear term cancels P(y) modulo Er+1 because b(u)P′(y)/ℓc ≡ 1 modulo E. Higher terms contain E2r. At depths zero and one, u already suffices.

Now write y − u = Ev and P(y) = Enw in S[1/ℓ]. Choose m large enough that ℓmy, ℓmv and ℓmew all belong to S. Taking q = ℓmy proves both integral congruences. For example, take π² = 3 and E = u² − 3 over ℤ3. At depth three one may use

q = u(3 − E/2 + E²/8),   q² − 27 = E³(40 − 5E + E²)/64.

Here q reduces to 3π modulo E. The second identity explains why the correction terms in the first expression matter at higher precision.

Under evaluation in the positive de Rham ring, E generates the first filtration ideal J, and u reduces to π modulo J. The elements q and ℓmπ therefore agree modulo J. The latter is an exact root of Pm. In the factorization of their polynomial-value difference, the divided difference reduces modulo J to ℓm(e−1)P′(π). This is a nonzero element of K and a unit in the de Rham valuation ring. Hence the evaluated q agrees with ℓmπ modulo Jn.

For two prism maps f, g to a common target (A, I), we also need ℓj(f(q) − g(q)) ∈ In for some j. Both maps agree on W and on u modulo I, and each image of E generates I. After inverting ℓ, simple roots that agree modulo I agree to depth n. Clearing the denominator gives the assertion for the same q.

Why does the comparison survive torsion in the target?

For n ≥ 1, in (A/In)[1/ℓ] both evaluations of q solve Pm exactly. Their divided difference is a unit modulo I by the scaled Bézout identity and remains a unit because I is nilpotent in this quotient. Their difference therefore vanishes after localization. By the definition of localization some power of ℓ kills its class in A/In. For n = 0 the asserted containment is automatic.

How does one uniformizer lift supply a whole coefficient matrix?

Every integral coefficient x in K has the form ∑i=0D wiπi with wi in W. Indeed S/(E) is the ring of integers of K, and division by the distinguished polynomial E gives a representative of degree below e. R. Sharifi, Iwasawa Theory, Proposition 2.2.1. Fix a depth n, take the uniformizer lift (m, q) at this depth, and form

s = ∑i=0D ℓm(D−i)wiqi,   κ = mD.

Upon de Rham evaluation one may replace q by ℓmπ modulo Filn. Every summand then has the same factor ℓκ, so s represents ℓκx to that depth. Under two prism maps the Witt coefficients have the same images. The difference between the two evaluations of each positive power of q is a multiple of f(q) − g(q). Consequently, for this pair a single power ℓj puts f(s) − g(s) in (f(E)n).

For a rectangular matrix C over the ring of integers, apply this construction entry by entry. Take the maximum κ of the scaling exponents, using zero for an empty index set. Multiply each lift by the missing power of ℓ. The resulting matrix s satisfies the two estimates below. Once f and g are fixed, the maximum of their finitely many entrywise exponents supplies j.

ev(s) − ℓκC ∈ Mat(Filn),   ℓj(f(s) − g(s)) ∈ Mat((f(E)n)).

The first estimate will control the filtration of period columns. The second will control the change of lattice between two prism maps. The matrix and κ work in every comparison. The exponent j may depend on the chosen pair.
