How many times does a unipotent fibre visit each orbit?

The constant-term calculation sums over individual unipotent cells. An orbital sum counts conjugacy classes of representatives, with a weight. Even in a finite group two classes can occur with different multiplicities. We compute the multiplicity before using the vanishing of the constant term.

Work in the block upper triangular group P. Let N be its subgroup with identity diagonal blocks, T the abelian centralizer of γ in the diagonal blocks, and L a subgroup of P. The function Ψ on P is invariant under T on the left and L on the right. For two commensurable subgroups U, W of T, define the weight relative to the fixed reference subgroup U by

V(U, W) = [U : U ∩ W] / [W : U ∩ W].

Inside the coarse double coset T m N L, choose a representative y = mn₀ of one fine double coset T y L, where n₀ ∈ N. Write S = T ∩ m(NL)m−1 and W = T ∩ yLy−1. Moving from m to y preserves S, since NL is a subgroup. The elements of S carry yL through exactly those right cosets in mNL which belong to TyL. Indeed, if t yL lies in mNL, then t ∈ T ∩ m(NL)m−1. The stabilizer of yL in S is W. Meanwhile the map n ↦ mnL identifies N/(N ∩ L) with all right cosets in mNL. Thus the unipotent sum visits TyL exactly [S : W] times.

Suppose these indices are finite. The index formula in a tower of subgroups, applied after intersecting U, S and W, gives V(U, W) = [S : W] V(U, S). For an inner sum of finite support we obtain

∑TyL ⊂ TmNL V(U, T ∩ yLy−1) Ψ(y) = V(U, S) ∑n ∈ N/(N ∩ L) Ψ(mn).

Take the group of affine maps of the line over the field with five elements. Let N act by translations and take T = L = U to be the four nonzero scalings. The right coset of an affine map is specified by its value at zero. The scalings have two orbits on these five values, the origin and the four nonzero points. Their stabilizers have orders four and one. Give the origin the value −4 and every other point the value 1. The two weighted orbital terms are 1 · (−4) and 4 · 1. Their sum recovers the cancellation among all five values. Simply adding the two representative values would give −3.

Why does the full local orbital sum vanish?

Let k be a nonarchimedean local field, G = GLn(k), and K₀ a compact open subgroup. Let f be a compactly supported, locally constant, bi-K₀-invariant complex function. Assume its constant terms along every conjugate of every proper two-block unipotent subgroup vanish, including every translate in the argument of f. If the characteristic polynomial of γ is separable and reducible over k, its orbital sum against f is zero. The primary decomposition supplies a basis in which γ and its centralizer T lie in the diagonal Levi. Conjugating γ, the level and the test together preserves the double cosets, stabilizer indices and constant-term hypothesis. Use that basis throughout.

We first check the finite index in the count of unipotent cells. Fix a right level conjugate K′ of K₀, set L = P ∩ K′, and take x ∈ P. In the previous notation W = T ∩ xLx−1 is open in T. For s ∈ S = T ∩ x(NL)x−1, write s = n x l x−1 using normality of N. Projection to the two diagonal blocks kills n and fixes s. Consequently S lies in the compact image of xLx−1 under this projection. Its closure in the closed subgroup T is compact. The open cosets of W cover that closure, so only finitely many can meet S.

Partition G into P y K₀. In one such piece put Ly = P ∩ y K₀y−1. The map m ↦ my identifies T∖P/Ly with the double cosets in this piece. Two representatives m and m′ give the same class exactly when m′ = t m l with t ∈ T and l ∈ Ly. Their centralizer weights agree, since T and m lie in P. Define fy(z) = f(y−1zy), δ = m−1γm. The values in the unipotent fibre are precisely

Ψy(mn) = fy(n−1δn),   n ∈ N/(N ∩ Ly).

The conjugated test inherits the constant-term hypothesis. The invertible change of block coordinate and the cell-volume calculation show that this finite sum of values is zero. The relative weight is constant on the coarse fibre, so every coarse fibre contributes zero. Summing first within the piece and then over the pieces proves the assertion. Here and below a sum over an arbitrary set is interpreted as a finite-support sum, assigned zero for infinite support. If the full summand has finite support the partition restricts to an ordinary finite partition. If its support is infinite the full sum is zero by this convention. The inner unipotent sums have actual finite support by compactness.

In the ordinary orbital sum the reference subgroup is U = T ∩ K₀. Its usual level average has the same integrand, since replacing a representative x by xk conjugates x−1γx by k ∈ K₀, under which f is invariant.
