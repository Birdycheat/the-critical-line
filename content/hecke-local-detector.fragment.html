Which local operator fixes the forms that fail the test?

Fix a tested split place w, write F = Ew, and let q be its residue-field cardinality. In the split unitary factor we may work with G = GLa(F) and Kw = GLa(𝒪F). Fix a nonzero polynomial Φ in the a Satake coordinates. A representation fails the test if some ordering z of its local Satake parameters satisfies Φ(z) = 0. To bound the dimension of its contribution, we need an operator that acts by one on such a representation and whose complex coefficients have a small sum of squares.

Local operator theorem. For every δ > 0 there is a finite sum of translations at w, with coefficients h(t), satisfying ∑t |h(t)|² ≤ δ. Its supporting elements give distinct cosets tKw. The operator acts as the identity on the Kw-fixed vectors in every smooth irreducible representation with a Satake tuple on the zero set of Φ.

Choose a symmetric Laurent polynomial p equal to one on that zero set and small in mean square on the unit torus. The condition on the zero set concerns all tuples in (ℂ×)a. Only the norm is computed on the unit torus. The following argument determines how small this norm must be. Construction of the polynomial.

From double classes to individual translations. Let μ be a Haar measure on G and put m = μ(Kw) > 0. By the Satake isomorphism there is a compactly supported, bi-Kw-invariant function H whose spherical transform, computed with μ, is p. In this normalization the integrated operator acts by p(z) on the fixed vectors with parameter z. Choose one representative t of each coset tKw in the support. This set T is finite by compactness of the support and openness of Kw. For any fixed vector v in a representation σ, integration on the cosets gives

∫G H(x)σ(x)v dμ(x) = ∑t∈T h(t)σ(t)v,   h(t) = mH(t).

A double class KwxKw contributes one term for every coset it contains. Their number is the index of Kw ∩ xKwx−1 in Kw, since the cosets are kxKw for k ∈ Kw. For GL₂, take x = diag(ϖ, 1) with ϖ a uniformizer. The lattices kx𝒪F² between ϖ𝒪F² and 𝒪F² reduce to the lines in the two-dimensional residue space. Each line occurs and determines its coset, so this double class has (q² − 1)/(q − 1) = q + 1 terms. The factor m in each coefficient is its coset volume. I. Satake, Theory of spherical functions on reductive algebraic groups over p-adic fields, Theorem 3.

The measure in the mean square. Give the torus (S¹)a its product Haar probability measure dz. Put s = q−1 and Va(s) = ∏j=1a[(1 − sj)/(1 − s)]. The spherical Plancherel measure on the ordered parameters is represented by the symmetric density

D(z) = (Va(s)/a!) ∏i<j |(1 − zi/zj)/(1 − s zi/zj)|².

Macdonald’s spherical inversion formula gives ∫ φz(y)D(z) dz = 1Kw(y), where φz is the spherical function normalized by φz(1) = 1. In particular D(z) dz has mass one. Since s < 1, the denominators stay nonzero on the torus, and its compactness gives a finite bound C on D. I. G. Macdonald, Spherical Functions on a Group of p-adic Type, Chapter V, §5.1.

To extract a single coefficient, use the eigenfunction identity ∑t∈T h(t)φz(yt) = p(z)φz(y). Set y = u−1 for one representative u ∈ T and integrate against D(z) dz. Spherical inversion keeps exactly the term with u−1t ∈ Kw, which is t = u. Thus h(u) = ∫ p(z)φz(u−1)D(z) dz. Multiply by the complex conjugate of h(u) and sum over u. On the unit torus φz(u−1) is the conjugate of φz(u) almost everywhere. The eigenfunction identity at y = 1 then gives

∑u∈T |h(u)|² = ∫ |p(z)|² D(z) dz ≤ C ∫ |p(z)|² dz.

This equality includes all the coset multiplicities and uses the coefficients mH(t). For example, the constant polynomial one has kernel 1Kw/m. Its unique coefficient is one for every normalization of μ. Choosing the polynomial at tolerance δ/(1 + C) proves the coefficient bound, while p(z) = 1 supplies the identity action, including for a failed Satake tuple outside the unit torus.

Transport to the global level. Splitness identifies G with the adelic unitary subgroup supported over the prime of the real subfield below w. The component at w determines the element of this subgroup, including its component at the conjugate place. Lift each representative through this identification and keep its coefficient. The lift is injective, so the squared coefficient sum is preserved. On this subgroup the global level K cuts out Kw, hence the lifted support still consists of distinct cosets modulo K on the right. Its components above the prime of the real subfield below the auxiliary place w0 are trivial because the two underlying primes differ.

To transfer the scalar as well, one needs a local realization. A failed test supplies a smooth irreducible local representation σ occurring in the global representation and an ordering z of its Satake parameters with Φ(z) = 0. The images of the local intertwining maps from σ span the irreducible global representation. Indeed their span is nonzero by occurrence, is stable under the local factor, and is preserved by the other adelic factor, which commutes with it. For a globally K-fixed vector, express it as a finite sum of such images and average over Kw. Smoothness makes this a finite coset average. It fixes the original vector and replaces each local preimage by a fixed vector. The local operator acts by p(z) on each such vector and commutes with every intertwining map. This proves the required identity action on the entire failing span used in the tower count. Counting the forms that fail the test.
