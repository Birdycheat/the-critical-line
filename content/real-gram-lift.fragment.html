How can a compact family of frames be lifted to isometries?

At a real place of the base field, a Hermitian space is a complex vector space V with a nonsingular form h, conjugate-linear in its first variable. For an ordered tuple of columns X = (x₁,…,xₙ), its Gram matrix T has entries h(xᵢ,xⱼ). Suppose det T ≠ 0. Any linear relation on the columns would give a vector in the kernel of T, so the columns are independent. If Y has the same Gram matrix, the map xᵢ ↦ yᵢ is an isometry of the column spans. Witt’s extension theorem gives an isometry of all of V extending that map. The shell estimate needs more. Given any compact set Ω of tuples, one compact set C in the unitary group must supply a lift g with gX = Y for every Y ∈ Ω of Gram matrix T. Sprehn and Wahl, Forms over fields and Witt’s lemma, Theorem 3.4.

First diagonalize T by one fixed change of columns in both tuples. Indeed, a nonzero Hermitian form has a vector with nonzero value on the diagonal, by the polarization identity. Split off its line by orthogonal projection and repeat on the nonsingular complement. The new columns are mutually orthogonal with real squared lengths bᵢ ≠ 0, which may have either sign. Undoing the common column change at the end preserves the equation gX = Y.

Move one column and keep the earlier columns fixed. Suppose the next column is currently x and must be sent to y, with h(x,x) = h(y,y) = b ≠ 0. Set t = h(y,x). For at least one choice of λ = ±1 we have |b − λt| ≥ |b|, since the mean of |b−t|² and |b+t|² is b²+|t|². With u = x−λy and δ = b−λt, define

A(z) = z − u h(u,z)/δ,   B(z) = z − (1−λ)y h(y,z)/b.

Here h(u,x) = δ, so A(x) = λy. To check that A is an isometry, expand h(Az,Aw). The coefficient of h(z,u)h(u,w) in the difference from h(z,w) is [h(u,u)−2 Re δ]/|δ|², which vanishes because h(u,u) = 2b−2λ Re t. The second map acts by λ on the line of y and fixes its orthogonal complement. Thus BA is an isometry sending x to y. Each previously matched column is orthogonal to both and stays fixed.

Keep the lifts in a compact set. The subset of Ω on which the Gram matrix equals T is closed in Ω, hence compact. Start there with the identity matrix. At the first column retain both closed subsets where the corresponding sign satisfies |δ| ≥ |b₁|. They cover the compact set, and on each the displayed matrix depends continuously on Y. At the next column use its image under the current matrix as x and repeat within each subset. After n columns there are at most 2n compact subsets. On each, the product of the successive matrices is a continuous function of Y. The union of their compact images is the required C. At a tie either sign supplies a lift.

In the shell argument the two initial vectors e, f are isotropic, with h(e,f) = 1. Their Gram matrix has determinant −1. The column change (e+f, e−f) gives squared lengths 2 and −2, so both steps have a denominator of absolute value at least 2. For instance, the pairs (a e, a−1f) for 1 ≤ a ≤ 2 lift to the compact family diag(a, a−1) on their plane, extended by the identity on its orthogonal complement. The one-vector orbit mean uses the same real-place assertion for a line of nonzero norm.
