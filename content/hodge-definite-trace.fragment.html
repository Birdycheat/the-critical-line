Why does one marked line lead to a sum over all the roots?

At the distinguished infinite place v the Hermitian form has one positive direction. Assume that v is the unique infinite place of positive index one and that the rank n is odd. Write g for the Hecke test away from p at level K, and use the indicator of the prescribed subgroup Kp at p. The function g is bi-invariant under K and supported on finitely many double cosets. At each element of its nonzero support, assume that the centralizer in the local algebra is commutative at some place away from p. For a rational similitude γ let Pγ denote the minimal polynomial of its algebra component over the centre F. If γ preserves the positive line ℓ, write μ for the scalar by which it acts on ℓ. The contribution of the line may now be weighted by any complex function w(Pγ, μ). The fibre labels furnish the weights used in the Hodge trace.

First group the coincidences by the rational element. A rational pair consists of a form β in the prescribed local class and a similitude γ of β with its rational multiplier. We identify pairs under rational change of coordinates and positive rational rescaling of the form. For a fixed pair, let O(β, γ; g) denote its orbital term, which collects the finite markings satisfying the two Hecke tests, with their stabilizer weights. To see how this term arises, retain both the point x and the element γ fixing its line. At a representative x, its stabilizer acts on the qualifying elements by conjugation. Dividing their sum by the order of the stabilizer gives one term per orbit, with denominator the order of the subgroup fixing both x and γ. Fixing γ first therefore leaves its rational centralizer acting on the markings. Scaling a generator of ℓ or conjugating the pair and its line together preserves μ. The regularity and finiteness theorems for these points give finitely many contributing point orbits and marked pair classes. This justifies regrouping the sums. Stabilizers of marked points.

On a contributing pair, Pγ is separable of degree n. Its complex eigenvalues at v are distinct. If a positive eigenline exists, its orthogonal complement is negative definite and γ-stable. Since the eigenvalue on the line occurs only once, every other eigenline belongs to the complement. Thus a pair with a marked line contributes w(Pγ, μ)O(β, γ; g) for a unique μ. A pair without a positive eigenline contributes zero. For the chosen Hecke test, the vanishing theorem for a marked pair gives O(β, γ; g) = 0 whenever the polynomial is reducible over F. Only irreducible polynomials remain in the root count.

The odd-rank comparison. For each irreducible P that occurs and each root μ at v, there is a bijection between pair classes with polynomial P marked by μ and definite pair classes with polynomial P. It preserves the orbital term. The arithmetic input changes the form while keeping γ and its multiplier. In the field F[γ], choose a polynomial A so that a definite form βd is sent to β = A(γ)βd. At v its sign is positive on the μ-line and negative on the other n − 1 lines. At the other infinite places the prescribed definite signs are preserved. The preceding sign-and-norm theorem constructs the same scalar A(γ) with these signs and as a norm away from p. The norm gives a finite adelic isometry away from p commuting with γ. The local comparison of orbital terms applies to this isometry. The reverse change divides by A(γ), and rational conjugation transports the polynomial in γ with the form. These two operations induce inverse maps on classes.

Enumerate the n distinct roots of each P at v. On the original side a given pair occurs at exactly one position in that enumeration. After the comparison, the same definite pair occurs once at every position, with the same orbital term each time. This gives the weighted fixed-point count

∑[βd, γ], Pγ irreducible O(βd, γ; g) ∑μ root of Pγ at v w(Pγ, μ).

For example, with w identically one and n = 3, a single contributing definite pair carries three times its orbital term. Each of the three original marked classes has a single positive eigenline. For the weights built from the coefficients cα, the root calculation evaluates the inner sum as the prescribed character weight times ∑i yhi.

How does the definite pair sum become a Hecke trace?

Fix a definite form B and write Γ for its rational similitude group as embedded in the product H of the finite adelic group away from p and the local group at p. Set U = K × Kp, and write f(t) = g(tp) 1Kp(tp). Let ξ be the given finite-dimensional complex coefficient representation, restricted to Γ. The automorphic forms at this level are functions Φ from H to its representation space V satisfying Φ(γxu) = ξ(γ)Φ(x). At a representative x of Γ ∖ H / U, this forces Φ(x) to belong to the invariants of Γx = Γ ∩ xUx−1. Conversely, choosing an invariant value at each representative defines Φ by that same formula. The definite class-number and stabilizer finiteness theorems give finitely many representatives for B and finite Γx. For the classical description of algebraic modular forms in these coordinates, see M. Greenberg and J. Voight, Lattice methods for algebraic modular forms on classical groups, §2, Proposition 2.3 and Lemma 2.4.

The level normalization gives each coset tU mass one. Thus (TfΦ)(x) = ∑tU f(t)Φ(xt). Only finitely many cosets meet the support. To take the trace, retain the cosets that return a representative x to its own double coset. Such a return has xtU = γxU. Its possible γ form a right coset of Γx. Averaging ξ over Γx projects V onto its invariant space. Taking the trace of this diagonal block therefore gives

tr(Tf | B) = ∑[x] |Γx|−1 ∑γ∈Γ f(x−1γx) tr(ξ(γ)).

For the indicator 1U the convolution is the identity. Its contribution at x is the average of tr(ξ(γ)) over Γx, hence the dimension of the invariants there. For a stabilizer of order two acting on a line by the sign character the average is (1 − 1)/2 = 0. For the trivial character it is (1 + 1)/2 = 1. This calculation tests the trace normalization before imposing any regularity condition. The index from the deeper geometric marking has already cancelled when the normalized test reaches this formula.

Group the returns by the Γ-conjugacy class of γ. The embeddings of a fixed γ are then counted modulo its rational centralizer, with the stabilizer denominator above. For Pγ separable, irreducible and of degree n, the resulting centralizer sum is precisely O(B, γ; g). For every other γ the two auxiliary places enforce vanishing for each definite form and permitted finite trivialization. A repeated root at the place of regular support excludes every conjugate of γ. In the remaining case the cuspidal place kills the corresponding orbital sum. Grouping the rational centralizer cosets over each adelic centralizer coset writes the rational sum as a common mass factor times that orbital integral. Thus the rational sum also vanishes. This permits cancellation inside an orbital integral even when individual test values are nonzero.

Finally assume that, on every definite form and every γ with separable polynomial of degree n, the prescribed weight χ(Pγ) equals tr(ξ(γ)). Summing the preceding trace over equivalence classes of definite forms defines D(g). Under these hypotheses the definite sum with weight χ equals D(g). An empty set of contributing pairs gives zero. Substituting the root sum in the preceding section gives D(g) ∑i yhi for every y. To extract a nonzero cohomology class one still needs a test for which D(g) ≠ 0. The coefficient and eigenspace argument explains where that condition enters.
