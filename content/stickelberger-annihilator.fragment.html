Which integral element kills the ideal classes?

Fix M > 2, let L = ℚ(ζM), and write C = Cl(L) additively. A class is zero when an ideal representing it is principal. For a unit t modulo M, let σt send ζM to ζMt. Write et = σt−1, so et sends the class of an ideal to the class of the ideal obtained by applying σt−1. In particular J = e−1 acts by complex conjugation. Write ⟨t⟩ for the representative in {0, …, M−1}.

Stickelberger’s annihilation theorem. In the rational group ring of G = Gal(L/ℚ) put ϑ = ∑t(⟨t⟩/M)et, summing over the units. If β ∈ ℤ[G] and βϑ has integer coefficients, then (βϑ)C = 0. In ideal language the product of the Galois translates, raised to those integer coefficients, is a principal fractional ideal. C. S. Yogananda, Stickelberger Revisited, p. 151, opening theorem and proof.

Take an odd positive integer c coprime to M and put k = (c−1)/2. For each unit t, set u = ⟨c−1t⟩. Division with remainder gives cu = M⌊cu/M⌋ + ⟨t⟩. Thus the coefficient g(t) = ⌊cu/M⌋ − k is an integer. Replacing t by −t replaces u by M−u and the quotient by c−1−⌊cu/M⌋. Hence g(−t) = −g(t). The half-sum θ used in the tower takes g(t)et once per pair, on the side 2⟨t⟩ < M. Multiplication by 1−J restores the other coefficients with opposite signs.

θ(1−J) = ∑t g(t)et = [c ec − (k+1) − kJ] ϑ.

To check the last equality, multiply its coefficient at et by M. The three terms give cu − (k+1)⟨t⟩ − k(M−⟨t⟩) = M g(t). The bracket therefore supplies precisely the integral multiple required by the theorem.

Why does a carry give a principal ideal?

Here is the ideal calculation behind this particular annihilator. Take any class x. Artin reciprocity for the Hilbert class field and Chebotarev’s theorem give a prime ideal 𝔭 in this class of rational prime norm p, with p coprime to M. Since L/ℚ is Galois and 𝔭 has residue degree one, p splits completely. The distinct ideals et𝔭 therefore multiply to (p).

For residues a, b let εt(a,b) equal 1 when ⟨at⟩ + ⟨bt⟩ ≥ M and 0 otherwise. For a, b, a+b all nonzero, the finite-field Jacobi sum attached to the two character powers divides p. Testing a value at et𝔭 amounts to applying σt and then reducing at 𝔭. Since σt raises the character values to the tth power, the two exponents become at and bt. The Jacobi sum vanishes on reduction at precisely the conjugate primes with εt = 0. The factorization of (p) is square-free, so the complementary product, over the primes with a carry, is the ideal generated by p divided by this Jacobi sum. For a = 0 or b = 0 the carry product is empty. For a+b = 0 with both nonzero it is (p). In all cases ∑t εt(a,b)etx = 0. Residue test for the Jacobi sum · Why the Jacobi sum divides the prime.

Adding ⟨t⟩ to itself h times produces ⌊h⟨t⟩/M⌋ carries. Indeed, increasing h by one increases the floor by εt(h,1). The carry relations show that Fh = ∑t⌊h⟨t⟩/M⌋et kills x. Also (∑tet)x = 0 because the product of all the conjugate prime ideals is (p). Reindexing the coefficient of Fc gives θ(1−J) = ecFc − k∑tet. Both summands kill x. Since the prime could be chosen in every class, this proves the required action on C.

How does the integral relation meet the calculation modulo ℓ?

Set E = (1−J)C. The integral computation gives θE = 0. This statement applies at ℓ = 2 as well, because its proof uses the image of 1−J without dividing by 2. For example, take M = 8 and c = 3. At t = 1,3,5,7 the four coefficients are 0,−1,1,0. The half-sum θ = −e₃ has nonzero reduction modulo 2, and it acts invertibly on C. Its integral annihilation of E therefore implies E = 0 in this example.

In the tower M = fℓⁿ take c = 2fℓ+1, with f > 1 prime to ℓ. The nonzero projected coefficient yields a separate group-ring relation (γ−1)N ∈ (θ, 1+J, ℓ) with N uniform for sufficiently large n. Apply it to E. The first generator acts by zero by integral annihilation. The second acts by zero since (1+J)(1−J) = 0. The third maps E into ℓE. Thus (γ−1)N acts by zero on E/ℓE, the group whose successive layers enter the class-rank bound.
