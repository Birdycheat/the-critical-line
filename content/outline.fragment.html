Follow one possible zero through the proof

Suppose, for the sake of following the argument, that ζ had a nontrivial zero ρ with real part 0.60. What would force a contradiction? Start with the zero, then work backwards to the arithmetic estimate needed to exclude it. The number 0.60 is an illustrative choice.

  1. A zero leaves a pole. Near a zero of multiplicity m, write ζ(s)=(s−ρ)mh(s), with h(ρ)≠0. Its logarithmic derivative therefore contains m/(s−ρ). The proof recovers this pole by integrating a function of prime powers against x−s−1. The function uses a ramp weight and subtracts an explicit main term. The arithmetic construction also introduces Dirichlet characters. When those are grouped, the multiplicities at the same zero have positive weights, so the pole survives. Compute its residue.
  2. A bound on distant intervals would exclude this pole. Choose a real exponent strictly between ½ and 0.60, for example 0.55. The analytic theorem excludes poles to its right if the real centered family sum is bounded above by a constant times x0.55 on arbitrarily distant intervals of the prescribed relative width. First fix a level whose weighted character sum sees the proposed zero. For each sufficiently small positive ε the arithmetic construction supplies auxiliary data giving windows for that same sum. Its exponent on the specialized route is ½+9ε. Taking ε ≤ 0.005, subject also to the construction’s threshold, puts the exponent at most 0.545. An exponent of 0.65 would leave a pole at 0.60 outside the reach of the theorem. The full argument makes this choice after the prospective zero. See the quantifiers and check the integral on that same interval.
  3. The interval comes from arithmetic. A prime-power count has two descriptions. Characters express its remainder in terms of centered sums. Maps between arithmetic lattices constrain the same count through divisibility at finite primes and size at the infinite places. Matching their main terms makes the residual error small enough. The length of a chain of theta lifts balances costs of size x/m and m. On those intervals the cohomological error fits inside the weight allowance for the same measurement. Follow the division by the total weight or see why the intervals coincide.

The contradiction rules out the proposed 0.60. Repeating it for every real part above ½ and using the functional equation excludes the other side of the strip. For characters the functional equation changes which character occurs. Follow the reflection step.

For a first reading, the prime-power calculation, the window theorem and reflection explain exactly what the arithmetic must deliver. Chapters 3–8 build that estimate and identify the deeper inputs whose explanations are still being developed. The reconstruction record gives their current scope.
