How do the rational period columns give initial data on the coefficient disc?

Fix the stable ℤℓ-lattice, the compatible integral and rational period rings, and a primitive period τ for the coefficient disc. We start with U and U−1 in Bcris. The integral lifting property for the crystalline ring gives a threshold for each of these two matrices. At or above the larger threshold N₀, their multiples tNU and tNU−1 have lifts Z and Z′ in Acris. There are only finitely many entries, and multiplying a lift by t raises the allowed exponent. The integral ring embeds in the rational one, so identities between the lifts can be checked there.

Use the two-sided change of lattice with this lower threshold. It gives the same height N for X, X′ and the two lifts. Write c₂ for the resulting exponent in XX′ = ℓc₂E2NI. Then ZZ′ = t2NI. Multiplying each filtration inequality for UX and X′U−1 by tN gives

ZX, X′Z′ ∈ Matd(Fil2N BdR+).

Here X and X′ are evaluated in the indicated ring. With F and c₁ from the Frobenius formula and κ from the Galois formula, substitution of the lifts also gives

tNℓc₁φ(Z) = φ(t)NZF,   tNℓk₀ρ(g)g(Z) = g(t)NZκ(g).

For example, the Frobenius equality follows by multiplying ℓc₁φ(U) = UF by tNφ(t)N. The factors in front of Z keep track of which copy of t has been acted on. The integral lifting and filtration claims, together with the witness at each depth, now concern the same lattice.

Passing to the disc. Write D for the coefficient-disc ring and γ for a bounded ring map Acris → D extending the given map from Ainf. Two comparison facts enter. First, the image under γ of an integral crystalline element of de Rham filtration degree r is divisible by er, where e is the image of E on the disc. Second, γ(t) = νeτ for a unit ν of D, with τ now denoting its image there. The disc is a domain, e is nonzero, and ℓ is a unit there. Put G = γ(Z) and G′ = γ(Z′). The first fact applied to the two filtration inequalities gives unique matrices Y and Y′ over D with

GX = e2NY,   X′G′ = e2NY′.

Here we have also mapped X and X′ to D. Multiplying the two equations gives e4NYY′ = G(XX′)G′. Substitute XX′ = ℓc₂e2NI and GG′ = (νeτ)2NI, then cancel e4N. The two disc matrices and their product are therefore

W = ν−NY,   W′ = ℓ−c₂ν−NY′,   WW′ = τ2NI.

There is also a pole condition for the action on the changed lattice. Multiply the integral Galois identity on the left by X′Z′ and on the right by g(X). Since Z′Z = t2NI as well, the resulting identity in de Rham coordinates is

g(t)Nt2N X′κ(g)g(X) = tNℓk₀(X′Z′)ρ(g)g(ZX).

Both parenthesized products on the right have filtration degree at least 2N, and Galois preserves the filtration. Since t and g(t) have order one, the right side has degree at least 5N. Removing the scalar on the left costs 3N. The entries of X′κ(g)g(X) therefore belong to Fil2N. On the disc the comparison gives divisibility by e2N, which persists after dividing the correction by the unit ℓk₀.

The normalized Galois correction on the disc is κD(g) = ℓ−k₀γ(κ(g)). In particular κD(g) = I when g fixes the chosen root system. For a compatible action of g on D, applying γ to the filtration error at depth m makes it divisible by em. Dividing by the unit ℓk₀ gives κD(g)g(Λm) ≡ Λm modulo em with the very same Λm for all such actions. Frobenius and Galois commute with γ for the compatible disc maps, so the two integral action identities descend after the same scalar normalizations.

This constructs the starting period data at a height N that can depend on the representation. In the application to a tower, a crystalline representation uses the separate crystalline construction with trivial correction from the start. The change of height then imposes the common bound read from the Hodge jumps.
