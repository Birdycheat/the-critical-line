Which integral model receives the tower?

At principal level N, the moduli space XN and its fibre power ZN are defined over the number field E. To study reduction at a place w, we need a space over its local ring R = 𝒪E,w. We build it by keeping two pieces of the full level marking. This specifies the target, as well as the map from the tower to its generic fibre.

Integral model and comparison theorem. Take the compact unitary moduli datum of the standard base theorem, with one distinguished embedding on each side of the link, algebra degree one and odd group rank. The identification F ≅ E carries the place 𝔴 of the datum to w and gives the chosen generic labels. At the good prime q ≠ p, the quadratic polynomial of ω has a simple root r modulo q, the lattice pairing is perfect over ℤq, and 𝔴 contains (q, ω − r). Use the level factorization 𝔫 = (N0)𝔞, where N0 ≥ 3 is prime to pq, 𝔞 + 𝔴 = 𝒪F, and 𝔞 contains a power of (q, ω − r).

There is a smooth projective R-scheme M representing the integral level-N0 moduli problem. A finite étale cover Y → M records, in addition, a marking of the torsion killed by every element of 𝔞. Write AY for the pullback of the universal abelian scheme on M. For k ≥ 0 its k-fold fibre product 𝒵 over Y is smooth and projective over R. At any principal tower level N prime to p with N ∈ 𝔫, a choice of auxiliary generator a as below gives a comparison

πN ∶ ZN → 𝒵E, 𝒵E = 𝒵 ×Spec R Spec E.

Construction. First extend the standard labels to R. The integral labels are the images of ω and the integers of F under the given field identification and localization. Integers prime to q are units in R. The degree-one algebra splits at q, so these labels define the same unitary datum there, with residue field of order qf at w. The integral representability theorem supplies M, of relative dimension n − 1 for lattice rank parameter n. Compactness gives properness, hence projectivity with the quasi-projective moduli embedding. The finite-level representability theorem applies to 𝔞 by its support and coprimality conditions above. It supplies Y and identifies its universal family with the one pulled back from M. These two representability results are geometric inputs. For the unitary moduli construction see Harris and Taylor, The Geometry and Cohomology of Some Simple Shimura Varieties, Chapter III.

The finite map makes Y projective over R, and étaleness preserves the relative dimension of M. The polarization gives relative projectivity of the abelian scheme. Forming another fibre factor pulls back a smooth abelian scheme of relative dimension g = dim(AY/Y). Starting at 𝒵 = Y when k = 0 gives relative dimension (n − 1) + kg over R. The Stacks Project, finite morphisms are projective and smooth morphisms under composition and base change.

It remains to specify the two markings. Since N ∈ (N0)𝔞, the rational number c = N/N0 belongs to 𝒪F and hence is a positive integer. In the Dedekind ring 𝒪F, write (N) = 𝔞𝔟. One can choose a with (a, N) = 𝔟 by prescribing its valuation at the finitely many primes dividing N using the Chinese remainder theorem. Thus

N = N0c, (a, N)𝔞 = (N), η0(y) = ηN(cy), λ(y) = ηN(ay).

Here ηN is the full level marking on the lattice Λ. The first mark retains level N0. To read the second, localize at a prime dividing N. If N and 𝔞 have valuations s and b, multiplication by a on Λ/NΛ has the same image and kernel there as multiplication by a uniformizer to the power s − b. Its kernel in Λ is therefore 𝔞Λ locally, and its image in Λ/NΛ consists exactly of the classes killed by 𝔞. At primes away from N the quotient vanishes. The full marking now identifies Λ/𝔞Λ with the required torsion on geometric fibres.

The two marks belong to the original polarized abelian scheme, with its prescribed endomorphisms. The representing property gives a map jN ∶ XN → Y over R and identifies the original family with the pullback of AY. Level-N0 rigidity makes this identification unique for the chosen marks. Applying it to each of the k points gives a map to 𝒵. Its structure map already factors through Spec E, so the universal property of the generic fibre gives πN.

  • Construction of the local model and the auxiliary cover. Relative dimension and projectivity of each fibre power.
  • The auxiliary generator in a Dedekind ring and the integer and the compatible scaled generator. Forgetting full level on the same abelian scheme, the two lattice statements and the induced auxiliary marking.

Why does changing the full marking leave the comparison unchanged?

Let K0 consist of the elements preserving the prime-to-p adelic lattice Λ̂ in both directions and inducing the identity modulo 𝔫Λ̂. An element u of this group can move a full level-N marking. We show that the two marks seen by Y, and the images of the points in the abelian scheme, survive such a move.

Invariance under transport and refinement. Keep c and a at level N. Let N′ = Nt be another admissible principal level, with marks given by ct and ta. These satisfy the same ideal identity, since (ta, Nt) = (t)(a, N). For every u ∈ K0 and every defined tower transition Tu from level N′ to level N,

πN ∘ Tu = πN′.

Proof. The transition on abelian schemes is an isomorphism to the transported family. Indeed, u and u−1 preserve Λ̂, so the inverse-isogeny construction can use the integer 1 in the two composites. The polarization is preserved with exponent one as well. The transition formula expresses its similitude multiplier as a positive integer d prime to p times an integral adelic unit away from p. The multiplier of a lattice automorphism and its inverse are themselves integral away from p. Hence d has zero valuation at every prime away from p, and coprimality also gives zero valuation at p. This forces d = 1.

The ideals (N0) and 𝔞 are coprime because N0 is prime to q and 𝔞 contains a power of (q, ω − r). The Chinese remainder theorem splits the congruence on u into the congruences modulo those two ideals. The first identifies the transported ηN′(cty) with ηN(cy). For the second, (a)𝔞 ⊆ (N) gives u(ay) − ay ∈ NΛ̂ for every y. Under the transition the mark ηN′(tay) therefore becomes ηN(ay). The two maps into Y classify the same marked object. Uniqueness identifies both their base maps and their maps of abelian schemes. Applying the latter to every point in the fibre power proves the formula.

At t = 1 this gives invariance at a fixed level. When K(N) ⊆ K0, the action descends to the finite group K0/K(N). With the right-action convention, z · δ is the transition for a representative of δ−1. Invariance shows that every such orbit lies in a fibre of πN. The finite étale comparison and the description of its fibres by the graphs of this action give the converse. A relation among correspondences after reduction at w requires the separate special-fibre congruence.

For example, take 𝔞 = 𝒪F, N0 = 3 and N = 6 at a site with p = 5 and q = 17. We can choose c = 2 and a = 6. The auxiliary marking is zero and the retained marking is η6(2y). At level 12 the choices become 4 and 12. Forgetting from 12 to 6 sends η12(4y) to η6(2y), two descriptions of the same 3-torsion point. This checks the factor t in the comparison.
