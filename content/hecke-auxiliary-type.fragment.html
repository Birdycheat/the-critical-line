How does a cyclic character impose a local type?

At the split auxiliary place w₀ above ℓ, we want approximating forms with a nontrivial local character. In the application the map from the local level onto a cyclic group is surjective, so a nontrivial character of the cyclic group stays nontrivial when pulled back. The starting form is invariant under its local level. Averaging its translates against a nontrivial character would therefore give zero. Instead, we multiply the form by each character of an equivariant label. Summing these new forms gives an error whose ℓ-adic size can be computed exactly.

Fixed-weight character estimate. Fix an algebraic weight W on the definite unitary group, a level K away from w₀, and a torsion-free compact open subgroup J of the split local group. Let N = ℓm, with m ≥ 1, and let θ be a homomorphism from J to the additive cyclic group ℤ/Nℤ. If a form f of weight W is fixed by KJ, it admits an approximation G in the span of K-fixed forms of weight W whose representations contain a vector with local character χ ∘ θ for some nontrivial character χ of ℤ/Nℤ. At each adelic point g, the error is a scalar multiple of f there, with multiplier of ℓ-adic absolute value at most ℓ−m.

Proof. The kernel of θ is open even if we start with an abstract group homomorphism. Indeed, on the matrix algebra over the completed number field the derivative of x ↦ xN at the identity sends X to NX. This is invertible in characteristic zero. Apply the multivariable Hensel lemma to the entries of xN − y at the identity. Its Jacobian determinant there is Na². As y approaches the identity the residual tends to zero, so the lemma gives a root approaching the identity, hence a root in J. The image of the power map thus contains a neighbourhood of the identity. Each such power belongs to the kernel. Apply the equivariant label construction with θ on J and the trivial map on K. It gives a label L(g) ∈ ℤ/Nℤ invariant on the left by rational elements and on the right by the archimedean group and K, with L(gj) = L(g) + θ(j) for j ∈ J. K. Conrad, A multivariable Hensel’s lemma, Theorem 3.3.

Choose a primitive complex Nth root of unity ζ. The characters are χb(x) = ζbx for 0 ≤ b < N. For each b ≠ 0 put Fb(v, g) = χb(L(g))f(v, g), where v is a weight vector. The multiplier is locally constant and archimedean invariant. Thus Fb has the same weight as f. Translation by j multiplies it by χb(θ(j)). The automorphic decomposition at a fixed character writes Fb as a finite sum of forms in irreducible representations with this same transformation law. Each nonzero summand has some weight vector at which its value is a nonzero function. That function is a K-fixed vector carrying χb ∘ θ.

Take G = −∑b=1N−1 Fb. Adding the trivial character back gives the finite geometric series ∑b=0N−1 ζbx. Its value is N at x = 0 and zero at every other residue class. Consequently

f(v, g) − G(v, g) = N 1{L(g)=0} f(v, g).

Here 1{L(g)=0} is the indicator of the zero label. Keeping the character sum unnormalized gives exactly |N|ℓ = ℓ−m on this fibre. For instance, with ℓ = 2 and N = 4, the three nontrivial characters at label 2 take the values −1, 1, −1. Their negative sum is 1, and G agrees with f there. At label 0 all three values are 1, so G equals −3f and the error is 4f, of relative 2-adic size 1/4 wherever f is nonzero.

For the simultaneous Hecke approximation, the weight at the second auxiliary place has already been chosen. The local type theorem supplies additional data at w₀. For any identity neighbourhood fixing the current form and any lower bound m₀, it gives m ≥ m₀, a subgroup J inside that neighbourhood as above and a surjective θ. In addition, every smooth irreducible representation of the local general linear group containing a nonzero vector with character χ ∘ θ, for any χ ≠ 1, is cuspidal. Surjectivity ensures that χ ∘ θ is itself nontrivial. Choosing ℓ−m₀ below the requested tolerance now gives approximants with the required local type at the chosen weight. Different summands may carry different nontrivial characters.
