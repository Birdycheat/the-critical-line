Why a real-place degree has the size of a Vandermonde product

The placewise mass estimate compares a row with the column belonging to a word. The row supplies a discrete series of the real unitary group U(p, q), an irreducible unitary representation σ with square-integrable matrix coefficients. For a nonzero vector w, its formal degree is ‖w‖4 divided by the integral of |⟨w, σ(g)w⟩|2. Multiplying the Haar measure by a positive number divides the degree by that number. How an integral fixes the missing constant

Imagine moving λ by integer steps. When an admissible step raises one coordinate by one, the degree and the Vandermonde change by exactly the same ratio, as the embedding of a shifted model shows. Along λi(t) = λi + t(N−1−i), for integers t ≥ 0, the steps can be ordered so that decreasing order and the gap survive. Thus d(λ(t))/V(λ(t)) stays constant. To find its value, let Tt be the lowest compact type, the finite-dimensional representation of U(p) × U(q) selected by the weight λ(t). Form Yt = tpq dim(Tt)/d(λ(t)). The fraction retained in the word count

For exp(−N) ≤ η < 1/2 the family theorem asks that every opposite-sign gap be at least 8192 min(p, q)(pq)³ log(N)/η². This implies the large-gap hypotheses above. It then retains the lower bound (1−η)V(λ)/FN. For example, in signature (1,1) take λ = (30000.5, −29999.5) and η = 1/3. The required gap is 73728 log 2 ≈ 51104.36. The actual gap and normalized degree are both 60000. Here Tt has dimension one and Yt = t/(60000+t) tends to one. The lower comparison keeps 40000.
