How can a Hodge class move while the polarization is preserved?

Keep the eigenvector and its separating functional. Write y₀ for the eigenvector in primitive cohomology P, and x for the functional with x(y₀) = 1. We are testing the longer local-ring case. In that case x vanishes on the Hodge subspace L ⊂ P and hence lies in Fil = L⊥ ⊂ H = P∨. To rule out this case we will make the image of x move out of Fil to first order. The motion must preserve the given symmetries, since the deformed variety will belong to the same moduli problem.

Polarized direction lemma. There is a linear map D ∶ H → H of rank at most two with D(x) ∉ Fil. On Fil it commutes with the coefficient action modulo Fil, and it satisfies the first-order polarization identity below. It also kills the relations defining the prescribed splitting of the Lie space.

Finding the two vectors. Denote the polarization pairing by φ and the action on H by σ. The pairing is nondegenerate, φ(u,v) = −φ(v,u), Fil is isotropic, and the adjoint of σ(c) is σ(c), where the bar is the coefficient involution. These properties come from crystalline duality. The polarization induces an isomorphism on the q-divisible group because its kernel is killed by an integer prime to q. Duality identifies the dual Hodge filtration with the annihilator of Fil, and the polarization carries Fil into that annihilator. The adjoint identity is the compatibility of the polarization with the action of the order. Chai and Oort, Theorem 2.11.

Nondegeneracy identifies H with its dual by placing a vector in the second slot. Take the unique α with φ(v,α) = v(y₀) for all v. Then φ(x,α) = 1. Since y₀ has character χ, evaluation gives φ(σ(c)v,α) = χ(c)φ(v,α). Adjointness therefore gives σ(c)α = χ(c)α.

Let E = σ(e) be the dual place projector. By the Lie calculation, its image in H/Fil contains a nonzero character line. Choose b = Eh₀ outside Fil. For every coefficient c, σ(c)b − χ(c)b lies in Fil. For the distinguished quadratic generator ω the stronger identity ΩE = rE holds on H, with Ω = σ(ω) and r the chosen residue root. The conjugate operator is t − Ω, where t is the trace of ω. Put r′ = t − r. The square of r − r′ is the discriminant of the defining quadratic, a unit at the residue prime. Since Ex = x, the adjoint identity φ(Ωx,b) = φ(x,(t − Ω)b) gives rφ(x,b) = r′φ(x,b). Thus φ(x,b) = 0, including in characteristic two.

D(v) = φ(v,α)b + φ(v,b)α,   D(x) = b ∉ Fil.

Checking admissibility. The image lies in the span of α and b. Expanding the next sum gives two pairs of opposite terms, by skew-symmetry, so

φ(D(u),v) + φ(u,D(v)) = 0.

For v ∈ Fil the error in σ(c)b ≡ χ(c)b modulo Fil pairs to zero with v by isotropy. Substitute this and the character identity for α in the formula for D. The commutator D(σ(c)v) − σ(c)D(v) reduces to φ(v,α)(χ(c)b − σ(c)b), which lies in Fil. The algebra-degree-one assumption ensures that every element of the acting order is the image of a coefficient c.

The remaining constraint concerns the subspace Z of Lie relations. It is spanned by vectors of the form ∑j λjσ(cj)(Ω − r′)h, where the same sum ∑j λjχi(cj) vanishes at every labelled coefficient character χi. Such a vector pairs to zero with α because χ is one of those characters. It pairs to zero with b term by term. Indeed σ(c)b is again an Ω-eigenvector of eigenvalue r, so the adjoint t − Ω − r′ kills it. Both coefficients in the formula for D vanish on Z. The next step turns these three checks into equations for a moving subspace.

Which part of the derivative does a moving Hodge subspace determine?

Over the dual numbers Δ = k[ε]/(ε²), a lifted vector has a constant term and a first-order coefficient. To compare two lifts we must first put their Hodge subspaces in the same ambient module. Evaluate the dual de Rham crystal of the special fibre on the square-zero thickening k ← Δ, with its trivial higher divided powers. Constant crystalline base change identifies this evaluation with HΔ = H ⊗k Δ. In these coordinates every vector is uniquely u + εv. The coefficient action sends its two entries to σ(c)u and σ(c)v. The filtration of a deformation is the part that can vary.

Graph lemma. For a linear direction D, the submodule G obtained by moving Fil in that direction is characterized by

u + εv ∈ G   ⇔   u ∈ Fil   and   v − D(u) ∈ Fil.

In fact 1 + εD is a Δ-linear automorphism of HΔ with inverse 1 − εD. Applying it to u + εw with u, w ∈ Fil gives u + ε(D(u) + w), proving the formula. It takes a basis of the constant Fil and any complementary basis to bases of G and a complement. Thus G is a direct summand, its quotient is free and reducing G gives Fil. For u ∈ Fil, the possible ε-coefficients of lifts of u form exactly one coset D(u) + Fil. In particular εv belongs to G exactly when v ∈ Fil. Two directions give the same graph precisely when they give the same map Fil → H/Fil. This is the local graph chart for a Grassmannian. The Stacks Project, Grassmannians, Lemma 27.22.1.

For the polarized direction, the commutator on Fil lies in Fil. Hence acting on a graph vector preserves its membership condition. In particular the whole order acts on the quotient. The distinguished vector x has a lift x + εb in G. The constant vector x has ε-coefficient zero, so it could lie in G only if b belonged to Fil. This is the test we will use after realizing the graph by an abelian scheme.

Why does the graph preserve the pairing and split the Lie quotient?

Polarization and character lemma. The graph G is isotropic for the extended pairing. On the r-eigenspace of Ω in HΔ/G, every class decomposes into classes carrying the prescribed coefficient characters.

Constant crystalline base change carries the special-fibre polarization correspondence to the same pairing extended Δ-bilinearly. Pair two graph vectors u + ε(D(u) + a) and v + ε(D(v) + b), with u, v, a, b ∈ Fil. The constant term vanishes by isotropy of Fil. The coefficient of ε is φ(D(u),v) + φ(u,D(v)), because the two terms containing a or b also pair elements of Fil. The polarization identity kills what remains. All quadratic terms vanish since ε² = 0.

Splitting a class. Let [h] denote a quotient class with Ω[h] = r[h]. Put δ = r − r′. Replacing h by h′ = δ−1(Ω − r′)h preserves this class. Both coordinates of h′ now lie in the image of Ω − r′. If g is a finite linear combination of coefficient actions that evaluates to zero at all labelled characters, then both coordinates of gh′ lie in the relation subspace Z by its definition. The original Lie signature gives Z ⊆ Fil. Indeed on the original Lie quotient, the image of Ω − r′ lies in the r-part by the quadratic relation, and this part already splits into the labelled characters. Since D(Z) ⊆ Fil, the graph membership formula gives gh′ ∈ G.

The labelled characters are distinct on the residue coefficients by the separation condition of the datum. Extend their evaluations to the commutative k-algebra of formal finite linear combinations of coefficients, with multiplication induced by multiplication of coefficients. Each evaluation is onto k and their kernels are distinct maximal ideals. The Chinese remainder theorem supplies elements pi whose values are 1 at the ith character and 0 at all others. Both 1 − ∑ipi and (c − χi(c))pi have zero evaluations. Applying the preceding paragraph first to one expression and then to the other gives

[h] = ∑i[pih′],   σ(c)[pih′] = χi(c)[pih′].

The sum is direct because applying pj to a sum of eigenclasses selects its jth term. The multiplicity calculation will identify the ranks after realization. The Stacks Project, Chinese remainder theorem.

A moving plane. In a space with basis e₁, e₂, f₁, f₂, pair ei with fj by δij, reverse the sign in the opposite order, and set all other basis pairings to zero. Start with Fil spanned by e₁ and e₂. Move its generators to e₁ + εf₂ and e₂ + εf₁. Their pairing is ε(−1 + 1) = 0. Multiplying the first generator by the unit 1 + ε changes its first-order coefficient to f₂ + e₁, which represents the same class as f₂ modulo Fil. The constant vector e₁ fails the membership test. Changing the sign of the second motion gives pairing −2ε, which vanishes exactly in characteristic two.
