Why does integral substitution preserve the logarithm’s functional equation?

The comparison uses two substitutions, first the linear series εT and then a polynomial consisting of the already known integral coefficients of the inverse coordinate. The input logarithm itself can have denominators. Work in the unramified coefficient field with integer ring W and vp(p) = 1. Set q = p² and τ = σ², so τ(a) ≡ aq mod p for every a ∈ W. For a series f with zero constant term put Δf = f(T) − (τf)(Tq)/p, with τ applied to the coefficients. Suppose Δf is integral. For every α ∈ TW[[T]] we need Δ(f ∘ α) to be integral as well. Put A = α(T)q and B = (τα)(Tq). They agree modulo p because taking a qth power in characteristic p raises both the coefficients and the monomials to that power. Substituting in the two terms of the functional equation in their indicated order gives

Δ(f ∘ α) = (Δf) ∘ α + ((τf)(A) − (τf)(B))/p.

The first summand has integral coefficients. For the second, write fₙ = [Tn]f and gₙ = [Tn]Δf. If q ∤ n, then fₙ = gₙ. Otherwise fₙ = gₙ + τ(fn/q)/p. Thus for n = qkm > 0 with q ∤ m, both pkfₙ and pkτ(fₙ) are integral. Each division of the index by q costs at most one factor of p. Now a pth power gains one power of p in a difference. If C − D ∈ prW[[T]] with C, D integral and r ≥ 1, expand (D + (C − D))p − Dp. Every intermediate binomial coefficient is divisible by p, and the last term gains enough because pr ≥ r + 1. Start with A ≡ B mod p and iterate 2k times. Raising the results to the remaining power m preserves their congruence by the difference-of-powers factorization. Hence An − Bn is divisible by p1+2k. Multiplying by τ(fₙ) and dividing by the outer p leaves a multiple of pk. Each coefficient receives only finitely many contributions since A and B have zero constant term. This proves stability. Test the Frobenius and the denominators at 5

Take p = 5, q = 25 and the standard logarithm ℓ = T + T25/5 + T625/25 + ⋯. In the unramified cubic extension take u to be the Teichmüller lift of a residue element of order 31, which exists since 31 divides 125 − 1. This lift is the unique root of unity of that order with the given residue. For α = uT, the degree-25 coefficient of Δ(ℓ ∘ α) is (u25 − τ(u))/5 = 0. Applying the identity to coefficients instead would give (u25 − u)/5 of valuation −1, since 31 does not divide 24.

Over ℤ₅ choose α = T + T². At n = 25 the degree-630 coefficient of A25 − B25 is the binomial coefficient (625 choose 5). Indeed A25 = T625(1 + T)625, while all exponents of B25 are multiples of 25. Its valuation is 3 because (625 choose 5) = 125 · (624 choose 4) and (624 choose 4) ≡ 1 mod 5. The coefficient ℓ₂₅ and the outer division each cost one factor of 5. One factor remains, as the bound predicts.
