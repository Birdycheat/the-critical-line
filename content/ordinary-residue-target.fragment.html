Which residue target does the subspace count use?

Fix a valuation-ring point of the marked fibre power 𝒵 whose closed point lies over a generic point of the uniformizer divisor as in the integral congruence. The map from 𝒵 to the underlying level moduli scheme M is flat. In fact the marking is finite étale, and a fibre power of the smooth abelian family is flat over its base: for the zeroth power the map is the identity, and each additional factor is obtained by flat base change followed by composition. The generic-point argument then sends the original point to a generic point of a special component of M. The generic ordinarity theorem thus applies at the point used in the correspondence. Stacks Project, going down for a flat map.

Let P and R denote the source and target maps on an integral correspondence. A section of P above the chosen point is a lift z over V. The source square on the marked fibre power is Cartesian, so forgetting the marking and the tuple identifies these lifts with the lifts on the underlying isogeny correspondence above the projected point. Conversely an underlying lift and the fixed point upstairs have a unique lift in the Cartesian square. The subspace bijection therefore labels the sections for every j by the j-dimensional subspaces of H. Write v(D) for the residue point obtained by applying the target to the section labelled by D.

Residue target theorem. Let F act on these residue points by precomposition with the map of spectra induced by the Q-power residue automorphism. For a subspace U of H/C, choose any linear section of H → H/C over U. Its image D misses C and projects onto U. Define A(U) = v(D). Changing the section gives another such subspace with the same dimension. Their reduced marked targets agree by the marking and coordinate tests, so this value is independent of the choice. Write π for the projection of H onto H/C. For an arbitrary subspace D we now have

v(D) = A(πD)   if C ∩ D = 0,   v(D) = F(A(πD))   if C ⊆ D.

For the second equality, put U = πD. The chosen section over U and D project onto exactly the same subspace and their dimensions differ by one. They meet the hypotheses of the comparison with relative Frobenius, including its level and coordinate equalities. This gives precisely the one twist on the right. It holds also for D = C, where the chosen section has zero image, and for D = H.

The ordinary subspace count can now be applied to actual residue points. Over each fixed U, the graphs of its linear sections and the full inverse image arrive at the same point after the prescribed number of twists. Their two total weights at that point are equal. Summing over U gives the identity of the even and odd weighted multisets for every allowed test point. Combined with the separately computed generic comparison degree, this supplies the point identity in the integral congruence.
