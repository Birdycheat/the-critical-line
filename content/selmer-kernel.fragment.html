What the detecting cycles can miss

The cycle bound controls the image of a twisted norm. Here is how much of the arithmetic count that image can miss. Keep the enlarged field L′ fixed, with r₂ complex places. At depth ν the cyclic layer F/L′ has generator γ and degree n = ℓν−c. Write q = ℓν, a = (1 + ℓc)e and f = vℓ(e) for the positive twist e. Write 𝓧 for the group of equivariant characters at this depth. Each element assigns a value φ(P) in ℤ/q to each prime ideal, vanishes on principal divisors when extended additively, and satisfies φ(γ−1P) = aφ(P). Define C by quotienting the ideal class group of F by the image of γ−1 − a. Each φ descends to C and is recovered from its values on primes. Its target is an ℓ-group, so there are at most |C|ℓ such characters, where |·|ℓ means the ℓ-part of a finite group order. Where the matching power of q comes from. Write E for the integral units 𝒪F× modulo roots of unity and the image of γ−1 − a, using additive notation. The finite group W in the norm construction satisfies |W| = |E| |C|. Indeed, its map to the class group has unit kernel E and image the kernel of γ−1 − a on that group. A finite group endomorphism has kernel and cokernel of equal order. To compute |E|, take logarithms of units at the infinite places and add one constant coordinate direction to complete the unit lattice. Above each complex place of L′ the action permutes n places in a cycle. On this block the absolute determinant of the permutation minus a times the identity is an − 1. The extra constant direction contributes a − 1. Thus |E|(a − 1) = (an − 1)r₂. The valuation formula vℓ((1 + ℓc)h − 1) = c + vℓ(h) holds for positive h, with c ≥ 1 and c ≥ 2 when ℓ = 2. Apply it with h = e and h = en. The unit identity gives |E|ℓ ℓc+f = ℓ(ν+f)r₂ ≥ qr₂. Why the kernel cost stays bounded. Write N for the twisted norm, additively, and δ = γ−1 − a. Expanding the geometric sum gives δN = Nδ = (1 − an)γ−1. If ℓbN(y) is a q-multiple modulo roots of unity, applying δ and dividing by q in the torsion-free quotient shows that ℓb(an − 1)/q kills the class of y in W. On its ℓ-primary part the prime-to-ℓ factor is invertible. The kernel there is killed by ℓb+f. Each multiplication by ℓ on W[ℓj] has image in W[ℓj−1] and kernel of size at most |W[ℓ]|. Those elements inject into invariant classes represented by α with all valuations divisible by ℓ. If ρ generates the roots of unity in F, their invariance gives γα/α = vℓρt, 0 ≤ t < ℓ. For each t the field norm of v has at most ℓ possible values, since its ℓth power is Norm(ρ)−t. Equal pairs (t, Norm(v)) give a norm-one ratio of the two v’s. Hilbert’s Theorem 90 then lets the ratio of the two α’s descend to the base field modulo ℓth powers. Its valuations away from ℓ remain divisible by ℓ because the layer is unramified there. The base-field classes with this property have at most ℓ2D+⌊log₂ h⌋ elements, where D = [L′:ℚ] and h is its class number. The three contributions come from units modulo ℓth powers, ℓ-torsion ideal classes, and the valuations modulo ℓ at primes over ℓ. The ≤ ℓ² choices of the pair thus give |W[ℓ]| ≤ ℓg for g = 2D + ⌊log₂ h⌋ + 2.
