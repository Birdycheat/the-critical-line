Write the diagonal entries of the Hermitian integration variable as x,y and its upper-right entry as u+iv. For a Hermitian target with diagonal a,b and upper-right entry c+id, the trace pairing is ax + by + 2cu + 2dv. Its negative Fourier phase is the exponential of this expression times −it. In these unscaled entry coordinates the determinant derivative is ∂x∂y − (∂u² + ∂v²)/4. Applying it to the phase gives (it)²(ab − c² − d²) times the same phase. Integration by parts twice transfers the derivative to the amplitude with positive sign.

For diagonal entries 2 and 3 and upper-right entry 1 + i, the multiplier is −4t², which we may divide out. For the matrix with every entry equal to one it is zero, although the target itself is nonzero. This is why the hypothesis concerns the determinant. In the derivative estimate the two permutations also have the same decay.
