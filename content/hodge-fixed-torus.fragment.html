How many points of one torus fibre are fixed?

A Hecke correspondence has two maps c₁ and c₂ with a common source and target. A coincidence point is a point of the source at which these maps have the same value. First take one base point where the two underlying maps on the base agree. We will count the coincidence points above it and then calculate how much they contribute to the trace on differential forms.

Write the complex fibre as W/Λ. Here W is a finite-dimensional complex vector space and Λ is a lattice, a discrete additive subgroup that spans W over ℝ. For a fibre power we use the product of the period lattices. In the period coordinates, every point of this fibre is represented by some z ∈ W, two representatives differing by a lattice vector give the same point, and the two correspondence maps satisfy c₂([z]) = c₁([Θz]). The complex-linear map Θ carries Λ into Λ. The first map is injective on this fibre. Consequently the two maps agree at [z] exactly when [Θz] = [z].

Torus count. Suppose 1 is not an eigenvalue of Θ. Put U = I − Θ. The elements of the kernel of U on the torus are represented by U−1Λ. Applying U to a representative gives the group isomorphism

U−1Λ/Λ → Λ/UΛ,   [z] ↦ [Uz].

Adding a period to z changes Uz by a member of UΛ. Conversely, given w ∈ Λ, the vector U−1w represents a fixed point, and changing w by UΛ changes this inverse by a period. Both directions use invertibility on the vector space.

Choose an integral basis of Λ. In this basis U is an integer matrix. Changing integral bases in the source and target reduces it to Smith normal form. If its nonzero diagonal entries are d₁,…,dr, the quotient has ∏ |dj| elements, the absolute value of the real determinant of U. To relate this determinant to the complex one, complexify the underlying real vector space. It splits into the original complex space and its conjugate, with induced maps U and its conjugate. The two complex determinants multiply to |detℂ U|². Thus the number of points on this fibre is |detℂ(I − Θ)|². A. Sutherland, Number Theory I, Lecture 6, determinant and lattice index.

If Θ has eigenvalue 1, each vector in the 1-eigenspace maps to a fixed point of the torus. Discreteness of Λ gives infinitely many distinct fixed points near zero. The isolated-point formula below requires that eigenvalue to be absent.

What distinguishes a point count from a trace on the square elliptic curve?

On E = ℂ/ℤ[i] take Θ to be multiplication by 4. The fixed-point equation says that 3z is a Gaussian integer. Thus the nine points are represented by (u + iv)/3 for u, v ∈ {0,1,2}. Their number is the same determinant count we just proved, |1 − 4|² = 9. The level-marking example used the kernel of an isogeny on this same curve. Here it is the kernel of the identity minus the given map that counts coincidences.

To put the trace on functions and the trace on one-forms in a single expression, introduce a variable y that records form degree. At an isolated fixed point with derivative H, the holomorphic Lefschetz formula gives a factor 1/det(I − H). Pullback on one-forms on this curve has eigenvalue 4. Weighting form degree one by −y gives (1 − 4y)/(1 − 4) per point. Summing over all nine gives

9 · (1 − 4y)/(1 − 4) = 12y − 3.

At y = 1 the answer is nine. At y = 0 the answer is −3, the alternating trace on cohomology of the structure sheaf. It can be negative even though every point in the count occurs once. M. F. Atiyah and R. Bott, A Lefschetz fixed point formula for elliptic complexes II, Theorem 4.12.

Now take Θ = 4i. There are |1 − 4i|² = 17 fixed points. The total is 17(1 − 4iy)/(1 − 4i) = (1 + 4i)(1 − 4iy). Its value at zero is 1 + 4i. Its value at one is 17. The imaginary part at zero makes the position of the conjugate visible. It is the factor 1 − 4i in the denominator whose conjugate survives the point count.

We can also check the constant coefficient directly on cohomology. The space H⁰(E, 𝒪) consists of constants. The space H¹(E, 𝒪) is generated by the class of dz, the translation-invariant antiholomorphic differential. Pullback by multiplication by 4i fixes the constants and multiplies this differential by −4i. The alternating trace is therefore 1 − (−4i) = 1 + 4i. A.-S. Kaloghiros, Hodge Theory, §4.2, Lemma 4.25.

What is the trace contribution of a whole fibre?

Fix a coincidence on the base. Let T be its complex tangent operator on the base, and Θ the linear operator just used on the torus fibre. More explicitly, in a fibre power the label acts on its respective coordinates and the isogeny acts on each resulting coordinate. Suppose both I − T and I − Θ are invertible. We write DB(y) = det(I − yB) for any complex endomorphism B.

At a coincidence point x, the first projection is étale, so its derivative identifies the source and target tangent spaces. The local action on the target tangent space is Ax = dc₂ ∘ (dc₁)−1. Its vertical subspace is preserved. On that subspace the action is Θ, and on the quotient by it the action is T. In fibre coordinates a variation of the base can change the fibre coordinate as well. Choosing any linear splitting gives (v,w) ↦ (Tv, Rxv + Θw), where Rx records this last change. The determinant of a block triangular matrix is the product of the diagonal determinants. Thus DAx(y) = DT(y)DΘ(y) for every coincidence in this fibre.

For form degree a, pullback on the cotangent exterior power contributes tr(∧aAx∗). Here the star means the algebraic dual. The identity ∑a(−y)a tr(∧aAx∗) = DAx(y) follows by triangularizing Ax: the diagonal of an exterior power lists the products of distinct diagonal positions. The coherent Lefschetz formula for a smooth proper complex correspondence, étale first projection and transverse coincidences, divides this numerator by DAx(1). G. Kondyrev and A. Prikhodko, Holomorphic Atiyah–Bott Formula for Correspondences, Theorem 1.6.

All fixed points in the fibre have the same two diagonal determinants. By the torus count, their number is |DΘ(1)|². Cancelling the nonzero factor DΘ(1) gives the fibre contribution

∑x in the fixed fibre DAx(y)/DAx(1) = [DT(y)/DT(1)] DΘ(y) DΘ(1).

The bar is complex conjugation at the fixed value 1. In particular y remains a polynomial variable. Setting y = 1 recovers the number of points in the fibre. The orbit calculation determines which base points supply such fibres.
