Its dimension D counts the independent ways the chosen archimedean representation occurs in automorphic L² after applying the finite-place projector e. The main term M for this integer is a real number. If dv is the formal degree at the real place v and V is the Haar volume of a fundamental domain F, then M = c0V, where c0 = (∏v dv) Re e(1). Let φ be the product of the normalized archimedean matrix coefficients, their formal degrees and the finite-place function e, with the archimedean coefficients conjugated. Summing φ(x⁻¹γz) over rational group elements γ gives a kernel K(x,z). It represents an orthogonal projection of rank D. Choose continuous representatives F1, …, FD of an orthonormal basis of the range. The geometric kernel and the basis expansion give the same action on compactly supported test functions. Continuity and uniqueness of the automorphic representatives identify the two kernels pointwise. In particular,

Re K(x,x) = ∑j |Fj(x)|² ≥ 0,   ∫F Re K(x,x) dμ = D.

Each basis vector contributes one to the integral. On the geometric side the identity γ = 1 gives the constant c0 on the diagonal. Its integral is M. Write n for the unitary rank, f for the number of real places, and put U = nf log n, α = exp(−U/4), δ = exp(−n²f). The indefinite count divides F according to the infimum of the normalized sizes of nonzero sublattices at x. On the moderate part this size meets a prescribed threshold. There the integral of |Re K(x,x) − c0| costs at most αM from near nonidentity summands and δM from far summands. Here “near” tests the conjugated rational element at every archimedean place. On the deep part, below the threshold, the rare-flag estimate bounds the volume by αV, so the identity term there costs at most αM. A separate concentration bound gives ∫deep Re K ≤ δD. Positivity of the full diagonal lets the last two bounds control the absolute value of their difference. Thus

|D − M| ≤ (2α + δ)M + δD.

For a two-point illustration, take counting measure and project onto the unit vector (2,1)/√5. The diagonal is (4/5, 1/5) and its sum is 1. Compare with the constant 3/5 on each point. The two local differences are +1/5 and −2/5, even though both diagonal entries are positive. Their sum −1/5 is precisely the rank minus the reference mass. This illustrates the sign in the trace subtraction. The exponential estimates come from the arithmetic of the unitary quotient.
