From local powers to a bound for the image

Why do reductions decide whether the ratio β is a qth power in F? Choose x in an algebraic closure with xq = β. At a tested prime choose an integral t whose residue is a nonzero qth root of the reduced β. The product ∏0≤i<q(x − ζit) = xq − tq vanishes upon reduction at a place above that prime. Some factor vanishes. Thus x has the same nonzero residue as an element ζit of F. A Frobenius σ fixes this residue, and σ(x)/x is a qth root of unity reducing to 1. Away from ℓ such a root equals 1. Indeed a nontrivial root would have geometric sum zero, reducing to q = 0 in the residue field. Now a degree-one Chebotarev theorem supplies, outside the finite excluded set, a Frobenius inducing any specified Galois action on x. Its residue field has prime order, so the required surjectivity from 𝒪L′ holds. Every Galois automorphism fixes x. Hence x lies in F, which proves global power detection. By the defining separation of S, the tuple of scaled evaluations at all tested primes is injective on S.
