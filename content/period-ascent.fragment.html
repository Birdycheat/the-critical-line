How a bottom period can detect a prescribed row

Fix the grid, its Haar measures and a nonzero row t. A row turns a smooth vector into an automorphic function on the bottom unitary group, which we call Y. The first core group V comes from adjoining a line to its Hermitian space. Write ι for the embedding of Y in V that fixes this line. For a core function F and a row function u, their initial period is the integral of their product over the rational quotient of Y, with the row measure,

P₀(F,u) = ∫[Y] F(ιh) u(h) dh.

Here is the small algebraic step that joins the places. Write β(p) for this period on tuples, grouping the two inputs at each place as pτ. If any bτ vanishes identically, so does β. Otherwise choose p⁰ with every bτ(p⁰τ) ≠ 0. Replace the coordinates of p⁰ by those of p one at a time. At each replacement, cross multiplication cancels the unknown scalar for that slice. This gives

β(p) ∏τ bτ(p⁰τ) = β(p⁰) ∏τ bτ(pτ).

Only the product at the reference tuple needs to be inverted. Pure tensors span both spaces, so bilinearity extends proportionality to every pair of inputs. Composing the resulting common bilinear form with the real row-vector map for w gives Aw(ρ,ξ). Every tested period now equals Aw(ρ,ξ) times a scalar depending on the row, column and finite datum. A successful first test fixes a pair with Aw ≠ 0. Each further nonzero row has its own successful column and finite datum and hence a nonzero scalar. The fixed pair detects that row.
