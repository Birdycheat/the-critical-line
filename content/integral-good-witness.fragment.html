How do the integral correspondences give good reduction for a whole family?

The residue identity involves selected operators. Good reduction asks for an integral correspondence for each operator in the family. Fix a place and a level satisfying the good-reduction group conditions described below. The marked moduli construction supplies a smooth projective fibre power Y over the local valuation ring and a common generic cover. The same Y must work for every operator. Its finer source level may depend on the operator.

Fix one operator. Write h for its translation, K for its group of labels, K₀ for the group at the integral target and K₁ ⊆ K₀ for the group at its finer source Y′. The group hypotheses give principal levels K(M) ⊆ K(N) ⊆ K ∩ K₀, with K(M) ⊆ K₁ normal in K₀. They also give K₀ = (K ∩ K₀)K₁. Thus every marking at the target can be moved to the finer level by an element of the label group. Let s ∶ Y′ → Y forget that finer level. Its degree throughout the integral model is [K₀ : K₁].

Extending a target map. Write h = h₁h₂ as in the group hypotheses. Both factors carry the integral lattice Λ into itself. The source integer level is N₀κ, with κ > 0 and κΛ ⊆ h₂Λ. For a label representative b ∈ K, the group at level one acts by lattice automorphisms, so bΛ = Λ. The same κ works for h₂b. The group conditions supply an intermediate level N₁ ∈ (N₀)𝔞′. Conjugation by h₂b takes K(M) into K(N₁), and conjugation by h₁ takes K(N₁) into K(N). These inclusions make the two successive generic transitions admissible. The integral moduli comparison for such a translation extends it to a map between the marked fibre powers. When h₁ is the identity, this gives the target map directly.

In the other case the hypotheses specify an auxiliary maximal ideal 𝔴′ and an elementary isogeny of type i ≤ n there. The ideal 𝔴′ contains the chosen split ideal (q, ω − r), the refined ideal 𝔞′ lies in 𝔞𝔴′ and N(𝔴′)i divides the intermediate level. These are the level conditions for the integral comparison of that isogeny. Use the intermediate model at the original integer level and refined ideal 𝔞′. Extend h₂b to this model, then extend h₁ from it to Y. Their composite tb extends the generic translation h₁h₂b. This step uses two moduli results about integral extensions. The coset calculation below explains how their maps fit together.

Counting source points. Take one copy of Y′ for each left coset b in K/(K ∩ K₀). Use s on every copy and the corresponding tb as its target. A fine tower label c ∈ K/K(N) maps to its coarser label b. For chosen representatives write c = bv with v ∈ K ∩ K₀. Acting by v before projecting to Y′ leaves the coarse source fixed. Its target is correct because (hb)v = hc. The integral source is a finite disjoint union of finite étale maps. Its degree is

d′ = [K : K ∩ K₀] [K₀ : K₁],   m = [K ∩ K₁ : K(M)].

Counting above one compatible pair. To see why m is the comparison multiplicity, fix a coarse copy b, a point on it and a compatible point in the tower. Choose an origin in the fine fibre. The second marking then has a representative u ∈ K₀. A fine lift has a copy label in K/K(N) and a marking within that copy in K(N)/K(M). After representatives for the first quotient have been chosen, multiplying the two representatives records both as one coset xK(M) in K. The orbit test for the prescribed pair is b−1xu−1 ∈ K₁.

By the factorization of K₀ choose a ∈ K ∩ K₀ with a−1u−1 ∈ K₁. Then ua ∈ K₁. Multiplication on the right by ua changes the test into b−1xa ∈ K₁. This element also belongs to K. Since a normalizes K(M), the substitution y = b−1xa is well defined on the fine cosets, with inverse x = bya−1. It identifies the allowed lifts with (K ∩ K₁)/K(M), giving exactly m > 0 above each compatible pair. Both sources over the generic tower are finite étale, so the comparison is finite étale of this degree by the finite étale fibre argument.

For a small example in which the order of multiplication matters, take K = K₀ = S₃, K₁ = {1, (12)}, both principal marking subgroups trivial and b = 1. With u = (123) choose a = (132). Using right-to-left composition, the two allowed labels are (123) and (23). Right multiplication by a sends them to 1 and (12), precisely the elements of K₁. Here three integral source points each have two lifts.

In general, the factorization of K₀ makes the map from (K ∩ K₀)/(K ∩ K₁) to K₀/K₁ a bijection. The product d′m therefore recovers the upstairs source degree [K : K(M)]. Repeating the construction for each operator gives the initial good-reduction witness at the chosen place. The restriction to the generic image then gives the surjective cover used later.
