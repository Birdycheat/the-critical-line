Which data can be chosen before the geometric prime?

Let E be a CM field with ring of integers 𝒪 and conjugation z ↦ z. Fix an embedding τ₀ ∶ E → ℂ and an element x with x² = −d, where d is a positive integer. Let Q be a finite set of rational primes such that −d is a square in ℚq for each q ∈ Q. Write M for their product, with M = 1 when Q is empty. We want one integral lattice that can serve at each prime in Q and later at an auxiliary prime chosen for a geometric construction.

Trace-lattice lemma. For every n ≥ 1 one can choose an integral alternating form on 𝒪n which identifies the lattice with its dual over ℤ(q) for every q ∈ Q. The same construction prescribes the signs at the infinite places. Once the datum is fixed, a single integer D suffices to guarantee perfection at every prime outside its divisors. This lets the geometric prime lemma select a further split prime.

First choose an integral quadratic generator ω for ℚ(x). Write d = g²d₀ with d₀ squarefree and put v = x/g. Use ω = (1 + v)/2 if d₀ ≡ 3 mod 4, and ω = v otherwise. Its polynomial P(X) = X² − T X + U has integer coefficients and negative discriminant Δ. In the first case T = 1 and U = (1 + d₀)/4. In the second, T = 0 and U = d₀. Thus 2ω − T is a nonzero rational multiple of x. At every prime where −d is a local square, P has two distinct roots modulo that prime. The valuation of −d₀ is even because it is a square and at most one because d₀ is squarefree. It is therefore zero. At an odd prime, a square root of −d₀ and its negative therefore give distinct residues. At 2 a unit square is 1 modulo 8, forcing d₀ ≡ 7 mod 8 and P ≡ X(X−1) mod 2. The place comparison explains how these roots label the two sides.

We also need an algebraic integer c fixed by conjugation with a prescribed sign at each infinite place. The pair of conjugate embeddings containing τ₀ defines one infinite place of E. By weak approximation choose z ∈ E within 1/2 of −1 at this place and within 1/2 of +1 at every other infinite place. Choose a positive integer a making az integral. Then w = a(z + z) is real integral, negative at the chosen place and positive at the others. For a sufficiently large positive integer k,

c = 1 + Mkw,   τ₀(c) < 0,   τ(c) > 0 at every other infinite place.

In particular c reduces to 1 at every prime above M. The congruence makes c a unit at those primes. Its signs will determine the signature of the form. Weak approximation at the infinite places.

How can a local generator have the right sign at every embedding?

The trace codifferent C consists of the elements z ∈ E for which Tr(z𝒪) ⊆ ℤ, where Tr denotes the trace from E to ℚ. We seek a totally positive element t′ in C that generates C after localization at each prime ideal 𝔓 above M. If M = 1 there is no local condition and t′ = 1 works. The codifferent and the integral dual.

Suppose M > 1. The codifferent is a nonzero fractional ideal of the Dedekind ring 𝒪. At each 𝔓 above M it is principal, so C/𝔓C is a line over 𝒪/𝔓. Choose a nonzero residue in each of these finitely many lines. Chinese remainder for the pairwise coprime ideals gives one y ∈ C with all those residues. Thus y generates each localization C𝔓. Conjugation preserves C because it preserves the trace and permutes the primes above M. Hence y generates each of the same localizations. A simultaneous generator at finitely many primes.

Adding y to its conjugate could cancel their residues. At each q ∈ Q, choose a root r of P and invert T − 2r modulo q. The Chinese remainder theorem gives integers R and S realizing these roots and inverses at all q at once. Put ε = S(ω − R). Modulo each 𝔓 above M, ε + ε̄ = 1 and εε̄ = 0, since their sum reduces to S(T−2R) and their product to S²P(R). One is 1 and the other 0 in the residue field. Therefore the real element t = yε + yε reduces to one of the two nonzero generators in each line.

t′ = t + MN,   t′𝒪𝔓 = C𝔓 for every 𝔓 above M.

Indeed, 1 ∈ C because the trace of an algebraic integer is an integer. Hence MN ∈ 𝔓C for every such 𝔓. Choose the positive integer N so large that MN exceeds every |τ(t)|. All embeddings now send t′ to positive real numbers, and all the chosen residues are preserved.

Why can ordinary conjugate addition lose a generator?

In ℤ[i] the codifferent is ½ℤ[i]. Its generator y = i/2 satisfies y + y = 0. At 5, set ε = i − 2. At the two primes above 5 the residues of i are 2 and 3, so the residues of ε are 0 and 1. Now yε = −½ − i and its sum with its conjugate is −1. Adding 5 gives 4, a positive generator at both primes.

Why is the weighted trace perfect at every prescribed prime?

Set u = 2ω − T. Then u = −u and u² = Δ. Each q ∈ Q is prime to Δ, so u is a unit at each 𝔓 above M. Choose σ = ±1 so that Im τ₀(σu) < 0. The element δ = σt′u still generates the codifferent at those primes. It is purely imaginary, and Im τ(δ) < 0 whenever τ(ω) = τ₀(ω), since τ(t′) > 0. This is the sign required of the trace form.

The inclusion δ𝒪 ⊆ C is between full lattices, so the quotient is finite. For each q ∈ Q its q-primary part is zero: a nonzero such part would survive at a maximal ideal over q, where δ already generates C. Thus the order e₁ of the quotient is prime to M, and it annihilates the quotient, giving e₁C ⊆ δ𝒪. Multiplication by c on the integral lattice 𝒪 is the identity modulo M, so its determinant is 1 modulo M. Its absolute value e₂ is the order of 𝒪/c𝒪. Thus e₂ is prime to M. It annihilates the class of 1 in 𝒪/c𝒪, so e₂ ∈ c𝒪.

Write Bδ(v,z) = Tr(δvz). Every integral linear functional L on 𝒪 extends rationally to E. Nondegeneracy of the trace pairing writes it uniquely as L(z) = Tr(a z), and its integral values put a in C. Choose w ∈ 𝒪 with e₁a = δw. For the same integer e = e₁e₂ and every such L,

Bδ(e₂w,z) = eL(z) for all z ∈ 𝒪,   cc′ = e for some c′ ∈ 𝒪.

At each q ∈ Q, division by e is allowed in ℤ(q). The map from the localized lattice to its dual is surjective by this identity and injective by rational nondegeneracy. This proves perfection at every prime prescribed before the datum was chosen. The integral trace-dual identification.

How does the scalar trace control the rank-n lattice?

For n ≥ 1 give 𝒪n the diagonal coefficients h₀ = −c and hj = −c² for 1 ≤ j < n. Define

F(X,Y) = Tr(δ Σj hj Xj Yj).

The sum inside the trace, before multiplication by δ, is integral. Since δ lies in the codifferent, F takes integral values. All hj are real and δ is purely imaginary. At Y = X the argument of the trace goes to its negative under conjugation, so F(X,X) = 0. Each coefficient is nonzero, and testing one coordinate at a time gives rational nondegeneracy from that of Bδ.

Let L be an integral functional on 𝒪n, with coordinate functionals Lj. Apply the scalar identity to z ↦ Lj(z), obtaining xj such that Bδ(xj,y) = eLj(y). Put X₀ = −c′e x₀ and Xj = −(c′)²xj for j > 0. Since cc′ = e, both choices give hjXj = e²xj. Summing yields

F(X,Y) = e³L(Y).

The same multiplier works in every rank. At each prescribed prime it is a unit, giving a perfect alternating form on the whole localized lattice.

Which further primes work for a datum already chosen?

Fix any datum with the sign and integrality conditions above. Take an integral basis b₁,…,bm of 𝒪. The trace-dual identification and δ ≠ 0 give, for each coordinate functional, a vector ξᵢ ∈ E representing it under Bδ. Choose one positive integer y that makes every yξᵢ integral. Then ηᵢ = y²ξᵢ represents y² times the i-th coordinate. For an integral functional L, the integral element Σᵢ L(bᵢ)ηᵢ consequently represents y²L.

D = y² · |𝒪/c𝒪|.

Multiplying the representing element by |𝒪/c𝒪| gives DL. This same D belongs to c𝒪, so it satisfies both parts of the scalar identity. At every rational prime coprime to D, the form is perfect. For the datum constructed from x, any further prime with −d a local square and prime to D also satisfies the splitting condition. The geometric prime lemma can now choose such a prime beyond D and any finite list of exclusions. In the Gaussian trace experiment, 17 splits and the pairing vanishes modulo 17, while 5 can divide D and still give a perfect pairing.

How can a split prime fail the trace test?

Take 𝒪 = ℤ[i], ω = i, Q = {5,13}, c = −64 and δ = −17i for the embedding with Im(i) > 0. Here c ≡ 1 mod 65. On the basis 1,i the trace pairing has diagonal entries zero and off-diagonal entries 34. If L(s + ti) = As + Bt, then 32(B + Ai) represents 1088L and (−64)(−17) = 1088. Thus 5 and 13 work. At 17 the polynomial X²+1 has the distinct roots ±4, while every entry of the trace pairing vanishes. A different phenomenon can occur at a divisor of the displayed D. Here the dual basis is i/34, 1/34. Choosing y = 170 makes 5 divide D, although the form is perfect at 5.
