Why does the rank sum leave exp(−kf log k)?

A sufficient numerical threshold is (16C + C³²)m⁶L ≤ k log k, with C ≥ 1 and f ≥ 1. It ensures k ≥ 8, k ≥ m and k ≥ C¹⁶. Since (j − 1)(k − 2 − j) ≥ 0, every exponent j(k − 1 − j) is at least k − 2. The base C/k is at most 1. Thus we can bound the sum by r times its first-rank bound. For r = 0 the sum is empty. For positive r put T = kf log k. In the logarithm of twice the squared sum there are three costs. The rank count contributes log(2r²) ≤ m⁶ ≤ T/8, the height contributes 2Cm⁶fL ≤ T/8, and the constant in the base contributes 2(k − 2)f log C ≤ T/8. The denominator in that base contributes −2(k − 2)f log k ≤ −3T/2. Together they give at most −T. Multiplying by du proves the promised bound.
