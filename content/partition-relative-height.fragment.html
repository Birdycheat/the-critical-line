Smooth cutoff thickening in the positive cone, lattice dilation count, and smoothness of the dyadic retraction

Why do the orbit-normalized bump around the compact core K and the dyadic outer-product retraction both define globally smooth objects on the positive-definite cone Pos(L, N)? Three analytic mechanisms govern their local finiteness and regularity. First, Pos(L, N) is an open subset of the finite-dimensional real normed space HS(L, N) of Hermitian matrix tuples, so any compact subset K ⊆ Pos(L, N) sits at a positive metric distance from the boundary of the cone. Smooth support separation on HS(L, N) therefore produces a smooth function whose nonzero locus is an open ε-neighborhood of K with compact closure K′ ⊆ Pos(L, N); dividing by its positive minimum on K yields a smooth bump b ≥ 1 on K whose orbit-normalized weight ψ = b/g(S) is compactly supported and globally Lipschitz. Second, when the squared radius of an integral lattice ellipsoid is dilated from s to αs, placing open qh-balls of squared radius s/4 around the dilated lattice points reduces discrete counting to additive Haar volume comparison on the archimedean space Vℝ: by the quadratic parallelogram inequality, all shifted balls lie inside a single dilated ellipsoid while overlapping centers at any point x ∈ Vℝ inject by subtraction into the undilated lattice ball of squared radius s, yielding a bound independent of both h ∈ Pos(L, N) and s ≥ 0. Third, in the dyadic sum R(h) = μ(h)h + ∑j∈ℤ θ(4jμ(h))T4j(h), the outer cutoff kills large scales 4j ≥ 4/μ(h) while the positive lattice minimum kills small scales 4 · 4j ≤ λ(h) on nonzero integral vectors (and v = 0 contributes the zero outer product), collapsing the infinite series near each base form h0 ∈ Pos(L, N) to a fixed finite sum over scales and lattice vectors.

Totally complex lattice norm floor, reciprocal-minima height bound, and moving-frame derivative closure

Why can the inhomogeneous prefactor 1 + H(h) in the composite modulus be absorbed into a pure first power of H(h) = [λ(h)λ∨(h)]−1 when differentiating the pulled-back partition functions in a moving frame? Everything reduces to proving that λ(h)λ∨(h) is uniformly bounded above—equivalently, that H(h) is bounded below by a strictly positive constant c0 > 0 across the entire archimedean frame group Gp(L, N) = ∏w GLN(ℂ). Over a totally complex field L of degree e = [L : ℚ] = 2r with N ≥ 1, every nonzero algebraic integer ui ∈ 𝒪L has rational norm |NormL/ℚ(ui)| = ∏w |ιw(ui)|² ≥ 1, forcing |ιw(ui)|² ≥ 1 at some complex place w; thus the standard form 1 has lattice minimum λ(1) ≥ 1, and bounded matrix-tuple distortion under each frame x ∈ Gp(L, N) keeps both λ(hx) > 0 and λ∨(hx) > 0 positive. Rescaling hx by λ(hx)−1 and hx∨ by λ∨(hx)−1 produces two forms of minimum ≥ 1 whose local determinant factors |det(xw−1)|² and |det(xwT)|² are exact reciprocals; applying the integer-basis Gram determinant lower bound to both rescaled forms and multiplying cancels the frame determinants identically, leaving [λ(hx)λ∨(hx)]Nr ≤ I0² and hence 1 + H(hx) ≤ (1 + c0−1)H(hx).

Theorem (Uniform height floor and exponent-one moving-frame derivative bound). For every totally complex field L and integer rank N ≥ 1, the standard form satisfies λ(1) ≥ 1, and every frame x ∈ Gp(L, N) satisfies λ(hx) > 0, λ∨(hx) > 0, and

[λ(hx)λ∨(hx)]Nr ≤ I0²,   H(hx) = [λ(hx)λ∨(hx)]−1 ≥ c0 := [max(1, I0²)]−1 > 0.

Consequently, with Cn = 3 and C∗ = N CψCRCn(1 + c0−1) = 3N CψCR(1 + c0−1) (exponent a = 1), every γ ∈ Γ, every frame x ∈ Gp(L, N), and every Hermitian perturbation v ∈ HS(L, N) obey

|Dφγ(hx)(x · v)| ≤ C∗ H(hx) ‖v‖.

Proof. Given any nonzero u ∈ 𝒪LN, choose an index i with ui ≠ 0. Because ui ∈ 𝒪L \ {0}, its rational field norm is a nonzero rational integer, so ∏w |ιw(ui)|² = |NormL/ℚ(ui)| ≥ 1. At least one complex place w0 must therefore satisfy |ιw0(ui)|² ≥ 1, giving q1(u) = ∑w,j |ιw(uj)|² ≥ |ιw0(ui)|² ≥ 1 and hence λ(1) ≥ 1. For each x ∈ Gp(L, N), bounded matrix-tuple distortion between 1, hx, and hx∨ implies λ(hx) > 0 and λ∨(hx) > 0. Rescaling sets k1 = λ(hx)−1hx and k2 = λ∨(hx)−1hx∨ in Pos(L, N) with λ(k1) ≥ 1 and λ(k2) ≥ 1. Applying the integer-basis Gram determinant lower bound 1 ≤ I0 ∏w det(kw) to both k1 and k2 yields 1 ≤ I0 λ(hx)−Nr ∏w |det(xw−1)|² and 1 ≤ I0 λ∨(hx)−Nr ∏w |det(xwT)|². Multiplying these two inequalities cancels ∏w |det(xw−1)|² |det(xwT)|² = ∏w |det(xw−1xw)|² = 1 and gives [λ(hx)λ∨(hx)]Nr ≤ I0². Since Nr ≥ 1, either λ(hx)λ∨(hx) ≤ 1 or 1 < λ(hx)λ∨(hx) ≤ [λ(hx)λ∨(hx)]Nr ≤ I0², so λ(hx)λ∨(hx) ≤ max(1, I0²) and H(hx) ≥ c0 > 0.

For the moving-frame derivative bound, first consider φ = ψ ∘ F at hx = x · 1 with direction x · v ∈ HS(L, N). Placewise quadratic comparison gives |qx·v(z)| ≤ N‖v‖qhx(z) for all z ∈ Vℝ. Along the affine ray hx + t(x · v) in HS(L, N) for 0 < t < ε/(N‖v‖ + 1) with ε = 1/[4(Cn + 1)] = 1/16, linearity of h ↦ qh(z) yields |qhx+t(x·v)(z) − qhx(z)| = t|qx·v(z)| ≤ (N‖v‖t)qhx(z), i.e. ClN‖v‖t(hx, hx + t(x · v)) with N‖v‖t ≤ 1/16 ≤ 1/12. By the composite modulus (which applies to arbitrary Hermitian perturbations in HS(L, N)) and the relative Lipschitz bound for ψ, |φ(hx + t(x · v)) − φ(hx)| ≤ CψCRCn(1 + H(hx))N‖v‖t. Dividing by t > 0 and letting t → 0+ bounds |Dφ(hx)(x · v)| ≤ N CψCRCn(1 + H(hx))‖v‖, and H(hx) ≥ c0 gives 1 + H(hx) ≤ (1 + c0−1)H(hx), hence |Dφ(hx)(x · v)| ≤ C∗H(hx)‖v‖. Finally, for every γ ∈ Γ, linearity of the congruence action gives Dφγ(hx)(x · v) = Dφ(γ−1 · hx)(γ−1 · (x · v)) = Dφ(hγ−1x)((γ−1x) · v), and left Γ-invariance H(hγ−1x) = H(hx) completes the proof.

Entrywise matrix-tuple distortion and linear ray quadratic perturbation in moving frames

For any tuple A = (Aw)w ∈ Gp(L, N), set dist1(A) = (1 + ∑w ∑i,j |(Aw − IN)i,j|)² ∈ [1, ∞). Writing Awyw = yw + (Aw − IN)yw and bounding |(Aw − IN)i,j(yw)j| ≤ |(Aw − IN)i,j| ‖yw‖2 yields ‖Awyw‖2 ≤ (1 + ∑i,j |(Aw − IN)i,j|)‖yw‖2 ≤ dist1(A)1/2‖yw‖2 at each place w, so q1(A y) = ∑w ‖Awyw‖2² ≤ dist1(A)q1(y) for every y ∈ Vℝ. Evaluating on y = B u for any B ∈ Gp(L, N) and nonzero u ∈ 𝒪LN gives inf0≠u∈𝒪LN q1(A B u) ≤ dist1(A) inf0≠u∈𝒪LN q1(B u). Taking A = x and B = x−1 yields 1 ≤ λ(1) ≤ dist1(x)λ(hx), so λ(hx) ≥ dist1(x)−1 > 0; taking A = (xT)−1 and B = xT (since uw∗(hx∨)wuw = uw∗(xwT)∗xwTuw = ‖xwTuw‖2²) likewise yields λ∨(hx) ≥ dist1((xT)−1)−1 > 0.

To check the moving-frame quadratic bound |qx·v(z)| ≤ N‖v‖qhx(z) for v ∈ HS(L, N), set yw = xw−1zw ∈ ℂN, so Re(zw∗(x · v)wzw) = Re(yw∗vwyw) and Re(zw∗(hx)wzw) = ‖yw‖2². Since |(vw)i,j| ≤ ‖v‖ for all i, j ∈ {1, …, N}, the elementary inequality |yw,i| ‖v‖ |yw,j| ≤ (‖v‖/2)(|yw,i|² + |yw,j|²) summed over all N² index pairs (i, j) gives |Re(yw∗vwyw)| ≤ ∑i,j=1N (‖v‖/2)(|yw,i|² + |yw,j|²) = N‖v‖‖yw‖2² at each place w, and summing over w produces the exact factor N‖v‖.
