Why is each diagonal block of a product scalar?

Let A be the unital associative algebra generated by the tangent space 𝔤. It contains every spectral projector pi, since interpolation expresses that projector as a polynomial in X. A diagonal block of an element of A starts in one weight space and returns to it. Even the product of two off-diagonal maps can make such a return.

Proposition. For every a ∈ A, the block pi a pi is a scalar multiple of pi, and pi a pj lies in 𝔤 for i ≠ j.

The commutator calculation gives both properties for a tangent matrix. The identity has the same properties, and both are preserved by linear combinations. It remains to check multiplication. Write aik = pi a pk and bkj = pk b pj. Insert the identity between a and b to obtain

pi (ab) pj = ∑k aik bkj.

If k=i or k=j, one factor acts by a scalar on the intervening space. The product has the required property because the other factor does. Suppose now that k differs from both endpoints. If i ≠ j, the reverse product bkj aik vanishes by pj pi=0. Thus aik bkj equals the commutator of two tangent matrices and lies in 𝔤. If i=j, the two orders of multiplication act on different spaces. The commutator lies in 𝔤 and its compression to space i is exactly aik bki. The diagonal conclusion for a single tangent therefore makes this return a scalar on space i. Summing over k proves the proposition.

For example, take two copies of K². Let S carry the second copy identically to the first and vanish on the first. Let T do the reverse. Then ST is the projector on the first copy and TS the projector on the second. Their difference acts by +1 on the first copy and −1 on the second. Compressing the difference to the first copy recovers the product ST. The calculation works even though the space on which the scalar acts has dimension two.

What guarantees an inverse for a nonzero block?

The next step needs the following test. For each nonzero x ∈ A there is some b ∈ A for which bx is not nilpotent. This is a condition on the whole algebra. An individual off-diagonal block can have square zero.

First consider the relation of A to Γ. Conjugation by any ρ(g) preserves the closure of ρ(Γ), fixes the identity and carries its tangent space into itself. The inverse conjugation gives equality. Conjugation therefore preserves the algebra generated by the tangents. Also, if W is Γ-invariant, take v ∈ W and any linear functional λ vanishing on W. The linear equation λ(Mv) = 0 holds on the group image and on its closure. Its derivative gives λ(Yv) = 0 for every tangent Y. Such functionals cut out W, so Y preserves W, as does every element of A.

Let J be the radical of A, the ideal of elements acting as zero on every simple A-module. For a finite-dimensional algebra it is the largest nilpotent two-sided ideal. To see nilpotence, filter the left regular module A by submodules with simple successive quotients. Such a filtration is obtained by choosing a maximal proper submodule at each step, with dimension decreasing. Each factor from J lowers the filtration by one step. A product of as many factors as there are steps annihilates A, hence annihilates its identity. Conversely, a nilpotent ideal I acts by zero on a simple module S, since otherwise IS=S would give InS=S for every n. This also shows that every algebra automorphism preserves J. P. Etingof et al., Introduction to representation theory, Lemma 2.8 and Proposition 2.11.

Write JV for the span of the vectors xv with x ∈ J and v ∈ V. Conjugation preserves J, so JV is Γ-invariant. Semisimplicity of ρ gives a Γ-invariant complement W. Every x ∈ J takes W into W by the preceding calculation and into JV by definition. Thus JW=0. It follows that

JV = J(JV + W) = J2V = ⋯ = JnV.

For sufficiently large n the last space is zero, so J acts by zero on V. Since A consists of actual endomorphisms of V, this gives J=0. Now take x ≠ 0 in A. Some simple module S contains a vector v with xv ≠ 0, by the definition of J. The submodule A(xv) equals S by simplicity, so some b ∈ A satisfies bxv=v. No positive power of bx can vanish in A. This is the required test.
