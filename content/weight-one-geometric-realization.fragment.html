How do smooth crystalline comparison, étale Galois realizations, and Hodge–Tate jump transfer assemble into the step package?

The conditional transfer to a geometric tower and the clopen cover restriction leave one concrete demand on the local-to-global machinery: given a smooth projective tower of varieties over a number field E with finite étale Hecke correspondences and a prescribed eigenvalue system θ, we must produce a continuous semisimple ℓ-adic Galois representation realizing the simultaneous eigenspace and verify how integral reduction and Hecke congruences control its local behavior at primes above ℓ. The smooth étale comparison side over finite extensions of ℚℓ resolves both sides in parallel, supplying natural Hodge–Tate spectral comparison frames and crystalline admissibility for smooth proper models, which then assemble into the complete comparison step package.

Theorem (Crystalline comparison, Galois realization, and the comparison step package). Fix a prime ℓ, a number field E with algebraic closure Ω, and Galois group Γ = Gal(Ω/E).

  1. Natural Hodge–Tate spectral comparison. For every finite extension K/ℚℓ and every cohomological degree n, the smooth étale comparison input produces the smooth projective Hodge–Tate comparison map, proves its bijectivity, and equips each smooth projective model M of pure relative dimension over K with a Hodge–Tate comparison frame whose Hodge dimensions satisfy ∑a ea = d and whose comparison matrix ΦM ∈ Md × d(ℂℓ) intertwines the étale and Hodge pullback matrices for every morphism u : M′ → M over K:
    E(u) ΦM = ΦM′ H(u).
  2. Crystalline admissibility and crystalline Frobenius annihilation. For every finite extension K/ℚℓ, every smooth proper integral model has Bcris-admissible étale cohomology. Consequently, whenever a nice Hecke tower H over E and eigenvalue system θ admit a surjective smooth integral cover over the discrete valuation ring 𝒪E,w at a prime w | ℓ, the realized Galois representation ρ is crystalline at w. Moreover, if the residue field of K has cardinality q = ℓf and n + 1 integral correspondences T0, …, Tn satisfy the point-correspondence identity on the special fibre with induced operators A0, …, An on Vj, then on every period-ring invariant class x there is an integer N ≥ 0 such that the crystalline Frobenius φ satisfies
    ∑i=0n (−1)i ℓf i(i−1)/2 (Ai ⊗ φf(n−i+N))(x) = 0,
    forcing the Satake congruence polynomial of any integral Hecke congruence at w | ℓ to annihilate the crystalline Frobenius on ρ|w.
  3. Continuous semisimple realization of nice Hecke towers. For every nice Hecke tower H over E and eigenvalue function θ into the algebraic closure of ℚℓ, there exist a nonnegative integer D and a continuous semisimple representation ρ : Γ → GLD(ℚ̄ℓ) realizing the simultaneous Hecke eigenspace piece of H for θ.
  4. Hodge–Tate jump occurrence at places above ℓ. Combining natural Hodge–Tate spectral frames, crystalline admissibility, and continuous Galois realization shows that whenever a nice Hecke tower H has good integral reduction at w | ℓ, every complex Hodge degree carrying a nonzero simultaneous θ-eigenclass appears as a Hodge–Tate filtration jump of ρ|w.
  5. Assembly of the seventeen-component step package. Pairing these four comparison outputs with Eichler–Shimura congruence away from ℓ, integral-to-local cut reduction, rank-one twisting of all four local comparison properties, Satake multiset cardinality, highest-weight jump transfer, lacunary richness extraction, and split-line character subtraction yields the complete comparison step package.
Proof

Step 1: From the smooth étale side to natural Hodge–Tate frames and crystalline admissibility. Over every finite extension K/ℚℓ, the smooth étale comparison input supplies both the smooth projective Hodge–Tate comparison morphism and crystalline admissibility of smooth proper models. On the Hodge–Tate branch, combining the comparison morphism with the square-class, diagonal-étale, and finiteness estimates establishes bijectivity of the Hodge–Tate map. Naturality of the Čech Hodge isomorphism together with finiteness of étale cohomology then builds, for each smooth model M and degree n, a comparison matrix ΦM satisfying the intertwining equation and the Hodge dimension sum ∑a ea = d. On the crystalline branch, the same smooth étale input gives Bcris-admissibility of the étale cohomology of smooth proper models. Pulling back along any surjective smooth integral cover over 𝒪E,w transfers admissibility to the realized Hecke eigenspace piece, establishing the crystalline step at every prime w | ℓ.

Step 2: Universal crystalline Frobenius annihilation. At a finite extension K/ℚℓ with residue cardinality q = ℓf, integral congruence lifts on a smooth proper model produce the universal alternating identity on crystalline invariants for all degrees j and all correspondence tuples satisfying the special-fibre point identity. Feeding this universal identity and smooth-model crystalline admissibility into the local geometric crystalline transport constructed in the next subsection translates any integral Hecke congruence at w | ℓ into annihilation of crystalline Frobenius by the corresponding polynomial on ρ|w.

Step 3: Constructing the continuous semisimple Galois realization. Fix a nice Hecke tower H over E and eigenvalues θ. Base-change splitting, finite-étale correspondence goodness, and Galois closure uniquely characterize each correspondence operator on total étale cohomology. As shown in the flag and descent analysis below, uniqueness forces every Galois automorphism in Γ to commute with each correspondence operator, so the simultaneous eigenspace piece Hθ is Γ-invariant. Smoothness and projectivity of the geometric fibre decompose it into finitely many integral components of fixed relative dimension, making every individual étale cohomology group finite-dimensional and vanishing outside a bounded range of degrees; hence the total étale cohomology is finite-dimensional. Finite-extension descent of torsion étale cohomology ensures that every finite-level stabilizer is open in Γ, making every matrix entry of the Galois action continuous in each degree and therefore on total cohomology and its extension of scalars. Choosing a Jordan–Hölder flag and passing to diagonal blocks produces a continuous semisimple representation ρ : Γ → GLD(ℚ̄ℓ) whose characteristic polynomials and polynomial annihilators match those on the eigenspace piece.

Step 4: Hodge–Tate jump occurrence and the seventeen-component step package. Let H have good integral reduction at w | ℓ. Base-changing the tower to the completion at w supplies a local integral smooth model carrying compatible correspondence operators, while Čech-cube Hodge base change and trace splitting transport the complex Hodge trace eigenspace to the lower piece of the integral model. Intertwining this lower piece with the étale side through the natural Hodge–Tate frame ΦM from Step 1, and invoking crystalline admissibility from Step 1 together with the Galois matrix realization from Step 3, forces every complex Hodge weight appearing on the eigenspace to occur as a Hodge–Tate filtration jump of ρ|w. Finally, combining Galois realization, Eichler–Shimura congruence away from ℓ, crystalline admissibility, jump occurrence, and crystalline Frobenius annihilation with cut reduction, rank-one character twists on all four local conditions, Satake cardinality, highest-weight jump transfer, lacunary richness, and split-line character subtraction completes the seventeen-component step package.

Worked check (block-triangular semisimplification and degree-two crystalline polynomial). First, consider a three-dimensional representation with block labels c = (0, 1, 1), so the lower subspace for index 1 is the coordinate line spanned by e0 and a block-upper-triangular matrix has the form M = ((a, u, v), (0, p, q), (0, r, s)). How do admissible fibre-power towers, integral models, Galois flag descent, and finite-level cohomology descent assemble the weight-one realization?

The step package of the preceding subsection turns any nice Hecke tower with good integral models and Hodge witnesses into Galois representations with controlled local data. To supply those towers from a PEL unitary Shimura datum, two complementary chains of arguments must lock together: on the moduli side, constructing fibre powers of the universal abelian variety at admissible congruence levels with good integral reduction at every good prime; and on the representation and cohomological side, proving the flag semisimplification, Galois–Hecke commutation, finite-extension cohomology descent, local crystalline transport, and integer-jump recovery theorems used throughout the step package. Together with automorphic plan existence, these assemble the full weight-one geometric realization conditional solely on the holomorphic Lefschetz fixed-point formula.

Theorem (Fibre-power towers, integral reduction, Galois descent mechanisms, and conditional assembly). Fix a prime p and a PEL unitary Shimura datum with universal abelian family linked to an algebraic group over ℚ, a number field E, an algebraic extension Ω/k, and a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme X over k.

  1. Admissible fibre-power Kuga–Sato towers. Whenever an integral Hecke element h satisfies k h−1(Λ̂) ⊆ Λ̂ for a positive integer k on the adelic reference lattice Λ̂, the congruence level Nk is admissible over level N. Translation isogenies compose, pull back, and act freely and transitively on level-structure orbits, descending along finite étale covers to yield smooth fibre powers, nice Hecke towers, and complete Kuga–Sato towers equipped with unique, composable, transitive, free witness maps.
  2. Good integral reduction at good primes. Over any prime w of E corresponding to an ideal 𝔴 where the group-side Hecke test is good, integral lattice generation, finite étale base covers, congruence quotients, and fibre-power extensions furnish a smooth integral model of the fibre-power Hecke tower at w.
  3. Jordan–Hölder flag semisimplification and Galois–Hecke commutation. For every finite-dimensional representation ρ0 : Γ → GLd(k), there exist a change-of-basis matrix P ∈ GLd(k), a block-index map c : {0, …, d − 1} → ℕ, and a semisimple representation ρ : Γ → GLd(k) such that P−1 ρ0(g) P is block upper triangular with respect to c and agrees with ρ(g) on all diagonal blocks:
    ρ(g)i,j = (P−1 ρ0(g) P)i,j   if c(i) = c(j),   and   ρ(g)i,j = 0   if c(i) ≠ c(j).
    Moreover, conjugation invariance and uniqueness of the correspondence operator characterization on total étale cohomology imply that every correspondence operator commutes with every Galois automorphism in Gal(Ω/k).
  4. Finite-extension descent of torsion étale cohomology, local crystalline transport, and jump transfer. Writing Ω as the directed colimit of its finite intermediate extensions L/k exhibits XΩ as the projective limit of the quasi-compact quasi-separated base changes XL with affine transitions, so finitariness of torsion Tate twists forces every torsion étale cohomology class to descend to some finite extension L/k:
    XΩ ≅ limL XL   ⟹   ∀ y ∈ Hnét(XΩ, μq⊗i),   ∃ [L : k] < ∞,   z ∈ Hnét(XL, μq⊗i)   with   πL∗(z) = y.
    Pulling back global Hecke congruences along a local completion embedding transports them into local crystalline Galois data at every w | ℓ, and injectivity of the integer-to-complex embedding transfers every highest-weight jump list to the local Hodge–Tate jump occurrence predicate.
  5. Conditional assembly of weight-one geometric realization. Combining the comparison step package of the preceding subsection, the six-part Kuga–Sato package (standard base, tower existence, niceness, good reduction, congruence, and Hodge trace comparison), and automorphic plan existence yields the weight-one geometric realization theorem conditional only on the holomorphic Lefschetz fixed-point formula entering the Hodge trace component.
Proof

Step 1: Admissible levels, translation orbits, and Kuga–Sato witnesses. Let h be an integral Hecke element with k h−1(Λ̂) ⊆ Λ̂. The congruence characterization of the level-N subgroup shows that any element of level 1 fixing Λ̂ modulo N lies in level N; rescaling by k then proves admissibility of level Nk over level N. Translation by rational elements acting on the universal abelian variety respects multiplication, composition, pullback, Tate twists, and kernels; rigidity of abelian-scheme morphisms yields uniqueness of point orbits and free transitive action on level structures. Finite étale descent transfers homomorphisms, polarizations, and translations from deeper covers to the quotient, proving smoothness of fibre powers, niceness of every tower cut out by finite families of Hecke elements, and existence of unique, identity-preserving, transitive, free witness maps for the full Kuga–Sato tower.

Step 2: Good integral reduction at good primes. Fix a prime w of E corresponding to a good ideal 𝔴 on the group side. In the first phase, multiplicity-unit lattice generation, trace pullback, and quotient compatibility establish the integral base lattice structure; Cartesian fibre powers and finite étale structure maps supply finite étale base covers. In the second phase, normal-extension descent, section injectivity over pulled-back kernels, level comparison, and surjectivity of the base projection establish integral lifts of all Hecke correspondences. Combining these with Chinese-remainder congruence quotients, fibre-power extensions, generic degree checks, and finite étale extension bounds yields a smooth integral reduction of the Kuga–Sato tower at w.

Step 3: Jordan–Hölder flag bases and Galois–Hecke commutation. For any finite-dimensional representation ρ0 of Γ over a field k, existence of a Jordan–Hölder flag 0 = W0 ⊆ ⋯ ⊆ Wd = kd and a basis matrix P ∈ GLd(k) with block labels c matching P · low(c, i) = Wi transports the Jordan–Hölder property to the conjugated representation P−1 ρ0 P. Invariance of every subspace low(c, i) makes P−1 ρ0(g) P block upper triangular, so its diagonal-block projection defines a group homomorphism whose invariant subspaces decompose as a direct sum of irreducible blocks, making ρ semisimple; moreover the associated-graded relation is preserved under precomposition with any group homomorphism. For Galois–Hecke commutation, let A be a correspondence operator and G the linear action of σ ∈ Gal(Ω/k) on total étale cohomology, with inverse G′ = G−1 coming from σ−1. Conjugating A by G yields G ∘ A ∘ G′, which still satisfies the correspondence characterization; uniqueness forces G ∘ A ∘ G′ = A. Composing on the right with G gives A ∘ G = (G ∘ A ∘ G′) ∘ G = G ∘ A ∘ (G′ ∘ G) = G ∘ A.

Step 4: Directed-colimit descent for torsion étale cohomology, local transport, and jump transfer. Let FI(k, Ω) be the poset of finite intermediate extensions k ⊆ L ⊆ Ω. It is nonempty (containing k) and directed, because the compositum of two finite extensions is finite over k. Since Ω/k is algebraic, every element x ∈ Ω generates a finite extension k(x), so Ω is the filtered colimit of FI(k, Ω) in commutative rings. Applying the contravariant functor Spec turns this colimit into a cofiltered limit Spec(Ω) ≅ limL Spec(L), and the universal property of fibred products lifts this to a limit cone XΩ ≅ limL XL. Every scheme XL inherits quasi-compactness and quasi-separatedness from X → Spec(k) by base change, and every transition morphism XL′ → XL is the base change of the affine morphism Spec(L′) → Spec(L), hence affine. Because the torsion Tate twist sheaf μq⊗i is finitary, cohomology of the cofiltered limit is the colimit of the cohomologies, yielding descent to a finite extension L/k (which in turn forces the stabilizer of every modulo-ℓm class to contain the open subgroup Gal(Ω/L)). Next, pulling back a global Hecke congruence along a local completion embedding at w | ℓ combines congruence base change, local tower base change, universal Frobenius annihilation, and smooth-model crystalline admissibility to transport the global Hecke eigenspace into local crystalline data. Finally, because the map ℤ → ℂ is injective, equality of images of finite multisets recovers the exact integer jump multiset of a dominant weight λ and transfers every prescribed complex Hodge weight at an induced embedding τ | w to the local jump-occurrence predicate.

Step 5: Conditional assembly. Given the step package from the preceding subsection, the six-part Kuga–Sato package (standard base, tower existence, niceness, good reduction, congruence comparison, and Hodge trace comparison), and automorphic plan existence, converting each plan into a tower package and applying the step package yields the full weight-one geometric realization theorem once the holomorphic Lefschetz trace formula supplies the sixth Kuga–Sato component.

Worked checks (commutation from conjugation, directed colimit of extensions, and rank-three jumps). (a) Take G = ((0, 1), (1, 0)) (so G−1 = G) and A = ((3, 1), (1, 3)). Direct multiplication gives G A G−1 = ((3, 1), (1, 3)) = A, and multiplying on the right by G yields A G = ((1, 3), (3, 1)) = G A. (b) In Ω = ℚ̄ over k = ℚ, the element α = √2 + √3 lies in the compositum ℚ(√2) · ℚ(√3) = ℚ(√2, √3) of degree 4 over ℚ; any torsion class descending to L = ℚ(√2, √3) is fixed by the open normal subgroup Gal(ℚ̄/ℚ(√2, √3)) of index 4. (c) For the rank-three dominant weight λ = (0, 0, −2), the jumps k − λk for k = 0, 1, 2 are 0 − 0 = 0, 1 − 0 = 1, and 2 − (−2) = 4, whose image {0, 1, 4} in ℂ determines the integer multiset {0, 1, 4} uniquely.

How does clopen decomposition close the holomorphic Lefschetz formula and deliver unconditional weight-one geometric realizations?

Only one hypothesis remained open in the conditional weight-one assembly: the holomorphic Lefschetz fixed-point formula for a finite étale correspondence on an arbitrary smooth projective complex variety Z of pure relative dimension d, without assuming that Z is connected or irreducible. Shimura varieties and fibre powers over ℂ naturally have multiple connected components, and a Hecke correspondence can map points of one component into another. Breaking Z into its finitely many integral clopen components turns the global Hodge cohomology into a finite product whose correspondence operator is a block matrix. Every off-diagonal block drops out of the trace, every fixed point lands inside a unique diagonal block, and summing the irreducible holomorphic Lefschetz formula over the diagonal blocks closes the formula in complete generality, delivering the weight-one geometric realizations unconditionally.

Theorem (Clopen holomorphic Lefschetz reduction and unconditional weight-one geometric realization).

  1. Holomorphic Lefschetz formula for finite étale correspondences on possibly disconnected varieties. Let s : Z → Spec ℂ be smooth and projective of pure relative dimension d, and let c1, c2 : Y → Z be finite étale morphisms over ℂ. Suppose the complex fixed-point locus F = {y ∈ Y(ℂ) | c1(y) = c2(y)} is finite and transverse: at every y ∈ F, the local derivative matrix Ay ∈ Md(ℂ) of c2 ∘ c1−1 satisfies det(Id − Ay) ≠ 0. Decomposing Z = ⊔j ∈ J Vj into its finitely many pairwise disjoint integral clopen components and setting Wj,k = c1−1(Vj) ∩ c2−1(Vk) with restricted finite étale projections c1,j,k : Wj,k → Vj and c2,j,k : Wj,k → Vk, every Hodge cohomology group Hb(Z, Ωa) is finite-dimensional over ℂ, the global correspondence trace reduces strictly to diagonal blocks
    tr(c1∗ c2∗ | Hb(Z, Ωa)) = ∑j ∈ J tr((c1,j,j)∗ (c2,j,j)∗ | Hb(Vj, Ωa)),
    and for every integer a ≥ 0 the alternating holomorphic Hodge trace satisfies
    ∑b=0d (−1)b tr(c1∗ c2∗ | Hb(Z, Ωa)) = ∑y ∈ F ([ta] det(Id + t Ay)) / det(Id − Ay).
  2. Unconditional weight-one geometric realizations. Discharging the holomorphic Lefschetz hypothesis in the conditional assembly proves unconditionally that for every odd positive integer m, CM field E, prime ℓ, coefficient isomorphism ι : ℚ̄ℓ ≃ ℂ, auxiliary places w0, v′ | ℓ with separated real-subfield primes and a lacunary weight at v′, and eligible auxiliary automorphic representation U of rank m: (a) there exists a continuous semisimple Galois representation ρ : Γ → GLD(ℚ̄ℓ) satisfying the base comparison for U; (b) for every eligible spherical prime w ∤ ℓ, there exists such a realization satisfying the Eichler–Shimura Satake annihilation with normalized Satake roots (Nw)(m−1)/2 Bw; and (c) for every separated prime w | ℓ distinct from w0 and v′, there exist realizations satisfying, respectively, crystallinity at w with all prescribed Hodge–Tate jumps and annihilation of crystalline Frobenius by the Satake polynomial at w. Together with the Hecke approximation estimates and lacunary rank extraction, this completes the geometric realization chain.
Proof

Step 1: Disjoint integral clopen decomposition and pullback grid. Because s : Z → Spec ℂ is projective of finite type, the underlying topological space of Z is quasi-compact. Smoothness of relative dimension d therefore decomposes Z into finitely many pairwise disjoint nonempty integral open subschemes Vj (j ∈ J) covering Z. For each j ∈ J, the complement of Vj is the union of the open sets Vi for i ≠ j, so Vj is both open and closed in Z; consequently Vj is smooth projective and integral of dimension d over ℂ. Set Wj,k = c1−1(Vj) ∩ c2−1(Vk) for (j, k) ∈ J × J. Continuity of c1 and c2 makes every Wj,k clopen in Y; disjointness and coverage of {Vj} make {Wj,k} a pairwise disjoint clopen cover of Y with ⋃k Wj,k = c1−1(Vj) for each j. Restricting the finite étale maps c1 and c2 to the clopen piece Wj,k produces finite étale morphisms c1,j,k : Wj,k → Vj and c2,j,k : Wj,k → Vk over ℂ.

Step 2: Block-diagonal trace reduction on Hodge cohomology. Fix a, b ≥ 0. Because {Vj} is a finite disjoint open cover of Z, open restriction yields a linear isomorphism e : Hb(Z, Ωa) ≃ ∏j ∈ J Hb(Vj, Ωa). Write Pj : Hb(Z, Ωa) → Hb(Vj, Ωa) for the j-th restriction map and Tj,k = (c1,j,k)∗ ∘ (c2,j,k)∗ : Hb(Vk, Ωa) → Hb(Vj, Ωa). Since c1−1(Vj) is the disjoint union of the clopen pieces Wj,k over k ∈ J, restricting the global correspondence operator u = c1∗ c2∗ to Vj splits as the finite sum Pj(u(x)) = ∑k ∈ J Tj,k(Pk(x)). Each component Vj is smooth projective and integral, so each Hb(Vj, Ωa) is finite-dimensional over ℂ, and hence Hb(Z, Ωa) is finite-dimensional as well. Writing any linear endomorphism f of ∏j ∈ J Nj as ∑i,k ιi ∘ (πi ∘ f ∘ ιk) ∘ πk and using cyclicity of trace gives tr(ιi ∘ g ∘ πk) = tr(g ∘ πk ∘ ιi) = 0 for k ≠ i and tr(g) for k = i. Conjugating by e therefore kills every off-diagonal block Tj,k (j ≠ k) and establishes the diagonal block-trace identity.

Step 3: Unique diagonal lifting of fixed points and component-wise summation. Let y ∈ F be a complex fixed point, so c1(y) = c2(y). Because {Wj,k} is a disjoint clopen cover of Y, y lifts uniquely to a complex point of a unique piece Wj,k. Then c1(y) ∈ Vj and c1(y) = c2(y) ∈ Vk; pairwise disjointness of {Vj} forces j = k. Thus F partitions into the disjoint subsets Fj of fixed points lying in the diagonal clopen pieces Wj,j. Because open inclusions are monomorphisms, the fixed points of the restricted correspondence (c1,j,j, c2,j,j) on Wj,j biject with Fj, and open restriction preserves the local tangent matrix Ay and nonvanishing of det(Id − Ay). Applying the irreducible holomorphic Lefschetz fixed-point formula on each integral component (Vj, Wj,j) yields ∑b=0d (−1)b tr(Tj,j) = ∑y ∈ Fj ([ta] det(Id + t Ay)) / det(Id − Ay). Summing over j ∈ J and substituting the block-trace identity proves the general holomorphic Lefschetz formula.

Step 4: Unconditional export and weight-one assembly. Exporting this clopen-assembled formula supplies the global finite-étale correspondence trace identity, which directly discharges the sole remaining holomorphic Lefschetz hypothesis in the conditional assembly and yields all three local-to-global clauses of the weight-one geometric realization theorem unconditionally.

Worked example (a disconnected two-component complex elliptic curve variety). Take d = 1 and let Z = E1 ⊔ E2 be the disjoint union of two complex elliptic curves, indexed by J = {1, 2}. Form a finite étale correspondence Y = W1,1 ⊔ W2,2 ⊔ W1,2 where: on W1,1 = E1 we take c1 = id and c2 = [2] (multiplication by 2); on W2,2 = E2 we take c1 = id and c2 = [3] (multiplication by 3); and on the off-diagonal piece W1,2 = E1 we take c1 = id : E1 → E1 and c2 : E1 → E2 an isogeny (which has 0 fixed points because E1 and E2 are disjoint, and enters only the off-diagonal block T1,2 with 0 contribution to the trace). On W1,1, the condition 2x = x selects 1 fixed point (x = 0) with tangent matrix A = (2), det(1 − A) = −1, and det(1 + t A) = 1 + 2t; its fixed-point contributions are 1/(−1) = −1 for a = 0 and 2/(−1) = −2 for a = 1, matching the cohomology alternating traces 1 − 2 = −1 on Hb(E1, 𝒪) and 2 − 4 = −2 on Hb(E1, Ω1). On W2,2, the condition 3x = x is 2x = 0, selecting the 4 points of E2[2], each with A = (3), det(1 − A) = −2, and det(1 + t A) = 1 + 3t; their summed fixed-point contributions are 4 · 1/(−2) = −2 for a = 0 and 4 · 3/(−2) = −6 for a = 1, matching 1 − 3 = −2 and 3 − 9 = −6. Globally on Z = E1 ⊔ E2, the diagonal block sum gives (1 + 1) − (2 + 3) = −3 = (−1) + (−2) for a = 0 and (2 + 3) − (4 + 9) = −8 = (−2) + (−6) for a = 1, with the off-diagonal isogeny W1,2 contributing 0 to both sides.
