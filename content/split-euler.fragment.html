Why the split Euler product has a polynomial correction

Fix the standard lattice Λ₀ = 𝒪a and an endpoint Λ = gΛ₀. To sum the split integral, the proof first counts two moves. First enlarge Λ₀ to a lattice H, then shrink H to Λ. Give each move of length k the weight Xk, where length means the module length of the quotient of the larger lattice by the smaller one. Several intermediate lattices give the same endpoint. Their total weight explains exactly which factor must be removed from the product of the two sums. The two lattice inclusions in matrix coordinates

Write d₁,…,da for the elementary divisors of Λ relative to Λ₀. The smallest possible intermediate lattice is M = Λ₀ + Λ. In elementary-divisor coordinates its exponents are min(dᵢ,0), so the two lengths through M add up to ∑|dᵢ|. For any larger H, additivity of length gives

length(H/Λ₀) + length(H/Λ) = ∑i|dᵢ| + 2 length(H/M).

Taking lattice duals identifies the superlattices of M with sublattices of M∨ of the same quotient length. For an upper triangular basis in Hermite normal form, a diagonal entry ϖuᵢ in row i leaves a − 1 − i entries to its right, each chosen modulo ϖuᵢ. Here 0 ≤ i < a and q is the residue-field size. Summing over uᵢ ≥ 0 gives ∏i(1 − qiX²)−1 after reversing the indices. This common multiplier is the same for every endpoint. To count each endpoint with its required weight X∑|dᵢ|, multiply the two-move sum by ∏i(1 − qiX²). Check the correction for a pair of rank-two lattices

Take q = 2 and Λ generated by ϖ²e₁ and ϖ⁻¹e₂. The sum M is generated by e₁ and ϖ⁻¹e₂, so the two moves through it have lengths 1 and 2. There are three intermediate lattices one step above M, one for each line in ϖ⁻¹M/M ≅ 𝔽₂². At length two above M, the three possibilities for the diagonal exponents in the dual Hermite form give 1 + 2 + 4 = 7 lattices. Thus the weight at this single endpoint starts X³(1 + 3X² + 7X⁴ + ⋯). Multiplication by (1 − X²)(1 − 2X²) cancels the next coefficients since 3 − 3 = 0 and 7 − 9 + 2 = 0.

The two sums also explain the linear Euler factors. Start with a spherical matrix coefficient normalized to equal 1 at the identity. Its sum on each double coset agrees with that of a right-K-invariant function whose value on an upper triangular matrix with diagonal valuations uᵢ is ∏iγiuᵢ. For Satake parameters αi, this triangular model has γi = αiqi−(a−1)/2. Summing the shrinking move in Hermite coordinates gives one geometric ratio qa−1−iγiX per row. Passing to the inverse transpose and reversing the basis turns the enlarging move into a shrinking move, with ratios qiγi−1X. For X = q−(a/2+s), these simplify respectively to αiq−(s+½) and αi−1q−(s+½). The proof first sums for small |X|, where absolute convergence permits either order. Inserting αi = zβi for the determinant twist gives the two sets of factors in the split-place formula.
