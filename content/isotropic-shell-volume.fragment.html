Which arithmetic volumes enter an isotropic-line shell?

Keep the hyperbolic plane with basis e,e′ and let W be its Hermitian orthogonal complement of dimension s = M−2. Let P be the stabilizer of the line Ee in the adelic unitary group. Its coordinates consist of a horizontal vector b ∈ 𝔸Es, a central coordinate z ∈ 𝔸F, an isometry g ∈ U(W)(𝔸F), and the scalar a ∈ 𝔸E× by which the line is multiplied. We need to bound the integral on the region where this last coordinate has logarithmic height near the integer j.

Reduce the four coordinates. The additive quotient 𝔸F/F has compact representatives. Indeed, the Chinese remainder theorem clears the nonintegral finite residues by subtracting a field element, and an algebraic integer then moves the infinite coordinates into a bounded lattice parallelepiped. The same argument works for E and in each of the s horizontal coordinates. Denote the resulting compact sets by Cz and Cb. For the scalar choose a fixed compact set ω of idèles such that every a belongs to E×τ(j)ω for some integer j. To see this, the norm-one idèle classes form a compact group. The idèle modulus of τ(x) is e2dx. Removing the real logarithmic height leaves a norm-one class. Its representatives can be taken in a compact set by lifting a finite cover by relatively compact open neighborhoods. Absorb the fractional part of the height in ω. Finally put g in a fundamental domain 𝒟W for its rational isometries. After the rational scalar and isometry have been reduced, subtract a rational horizontal vector. This changes the central coordinate by the cross term in the unipotent group law, so subtract a rational central element last. The resulting shells cover modulo rational elements of P. Summing over possible overlaps gives an upper bound. Borel, Some finiteness properties of adele groups over number fields, Theorem 5.8 and its idèle example, p. 22

Why the complementary quotient has finite volume. Borel’s finite-volume theorem applies to a connected linear group over a number field whose characters defined over that field are all trivial. Over E, U(W) becomes GLs. Its algebraic characters are determinant powers. Indeed, on the diagonal torus a character is a monomial whose exponents must be equal by permutation symmetry, and a character is trivial on each elementary unipotent subgroup. Conjugation over F takes the determinant to its inverse. A power descends only when its exponent is zero. Thus 𝒟W has finite volume for any Haar measure on U(W)(𝔸F). The same argument supplies the finite volume of the complement of dimension M−1 on a nonzero norm fibre. Borel, Theorem 5.6(i), p. 21

In these coordinates a left Haar measure on P is the product of the two additive measures, a Haar measure on U(W), and χ(a) d×a. Here χ is the pushforward multiplier of conjugation on the two additive coordinates. Left translation in the unipotent group translates the centre by a quantity depending on the horizontal variable and preserves product measure by integration in the centre first. The isometries preserve additive measure. The scalar multiplier in the additive variables is exactly canceled by transforming the density χ(a) in the multiplicative variable. On τ(j)ω one has χ(τ(j)c) = e−2d(s+1)jχ(c). Multiplicative Haar measure is unchanged by translation. The mass of this shell is consequently bounded by

vol(Cb) vol(Cz) vol(𝒟W) vol×(ω) supω χ · exp(−2d(s+1)j).

Every factor before the exponential is finite and independent of j. The bound grows as j tends to −∞. The compact support of the original indicator and the discreteness of rational scalars supply the lower cutoff in the geometric sum.

How can a shell estimate on the stabilizer bound an integral on the whole group?

Write G = U(J)(𝔸F). We show G = PC for one compact set C of isometries. Given h ∈ G, the vector h−1e has an adelic linear functional taking the value 1 on it, obtained by pairing with h−1e′. The primitive-column normalization supplies an idèle a and a new functional taking the value 1 on v = ah−1e. The column and its new functional lie in fixed compact adelic sets. At a split place one can choose a different pivot in each component. Almost everywhere the original integral column and functional already work together with scalar 1.

Use the inverse Hermitian matrix to write the functional as ⟨z,·⟩, and replace z by z − ½⟨z,z⟩v. This continuous operation leaves the pairing with v equal to 1 and makes the second vector isotropic. The resulting pairs lie in a compact set and all have the same invertible two-by-two Gram matrix as (e,e′). The compact representative theorem on this regular fibre supplies a compact family of isometries k carrying the reference pair onto them. Then hke = ae, hence hk ∈ P. Taking inverses of the compact family gives C.

Compare the two integrals. Choose a continuous cutoff 0 ≤ ψ ≤ 1 equal to 1 on C, with compact support C′. For x ∈ PC, the integral ∫P ψ(p−1x) dν(p) has a uniform positive lower bound m. By left invariance it suffices to take x ∈ C. Positivity in a neighborhood of each such x and a finite cover of C give m. Write S for the union of the shells, and β(x) for the same integral restricted to S. Its sum over the rational translates of x by Γ ∩ P is at least m, since the translates of S cover P up to measure zero. Multiplying by the nonnegative invariant test f and unfolding shows that m times its quotient integral is at most ∫G β(x)f(x) dμ(x). By Tonelli, integrate first over x = pc. Only c ∈ C′ contribute. If bj bounds f(pc) there for p in shell j, the result is at most μ(C′) ∑j bj ν(Sj). This gives the constant μ(C′)/m in the isotropic-line integral bound.
