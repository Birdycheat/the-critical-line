Why the order of a stabilizer kills positive-degree homology

Suppose a group G acts transitively on a nonempty set X of faces. Choose a face x and write S for its stabilizer. The module ℤ[X] consists of finite integral sums of faces, with G permuting their labels. To compute its group homology we can keep just x and the group that fixes it.

Shapiro’s lemma for homology. For a subgroup S of G, a left ℤ[S]-module M, and any degree j ≥ 0, there is a natural isomorphism

Hj(G, ℤ[G] ⊗ℤ[S] M) ≅ Hj(S, M).

One proof starts with a free resolution of the trivial right ℤ[G]-module ℤ. On restriction to S it is still a free resolution, since the elements of each coset gS give a copy of the regular right module ℤ[S] in ℤ[G]. Tensoring that resolution over ℤ[G] with the induced module on the left of the formula gives, by associativity of tensor product, the same chain complex as tensoring it over ℤ[S] with M. For trivial M = ℤ, the induced module identifies with ℤ[X] by g ⊗ 1 ↦ [gx]. Indeed gx = g′x exactly when gS = g′S. C. Weibel, An Introduction to Homological Algebra, lemma 6.3.2 and corollary 6.3.3.

Now suppose S is finite of order h. Give ℤ the trivial S-action and ℤ[S] the left regular action. Define two coefficient maps

ℤ → ℤ[S] → ℤ,   1 ↦ ∑t∈S[t],   [t] ↦ 1.

Left multiplication permutes the summands of the first image, and the second map sums coefficients. Both maps therefore respect the action. Their composite multiplies by h. Applying homology in degree j gives maps in the same order. On a representative chain their composite multiplies each coefficient by h, so it also multiplies the homology class by h. But the middle group is Hj(S, ℤ[S]) ≅ Hj({1}, ℤ) by Shapiro applied to the regular orbit. For j > 0 this group vanishes, since ℤ itself is a free resolution of length zero over the trivial group. Thus h kills every class in Hj(S, ℤ). In degree zero the middle group is ℤ. All basis elements [t] become equal in the coinvariants, so the first map multiplies by h and the second sends their common class to 1. The composite on H0(S, ℤ) = ℤ is still multiplication by h.

How many classes can one orbit contribute?

In positive degree j, the integral bar chain group with trivial coefficients has one generator for each ordered j-tuple of elements of S, including tuples with identity entries. Its rank is hj. The cycles form a subgroup of this free abelian group, hence are free of some rank r ≤ hj. One can see the rank bound by projecting onto the first coordinate. The image is cyclic, and the kernel lies in a free abelian group with one fewer coordinate. A lift of a generator of a nonzero image extends a basis of that kernel. The images of the r basis cycles generate homology. Since h kills each image, every class can be written with all coefficients between 0 and h − 1. This gives

|Hj(S, ℤ)| ≤ hr ≤ hhj.

Suppose now X has at most A orbits and every stabilizer is finite of order at most s, with s ≥ 1. Splitting a sum of faces by orbit splits ℤ[X] as a finite direct sum of permutation modules. The bar differential preserves these summands, so their homology groups also form a direct sum. Shapiro and the one-orbit bound give each summand order at most ssj. Multiplying these orders gives |Hj(G, ℤ[X])| ≤ sA sj. For X empty the group is zero and its order is one. This is the factor used once at each positive degree of the homology bound for the face complex. Weibel, §§6.5.1–6.5.4, the bar resolution.

A stabilizer of order two. Let S = {1, t} with t² = 1. Its free resolution has one copy of ℤ[S] in each degree. The differential is multiplication by t − 1 in odd degrees and by 1 + t in positive even degrees. To check exactness, write an element as a + bt. The first multiplier kills exactly the multiples of 1 + t, and the second kills exactly the multiples of t − 1. The augmentation also has kernel generated by t − 1. Tensor with the trivial module ℤ. The two differentials become 0 and 2, giving H1 ≅ ℤ/2 and H2 = 0. The actual orders are 2 and 1, within the respective one-orbit bounds 4 and 16. Weibel, calculation 6.2.1 and theorem 6.2.2.

How a stabilizer bound enters the next homological degree

Let ⋯ → C1 → C0 → M → 0 be a complex of integral G-modules, where Cp = ℤ[Xp] and G permutes the basis of faces. Fix j ≥ 1. Assume the complex is exact at M and at C0, …, Cj. Suppose each Xp up to degree j has at most A orbits and stabilizers of order at most s ≥ 1. Put K = ker(C0 → M). Exactness identifies K with the image of C1 → C0. Thus 0 → K → C0 → M → 0 is short exact. The tail ⋯ → C2 → C1 → K → 0 gives the complex for the next step. Its last map is the same boundary with its target restricted to its image, so its kernel is still the image of C2.

The connecting map. Apply the long exact homology sequence to the short exact sequence of coefficients. Indeed the bar resolution is free in each degree, so tensoring it with the three modules preserves exactness degree by degree. The resulting homology sequence has the following segment, exact at its middle term.

Hj(G, C0) → Hj(G, M) →δ Hj−1(G, K).

To see the degree drop, lift a cycle with coefficients in M to a chain with coefficients in C0. Its boundary maps to zero in the chains with coefficients in M, hence has coefficients in K. The class of this boundary is δ of the original class. Changing the lift adds a boundary with coefficients in K. If the original cycle changes by a boundary, lift a chain that produces that boundary and add its boundary to the old lift. Taking the boundary again gives zero for the added term. If δ vanishes, subtract a chain with coefficients in K whose boundary is that of the lift. The corrected lift is a cycle, which explains exactness at the middle term. Weibel, theorem 1.3.1, the long exact homology sequence.

In an exact segment U → V → W of abelian groups, suppose U is finite and the torsion subgroup of W is finite. The homomorphism tors V → tors W has kernel contained in the image of U, and its image lies in tors W. Counting a kernel and its cosets gives |tors V| ≤ |U| · |tors W|. The distinction matters even for the familiar exact sequence 0 → ℤ →×n ℤ → ℤ/n → 0, with n > 1. Its last three terms are ℤ → ℤ/n → 0. Both outer torsion subgroups have order one, yet the middle torsion subgroup has order n.

The permutation-module estimate bounds the whole group on the left of the homology sequence, so the comparison becomes

|tors Hj(G, M)| ≤ sA sj · |tors Hj−1(G, K)|.

If each basis boundary Cj+1 → Cj has sum of absolute coefficients at most b ≥ 1, this same boundary remains at the top of every shortened complex. At degree zero the integral presentation of the coinvariants therefore contributes at most bA. Unwinding the inequality supplies one power of s for each positive degree, with total exponent A(s + ⋯ + sj). Since each of the j summands is at most sj, the product is at most bA sj A sj. For the face complex take M = ℤ with trivial action, and b = j + 2 because a basis element in degree j + 1 has that many face terms. This recovers the stated torsion bound.
