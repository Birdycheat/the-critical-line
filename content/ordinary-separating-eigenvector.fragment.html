Which eigenvector can a functional separate from the Frobenius image?

Let P be the space of primitive de Rham classes over the algebraically closed field k of characteristic q, and let L be its first Hodge subspace. The coefficient ring 𝒪F acts by ρ. Write χ for the residue character at 𝔴, Q = qf for the size of its image, and ΦQ = Φf for the iterated Frobenius, with f ≥ 1. This additive map commutes with each ρ(c) and sends λz to λQΦQz. Put E = ρ(e) and D = ρ(d), using the local coefficients of the pointwise Frobenius inclusion.

Separating eigenvector lemma. The local coefficient and Hodge data give a vector y in the image of D and a linear functional x on P such that

W = spank{DΦQz ∣ z ∈ P},   ρ(c)y = χ(c)y   for every c,
x(W) = 0,   x(y) = 1,   x ∘ E = x.

The local inputs. The projected space EL is a nonzero line on which every coefficient acts by χ. Frobenius kills L, so a nonzero v in EL satisfies ΦQv = 0. Put S = ρ(a), where a is the chosen coefficient in 𝔴. The projector satisfies E² = E and χ(e) = 1. The local nilpotence and annihilator relations give ShE = 0 for some h and ker D ⊆ SP + (1 − E)P. We will also use three congruences modulo q. They are ed ≡ d, ad ≡ 0, and, for each z ∈ 𝔴, ez ≡ ab for some coefficient b. Congruent coefficients act equally on P in characteristic q.

Finding a vector outside the image. Let G be the simultaneous generalized χ-eigenspace. For each c and u ∈ G, some power of ρ(c) − χ(c) kills u. Write P = G ⊕ G′, where G′ is the sum of the other simultaneous generalized eigenspaces. For one coefficient, the Chinese remainder theorem applied to the relatively prime powers of the linear factors of its minimal polynomial gives the projections onto its generalized eigenspaces. The commuting coefficients preserve every summand. Repeatedly splitting a summand whenever a coefficient has two distinct eigenvalues terminates in finite dimension. This gives the asserted decomposition. The Stacks Project, Chinese remainder theorem.

The map ΦQ sends generalized eigenvalues ψ(c) to ψ(c)Q by semilinearity and commutation. Every χ(c) belongs to the field of Q elements, and raising to the Qth power is injective on k. Therefore ΦQ preserves both G and G′. On G, the idempotent E acts as the identity because its only generalized eigenvalue there is 1. Write πχ for the projection onto G along G′. The place projector E preserves both summands and fixes G, so πχE = πχ. It can also fix other summands belonging to conjugate characters above 𝔴. Set U = spank ΦQ(G). The vector v above belongs to G. Extend it to a basis of G. The images of the remaining basis vectors span U, so dim U < dim G.

Suppose D(G) ⊆ W. For each u ∈ G, choose s in the span of ΦQ(P) with Du = Ds. The relation for ker D writes u − s = Sb + (1 − E)c for vectors b, c. Apply πχ to this equality. The projection of s lies in U because ΦQ preserves both summands. The projection of Sb is S applied to the projection of b, and the last term has zero projection because E acts as 1 on G. This gives G ⊆ U + SG. Both summands lie in G, and S preserves U because it commutes with ΦQ. Iterating gives G = U + ShG = U, contrary to the dimension bound. Choose u ∈ G with y = Du outside W.

Why the generalized eigenvector is an eigenvector. Since D preserves G, this y lies in G. For z ∈ 𝔴 the congruences give zd ≡ zed ≡ abd ≡ 0 modulo q, so ρ(z)y = 0. In particular cQ − c belongs to 𝔴 for every coefficient c, since χ(c)Q = χ(c). Thus two polynomials in ρ(c) kill y, namely XQ − X and a power of X − χ(c). The first has derivative −1, so their greatest common divisor is X − χ(c). Indeed the ideal generated by these two polynomials is generated by its monic element of least degree, since division with remainder would otherwise produce an element of smaller degree. That generator is their greatest common divisor and so is a polynomial combination of the two. Hence ρ(c)y = χ(c)y.

Extend a basis of W by y and then to a basis of P. Define ξ to take value 1 on y and 0 on all the other basis vectors. Set x = ξ ∘ E. Since ED = D, the projector fixes y and all of W. This proves the lemma. If the Frobenius inclusion EL ⊆ W holds, then x also vanishes on L. It belongs to the dual Hodge subspace used in the deformation at the generic point.

A two-dimensional example. Take k to be an algebraic closure of 𝔽₃, with 𝔽₃ acting by scalars on k². Put Q = 3, E = D = 1, S = 0 and ΦQ(λe₁ + μe₂) = μ³e₁. Take L = ke₁. Both ker ΦQ and W equal this line. Starting with v = e₁ proves the image has dimension at most one. Every functional vanishing on W also vanishes on this v. Choosing y = e₂ instead gives the required functional x(λe₁ + μe₂) = μ.
