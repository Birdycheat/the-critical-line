How can one coefficient select a single place above q?

In the factorization through Frobenius, the local coefficients have to satisfy identities in the ring of integers before they act on an abelian variety. Let C = 𝒪F for a number field F. Fix the rational prime q which will be the characteristic of the geometric fibre, and a maximal ideal 𝔴 of C containing q. Two integers enter the construction. The ramification index r is the exponent of 𝔴 in the ideal (q). The residue degree f is determined by |C/𝔴| = qf. Both are positive.

Projector at the chosen place. Factor (q) = 𝔴r𝔟, collecting the powers of all other primes above q in 𝔟. There is an element e of C satisfying

e ≡ 1   (mod 𝔴rf),   e ≡ 0   (mod 𝔟f).

Indeed ideals of C factor uniquely into prime ideals. Thus 𝔴 and 𝔟 are comaximal, as are 𝔴rf and 𝔟f. Their product is (qf). The Chinese remainder theorem identifies C/(qf) with the product of the two quotient rings. Take e to be any lift of (1, 0). If 𝔴 is the only prime above q, then 𝔟 = C and one can take e = 1. Milne, Algebraic Number Theory, Theorem 3.7 and the Stacks Project, Chinese remainder theorem.

Modulo q, multiplication by e keeps the coordinate in C/𝔴r and kills the coordinate in C/𝔟. In particular e² ≡ e modulo q, e − 1 lies in 𝔴, and e lies in every other prime above q. When r > 1 the selected ring still contains nilpotents. The stronger congruences modulo the factors of qf will give the integral identity used to extract f Frobenius factors.

Which integral products connect the local parameter to Frobenius?

Local parameter and complementary power. With e as in the projector construction, choose a ∈ 𝔟f having valuation exactly one at 𝔴. Set d = ear−1. There are coefficients s, c, c′ ∈ C such that

a(1 − e) = qfs,   ad = qc,   c′ce ≡ e   (mod q).

Choosing a. The valuation v𝔴(x) of a nonzero element counts the power of 𝔴 in its principal ideal. Since nonzero ideals in a Dedekind domain are invertible, 𝔴² is strictly smaller than 𝔴. Otherwise cancellation of 𝔴 would give 𝔴 = C. Pick t in 𝔴 outside 𝔴². By the Chinese remainder theorem there is an a congruent to t modulo 𝔴² and to 0 modulo 𝔟f. It has the required valuation. Now a(1 − e) belongs to 𝔟f𝔴rf = (qf), giving s in the displayed formula. Milne, prime ideal factorization in Dedekind domains and the Stacks Project, Chinese remainder theorem.

Dividing by q. The element ad = ear belongs to 𝔴r because a ∈ 𝔴, and to 𝔟 because e ∈ 𝔟. These two ideals are comaximal, so their intersection is (q). This proves that c = ad/q is integral. At 𝔴 the numerator and denominator both have valuation exactly r, since e is a unit there. Hence c ∉ 𝔴. The ideals (c) and 𝔴r are comaximal, and Bézout’s identity supplies c′ with c′c ≡ 1 modulo 𝔴r. Multiplying by e makes both sides zero modulo 𝔟 as well. This proves the last congruence on precisely the selected factor.

Modulo q we also have ed = d, because e is idempotent there, and are = 0 by the second integral identity. In C/𝔴r, d has the class of ar−1. It generates the last nonzero power of the maximal ideal, with the zeroth power understood as the whole ring when r = 1. The two integral products enter different steps of the proof. The first controls the complementary Hodge summand in the Frobenius factorization. The second relates the coefficient action to multiplication by q and Verschiebung.

What are the two annihilators in the coefficient ring?

Write S = C/(q) and use the same letters for the images of e, a and d in S. For t ∈ S, let Ann(t) consist of the elements x ∈ S with tx = 0. The first equality below describes the image of the residue classes of 𝔴 under the projector. The next two identify the two kernels of multiplication.

e(𝔴/(q)) = aS,
Ann(d) = aS + (1 − e)S,   Ann(a) = dS + (1 − e)S.

Approximation by powers of a. In C, write (a) = 𝔴𝔟′ with 𝔟′ coprime to 𝔴. This follows from the exact valuation of a. For any nonnegative m, k, the sum (am) + 𝔴m+k equals 𝔴m, because factoring out 𝔴m leaves 𝔟′m + 𝔴k = C. Thus every x ∈ 𝔴m is a multiple of am modulo 𝔴m+k. This gives the same uniformizer calculation as in the discrete valuation ring C𝔴, with all the coefficients chosen in C. Milne, Algebraic Number Theory, Lemma 3.10 on prime-power quotients and localization.

For x ∈ 𝔴, take m = 1 and k = r − 1. We obtain x ≡ au modulo 𝔴r. On that factor e acts as 1. On the complementary factor both e and a vanish. Hence ex = au in S. Conversely every au comes from 𝔴 and is fixed by e, proving the first equality.

Multiplication by d. Suppose dx = 0 in S, and choose a representative of x in C. At 𝔴 the element d has valuation r − 1. If the representative were a unit there, the product would still have valuation r − 1 and could not vanish modulo 𝔴r. Thus x comes from 𝔴. The first equality supplies ex = au. Adding (1 − e)x gives the required decomposition of x. Conversely d kills aS since ad = 0 in S, and it kills (1 − e)S since ed = d. Its cohomological counterpart is the kernel relation used to find a separating eigenvector.

Multiplication by a. Suppose ax = 0 in S. Since a has valuation one, a representative of x belongs to 𝔴r−1. Apply the approximation with m = r − 1 and k = 1. It gives x ≡ ar−1u modulo 𝔴r, so ex = du in S. This is the projected annihilator relation used for the last Frobenius preimage. Restoring (1 − e)x proves the indicated containment in the full ring. Conversely a kills both d and 1 − e by the two integral product identities.

When r = 1 the selected factor is the residue field. In S we have a = 0 and d = e. The formulas give Ann(d) = (1 − e)S and Ann(a) = S, as direct multiplication confirms. All three equalities describe the ring acting on itself. Passing from these kernels to the coefficient action on de Rham classes requires the projectivity of de Rham homology over k ⊗ℤ C, where k is the field of the geometric point.
