How do different residual stabilizers fit inside one volume?

The residual group has a real factor G∞, a finite-adelic factor Gf, and a diagonal subgroup Γ of rational isometries. Let K be the compact open lattice stabilizer in Gf. For a finite representative d, the real group acting on that slice consists of the real components γ∞ of rational elements with γf in dKd−1. Call it Γd. It acts discretely. In any compact real set, the corresponding diagonal points belong to the product of that set with the compact dKd−1, which meets the discrete rational lattice in finitely many points. A rational matrix with identity real component is itself the identity, since a field embedding is injective.

Choose one d per finite double class and a measurable fundamental domain Fd for Γd in the real group. Such a domain exists by the measurable section theorem for a closed discrete subgroup. Consider the rectangles Fd × dK. To put a point in one of them, first move its finite coordinate into dK by a rational element. Next move its real coordinate into Fd using Γd, which preserves dK. Conversely, if a rational element takes a point in one rectangle to another, the finite coordinates force the same double class. Within that rectangle the real coordinates force γ∞ = 1, so γ = 1. The rectangles give one domain for the diagonal rational action.

μf(K) ∑d covol(Γd) ≤ vol(Γ ∖ (G∞ × Gf)).

Indeed each stabilizer covolume is at most the measure of its chosen real domain. Each finite-side translate dK has the same positive finite measure, and there are at most countably many, since K is open and the group of finite-adelic points is second countable in its topology. Multiplying by the common product of real gauge factors and dividing by μf(K) yields the normalized residual volume V₀ used in the flag comparison.
