Which sublattices of a line survive a discriminant cutoff?

Fix the rational line W perpendicular to one hyperplane. The bound on the perpendicular discriminant allows several orders for the lattice Q = L ∩ W. To sum over those possibilities, first keep a containing integral lattice A in W fixed. Let n(Q) = |Q∨/Q| and let X be a positive integer. Which full sublattices satisfy n(Q) ≤ X²?

Choose a nonzero vector in W as coordinate and write R for the integers of E. The coefficient modules of A and Q are fractional ideals J and I with I ⊆ J. Their quotient ideal 𝔞 = IJ−1 is integral and Q = 𝔞A. This works even when A has no single generator over R. Multiplying I and J by the same denominator makes them integral. The index is the ratio of their absolute norms and the common denominator cancels. Applying the index-square identity then determines the discriminant order from 𝔞. Milne, theorem 3.32 on invariant factors and proposition 4.2 on relative index.

[A ∶ Q] = N(𝔞),   n(Q) = N(𝔞)² n(A).

Since n(A) ≥ 1, it suffices to count ideals with N(𝔞) ≤ X. If D = [E∶ℚ], the count by prime factors gives at most klog₂ D ideals of norm k. There are at most X possible integers k, hence at most X1+log₂ D candidates inside A. This one sum includes all permitted discriminant orders.

Where does the first new lattice enter? Take the finite-unramified example E = ℚ(ζ₁₂) and the standard line A = R with Gram matrix (1). Here n(A) = 1. The integer ring is generated by ζ₁₂, whose polynomial is x⁴−x²+1. It reduces to (x²+x+1)² modulo 2 and (x²+1)² modulo 3. Both quadratics are irreducible in their respective residue fields. Thus there is no ideal of norm 2 or 3, and precisely one of norm 4, the kernel of reduction to the quadratic residue field at 2. Indeed an ideal of norm 4 must be prime, since a proper factor would have norm 2. Inside this fixed A, the bound X = 3 retains only A. Increasing X to 4 adds exactly one sublattice, at discriminant order 16. Milne, theorem 6.4 on cyclotomic integers.

What is the total mass of the eligible perpendicular lattices?

Now let the integral lattice vary over the entire fixed Hermitian line W, totally definite over a CM field of degree 2f and unramified over its real subfield at all finite places. For each order n, write mW(n) for the sum of 1/|Aut(Q)| over the isometry classes with n(Q) = n. Each Q has a maximal integral enlargement A. Its automorphism group permutes these enlargements. By counting the marked pairs (Q, A), we count the mass of each Q at least once. Grouping the pairs by A instead gives

∑1 ≤ n ≤ X² mW(n) ≤ ∑[A] maximal #{Q ⊆ A ∶ n(Q) ≤ X²} / |Aut(A)|.

The numerator counts actual full sublattices in each representative A. Partitioning them into Aut(A)-orbits gives the reciprocal stabilizer weights of the marked pairs. All maximal A in W belong to one genus by the local classification. In the maximal-lattice mass formula, a bad inert place of residue size q contributes (q+1)/(q+1) = 1 in rank one. The genus therefore has the same mass M₁ as the genus of the standard orthonormal line, even when W has a different determinant class. Multiplying this mass by the bound for one A gives the theorem, since 1 + log₂(2f) = 2 + log₂ f.

∑1 ≤ n ≤ X² mW(n) ≤ M₁ X2 + log₂ f.
Can one lattice really have two maximal enlargements?

Keep E = ℚ(ζ₁₂) and write ζ = ζ₁₂. Put α = 2−ζ, β = 2−ζ⁻¹ and γ = α/β. Conjugation exchanges α and β, so γ has Hermitian norm one. Thus R and γR are isometric self-dual lattices and both are maximal. They both contain Q = αR, since α = γβ. To see that the two enlargements are distinct, the norm of α is Φ₁₂(2) = 13. In R/(α) = 𝔽₁₃, the images of ζ and ζ⁻¹ are 2 and 7. Thus β has nonzero residue, so the ideals (α) and (β) differ. This rules out γR = R. Every scalar stabilizing Q fixes R as well, by cancelling α. Consequently the two choices of maximal enlargement give distinct marked pairs above the same unmarked lattice. Its discriminant order is 13².

In the row, the hyperplane lattice has discriminant order d² and the ambient lattice has order t². The perpendicular order is at most d²t². Taking X = dt gives the line factor M₁(dt)2+log₂ f in the marked hyperplane mass. The separate gluing factor counts the ways to put two chosen intersection lattices back together.
