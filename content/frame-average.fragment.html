How can the mean of the coefficients define a form at every point?

The previous average over central scalars produced a form ω′ with the original periods. Now we want a form invariant under every archimedean matrix. Let G = ∏w GLN(ℂ), let S = ∏w SLN(ℂ), and write Agh = (g−1)*h g−1 for their action on Hermitian matrices. The tangent spaces of the positive cone are copies of the same real vector space. At y ∈ S pull the form at AyI back to the identity. This gives an alternating multilinear form F(y) = ω′(AyI) ∘ Ay, with Ay applied to each tangent argument. It is left invariant under the arithmetic subgroup Λ = SLN(𝒪L′) in its archimedean embedding. Let D be a fundamental domain for Λ in S of positive finite Haar volume. The height moment makes F square-integrable there, hence integrable. We can now take an ordinary vector-valued mean in a fixed space. Why do two matrices taking the identity to the same point give the same answer?

The ambiguity at the identity is a tuple of unitary matrices u. At each place choose a unit-modulus Nth root of det u and divide by it. The resulting k belongs to S and acts exactly like u on Hermitian matrices. Hence F(y) ∘ Au = F(yk). Right translation by k preserves Haar measure on S. It also carries a fundamental domain to another one, since it commutes with left multiplication by Λ. Integrating a Λ-invariant integrable function over either domain gives the same integral. This proves M ∘ Au = M. Every positive Hermitian tuple can be written AgI by factoring each matrix in the tuple as B*B and choosing g = B−1. Two choices differ on the right by a stabilizer of I. The displayed transport rule therefore gives a single value at every point. The group law then makes ω̄ invariant under all of G, with constant moving-frame coefficients M. Check it on a matrix. For one complex place and degree one take M(V) = tr(V). Unitary conjugation preserves this real functional on Hermitian matrices. At h = AgI the transported value on V is tr(g*Vg) = tr(h−1V). For h = diag(4,9) and V = diag(4,0) it equals 1. Taking the upper-left entry instead would already fail at h = I.
