Which places must be tested on the same geometric tower?

Two places above ℓ have fixed roles from the start. The place w₀ carries the cuspidal type. The place v′ carries the weight test that will later be used in rank extraction. They have different restrictions to the maximal real subfield E⁺ and the same restriction to the quadratic field K. Assume the field and type hypotheses and sphericity at v′. In the application U is unramified at nonsplit places off a finite exceptional set, and spherical at split places whose restrictions to E⁺ avoid the type place and the specified exceptional places. To select the extra tests below, sphericity at each named place is given explicitly.

For a place in H we obtain an unramified auxiliary character, the group-side good-reduction condition, and the prescribed exponents for every embedding above it. For a place in C we obtain the polynomial for every multiset of spherical parameters there. Each row gives a single application of the level construction.

Why the choices satisfy the hypotheses. For the base choice, every place above ℓ is split over E⁺ by the field hypothesis, and −d is a square in ℚℓ. The two specified restrictions of v′ give the remaining compatibility. At an additional spherical place w away from ℓ, assume it is split over E⁺ and −d is a square at its residue characteristic. That characteristic differs from ℓ. Consequently w cannot lie above the same prime of E⁺ as w₀, and there is no common residue characteristic with v′ at which to compare quadratic roots.

For an additional spherical w above ℓ, assume its real-subfield restriction is different from both those of w₀ and v′ and that its restriction to K equals that of v′. Transitivity gives agreement with w₀ on K. This checks the common-root condition in each of the last two rows. The different real-subfield restrictions imply w ≠ v′, so H and C in the last row are disjoint.

To see the two restrictions separately, take a primitive eighth root ζ, put E = ℚ(ζ) and choose ℓ = 17. We have K = ℚ(ζ²) = ℚ(i) and E⁺ = ℚ(ζ + ζ−1) = ℚ(√2). The discriminants of the displayed quadratic generators are −4 and 8. At 17 their residues determine their primes by the quadratic residue calculation, whose trace argument applies also to the real quadratic generator. At the type place take ζ ≡ 2. The choice ζ ≡ −2 has the same square and the opposite real trace. The choice ζ ≡ 2−1 has the same real trace and the opposite square. There are just two primes of E⁺ above 17 in this example. They exhibit the choice of v′ relative to w₀. The extra w in the last two rows requires a third prime of E⁺ above ℓ. At 2 the polynomials (X + 1)⁴ + 1 and X² − 2 are Eisenstein. The local fields generated by ζ − 1 and √2 therefore have ramification indices four and two. Their relative index is two, so this example illustrates the local test at 17. A. V. Sutherland, Number Theory I, Lecture 11, Lemma 11.4 and Theorem 11.5. The level example computes the prime ideals.

Each row transfers as a whole to one tower under the geometric comparison hypotheses. Both of the last two towers retain the weight test at v′. Their levels, auxiliary characters and finite exceptional sets can be chosen separately. After the auxiliary character of each tower has been removed, the underlying spherical parameters again belong to the same U. The later comparison on a dense set of Frobenius elements uses this agreement. The large gaps in the weight enter when extracting a representation from cohomology.
