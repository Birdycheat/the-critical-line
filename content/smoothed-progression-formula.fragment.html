Why does a smoothed prime sum produce this list of zeros?

Fix a modulus m ≥ 3, a Dirichlet character χ modulo m, a height T ≥ 2 and a strip width 0 < κ ≤ 1/20. The sharp sum we seek is ψχ(y) = ∑n≤y Λ(n)χ(n), where Λ equals log p on positive powers of a prime p and vanishes elsewhere. Start with the continuous weight y/n − 1 on 1 ≤ n ≤ y, zero beyond that range. Call the weighted sum Sχ(y).

For Re s > 1, integrating (x − 1)x−s−1 from 1 to infinity gives 1/[s(s − 1)]. Mellin inversion recovers the weight at x = y/n. Multiplying by Λ(n)χ(n) and summing gives Perron’s integral for Sχ(y), with integrand (−L′/L)(s,χ)ys/[s(s − 1)]. On the initial vertical line Re s = 1 + 1/log y the Dirichlet series converges absolutely and the kernel decays quadratically with height. Mellin inversion, DLMF 2.5.2.

Move this line left to σ₁ between 1 − κ and 1 − 3κ/4 and truncate at a height T′ between T and T + 1. The left side and both heights are fixed before y varies. The right side moves with y but stays to the right of 1. All enclosed zeros therefore stay in the same list at both arguments of the difference. The local expansion of the logarithmic derivative bounds the number of zeros, counted with multiplicities, in the strip Re ρ ≥ 9/10, |Im ρ − t| ≤ 1 by C log(m(|t| + 2)). Adding these estimates along the strip bounds the number of zeros up to T + 3 by a constant times (T + 1)log(m(T + 2)). Exclude short intervals around their real parts and absolute ordinates when choosing the sides. Their total length leaves room for a clearance of at least κ/[C(T + 1)log(m(T + 2))].

Write Zχ for the zeros strictly inside the resulting rectangle and aρ for their orders of vanishing. Every listed zero satisfies 1 − κ < Re ρ < 1 and |Im ρ| ≤ T + 1. Every zero with Re ρ ≥ 1 − κ/2 and |Im ρ| ≤ T is included. Between those two boundaries the list may depend on the chosen rectangle. At a zero of order aρ, −L′/L has residue −aρ. To read the term at 1, put Fχ(s) = (s − 1)L(s,χ) with its value continued at 1 for the principal character, and Fχ = L(·,χ) otherwise. Then Fχ(1) ≠ 0. Put Kχ = −F′χ(1)/Fχ(1). The residue at 1 contributes yKχ. For the principal character there is also the double pole from the kernel times 1/(s − 1). Differentiating ys/s at 1 gives y(log y − 1). The residue theorem equates the sum of these contributions to the integral around the rectangle. Dividing by y gives the following identity for every y ≥ 2. Residue theorem, DLMF 1.10.8.

Sχ(y)/y = Kχ + 𝟙χ principal(log y − 1) − ∑ρ∈Zχ aρyρ−1/[ρ(ρ − 1)] + Rχ(y),
|Rχ(y)| ≤ C[(mT)Cy−κ/2 + (log y)(log(mT))²/T].

Here C depends only on κ. On the three new sides the local logarithmic-derivative estimate costs a factor of at most a constant depending on κ times T(log(mT))², apart from the known principal pole. Each horizontal segment gains T′−2 from the kernel. The omitted tails of the original line gain 1/T′ after integrating the inverse square. These give the second error. The left side has length 2T′, and after division by y it contains yσ₁−1 ≤ y−3κ/4. Its cost fits inside a constant times (mT)⁴y−κ/2. Enlarging C gives the displayed bound. The same local counts give the multiplicity estimate used in the count of zero strips.

How does the difference recover the sharp prime count?

Choose 0 < δ ≤ ½, put u = (1 + δ)y and b = yu/(u − y) = y(1 + δ)/δ. Multiply the difference Sχ(u)/u − Sχ(y)/y by b. For every n ≤ y, the two weights differ by 1/y − 1/u = 1/b. Thus these terms contribute exactly ψχ(y). An integer in (y,u] contributes with weight b(1/n − 1/u) between zero and one. Their total in absolute value is at most (δy + 1)log(2y).

Check the weights at 4, 5 and 6

Take the principal character modulo 3, y = 4 and u = 6. Then b = 12. Every integer up to 4 has weight one in the scaled difference. Only 2 and 4 contribute, giving 2 log 2. At 5 the weight is 12(1/5 − 1/6) = 2/5. At 6 it is zero. The difference equals 2 log 2 + (2/5)log 5, so its entire discrepancy from ψχ(4) comes from the short interval.

The same difference cancels Kχ exactly. The principal logarithm becomes b log(1 + δ), within δy of y since δ/(1 + δ) ≤ log(1 + δ) ≤ δ. For a zero ρ the scaled kernel difference is yρ/ρ times Q = ((1 + δ)/δ)((1 + δ)ρ−1 − 1)/(ρ − 1). To bound Q − 1, differentiate (xρ−1 − 1)/(ρ − 1) + 1/x − 1. Its derivative is (xρ − 1)/x². For 1 ≤ x ≤ 1 + δ, integration of ρxρ−1 gives |xρ − 1| ≤ |ρ|(x − 1) because Re ρ < 1. Integrating again and multiplying by (1 + δ)/δ gives |Q − 1| ≤ 2δ|ρ|. This cancels the divisor |ρ| in the error, giving at most 2δyRe ρ per occurrence.
