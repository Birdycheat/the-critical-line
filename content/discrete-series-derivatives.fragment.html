How large can an archimedean derivative be?

At order P, let VP be the span of vectors obtained from tensors of lowest compact-type vectors by applying at most P derivatives. A derivative in direction X is ∂Xw = (d/dt)σ(exp(tX))w at t = 0. A direction supported at one real place v belongs to the Lie algebra of its unitary group. Write ‖X‖HS for its Hilbert–Schmidt norm. Use the indefinite counting hypotheses. In particular n ≥ 8, the representations are unitary discrete series, and each coordinate of the Harish-Chandra parameter λ at each real place satisfies |λᵢ| ≤ b².

Derivative bound. The spaces VP are finite dimensional and preserved by the product of the maximal compact groups. Their vectors are differentiable along every real one-parameter subgroup, and one derivative takes VP into VP+1. For w ∈ VP and any one-place direction,

‖∂Xw‖ ≤ ‖X‖HS βP ‖w‖,   βP = √n b² + (3/2)n3/2 + 3P.

Here is why the dependence on P is linear. At one place write Eij for the operators representing the matrix units in the complexified Lie algebra. The compact roots eᵢ−eⱼ join two coordinates with the same signature sign. The noncompact roots join opposite signs. Let ρc and ρn be the half sums of the respective positive roots (i < j). The starting compact highest weight is Λ = λ + ρn − ρc. The counting hypotheses separate opposite-sign parameter coordinates by a common gap h ≥ 120nq3/2, where q is the real Witt index. The lowest-type multiplicity and infinitesimal-character theorems identify this compact type and give the scalar of the full quadratic Casimir operator on its generated module. Harris, Kobayashi and Speh, §2.3, formula (2.24).

Moving a compact generator past a noncompact one adds their commutator, which is again a sum of noncompact generators. Thus words of length at most P lie in the compact-stable space obtained by applying at most P noncompact generators to the starting compact type. In one such step, the tensor-product rule with the off-diagonal block permits the old compact highest weight or a shift by a root joining opposite signs. The root eᵢ−eⱼ has Euclidean norm √2. By compact-type decomposition and induction, every highest weight μ seen by order P satisfies ‖μ−Λ‖ ≤ √2P. This bound on possible compact types holds for the entire span of words.

How does a bound on weights control every vector?

In matrix-unit coordinates let Ω be the sum of Eᵢⱼ Eⱼᵢ over all pairs and let ΩK be the same sum over same-sign pairs. On the common space of smooth vectors, unitarity gives Eᵢⱼ* = Eⱼᵢ for equal signs and Eᵢⱼ* = −Eⱼᵢ for opposite signs. Summing the squared norms therefore gives the energy identity

∑i,j ‖Eᵢⱼ w‖² = Re ⟨w, (2ΩK−Ω)w⟩.

On a compact type μ the scalar of ΩK is ‖μ+ρc‖²−‖ρc‖². On the generated module the scalar of Ω is ‖λ‖²−‖ρc+ρn‖². Put L = ‖λ‖ and A = ‖ρn‖ + √2P. The preceding weight bound gives ‖μ+ρc‖ ≤ L + A. Since the compact action is unitary, its finite-dimensional space splits orthogonally into compact types. The same upper bound for the compact Casimir quadratic form then applies to their sum, including linear combinations w.

The full half sum has coordinates (n+1−2i)/2 for 1 ≤ i ≤ n, giving squared norm n(n²−1)/12. Each coordinate of ρn has absolute value at most n/2. Thus ‖ρn‖ ≤ n3/2/2, and L ≤ √n b². For a unit vector the energy is consequently at most

2(L+A)²−L²+n³/12 ≤ (L+2A+0.3n3/2)² ≤ βP².

For the first inequality, (L+2A)² already exceeds 2(L+A)²−L² by 2A², and 0.3² ≥ 1/12. For the second, 2√2 ≤ 3 and 1 + 0.3 ≤ 3/2. This proves a bound on the sum of the squared generator norms. Applying Cauchy–Schwarz to ∂Xw = ∑ Xᵢⱼ Eᵢⱼ w gives the one-place estimate. Tensoring with the other places preserves the squared bound, as one sees by expanding in an orthonormal basis of the other factors. For simultaneous directions X = (Xᵥ), the derivative is the sum of the one-place derivatives. The product rule, the triangle inequality and Cauchy–Schwarz therefore give

‖∂Xw‖ ≤ √f (∑v ‖Xᵥ‖²HS)1/2 βP ‖w‖.

The descent in the cusp starts at order P₀ = 8qN, where N = n²f, and uses the simultaneous-direction bound. The Sobolev estimate at the starting order needs up to N more derivatives, each in a unit direction supported at a single place. Since βP increases with P, β = βP₀+N works for both steps. For example, the dimensions n = 8, f = q = 1 give N = 64 and P₀ = 512. Taking one constant through order 576 contributes 1728 to β from differentiations. This example counts orders and leaves the parameter gap and the bound b in force.
