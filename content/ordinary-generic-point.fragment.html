What does a deformation at the generic point of the special fibre remember?

The residue-point identity is tested over a generic point of a component of the special fibre. The local ring there lets us compare an abelian variety with an infinitesimal deformation of it. Both come from one object over a field, although the two maps from that field can differ.

Factorization at the generic point. Let R be a discrete valuation ring with maximal ideal 𝔪 and let X be smooth over R. Write Xs for its special fibre and let z be the generic point of an irreducible component of Xs. Suppose a morphism v ∶ Spec T → X from a local ring sends the closed point to z, and the structure map R → T kills 𝔪. Then v factors through Spec κ(z), where κ(z) is the residue field at z.

Proof. The assumption on 𝔪 puts the whole test scheme in the special fibre. Its local ring at z is 𝒪X,z/𝔪𝒪X,z. Smoothness is preserved by base change, so Xs is smooth over the residue field and hence reduced. In an affine neighbourhood of z in this fibre, the prime defining z is minimal. Localizing at it leaves just one prime ideal. That prime is the nilradical, which is zero because localization preserves reducedness. A local ring with zero maximal ideal is a field. Thus

𝒪X,z/𝔪𝒪X,z = 𝒪Xs,z ≅ κ(z).

The map v corresponds to the local homomorphism 𝒪X,z → T. This homomorphism kills 𝔪𝒪X,z, so it descends to the displayed field. Taking spectra gives the claimed factorization. The Stacks Project, smooth schemes over a field are geometrically reduced and morphisms from the spectrum of a local ring.

In the application the test point first lies on a space flat over the underlying moduli scheme. Flat morphisms lift generalizations. Any generalization of its image within the special fibre would lift to another generalization in the special fibre upstairs. The original point is already generic in its component, so the lifted point, and hence its image, must be unchanged. This permits the same argument on the moduli scheme. The Stacks Project, going down for flat ring maps.

An example with dual numbers. Take the affine line over ℤ(5) and the generic point of its special fibre. Its residue field is 𝔽5(t). Take k = 𝔽5(u) and let ε² = 0. The map t ↦ u + ε gives a point with values in k[ε]. Every nonzero denominator b(t) has invertible image because its reduction b(u) is nonzero. Thus the assignment extends to the whole residue field. Reducing modulo ε gives t ↦ u. Both points factor through 𝔽5(t), and the coefficient of ε still distinguishes them.

Why is the geometric generic point ordinary at the chosen place?

Generic ordinarity theorem. Let R be a discrete valuation ring of characteristic zero and residue characteristic q. Take the smooth integral moduli scheme M for the unitary datum with algebra degree m = 1 and principal level N ≥ 3 prime to q. Choose one of the coefficient maps 𝒪F → R of the datum. Its inverse image of the maximal ideal is a prime 𝔴. Write Q = |𝒪F/𝔴| = qf. The prime contains q, so it is nonzero, maximal, and f ≥ 1. Let y ∶ Spec k → M be a point over an algebraically closed field with image the generic point z of a special-fibre component. For the abelian variety A at y and the rank parameter n,

#A[𝔴](k) = Qn−1.

Here A[𝔴](k) consists of the geometric points killed by every element of 𝔴. This cardinality is the meaning of ordinarity at 𝔴 in the congruence argument. For example, when Q = 5 and n = 3, the total rank is 125. Translation gives every point the same local length. The theorem says that length is 5, so there are 125/5 = 25 geometric points. A local length of 25 would give only five points. The deformation below rules out this longer local ring at the generic point. Describing how kernels above these points reduce is the further geometric input to the ordinary subspace lemma.

The alternative if the count fails. The local length calculation and the action of Frobenius give the alternative used in the deformation argument below. Let P be the space of primitive classes in first de Rham cohomology of A, and let L be its intersection with the first Hodge filtration. Primitive means that pullback by the group law is the sum of the pullbacks by the two projections. We work on the dual H = P∨, with Hodge subspace Fil = L⊥. Write Φ for the q-semilinear Frobenius on P and ρ for the coefficient action there. Reduction at the chosen embedding gives the character χ ∶ 𝒪F → k with kernel 𝔴.

The local parameter calculation chooses coefficients e and d. The action of e projects to the factor at 𝔴 modulo q, and ed = d modulo q. The torsion and Frobenius calculation gives the following alternative. Either the stated count holds, or

ρ(e)L ⊆ W, W = spank{ρ(d)Φf(v) ∣ v ∈ P}.

This implication uses the local structure of the finite 𝔴-torsion scheme. If the point count fails, the local coordinate at the identity gives a nonzero tangent vector with a lift through the Qth-power map. Comparing Frobenius with the endomorphism on the 𝔴-factor then constructs a preimage of each projected Hodge class. In the alternative under consideration the nonzero projected Hodge line lies inside W. Its vanishing under Φ detects a loss of rank. The separating eigenvector lemma uses that loss and the coefficient annihilator relations to produce a simultaneous χ-eigenvector y0 in the image of ρ(d) outside W. Given y0, extend a basis of W by this vector and choose a functional ξ vanishing on W with ξ(y0) = 1. Put x = ξ ∘ ρ(e). Since ρ(e) fixes both W and y0 pointwise, x still vanishes on W and x(y0) = 1. For each l ∈ L, the inclusion gives ρ(e)l ∈ W, so x(l) = ξ(ρ(e)l) = 0. This is exactly x ∈ Fil. Idempotence also gives x ∘ ρ(e) = x.

A deformation that moves the Hodge subspace. Let φ be the nondegenerate skew-symmetric de Rham pairing from the polarization. It represents evaluation at y0 by a vector α in H, so φ(x, α) = 1. The local Lie-algebra calculation supplies a vector b outside Fil in the image of the dual projector. Its pairing with x is zero. Indeed, the quadratic generator acts by the chosen root on both projected vectors, and adjointness for conjugation gives the other root on the opposite side of the pairing. The two roots are distinct in the residue field. Define

D(v) = φ(v, α)b + φ(v, b)α, D(x) = b ∉ Fil.

The polarized direction lemma checks the infinitesimal moduli conditions. The coefficient actions are adjoint under conjugation, and the eigenvector relation for y0 makes D commute with these actions on Fil modulo Fil. The two quadratic root spaces give the prescribed Lie relation. For the polarization, the two summands cancel in φ(Dv, w) + φ(v, Dw) by skew-symmetry. Realizing the Hodge graph with its order action, polarization and level marking gives a deformation over T = k[ε]/(ε²). In the crystalline comparison with the special point the lifted filtration has an element with constant term x and first-order coefficient D(x). The coset of this coefficient in H/Fil measures the motion of the filtration.

Why the same coefficient must vanish modulo Fil. Representability gives a morphism Spec T → M reducing to y. Both maps kill the maximal ideal of R. The generic-point lemma factors them through κ(z). Thus A and its deformation are pullbacks of the same marked abelian variety over κ(z). The Frobenius-span inclusion descends from k to κ(z) and then extends to T. For descent, de Rham base change identifies the filtration and the coefficient actions, and extension of fields detects membership in a subspace. For ascent, the pulled-back Hodge subspace is generated by pullbacks of its elements, and Frobenius commutes with these pullbacks. Using the common residue field is essential here because its two maps can send elements to different first-order lifts.

In characteristic q, (a + εb)q = aq. Because f ≥ 1, at least one Frobenius occurs in every generator of W. Under the crystalline comparison, its value against the constant lift of x is the constant lift of its value at the special point, hence zero. The inclusion over T shows that the functional obtained by applying the dual projector to this lift annihilates the lifted first Hodge subspace on the primitive classes. It therefore belongs to the lifted filtration on the dual. On constant classes it evaluates as x ∘ ρ(e) = x with first-order coefficient zero. This constructs a second lift of x in the very same filtration.

For a fixed x, two possible first-order coefficients of a filtered lift differ by an element of Fil. Indeed the difference of the two lifts has zero constant term and lies in ε Fil under the Hodge comparison. Our two coefficients are D(x) and 0. Their equality in H/Fil contradicts D(x) = b ∉ Fil. The second branch of the alternative is impossible at z. This proves the point count, with the local torsion calculation, the coefficient-space calculation and the unitary deformation and crystalline comparison theorems as its geometric inputs.
