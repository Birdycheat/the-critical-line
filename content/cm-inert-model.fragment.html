Building the curve at an inert prime

The local theta estimate needs an elliptic curve with good reduction and a coordinate over the same ring in which to compare its formal logarithm with the height-two logarithm. Fix a prime p ≥ 5 with (−d/p) = −1 and an embedding of the algebraic numbers into ℂp. Write U for the completed maximal unramified extension of ℚp inside ℂp and W for its ring of integers. The curve construction works for this embedding and every permitted curve ideal. The global theorem uses it at inert-class and signal primes. Level freeness is an additional condition when the resulting theta estimate is applied to matrix entries. Let 𝔠 be the nonzero ideal in the imaginary quadratic field K = ℚ(√−d), with norm prime to 2d. In the chosen complex embedding write Λ = δ𝔠, where δ12 = Δ(𝔠) and Δ denotes the lattice discriminant. The local construction first needs the ideal norm prime to p. Inertness makes (p) a prime ideal, so we can factor 𝔠 = (p)k𝔠′ with p coprime to the norm of 𝔠′. Take δ′ = pkδ. Then δ′𝔠′ = Λ exactly, and Δ(𝔠) = p−12kΔ(𝔠′) gives (δ′)12 = Δ(𝔠′). The theta section will see the same lattice. Obtaining a good equation. Let J be the embedded j-invariant of Λ. The CM integrality theorem gives v(J) ≥ 0, where v(p) = 1. To put J in U, the proof shows inertia fixes it. A finite set of primes is discarded so that distinct roots of a monic integral polynomial for J remain distinct after reduction. Chebotarev gives a degree-one Frobenius with the same action as a specified inertia element on these roots and the same Artin class. This class is trivial on inertia. The CM Frobenius congruence at the resulting principal prime identifies the reduction of the Frobenius image with that of J. Separation makes the two roots equal. When J differs from 0 and 1728, two CM valuation theorems give integers a,b ≥ 0 with v(J) = 3a and v(J − 1728) = 2b. Set C = 27J/(J − 1728), t = pb−a, and put

G₂ = t²C,   G₃ = t³C,   G₂³ − 27G₃² = t⁶C²(C − 27).

Since 27 and 1728 are units, v(C) = 3a − 2b and v(C − 27) = −2b. The two invariants therefore have valuations a,b, while the discriminant has valuation 6(b − a) + 2(3a − 2b) − 2b = 0. This gives the equation y² = x³ − (G₂/4)x − G₃/4 over W, with nonsingular reduction. For J = 0 and 1728 the corresponding invariant pairs are (0,1) and (1,0). In each case a change of scale over ℂp identifies the equation with the original curve. The normalized lattice has discriminant one, so this scale has norm one. The same comparison proves the normalized period invariants algebraic, ensuring that extending the given embedding to the complex numbers respects their original images. Obtaining a coordinate over the same ring. Let LE be the curve’s formal logarithm, eE its compositional inverse and σ the unramified Frobenius. Put ℓ(T) = ∑j≥0 Tp2j/pj. Inertness exchanges the CM generator ω = (1 + √−d)/2 and 1 − ω under σ. Their difference is a unit and σ² fixes ω. Using the integral CM endomorphism and a unit coefficient of multiplication by p in degree at most p², the formal-group theorem gives a unit c such that LE(T) − (c/p) · (σ²LE)(Tp²) has integral coefficients. Here σ acts on the coefficients. Choose a unit ε with ε = cσ²(ε). Dividing the logarithm by ε now gives a functional equation with factor 1/p, the same factor seen in ℓ(T) − ℓ(Tp²)/p = T. The comparison theorem gives

Θ(T) = eE(εℓ(T)) ∈ W[[T]],   Θ(0) = 0,   Θ′(0) = ε.

This Θ is a formal change of coordinate. Because its linear coefficient is a unit, its inverse also has coefficients in W. Substitution gives ℓ(Θ−1(T)) = ε−1LE(T), the isomorphism required by the genus-one datum. Check the ideal and the Frobenius for discriminant −23 at 5

Take K = ℚ(√−23) and ω = (1 + √−23)/2. Its integer ring is ℤ[ω], with ω² − ω + 6 = 0. Modulo 5, the discriminant −23 is 2, a nonsquare. Thus (5) is inert and has norm 25, coprime to 46. Start with 𝔠 = (5) and δ12 = Δ(5𝒪K). The replacement is 𝔠′ = 𝒪K, δ′ = 5δ. Both choices give exactly the lattice 5δ𝒪K. In the residue field ℤ[ω]/(5) one has ω² = ω − 1, hence ω³ = −1 and ω⁵ = 1 − ω. Arithmetic Frobenius really does exchange the roots, while (1 − 2ω)² ≡ 2 mod 5 verifies the unit difference. The first terms of the matching logarithm are T + T25/5 + T625/25.
