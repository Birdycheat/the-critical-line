Which lattice realises both finite coordinates?

The two finite readings recover a lattice from its localizations. Given the coordinates in advance, we now construct a lattice realising them. Let Λ₀ be the reference integral lattice, V = Λ₀ ⊗ ℚ and Λ̂⁽ᵖ⁾ the product of its completions at primes different from the distinguished prime p. The coordinate at p specifies an order-stable lattice M over ℤ(p) inside V. This means that M is generated over ℤ(p) by finitely many vectors spanning V over ℚ. The away-from-p coordinate specifies an adelic linear map T with left inverse S. It commutes with the order action. Write r(w) = T(1 ⊗ w) for its restriction to V.

L = {w ∈ M : r(w) ∈ Λ̂⁽ᵖ⁾},   L ⊗ ℤ(p) = M,   L[1/p] = r−1(Λ̂⁽ᵖ⁾).

Proof. For w ∈ M, the finite adele r(w) has only finitely many nonintegral coordinates. One positive integer prime to p clears all their denominators, putting that multiple of w in L. Conversely M is already closed under division by integers prime to p. For the other equality, every rational w has some multiple pkw in M since the generators span. If r(w) is integral away from p, this multiple is in L. The reverse implication uses that p is a unit at every place in Λ̂⁽ᵖ⁾. Since M spans V, the first equality gives the full rational span of L. The order preserves L because it preserves both local lattices and commutes with T. Finally, a single positive integer d carries L into Λ₀. Multiplication by d thus identifies L with a subgroup of a finite free abelian group, so L is finite free.

Why does one denominator work for the entire lattice?

Choose fixed positive integers k and j so that k clears the denominators of the chosen generators of M in Λ₀, and jS carries Λ̂⁽ᵖ⁾ into itself. For each w ∈ L there are integers m prime to p and b ≥ 0 for which kmw and pbjw belong to Λ₀. The latter assertion follows from Sr(w) = 1 ⊗ w by reading the rational coordinates in an integral basis. Use Bézout’s identity for m and pb to deduce kjw ∈ Λ₀. Thus d = kj works for every w. For completeness, a subgroup of ℤr is finite free by induction on r. Its image on the last coordinate is an ideal of ℤ. If this ideal is nonzero, lift its positive generator to split off a copy of ℤ. The kernel lies in ℤr−1.

How can the markings at every level be chosen together?

The finite approximation theorem makes the rational images r(w) meet each coset modulo nΛ̂⁽ᵖ⁾. Here only positive n prime to p are needed. One can choose additive maps fn : Λ₀ → L whose reductions are compatible, order-linear isomorphisms Λ₀/nΛ₀ → L/nL. The marking at a prescribed admissible level N is t = fN. In the first congruence a also denotes the diagonal image of the label. The second holds for all positive integers n and m with gcd(nm,p) = 1.

r(fn(a)) ≡ a (mod nΛ̂⁽ᵖ⁾),   fnm(a) ≡ fn(a) (mod nL).

Construction. Choose a rational w with r(w) ≡ a modulo nΛ̂⁽ᵖ⁾. This already makes r(w) integral away from p. Take k with pkw ∈ M. For n > 1, multiplication by p permutes the φ(n) units modulo n. Cancelling their product gives pφ(n) ≡ 1. The exponent b = kφ(n) is at least k, so pbw lies in L and has the required residue. For n = 1 both quotient groups are zero and one may take f1 = 0. Make these choices for an integral basis of Λ₀ and extend additively to obtain fn.

The intersection criterion detects the kernel of the residue map. For u ∈ L, the vector u/n already belongs to M because n is prime to p. Thus u ∈ nL exactly when r(u) ∈ nΛ̂⁽ᵖ⁾. Each residue therefore has at most one lift in L/nL. The Chinese remainder theorem identifies Λ₀/nΛ₀ with all the residues in Λ̂⁽ᵖ⁾/nΛ̂⁽ᵖ⁾, and the construction lifts each of them. This proves bijectivity. Acting on a lift by an element of the order, or reducing a lift from level nm to level n, gives the prescribed residue in each case. Uniqueness gives order-linearity and compatibility.

A lift at levels five and ten

In a rational symplectic plane with Λ₀ = ℤ² and the usual determinant pairing, take p = 3, T = diag(1/2,2) away from 3, and M = 3ℤ(3)e₁ ⊕ (1/3)ℤ(3)e₂. The two integrality tests give L = 6ℤe₁ ⊕ (1/6)ℤe₂. In this basis T sends its generators to 3e₁ and e₂/3. For n prime to 3 take cn with 3cn ≡ 1 modulo n. Choose fn(e₁) = 6cne₁ and fn(e₂) = e₂/2. Choosing c₅ = 2 and c₁₀ = 7 gives the different first marks 12e₁ and 42e₁. Their difference is 5(6e₁), as required in L. Relative to the indicated bases the integral matrices are diag(2,3) and diag(7,3). The determinants 6 and 21 are units modulo 5 and 10, respectively, while the images of the integral maps have indices 6 and 21 in L.

Which integral alternating form carries the markings?

Let B be the prescribed rational alternating nondegenerate pairing, adjoint for the order involution. Use the common positive rational scale s₁ for which M is its own dual under s₁B over ℤ(p). The comparison away from p gives s₁B(u,w) as ε times the reference pairing E₀(r(u),r(w)), with both ε and ε−1 integral away from p. For u,w ∈ L, this rational number is integral at p by the first condition and away from p by the second. Thus E = s₁B|L is integer-valued. The polarised lattice uses E′ = 2E, in keeping with the reference convention 2E₀. Alternation and adjointness restrict from B. If E′(u,L) = 0, the rational span of L forces B(u,V) = 0, so u = 0.

E′ = 2s₁B|L,   E(fn(a),fn(b)) ≡ νnE₀(a,b) (mod n).

Here νn is an integer representing ε modulo n away from p. The inverse of ε supplies an inverse residue, and reduction from nm to n gives the same multiplier. Expanding the bilinear pairing of two lifted residues proves the displayed congruence in the completed lattice. Both sides are integers, so divisibility there implies divisibility in ℤ. Multiplying the congruence by two gives the required comparison of the full alternating forms, even at even levels. In the example at 5 and 10, take s₁ = ε = 1. The doubled pairings of the two marked generators are 12 and 42. Both reduce to the reference value 2 at their respective levels.

The half-form is perfect after localization at p. More precisely, a single integer d prime to p carries every integral functional on L into the image of E. This is the condition required also at p = 2, where doubling the form need not preserve perfection.

One multiplier for the integral dual

Extend the dual basis of L to rational functionals and represent each by a vector under the nondegenerate form s₁B. Each such vector belongs to M since its functional is integral on L and therefore p-integral on L ⊗ ℤ(p) = M. Multiplying the finitely many representatives by integers prime to p puts them in L. The product of these integers works for the whole dual basis, hence for every integral functional.

The maps fn and the multipliers νn now give the required markings of the polarised lattice at every level prime to p. In particular the prescribed pair of finite coordinates occurs in the classification of complex points.
