Reading the four coordinates of a line stabilizer from its matrix

Fix a hyperbolic pair e, e′ with pairing 1, and write W for its nondegenerate orthogonal complement of dimension s. In the order (e, e′, W) the Gram matrix has a hyperbolic two-by-two block and the Hermitian matrix J of W. A bar denotes the conjugation of the quadratic CM extension E/F, extended to the adèles. The form on columns is H(b,c) = b*Jc. We want to integrate over all isometries carrying e to a scalar multiple of itself. We first need coordinates which describe each such isometry exactly once.

Choose a nonzero δ ∈ E with δ̄ = −δ, for instance the difference of an element outside F and its conjugate. A unipotent element fixes e, sends e′ to e′ + b + y e, and sends x ∈ W to x − H(b,x)e. Orthogonality with the image of e′ forces the last correction. Isotropy of that image forces y + ȳ = −H(b,b). Thus y = −H(b,b)/2 + δz for a unique z ∈ 𝔸F. Call this isometry n(b,z). Let ι(U) act by U on W and fix the pair. Let m(a) act by a on e, by ā−1 on e′ and by the identity on W.

Ψ(b,z,U,a) = n(b,z) ι(U) m(a),   b ∈ 𝔸Es,   z ∈ 𝔸F,   U ∈ U(W)(𝔸F),   a ∈ 𝔸E×.

Recover the coordinates. For an isometry g stabilizing the line, the first column gives a through ge = ae. Pairing it with ge′ fixes the e′ coordinate of ge′ at ā−1. Hence āge′ = e′ + b + y e gives b and y, and the preceding trace equation recovers z. After removing n(b,z) on the left and m(a) on the right, the isometry fixes both vectors, so its restriction to W gives U. All recovery operations are continuous. In particular the matrix displays both a and ā−1, as required by the topology on the idèles. The image is closed because all entries below the first entry in the first column vanish, and the unitary pairing makes that first entry invertible. These coordinates therefore give a homeomorphism onto the stabilizer and carry Borel rectangles to measurable sets.

Where the centre changes. Apply the product of two unipotents to e′. The two horizontal columns add, and the coefficient of e picks up −H(b,b′). Removing the real part prescribed by the new column leaves

n(b,z) n(b′,z′) = n(b+b′, z+z′ + [H(b′,b)−H(b,b′)]/(2δ)).

For the positive line over ℚ(i) take δ = i, b = 1, b′ = i and both central coordinates zero. The product has horizontal column 1+i and central coordinate −1. Reversing the factors gives the same column and central coordinate +1. This is why a reduction by rational elements adjusts the horizontal coordinate before the central one.

Which density makes this product of measures left invariant?

Take additive Haar measures db and dz in the first two variables, a left Haar measure dU on the smaller group, and multiplicative Haar measure d×a on the idèles. Write νN = db dz. Conjugation by m(a) sends the pair (b,z) to La(b,z) = (āb, aāz). Indeed the coefficient of e gains aā and its forced real part changes by that same factor. Define χ by pushforward, (La)∗νN = χ(a)νN. Thus χ measures the preimage of a fixed box. If the image of a box doubles in volume, the pushforward measure of that box halves.

Let |·|𝔸F denote the product of the normalized local absolute values. Over F, multiplication by ā on a copy of E has determinant aā, its field norm. On the centre multiplication by aā has the same determinant in one F-coordinate. The change of variables formula over a local field therefore gives χ(a) = |aā|𝔸F−(s+1). This works at split places as well, where the norm is the product of the two entries. At all but finitely many finite places the idèle is a local unit, so the factor is 1. This gives a positive continuous character on the idèles. Multiplying the additive Haar measures by constants leaves χ the same.

dνP = Ψ∗(db dz dU χ(a) d×a).

To verify left invariance, integrate a nonnegative test function. Left multiplication by n(b₀,z₀) translates the central coordinate by a quantity depending on b. Integrating first in z removes this translation, and translating b removes b₀. Left multiplication by ι(U₀) sends b to U₀b and U to U₀U. The first change preserves additive Haar because det(U₀) times its conjugate is 1, and the second preserves dU. Finally left multiplication by m(a₀) acts by La₀ on the first two variables and by a ↦ a₀a on the last. In the integral the first substitution contributes χ(a₀). Setting a′ = a₀a changes the density to χ(a′)/χ(a₀). The factors cancel. These three kinds of multiplication generate the whole stabilizer by the coordinate formula. Positivity of χ gives positive measure to every open identity neighbourhood, and continuity gives finite measure on compact sets.

What changes when the shell moves one step?

Write τ(x) for the idèle with multiplier exp(x) at each infinite place and multiplier 1 at each finite place. Let d = [F : ℚ]. There are d real places of F and a complex place of E above each one. In the horizontal coordinate τ(x) dilates 2ds real directions by exp(x). In the centre its norm dilates d real directions by exp(2x). Taking the reciprocal of the two volume changes gives χ(τ(x)) = exp(−2d(s+1)x).

Fix compact additive representatives Cb, Cz, a measurable fundamental domain 𝒟W for the smaller unitary quotient, and the compact set ω of idèle representatives modulo the rational scalars and the integer flow. Define the jth shell Sj as the image under Ψ of these first three sets and τ(j)ω. Because Ψ is injective the measure of this image equals its coordinate integral. Multiplicative Haar measure allows the substitution a = τ(j)c. Only the scalar range moves, giving the exact identity

νP(Sj) = exp(−2d(s+1)j) · vol(Cb) vol(Cz) vol(𝒟W) ∫ω χ(c) d×c.

The equality applies to each coordinate rectangle. Rational translates of these rectangles cover almost every point and may overlap, so their sum gives an upper bound in the unfolding argument. The finite covolume theorem applies to the same dU used in the product. Replacing it by αdU multiplies both sides by α. Bounding the integral over ω by its multiplicative volume times the maximum of χ gives the constant used in the shell upper bound. Along the integer shells the mass gains a factor exp(−2d(s+1)) at each step. By comparison, translating the entire box by m(τ(1)) also dilates its additive ranges. Their growing volume cancels the density change, as left invariance requires.

For the Gaussian example with s = d = 1 the normalized smaller covolume is 1/4. Write A for the two additive volumes times the integral of χ on ω. The initial rectangle then has mass A/4. At real time log 2 its scalar range moves to τ(log 2)ω and its mass becomes A/64. Translating the whole rectangle preserves its original mass A/4.

The count of nonzero rational scalars on an isotropic line increases the upper bound by at most a constant times exp(2d(j+log r)) above its cutoff, for dilation parameter r > 0. Multiplying by the shell mass leaves a geometric tail with ratio exp(−2ds) for s ≥ 1. For shells below the cutoff the counting function is zero. This is where the complementary rank enters convergence. The scalar lattice itself has real dimension 2d. The scalar count and its threshold · The summed shell bound.
