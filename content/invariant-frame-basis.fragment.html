Why do independently chosen period witnesses agree?

Fix one integral frame of rank d and height N over a finite extension K/ℚℓ, for an action ρ of its absolute Galois group GK on a free ℤℓ-module of rank d. Let 𝔖 = W(k)[[u]], where k is the residue field. The Eisenstein polynomial E of the chosen uniformizer identifies 𝔖/(E) with 𝒪K. Evaluation in Ainf sends E to a generator η of the kernel of Fontaine’s map θ to 𝒪ℂℓ. Write δ(g) = φ(ε(g)), the entrywise Witt Frobenius of the frame’s Galois correction. The integral de Rham condition gives, for each integer m ≥ 0, an integer a ≥ 0 and a matrix β over 𝔖 such that

β ≡ ℓaI   (mod E),
δ(g) g(ev β) ≡ ev β   (mod ηm)   for all g ∈ GK.

Here m specifies precision on the same frame. Its witnesses may have different powers of ℓ. Relative uniqueness. If (a, β) and (a′, β′) are witnesses at precision m, every entry of D = ℓa′β − ℓaβ′ belongs to Em𝔖. Cross multiplication states the assertion inside the integral coefficient ring.

For m = 0 the conclusion is automatic. For m ≥ 1 the scalar residues of the two terms of D agree, so E divides D. Choose g with g(η) = ηw such that λ = θ(w) has infinite multiplicative order. Its construction is given below. Evaluated coefficients in 𝔖 have residues in 𝒪K, fixed by g. Reducing the second witness equation for β modulo η therefore gives ℓaθ(δ(g)) = ℓaI. Cancellation in the characteristic-zero domain 𝒪ℂℓ shows that θ(δ(g)) = I.

Suppose D = EiDi with 1 ≤ i < m. The two witness equations give δ(g) g(ev D) − ev D ∈ (ηm) entry by entry. Substitution and cancellation of ηi in the domain Ainf leave an error divisible by ηm−i. Reduction by θ now gives

(λi − 1) θ(ev Di) = 0.

The first factor is nonzero, so every entry of Di has zero residue. The map 𝔖/(E) ≃ 𝒪K ↪ 𝒪ℂℓ is injective. Hence E divides Di, proving the induction step. One choice of g works at every step. Only nonvanishing of λi − 1 is needed.

Where does the multiplier of infinite order come from?

Write x = [π♭] for the Teichmüller lift of a compatible system of ℓ-power roots of the chosen uniformizer π. Then θ(x) = π and η = E(x). Fix a primitive system εcyc of ℓ-power roots of unity and a primitive period τ for the datum, chosen together so that φ(τ) = ητ = v([εcyc] − 1) for a unit v of Ainf. The construction of τ is given in the next proof. Inverse Witt Frobenius shows that θ(τ) is a unit times ζℓ − 1, which is nonzero for every prime ℓ. The polynomial E is separable in characteristic zero, so E′(π) ≠ 0. Consequently c = θ(v−1τ) πE′(π) ≠ 0.

Choose h so large that 0 < |ℓhc|ℓ < 1/ℓ. Then 1 + ℓhc has infinite order. Indeed, if 1 + b had finite order greater than one with |b|ℓ < 1/ℓ, some power ζ would have prime order q. Every power ζj differs from 1 by at most |b|ℓ, by the binomial formula. Summing 1 − ζj for 0 ≤ j < q gives q. The ultrametric inequality would give |q|ℓ < 1/ℓ, whereas |q|ℓ is 1 or 1/ℓ.

There is an element g ∈ GK with g(x) = [εcyc]ℓhx. At depth n, this asks g to carry one root of Xℓn − π to another. This polynomial is Eisenstein at π. Indeed, a proper monic factorization over K would have integral coefficients, since its roots are integral and 𝒪K is integrally closed. Reducing the factors modulo π would give two positive powers of X, forcing both constant terms to be divisible by π and their product by π². Irreducibility and separability give transitivity on the roots. The sets of elements of GK realizing the prescribed translations through depth n are nonempty, closed and nested, since raising roots to the ℓth power recovers the previous level. Compactness gives one element for the entire system. Milne, Fields and Galois Theory, Proposition 4.5 and Propositions 7.7–7.8.

Put μ = [εcyc] − 1 = ηv−1τ. Modulo η² the preceding action sends x to (1 + ℓhμ)x. The coefficients of E are fixed by g, so expansion of the polynomial gives g(η) ≡ η + ℓhμxE′(x) modulo η². Thus g(η) = ηw with θ(w) = 1 + ℓhc. The multiplier on η used in the induction is λ = θ(w). The reciprocal convention zθ(w) = 1 used for the action on weights gives z = λ−1.

How can one choose the primitive period?

Put y = [εcyc1/ℓ]. Then φ(y) = yℓ = [εcyc]. The geometric sum ξcyc = 1 + y + ⋯ + yℓ−1 generates ker θ. Indeed θ(y) is a primitive ℓth root of unity, so the sum lies in the kernel. Its first Witt component is (εcyc1/ℓ − 1)ℓ−1. In the normalization giving ℓ valuation one before tilting, εcyc − 1 has tilt valuation ℓ/(ℓ−1). Thus the first component of ξcyc has valuation one. Fontaine’s generator of ker θ also has first component of valuation one. Write ξcyc as a multiple of that generator. The first component of the multiplier has valuation zero, so the multiplier is a Witt unit. Write η = ωξcyc with ω a unit of Ainf. Brinon and Conrad, Example 4.3.4 and Proposition 4.4.3.

Choose a Witt unit U satisfying φ(U) = ωU. To construct it, write ω₀ for the first component of ω. Modulo ℓ choose a root of Xℓ−1 = ω₀. This root is a unit. Given a lift satisfying the equation modulo ℓj for j ≥ 1, add ℓj times a Teichmüller lift. To correct the next residue one must solve Xℓ − ω₀X = b, where b is the negative residue of the old error divided by ℓj. Both monic equations have roots in the tilt valuation ring because its fraction field is algebraically closed and the valuation ring is integrally closed. Witt completeness yields U with its original unit residue. Now set τ = (y − 1)U. The geometric-sum identity gives φ(τ) = ([εcyc] − 1)ωU = ητ, so the unit v in the preceding proof is ωU. Brinon and Conrad, Theorem 4.3.5.

How do the witnesses define an invertible matrix over the de Rham valuation ring?

Let R+ = BdR+, let ξ be the image of η there and write a bar for passage from Ainf to R+. This is a complete discrete valuation ring with residue field ℂℓ and filtration Film = ξmR+. In particular ℓ is invertible in R+. The resulting completion is taken with respect to ξ. Brinon and Conrad, CMI Summer School Notes on p-adic Hodge Theory, Proposition 4.4.6.

Choose one witness (am, βm) at each precision and put bm = ℓ−am ev(βm) in R+, using evaluation followed by the bar map. A witness valid at n ≥ m is valid at m. Apply relative uniqueness to the two choices, then divide by ℓam+an in R+. This gives bn ≡ bm modulo ξm. Completeness supplies a matrix B with these residues. Separatedness makes B unique, and the same argument compares it with every other valid witness at each order. No bound on the integers am enters the proof.

B ≡ bm   (mod ξm)   for every m,   det(B) ≡ 1   (mod ξ).

Indeed, at m = 1 the scalar residue of β1 gives b1 ≡ I. Hence det(B) is a unit in the local ring, and the adjugate formula gives B−1 over R+. To obtain its transformation law, fix any g ∈ GK. Equivariance of θ makes its kernel stable under g, so g preserves every filtration ideal. Replacing B by bm in δ̄(g) g(B) − B leaves the same class modulo ξm. That class vanishes by the witness equation. Intersecting the filtration ideals gives the exact identity δ̄(g) g(B) = B. It is this twisted identity that corrects the period columns.

Which columns give an invariant basis, and what bounds their poles?

Keep this one frame and let R = BdR be the fraction field of R+. Write T for the invertible frame matrix in the Witt ring W(ℂℓ♭) of the tilt field, which contains Ainf = W(𝒪ℂℓ♭). The Frobenius matrices A, A′ over 𝔖 satisfy AA′ = E2NI. In the larger Witt ring the frame identities are ηNφ(T) = T ev(A) and ρ(g) g(T) = T ε(g). Let τ be the primitive period above and set s = φ(τ) = ητ. Its image s̄ = ξτ̄ is ξ times a unit in R+, since θ(τ) ≠ 0.

The integral matrices Y and Y′ constructed below represent τNT and τNT−1 in the larger Witt ring. They satisfy YY′ = τ2NI. Let P be the image of φ(Y) in R+ and P′ that of φ(Y′). Then PP′ = s̄2NI. With the matrix B on the right, put

Qf = s̄−NPB,   Qf−1 = s̄−NB−1P′.

Multiplying in this order gives the identity. A right inverse of a square matrix over the field R is its inverse. Multiplication by ξN therefore makes every entry of both displayed matrices integral in R+. These are upper bounds on the pole orders for the initial height N.

To verify invariance, clear denominators in the Galois identity for T to obtain τNρ(g) g(Y) = g(τ)NY ε(g). This equality descends along the injective inclusion of Witt rings. Apply Frobenius in Ainf, where it commutes with g and fixes the entries of ρ(g) in ℤℓ. Mapping the result into R gives s̄Nρ(g) g(P) = g(s̄)NPδ̄(g). Thus

ρ(g) g(Qf) = s̄−NPδ̄(g) g(B) = Qf.

The columns of Qf form an invariant R-basis for the representation after scalar extension. The Frobenius identity for T likewise gives φ(Y) = Y ev(A) in Ainf. Consequently s̄NQf = ȲĀB, the identity used in the factorization of the two height matrices. There the invariant basis and the Hodge-adapted basis must describe the same representation in the same coordinates. The present argument assumes the integral de Rham frame at one level and constructs its invariant basis.

Why do the two scaled Witt matrices have integral entries?

In W(ℂℓ♭) the elements τ and η are units. Indeed, the first Witt component of [εcyc] − 1 is the nonzero element εcyc − 1 of the tilt field. From φ(τ) = v([εcyc] − 1) and invertibility of Witt Frobenius there, τ is a unit, and η = φ(τ)/τ is a unit as well. The Frobenius equations now give φ(τNT) = (τNT) ev(A) and φ(τNT−1) = ev(A′)(τNT−1). For the second equality, invert ηNφ(T) = T ev(A) and use ev(A′) = η2Nev(A)−1 in that ring. Thus each row in the first equation and each transposed column in the second obeys a Frobenius equation with coefficients in Ainf.

Here is the integrality argument for such a finite row Z. More generally suppose φ(Z) = ZC + L, with C and L over Ainf. Reduce modulo ℓ and let M be the largest absolute value of an entry of the residue of Z in the tilt field. If M > 1, the left side has largest absolute value Mℓ while the right side has largest absolute value at most M. This contradicts ℓ > 1. Hence every residue is integral. Subtract its entrywise Teichmüller lift Z0 and divide by ℓ in the large Witt ring. The resulting row still satisfies a Frobenius equation with integral coefficients and integral constant term (Z0C + L − φ(Z0))/ℓ. Its numerator has zero first Witt component, which is precisely divisibility by ℓ over the perfect tilt valuation ring. Iteration supplies integral approximants modulo every ℓj. The first j Witt components of each entry then belong to the tilt valuation ring. Taking all j proves that every entry lies in Ainf. This use of the ℓ-adic Witt coordinates precedes the ξ-adic limit in the de Rham valuation ring. Brinon and Conrad, §4.2, Witt vectors over a perfect ring.

Apply the row calculation to both scaled matrices to get Y and Y′. Multiplying their images in the larger ring gives τ2NI. Injectivity on Witt components gives the same identity over Ainf. It also identifies Y with any prescribed integral lift of τNT.
