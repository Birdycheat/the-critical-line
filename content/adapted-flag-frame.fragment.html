What must an adapted frame remember?

A rational basis that follows a flag identifies its vector spaces. The volume calculation also needs to know which coefficients give integral vectors. Let E/F be the CM extension, unramified at every finite place. Write O for the integers of E, h for the nonsingular Hermitian form (linear in the second entry), and L for the self-dual lattice belonging to the chosen finite point. Self-duality means that x lies in L exactly when h(y,x) lies in O for all y in L. Fix a saturated flag 0 = S₀ ⊂ ⋯ ⊂ Sₐ whose last space is totally isotropic. Total isotropy means that every two vectors in it pair to zero. Put jᵢ = rank(Sᵢ/Sᵢ₋₁), d = ∑jᵢ, and suppose each real signature is (p,q) with d ≤ min(p,q).

Saturation says Sᵢ = L ∩ spanE(Sᵢ). If x ∈ Sᵢ and ax ∈ Sᵢ₋₁ for some nonzero a ∈ O, then x already lies in Sᵢ₋₁. Thus the quotient is torsion-free. It is finitely generated as an O-module. Over the Dedekind domain O it is projective, so the quotient map admits a section. Its image Aᵢ gives Sᵢ = Sᵢ₋₁ ⊕ Aᵢ. Choose a basis over E for the span of each Aᵢ, and let Λᵢ ⊂ Ejᵢ be the coefficient vectors of its integral elements. This construction also works when Aᵢ is a nonprincipal ideal and has no one-element integral basis.

Set W = spanE(Sₐ). We need a second isotropic space pairing perfectly with W and a decomposition that preserves L. Apply the same projectivity argument to L/Sₐ. If π is the quotient map and s a section, then 1 − sπ projects L onto Sₐ. Extend it by scalars to a projector P on the full rational space, with image W and P(L) ⊆ L.

How can the opposite isotropic space stay integral?

Take the Hermitian adjoint Q = P†, defined by h(x,Qy) = h(Px,y). For x,y ∈ L the right-hand side is integral, so self-duality gives Qy ∈ L. Since the image of P is totally isotropic, QP = 0. Both P and Q are idempotent. The unramified hypothesis supplies an integral w of relative trace one, as in the real-layer calculation. The corrected projector satisfies

T = Q − wPQ,   QT = Q,   T² = T,   T†T = (1 − w − conj(w))PQ = 0.

Indeed (PQ)† = PQ and PQP = 0. The last identity makes W′ = im T isotropic. On im Q the map u ↦ u − wPu has left inverse Q, so dim W′ = rank Q = rank P = d. For x ∈ W, h(x,Tz) = h(x,z). Thus if Tz pairs to zero with all of W, then Qz pairs to zero with the full space, so Qz = 0 and Tz = 0. Equal dimensions give a perfect pairing between the opposite spaces.

For an integral vector z take y = Tz and x = P(z − y). The remainder r = z − x − y lies in both ker P and ker Q. It is orthogonal to W = im P and to W′ = im T, because T†r = 0. All three summands remain in L. The perfect pairing makes their sum direct.

For a two-coordinate check, take the Gram matrix [[0,1],[1,1]]. The form takes value one on its second coordinate vector. Subtracting half of the first coordinate vector would make that value zero. In the Eisenstein integers O = ℤ[ω] with ω = (1 + √−3)/2, the integral vector (−conj(ω),1) also has form value zero, since 1 − ω − conj(ω) = 0. It still pairs to one with (1,0), and these two vectors have determinant one. This plane illustrates the trace-one correction over a ring where 1/2 is unavailable. The global theorem imposes additional unramified conditions on the field.

Which lattice and frame data determine the marking?

Choose in W′ the basis dual to the concatenated rational bases of the Aᵢ. The Hermitian pairings between corresponding vectors are one, and all cross pairings are zero. Write V₀ = (W ⊕ W′)⊥ and L₀ = L ∩ V₀. For a coordinate lattice Λᵢ, its Hermitian dual consists of the vectors v for which conj(u) · v ∈ O for every u ∈ Λᵢ. A vector in W′ with these dual coefficients pairs integrally with L because only the W component contributes. Conversely, every integral vector pairs integrally with the marked lattices. Self-duality of L identifies the opposite coefficients exactly. The same pairing test on V₀ proves that L₀ is self-dual there. Thus in the chosen coordinates

L ≃ (⊕i Λᵢ) ⊕ (⊕i Λᵢ∨) ⊕ L₀,   Sₖ ≃ ⊕i≤k Λᵢ.
Why are the residual lattices equivalent at a finite place?

At an inert finite place, work first over the completed unramified quadratic extension. A self-dual lattice has an integral Gram matrix with unit determinant in an integral basis. Its reduction is a nonsingular Hermitian space over a quadratic extension of finite fields. In positive dimension it has a vector whose Hermitian value is nonzero. Otherwise, expanding the value on x + ay would give Tr(a h(x,y)) = 0 for every residue scalar a. The trace pairing of finite fields is nondegenerate, so every pairing would vanish. This argument includes residue characteristic two.

Lift such a vector. Its value is a unit of the base ring. Every unit is a norm in an unramified quadratic local extension, so scaling the vector gives Hermitian value one. Integral orthogonal projection now splits off its line and leaves a unimodular complement. Induction gives an orthonormal basis, with the empty basis as the rank-zero case. At a split place the two integral components pair perfectly and one can take an arbitrary basis on the first and its dual on the second. Thus in both cases a completed integral isometry matches any two self-dual lattices of the same rank in a common Hermitian space.

For a finite place v of the real base field, a rational vector belongs to the localized lattice when some base integer outside v takes it into the original lattice. At a split place this condition retains both primes above v. The genus comparison uses a rational isometry that matches these localized lattices. The condition on the completed lattices is open because their stabilizers are open. To see why rational isometries meet this open set, take a local isometry U. Multiply by a rational scalar λ of norm one so that 1 + λU is invertible. Only finitely many λ are excluded by the determinant polynomial, and the ratios (1 + kθ)/(1 − kθ) for integers k give infinitely many choices when θ is a nonzero purely imaginary element. Set

X = (1 − λU)(1 + λU)−1,   X† = −X,   λU = (1 − X)(1 + X)−1.

Approximate X by rational matrices Y and replace each by (Y − Y†)/2. They are exactly skew-adjoint and still approximate X. At a place above two the denominator costs a fixed precision, which can be supplied by a closer initial approximation. At a split place use simultaneous approximation in the two completions. The inverse displayed transformation is continuous on its domain and sends these rational skew-adjoint matrices to rational isometries. Undo the scalar λ. A sufficiently close choice lies in the same open lattice coset as U, so it takes the first localized lattice into the second. Duality reverses this inclusion, and both lattices are self-dual, giving equality. The rational choice here may depend on the finite place.

From the local lattices to a marked class

Each pair of opposite basis vectors contributes one positive and one negative direction at a real place, by taking their sum and difference. The residual signature is therefore (p−d,q−d). To compare it with the prescribed residual model, identify the CM fields by the given isomorphism respecting conjugation. Both forms now have self-dual lattices. At a finite place the extension is split or unramified, and local classification identifies their unimodular forms. At infinity their signatures agree. The local-to-global theorem for Hermitian forms gives a rational isometry of the residual spaces. In the resulting common space the transferred lattice is locally isometric to the reference lattice everywhere. A finite family of nontrivial local changes carries one to the other, since two full lattices agree at almost all places. Their product is the finite adelic representative of this residual lattice class.

Finally compare two markings expressed with the same coefficient lattices and residual representative. Their full frame matrices B and B′ have the same nonsingular Gram matrix, so B′B−1 is a rational unitary isometry. The coefficient description takes each integral partial sum to its namesake and gives the equality of the full lattices. Their finite representatives then differ, after this rational change of frame, by an element of the compact lattice stabilizer. The sizes of the graded pieces agree in the transported archimedean frame. This is the identification used in counting the marked slices.
