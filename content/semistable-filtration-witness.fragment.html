Which denominator makes every coefficient of the Galois correction integral?

In the specialization at logarithm zero we obtained a Witt matrix M with Md = 0. We write M for the integral monodromy matrix, so M/ℓk is the rational monodromy in these columns. For g ∈ GK, let c(g) ∈ ℤℓ be the displacement of the chosen logarithm in units of the fundamental period t. Its action on the columns involves the finite exponential of c(g)tM/ℓk. We now want a single power of ℓ that places this whole matrix in the integral crystalline ring Acris for every g.

Write d! = ℓeh, with ℓ coprime to h, and take k₀ = e + kd. For 0 ≤ i < d, the coefficient of (c(g)tM)i becomes

ℓk₀ / (i! ℓki) = ℓk(d−i) (d!/i!) / h   ∈ ℤℓ.

The exponent on the right is nonnegative, the factorial quotient is an integer, and h is an ℓ-adic unit. The same denominator works for all entries and all g. For ℓ = 3, d = 4 and k = 1 it gives k₀ = 5. In order of increasing degree, the four coefficients are 243, 81, 27/2 and 3/2. For instance, the last term needs both the factor 3 in 3! and the three factors of 3 from the scaled monodromy. The remaining 2 in its denominator is invertible in ℤ₃.

κ(g) = ∑0 ≤ i < d [ℓk₀ / (i! ℓki)] (c(g)t)i Mi,   ℓk₀ρ(g)g(U) = Uκ(g).

In κ we map the Witt entries of M to Acris. The last equality is read in Bcris, using the inclusion of the integral ring. If g fixes the chosen compatible system of roots used to define the logarithm, its displacement is zero. All positive-degree terms disappear and κ(g) = ℓk₀I. In the numerical example this gives 243I. Division by ℓk₀ later recovers the identity on the coefficient disc. In rank zero all the displayed matrix equalities hold on the zero space.

How does a scalar approximation correct all the columns at once?

The matrix κ is integral. To use it on the coefficient disc, we also need a change of columns whose Galois error vanishes to any prescribed order. Let S = W(kK)[[u]], where kK is the residue field of K, and let E be the Eisenstein polynomial of the chosen uniformizer. We use the fixed compatible evaluation maps to view elements of S in the period rings. Write Film for the m-th step of the filtration on BdR+. The approximation from the chosen logarithm supplies a scalar input. For each natural number m there exist j ≥ 0 and s ∈ E S such that, after evaluation,

g(s) − s ≡ ℓj c(g)t   mod Film   for every g ∈ GK.

Fix these two witnesses before choosing g. Set hm = vℓ(d!) + (j + k)d and define the following matrix in S. The exponential denotes the polynomial of degrees 0 through d − 1 in the nilpotent matrix M.

Λm = ℓhm exp(−sM/ℓj+k),   Λm ≡ ℓhmI   mod E.

The factorial bound with k replaced by j + k makes every coefficient integral. Every term of positive degree is divisible by s, hence by E. For the Galois calculation work in BdR+/Film, where division by ℓ is allowed. The coefficients of M are fixed. Each exponential is a polynomial in this same matrix. The coefficient of Mi in exp(aM) exp(bM) is ∑r+v=i arbv/(r!v!) = (a + b)i/i! for i < d. Every higher power vanishes. The two exponents in κ(g)g(Λm) therefore add. Their scalar coefficient is c(g)t/ℓk − g(s)/ℓj+k, which the scalar congruence identifies with −s/ℓj+k. Restoring the powers of ℓ gives

κ(g) g(Λm) − ℓk₀Λm ≡ 0   mod Film,   for every g.

For example, take d = 2, ℓ = 3, k = 0 and a square-zero matrix M. If a scalar witness at depth m satisfies g(s) − s ≡ 3z with z = c(g)t, take j = 1. Then κ = I + zM and Λm = 9I − 3sM. Multiplication gives 9I + (9z − 3g(s))M, which is congruent to 9I − 3sM. The matrix Λm works for the entire group at this depth. Increasing the depth may require a different j and a different s.
