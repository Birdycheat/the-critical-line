Why does removing a few directions cost so much volume?

Consider a short isotropic flag of total rank J in signature (p, q). Removing it and its dual changes the signature to (p − J, q − J). The flag coefficient involves a quotient of compact volumes. Its largest decrease comes from the last J sphere factors on the p side. To isolate them, write Am for the area of the unit sphere in ℂm+1 and S(r) for the product of the first r such areas, with S(0) = 1.

Am = 2πm+1/m!,   S(p)/S(p−J) = ∏m=p−Jp−1 Am.

The area formula follows by integrating exp(−‖z‖²) over ℂm+1. The integral is πm+1 by taking products of the plane integral. Polar coordinates give the same value as the area times ∫₀∞ exp(−r²)r2m+1 dr = m!/2 times the area. The quotient above cancels all the lower-dimensional spheres. NIST, the radial gamma integral · Gamma at positive integers.

Large-rank comparison. Suppose n ≥ 18, 0 ≤ J ≤ p ≤ n, and p − J is at least both n/2 and n − 2√n. Put c = log(2π). Then

log[S(p)/S(p−J)] ≤ J[c − (n − 2√n) log(n/(2πe))].

To prove this, keep the mth term of the exponential series at m. It gives mm/m! ≤ em, hence Am ≤ 2π(πe/m)m. Every surviving index satisfies m ≥ n/2, so the base of this power is at most 2πe/n. It is smaller than one since 2πe < 18. We may therefore replace the exponent m by its lower bound n − 2√n to get an upper bound. Summing the logarithms of the J factors proves the displayed inequality. NIST, exponential series.

What can the smaller blocks add back?

Let n = p + q, s = √n and L = log n. The short-flag estimate assumes L ≥ 15, q² ≤ n, and positive rank jumps j₁,…,ja with sum J ≤ q. Then p − J ≥ n − 2s ≥ n/2. At each of the f real places the compact factor is

C = [S(p)/S(p−J)] [S(q)/S(q−J)] ∏i S(jᵢ)−1.

The second bracket contains just J sphere areas. Consecutive areas have ratio Am+1/Am = π/(m+1), so the largest is A₃ = π⁴/3 < 32.5 < exp(3.49). The last inequality already follows from the terms of degree zero through nine in the exponential series. Thus this bracket costs at most 3.49J in the logarithm.

For a jump of rank j each reciprocal area with 0 ≤ m < j is at most m! ≤ jm. Multiplying gives S(j)−1 ≤ jj(j−1)/2. Since j ≤ s, its logarithm is at most (js/2) log s. Summing over the jumps and using log s = L/2 bounds the total cost by JsL/4. Together the three estimates give

log(Cf) ≤ fJ[c − (n − 2s)(L − c − 1) + 3.49 + sL/4].

For a check of the factors at one place, take (p, q) = (32, 4) and two jumps of rank one. The p bracket is A₃₀A₃₁ and has logarithm about −79.25. All the other factors multiply to A₂A₃/A₀² = π⁵/12, adding about 3.24. The compact logarithm is about −76.01. This rank satisfies the large-rank comparison above. The full short-flag probability uses the stronger assumption log n ≥ 15 and also includes the discriminant and zeta factors. It bounds the mass lost in the row-count estimate, which enters the lower bound for the common span of the period columns.
