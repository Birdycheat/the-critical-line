Which signed parameters give a copy of the smaller representation?

Let G = U(p,q), let z be a positive unit coordinate, and let H = U(p − 1,q) fix z. Write m = p + q = n + 1. At each position of the Harish-Chandra parameters λ of G and μ of H, record its sign in the Hermitian space and its group of origin. Merge the two decreasing lists in numerical order. The signed interlacing rule requires neighbouring entries from different groups to have the same sign, and neighbouring entries from the same group to have opposite signs.

Occurrence theorem used here. Suppose 1 ≤ q ≤ p, n ≥ 2, and each parameter is strictly decreasing in its Harish-Chandra lattice (in rank r, each entry belongs to ℤ + (r − 1)/2). Within each group require an absolute gap of at least 200m³ between entries of opposite sign. If the merged list obeys the signed rule, every pair of unitary discrete-series models π and σ with these signed parameters admits a nonzero bounded map j from the space of σ into the space of π with jσ(h) = π(h)j on H. He’s branching theorem gives the occurrence criterion for discrete series. The argument below follows the descent used for this separated range. Hongyu He, Definition 1 and Theorem 0.1.

Where the descent starts. Denote an entry of the merged target list by ax, and set dx = 2ax + K, choosing an integer K large enough to make all directions positive. These are distinct integers in the same order as the target. The signed frame theorem realizes the two lists of directions as an ambient diagonal operator and its compression to the smaller space after conjugation. The distant-parameter occurrence estimate then supplies an actual smaller parameter μ′ occurring under λ + t dG, with |μ′j − t dH,j| ≤ C√t for sufficiently large admissible t. Take t to be a large integer square. The displacements on the two sides are bG,i = t dG,i and bH,j = μ′j − μj. They are integers by the parameter lattices. The error from t dx is bounded by C√t + max |ax|. Consecutive predictions differ by at least t and the smallest prediction is at least t. Taking t large makes every displacement nonnegative, with bx ≤ by whenever ax < ay. Thus the offsets decrease weakly as we read the displayed lists from largest to smallest. The chosen starting pair therefore has an occurrence.

How to choose a step. The current entries are ay + by. Among the positions with bx > 0, choose the one with the smallest target entry. Lower its displacement by one. Every smaller position now has displacement zero, and every larger one has displacement at least the old bx. This proves monotonicity after the change. At either stage regularity and both gap bounds follow because monotone displacements can only enlarge a difference of target entries.

In particular, an entry from the other group cannot be the midpoint of this step. A smaller target entry has zero displacement and lies strictly below ax, the lowest possible endpoint. A larger one is already strictly above the starting value. The two ranks have opposite integral and half-integral lattices, so there are no equal target entries between groups. A step on the λ side therefore satisfies

λscurrent − ½ ≠ μjcurrent for every j.

The one-step argument below transports the occurrence across this lowering. A μ-step uses derivatives transverse to the smaller group. It assumes an occurrence before the step, admissible parameters on both sides of the step, and μj − ½ distinct from every current λs. It translates σ, proves that a projected smooth vector in the smaller tensor is nonzero by applying words in the infinitesimal operators, and uses Schur orthogonality to exclude absence of the shifted σ. At the starting pair the trial vector supplies the estimate that locates a constituent. At each smaller-group move the transverse derivative identity transfers it. Each step reduces ∑xbx by exactly one. When it reaches zero the occurrence is at the prescribed parameters.

Why is there still a nonzero coefficient after lowering λ?

Write λ, μ for the current parameters. Assume they satisfy the rank, signature, regularity and gap hypotheses above, that λ − es still satisfies them, that the midpoint condition holds, and that σ already occurs in π restricted to H. The tensor translation theorem supplies a shifted ambient model πs of parameter λ − es, its smooth tensor projection ps, its coordinate map rs into that model, and its signed-form scalar cs. We must show that σ occurs in πs restricted to H.

Use the normalized test at an existing occurrence. Fix Haar measure ν on H, let κ be the reciprocal formal degree of σ for ν, and choose a unit vector w in its lowest compact type. Set φ(h) = ⟨w,σ(h)w⟩∗, where the star means complex conjugation. The coefficient is integrable under the stated smaller-group gap. We use the following coefficient projection for the known occurrence. There is an isometric H-intertwiner i and a smooth ambient vector η with ⟨η,iw⟩ ≠ 0 such that, for every ambient vector y,

∫H φ(h) ⟨η,π(h)y⟩ dν = κ ⟨η,iw⟩ ⟨iw,y⟩.

Let P = i* and put η into the single positive tensor coordinate z. Write y₀ = (ps(ezη))z and b = rs(ezη). The cofactor identity selects ps. In its z,z entry the deleted coordinate leaves precisely the infinitesimal operators of H. The adjoint P intertwines those operators on the smooth vectors. With the smaller rank one less than the larger, their scalar shifts differ by ½. The specialized identity gives

DsPy₀ = ΓsPη,   Ds = ∏k≠s(λk − λs),   Γs = ∏j(μj − λs + ½).

Regularity gives Ds ≠ 0. The noncrossing condition makes each factor of Γs nonzero. Pair with w and use the adjoint relation. Since ⟨iw,η⟩ ≠ 0, this gives ⟨iw,y₀⟩ ≠ 0. For h ∈ H, the group fixes the positive coordinate z. Evaluating the signed tensor form with first vector ezη therefore gives cs⟨b,πs(h)b⟩ = ⟨η,π(h)y₀⟩. Multiply by φ and integrate.

cs ∫H φ(h) ⟨b,πs(h)b⟩ dν = κ ⟨η,iw⟩ ⟨iw,y₀⟩ ≠ 0.

Consequently the integral itself is nonzero. Absolute convergence follows from φ ∈ L¹ and |⟨b,πs(h)b⟩| ≤ ‖b‖². The coefficient criterion below gives a bounded H-intertwiner into πs. Unitary equivalence of models with a fixed signed discrete-series parameter transports it to every pair of realizations. This proves the λ-step.

A signed example. For G = U(3,1) and H = U(2,1), take T = 64,000 and target lists λ = (6T + ½, 4T + ½, 3T + ½, ½) of signs (+,+,−,+), and μ = (5T, 2T, T) of signs (+,−,+). The merged sequence is G+, H+, G+, G−, H−, H+, G+. Both lists have opposite-sign gaps at least T. To check a single step, give all seven entries displacement one. The selected position is the last λ, currently 3/2. Its midpoint is 1, below all three current μ entries. Here Ds = 72T³ and Γs = 10T³. The compression multiplies Pη by exactly 5/36, and the step lowers the displacement sum from seven to six.

To see why the order of the moves matters, change the last target μ in this example from T to 1. The signs, the merged order and the stated gap bound all survive. With the seven displacements again equal to one, the last λ and μ now stand at 3/2 and 2. Moving μ first would put its midpoint 3/2 exactly on λ. The prescribed move lowers λ to ½. The step from μ = 2 to 1 can now pass through 3/2 with all ambient entries clear of its midpoint.

How does a single nonzero integral produce a bounded intertwiner?

Let ρ be the restriction of the shifted representation to H. Use the Hilbert inner product linear in its second argument. For any ambient vectors a,b, write I(a,b) = ∫ φ(h)⟨b,ρ(h)a⟩ dν. Keep the irreducible square-integrable σ, its nonzero unit vector w, its integrable coefficient φ, and its Schur constant κ > 0. We construct a bounded intertwiner J with ⟨b,Jw⟩ = I(a,b).

Take the subspace of vectors y for which h ↦ ⟨σ(h)w,y⟩ is integrable. This subspace contains w, and left invariance of ν makes it stable under σ. Its closure is the whole representation by irreducibility. On this dense subspace define

Jy = ∫H ⟨σ(h)w,y⟩ ρ(h)a dν(h).

The vector integral converges since ρ is unitary. To bound its norm, expand ‖Jy‖² as a double integral and write the second group variable as ht. For each fixed h, left invariance of Haar measure leaves dν(t) unchanged. The two σ coefficients become the conjugate of ⟨σ(h)w,y⟩ and the coefficient ⟨σ(ht)w,y⟩. Their product is absolutely integrable in the original two variables and the remaining coefficient of ρ has modulus at most ‖a‖². Fubini and Schur orthogonality for σ now integrate out h, giving ‖Jy‖² = ‖y‖² κ I(a,a). This bounds J on its dense domain. Left translation in the vector integral gives Jσ(g) = ρ(g)J, and both sides extend continuously. At y = w its coefficient against b is exactly I(a,b). A single nonzero value therefore forces a nonzero bounded intertwiner. Applied to the preceding step, the two test vectors both equal the shifted vector b. Duflo and Moore, orthogonality of square-integrable representations, pp. 209–243.
