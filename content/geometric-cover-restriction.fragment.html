Which components of the integral model does the tower reach?

Fix a finite place w of the ground field. Write O for its discrete valuation ring, η for the generic point of Spec O, and Y for the smooth projective integral model supplied by the local comparison. Here “integral model” means a model over O. It may have several connected components. Its generic fibre Yη is obtained by allowing division by a uniformizer. The tower space Z maps to this fibre by a finite étale map π. The later cohomological comparison needs every geometric point of the chosen generic fibre to have a lift.

Image lemma. There is a unique open and closed part Y′ of Y for which Y′η is the image of π. The induced map Z → Y′η is finite étale and surjective. The new integral model is smooth and projective over O, with the same relative dimension as the old one wherever it has points.

Proof. The image U of π is closed because a finite morphism is integral and universally closed. It is open because an étale morphism is flat and locally of finite presentation, hence open. Consequently U is a union of entire connected components of the generic fibre. Stacks Project, Lemma 29.45.7 and Lemma 29.26.10.

The two facts needed to extend U across the special fibre are density and separation of components. Any nonempty open of Y has open image in Spec O by smoothness, so it meets the generic fibre. Moreover O is normal and locally Noetherian. A scheme smooth over it is again normal and locally Noetherian. Its irreducible components are disjoint and open. On any such component C, the intersection with the generic fibre is a nonempty open of an irreducible space and hence is irreducible. Since U is both open and closed there, it contains either all of that intersection or none. Take Y′ to be the union of the components selected by the first alternative. Its complement is the union of the others. Density shows that no different open and closed selection can give the same generic image. Stacks Project, §35.18 and §28.7, Lemma 28.7.6.

Now π factors through the open subscheme Y′η. Over that part its finite étale fibres are the original ones, and every fibre contains a point. Over an algebraically closed field a nonempty finite étale fibre is a finite collection of rational points, which gives the required lifts. Smoothness and relative dimension restrict to an open subscheme. The inclusion Y′ → Y is also a closed immersion, so composing with a projective embedding of Y gives one for Y′.

Why use smoothness here? Over ℤ localized at 5, consider two generic points labelled 0 and 5. In the model with equation X(X − 5) = 0 their reductions coincide. The indicator of the first branch would have values (1, 0), yet any integral polynomial takes congruent values at 0 and 5. The experiment compares this model with the split model X(X − 1) = 0. Over the fraction field the coordinate X/5 in the first model has values 0 and 1, just as X does in the split model. Thus the two models have the same generic branch indicator. Its ability to separate the reductions depends on the integral model.

Why does the restriction preserve good reduction?

Let s, t ∶ T → Y be the source and target of one integral correspondence representing a tower operator. To restrict both maps to the open and closed part Y′, we must keep precisely the points of

T′ = s−1(Y′) ∩ t−1(Y′).

Intersecting two preimages could in principle discard some points of a source fibre. At a place of good reduction the source is finite étale of a fixed degree d over all of Y. The compatibility with the tower includes a map from the tower correspondence to the pullback of T along π by its source leg. That map is finite étale of positive degree m and its target projection agrees with the tower target after applying π. These properties imply that T′ is the whole inverse image by s of Y′. In particular the restricted source has degree d and the comparison still has multiplicity m.

Proof. Start on the generic fibre. If s(x) belongs to Y′η, lift it by π, then lift the resulting pair over x by the comparison map of degree m. A finite étale map of positive constant degree is surjective, since its geometric fibres have positive cardinality. Following the tower target now writes t(x) as the image of a tower point under π. Thus t(x) also belongs to Y′. On the whole model the possible failure set is open, since Y′ and its complement are both open. The scheme T is smooth over O by composition of the étale map s and the smooth structure map of Y. Every nonempty open of T meets its generic fibre. The failure set has no generic points above η, so it is empty.

The restricted source is therefore the base change of s along Y′ → Y. It is finite étale with the same geometric fibres. Both ends of the comparison map from the tower already land in the chosen parts. The pullback of T along the tower source used for the multiplicity is canonically the pullback with T′ in its place. Its points have the same two coordinates and the same compatibility equation, and the factorization through an open immersion is unique. This preserves m for each operator. Applying the image lemma once to the common model therefore gives a good-reduction witness with surjective generic cover for the entire family.

What happens to the congruence in residue characteristic?

Let p be the residue characteristic of w, q = pf the residue cardinality and n the degree of the congruence polynomial. Here each of the n + 1 source maps is finite on the integral model and étale of known degree on the open where p is invertible. On the special fibre the hypothesis is an identity of multisets of points. We show that restriction to Y′ preserves both assertions.

In the valuation ring O the only prime ideals are zero and the maximal ideal. The latter contains p. A point of Y where p is invertible must therefore lie over η. The generic lifting argument just given applies there to each correspondence. Its intersection T′ has all the old source lifts over this open, so the étale degree there stays fixed. Over the entire model T′ is open and closed in s−1(Y′). Its closed inclusion followed by the finite base change of s is finite. This proves exactly the source-map properties needed at w.

To read the special-fibre identity, take a valuation ring B with algebraically closed fraction field and p nonzero in B. Choose a point y ∶ Spec B → Y′ whose closed point maps to a generic point of the uniformizer divisor. For each index j enumerate the sections over y of its source map. Write vj,a for the residue point obtained by applying the target to section a. Write F for the twist by the residue-field endomorphism z ↦ zq in the test. For each j, count the points Fn−j(vj,a) with weight qj(j−1)/2. The initial congruence says that the even indices and the odd indices give the same multiset, including all repetitions.

As a finite check on these weights, take n = 3, q = 5 and a single point fixed by F. Index the four lists by subspaces of dimensions 0, 1, 2 and 3 in the three-dimensional space over 𝔽₅. There are (5³ − 1)/(5 − 1) = 31 lines. Duality gives 31 planes, and there is one zero space and one whole space. Send every index to the fixed point. Its multiplicity on the even side is 1 + 5 · 31 = 156. The odd side gives 31 + 5³ = 156. In the full identity this equality must hold separately at each residue point.

Every original section over y factors uniquely through the restricted correspondence. Indeed its generic target lies in Y′ by the preceding argument, since p is nonzero in the fraction field. The target of its whole section lies there because Y′ is closed and every point of Spec B is a specialization of the generic point. Its source already lies there by the choice of y. We thus have a bijection between the two lists of sections. The inclusion of Y′ commutes with the residue twist and is injective on residue points. Applying this inclusion to the two weighted lists gives exactly the old identity, so the lists in Y′ are equal. The condition on the generic point of the divisor is preserved by an open and closed inclusion.

The residue degree, uniformizer, indices of the chosen tower operators, multiplicities and polynomial coefficients can therefore be retained. This proves the passage from an initial congruence witness to one with a surjective generic cover. Existence of the initial integral correspondence and its point identity is an earlier mathematical input.
