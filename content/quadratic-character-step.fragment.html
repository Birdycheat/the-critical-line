Why does adjoining a quadratic field at most double the number of character generators?

Enlarging the field shrinks its Galois group. A character of the smaller group may fail to extend to the larger one. The dyadic argument controls this failure and obtains a square bound on the number of characters, hence a factor of two in their dimension over 𝔽₂. What prevents an invariant character from extending?

Conjugation acts on a character by (Jφ)(u) = φ(σ−1uσ). The first condition on S ensures this action preserves C(U). For a fixed character Jφ = φ, put b(φ) = φ(σ²). Every character on V takes value zero on σ², since twice any value in 𝔽₂ is zero. Conversely, if b(φ) = 0, define its extension Eφ to equal φ on U and to take v outside U to φ(vσ). Then Eφ(σ) = 0. In multiplying two outside elements v, w, the sum of their values becomes φ(vσ) + φ(σ−1w) = φ(vw). Here invariance and φ(σ²) = 0 give φ(wσ) = φ(σ−1w). The mixed products use invariance alone. The formula is locally constant on both open cosets and agrees with φ on S ∩ V. Count the other characters using a square-zero map

Put D = 1 + J on C(U). Conjugating twice is conjugation by σ² inside U, which acts trivially on additive characters of U. Hence J² = 1 and in characteristic two D² = 0. The kernel consists of the fixed characters just counted, and the image is contained in the kernel. The quotient by the kernel is isomorphic to the image. In particular, finiteness of C(V) gives finiteness of both factors below. In the arithmetic application, S is the union of inertia groups at places away from the primes dividing N. Adjoining the quadratic datum of prime modulus p intersects V with an open subgroup of index dividing two. Conjugation carries inertia to inertia at a conjugate place. Since p divides N, every inertia group still required to vanish is away from p and acts trivially on the pth roots of unity. This gives S ∩ V ⊆ U. Applying the square bound once for each of the two primes in the generator count takes an initial cardinality bound B first to B², then to B⁴. A fixed character that detects σ². Let V = 𝔽₂² ⋊ ℤ/4 with generator σ of ℤ/4 exchanging the two coordinates. Use the discrete topology and impose vanishing only at the identity. Take U to be the triples (x,y,2z) with z ∈ 𝔽₂. Its eight characters have the form ax + by + cz. The four fixed characters have a = b and take value c on σ². Taking φ₀ = z, the corrected character is a(x + y). Its encoding on a general triple (x,y,k) in V is a(x + y) + c(k mod 2). These are exactly the four characters on V, since a character must agree on the two exchanged generators and is then determined by its value on σ. At c = 1 the original character takes value one on σ² and the encoding takes value zero there. Finally D sends (a,b,c) to (a + b,a + b,0), so |C(U)| = 4 · 2 = 8 and the square bound gives 16.
