How close are the period operators to a common orthonormal frame?

Two periods can each preserve lengths and still take their values on the same line. A slope estimate that uses many words therefore needs two measurements. For a fixed family member let Pu carry the full complex space of word u, of dimension du, into one Hilbert space. Normalize by the real number cu, the product of its two unfolding volumes divided by the product of its column representation dimensions over the infinite places. The operator from the space of v to the space of u is

Kuv = (cucv)−1/2 Pu∗Pv,   Q(A) = ∑j ‖Aej‖².

Here the ej run through an orthonormal basis of the domain. For u = v we measure Q(Kuu − I). For distinct words we measure Q(Kuv), which adds the squared absolute values of all correlations between the two normalized families of columns. The estimates are for the full word dimension. Separating the nonidentity terms with zero Gram label

The geometric expansion of Kuv converges absolutely. Write Buv for its subseries of terms with Gram label zero and with the identity excluded. Set D∗ equal to the positive reference size for the target dimension and ξ = exp(−kf log k). The proof uses four estimates. On different words the subseries vanishes, and the remaining squared norm is at most (1 + b−6)dudv/D∗. On the diagonal it gives

Q(Buu) ≤ ξdu,   Q(Kuu − Buu − I) ≤ Γdu²/D∗.

The four estimates have different suppliers. Local types kill the unwanted off-diagonal terms. A positive-kernel estimate controls their diagonal subseries. The selected different-word estimate controls the rest off the diagonal. On the diagonal a geometric row estimate controls what is left after subtracting the identity. Putting the words in the same row coordinates

Choose one orthonormal basis of the target, adapted to the common arithmetic row subspace, and one orthonormal basis for each full word space. Let Mu record the coordinates of Pu in those bases. In Mu∗Mv the sum over the shared row index gives exactly the inner products between the two sets of images, by Parseval. This is why the previous bounds apply simultaneously to the matrices. The unfolding volumes and dimensions give cu ≥ 0. The lower dimension proviso gives du > 0, and the scale bound gives e0 < 1. Together they force strict positivity. With the source convention for a zero normalizer the normalized block would be zero, so its squared distance from the identity would be the full positive dimension.
