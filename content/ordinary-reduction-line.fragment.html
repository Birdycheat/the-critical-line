Why do points with the same reduction differ by exactly one line?

The special fibre enters through one number. At an ordinary geometric point there are Qn−1 geometric points killed by 𝔴. The residue field κ of our valuation ring is algebraically closed: lift a monic polynomial over κ coefficient by coefficient to V. A root in K is integral over V and hence lies in V. Its reduction is a root of the original polynomial. We can therefore use the ordinary count for A[𝔴](κ).

The reduction line. Write ρ for reduction on H = A[𝔴](V). It respects addition, and the action of each residue scalar is induced by an integral endomorphism. Thus its kernel C is a 𝔽-subspace. By the lifting lemma the image has exactly Qn−1 elements. Subtracting two sections shows that ρ(x) = ρ(y) precisely when x − y ∈ C. Every nonempty fibre is a translate of C, so

Qn = |H| = |C| · Qn−1,   dim𝔽 C = 1.

The same C works for every isogeny at this source point: we defined it using only the abelian scheme and the ideal. Reduction identifies H/C with the group of special torsion points. In particular, for a subspace D of H, its reduced points are indexed by (D + C)/C. Their number is Qj if the j-dimensional D misses C and Qj−1 if D contains C. The quotient comparison explains how much of the subgroup scheme can be recovered from these points.

For a concrete group scheme, take V with fraction field of characteristic zero and residue characteristic three as above. In μ₃ × (ℤ/3ℤ)V the nine generic sections are (ζ3a, b) with a, b ∈ ℤ/3ℤ. All three cube roots of unity reduce to 1 because T³ − 1 = (T − 1)³ in characteristic three. A fixed value of b consequently has three lifts. The line lost on reduction is exactly the first coordinate. Its special coordinate ring κ[t]/(t³), with t = T − 1, still has length three. This product illustrates the point count used above. The experiment fixes b = 1 and tests the resulting component over 𝔽q[ε]/(εe). Its local length is q for every test, while the number of solutions depends on e.

How a section acquires its subspace. For a section of the type-j isogeny correspondence take the elements of H whose marked torsion points belong to its distinguished kernel. They form a subspace D. Over the fraction field the prescribed isogeny type gives Qj such points, hence dim D = j. Two sections giving the same D have the same distinguished kernel on the generic fibre, where it is finite étale. The polarized kernel comparison there identifies the entire quotient isogenies with their markings, so the generic sections coincide. The source of the correspondence is finite over the moduli base and therefore separated. The sections agree over V by the valuative criterion.

Conversely a j-dimensional subspace specifies a finite constant subgroup in the generic abelian variety, stable under the prescribed coefficient action. The marked quotient theorem for the selected type supplies the quotient isogeny realizing that subgroup as its distinguished part. Its moduli point extends uniquely over V because the source map is finite and hence proper. This gives a bijection between sections over our fixed source point and j-dimensional subspaces of H.
