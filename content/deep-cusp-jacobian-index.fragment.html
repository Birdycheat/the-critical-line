Riemannian Haar gauge and the ordered exponential-cube Jacobian bound

Two local calibrations underpin the Sobolev cube estimate and the deep-trace cancellation. On an abstract real unitary group U(ε), a Haar measure με is determined only up to a positive scalar multiple, yet its normalization must cancel between the local passage from Euclidean parameter coordinates to με and the global comparison of με with Riemannian sphere volumes. Fixing the unique scalar cε > 0 for which cεμε assigns unit asymptotic density relative to the Euclidean ball volume ωn²rn² in Hilbert–Schmidt distance provides a single intrinsic gauge connecting both ends. At the same time, the iterated endpoint inequality integrates along the ordered product of one-parameter subgroups Θ(t) = exp(t₁E₁) ⋯ exp(tn²En²) rather than the flat Lie algebra exponential. Because the factors do not commute, translating a perturbation in the ith coordinate across the remaining factors conjugates Ei and distorts Euclidean balls by at most δ = n(exp(2n²s) − 1) in operator norm; when δ ≤ 1/2, contraction inversion guarantees that the image of a closed Euclidean ball of radius r contains a left-translated Hilbert–Schmidt ball of radius λr for every 0 < λ < 1 − δ, and Vitali–Besicovitch covering converts this infinitesimal inclusion into a global Jacobian bound of at most (1 − δ)−n² ≤ exp(2n³(exp(2n²s) − 1)).

1. Existence and uniqueness of the Riemannian Haar gauge. Let n ≥ 0 be an integer and let ε = (ε₁, …, εn) ∈ {±1}n be a sign vector with diagonal signature matrix Jε = diag(ε₁, …, εn) ∈ GLn(ℂ). Define the real unitary group U(ε) = {u ∈ GLn(ℂ) : u*Jεu = Jε} and its real Lie algebra 𝔤ε = {X ∈ Mn(ℂ) : X*Jε + JεX = 0} of real dimension n², equipped with the real trace pairing ⟨X, Y⟩ = Re tr(XY*) and Hilbert–Schmidt norm ‖X‖HS = (Re tr(XX*))1/2. Let ωn² denote the Lebesgue volume of the unit Euclidean ball in ℝn², and fix any left Haar measure με on U(ε).

Theorem (Riemannian Haar gauge). There exists a unique real scalar cε > 0 such that

limr → 0+ cε με({u ∈ U(ε) : ‖u − 1‖HS ≤ r}) / rn² = ωn².

Proof. When n = 0, ℝ⁰ = {0} has ω₀ = 1, U(ε) = {1}, and {u ∈ U(ε) : ‖u − 1‖HS ≤ r} = {1} for every r > 0, so cε = 1/με({1}) > 0 is unique. For n ≥ 1, the matrix exponential exp : 𝔤ε → U(ε) is strictly differentiable at 0 with derivative equal to the Hilbert–Schmidt isometric inclusion ι : 𝔤ε ↪ Mn(ℂ). Hence exp restricts to a homeomorphism from a small closed ball B̄(0, δ₀) ⊂ 𝔤ε onto a compact neighborhood of 1 in U(ε), and because group multiplication in exponential coordinates agrees with vector addition to first order near 0, left-invariance of με makes the pulled-back measure asymptotically translation-invariant at small scales, yielding a finite positive limit A₀ > 0 for με(exp(B(0, r))) / (ωn²rn²) as r → 0+. Since ‖exp(X) − 1 − X‖HS = o(‖X‖HS) as X → 0, for every κ > 1 and all sufficiently small r > 0 the group ball {u ∈ U(ε) : ‖u − 1‖HS ≤ r} contains exp(B(0, κ−1r)) and lies inside exp(B(0, κr)). Dividing by rn², letting r → 0+, and then letting κ → 1+ identifies limr → 0+ με({u ∈ U(ε) : ‖u − 1‖HS ≤ r}) / rn² = A₀ωn² = A > 0, so cε = ωn²/A > 0 is unique.

2. Ordered exponential-cube Jacobian bound. Retain U(ε), με, and its Riemannian Haar gauge constant cε > 0. Let E₁, …, En² ∈ 𝔤ε be a real orthonormal basis for Re tr(XY*), define the ordered product exponential chart Θ : ℝn² → U(ε) by Θ(t) = exp(t₁E₁) ⋯ exp(tn²En²) for t = (t₁, …, tn²) ∈ ℝn², and let s > 0 satisfy n(exp(3n²s) − 1) ≤ 1/2.

Theorem (Ordered exponential-cube integral inequality). For every nonnegative measurable function Φ : U(ε) → [0, ∞],

∫[0,s]n² Φ(Θ(t)) dt ≤ exp(2n³(exp(2n²s) − 1)) · cε ∫Θ([0,s]n²) Φ(y) dμε(y).

Proof. On the cube [0, s]n², expanding Θ(t) − Θ(t′) telescopically around its linear part ∑i=1n² (ti − t′i)Ei yields ‖Θ(t) − Θ(t′)‖HS ≥ (1 − n(exp(3n²s) − 1))‖t − t′‖₂ ≥ (1/2)‖t − t′‖₂, so Θ is injective on [0, s]n² and hence on the interior (0, s)n², whose complement in [0, s]n² has Lebesgue measure zero. At each interior point t ∈ (0, s)n², left-multiplying the strict derivative Lt of Θ by Θ(t)−1 differs from the Euclidean-to-Hilbert–Schmidt isometric basis map v ↦ ∑i=1n² viEi by at most δ = n(exp(2n²s) − 1) ≤ 1/2 in operator norm. Quantitative contraction inversion on small closed balls around t therefore shows that for every 0 < λ < 1 − δ and all sufficiently small r > 0 with B̄(t, r) ⊆ (0, s)n², every g ∈ U(ε) with ‖g − 1‖HS ≤ λr satisfies Θ(t)g ∈ Θ(B̄(t, r)). By left-invariance of με and the Haar gauge limit at scale λr, letting r → 0+ and λ ↑ 1 − δ bounds ωn²rn² ≤ K · cεμε(Θ(B̄(t, r))) up to any slack factor above (1 − δ)−n² ≤ exp(2n²δ) = K, where K = exp(2n³(exp(2n²s) − 1)) uses 1 ≤ (1 − δ)e2δ on 0 ≤ δ ≤ 1/2. Applying the Besicovitch–Vitali covering theorem on ℝn² together with injectivity of Θ on (0, s)n² bounds Lebesgue measure on (0, s)n² by (K cε) times the pullback of με, and monotone approximation by simple functions integrates every nonnegative measurable Φ. Under the small-cube conditions 8n⁵s ≤ 1 and 12n³s ≤ 1 of the Sobolev multiplicity bound, one has n(exp(3n²s) − 1) ≤ 1/2 and 2n³(exp(2n²s) − 1) ≤ 1, so K ≤ e at each of the f real places of F; taking the product over the real places with P∞ = ∏v cv produces the prefactor efP∞.

Telescopic expansion of the ordered exponential, ball inclusion, and small-cube constants

Each orthonormal basis matrix Ei ∈ 𝔤ε satisfies ‖Ei‖op ≤ ‖Ei‖HS = 1, so for t, t′ ∈ [0, s]n² every partial product of exp(±tjEj) or exp(±t′jEj) has operator norm at most exp(n²s). Writing Pi−1(t) = ∏j<i exp(tjEj) and Si+1(t′) = ∏j>i exp(t′jEj), the telescopic sum Θ(t) − Θ(t′) = ∑i=1n² Pi−1(t)(exp(tiEi) − exp(t′iEi))Si+1(t′) differs from ∑i=1n² (ti − t′i)Ei in Hilbert–Schmidt norm by at most ∑i=1n² |ti − t′i|(exp(3n²s) − 1) ≤ n(exp(3n²s) − 1)‖t − t′‖₂ by Cauchy–Schwarz across the n² coordinates. Since ‖∑i (ti − t′i)Ei‖HS = ‖t − t′‖₂ by orthonormality, the reverse triangle inequality gives ‖Θ(t) − Θ(t′)‖HS ≥ (1 − n(exp(3n²s) − 1))‖t − t′‖₂.

Similarly, at t ∈ (0, s)n² the ith partial derivative of Θ equals Pi−1(t)Ei exp(tiEi)Si+1(t) = Θ(t)(Si+1(t)−1EiSi+1(t)), and ‖Si+1(t)−1EiSi+1(t) − Ei‖HS ≤ exp(2n²s) − 1; Cauchy–Schwarz over 1 ≤ i ≤ n² bounds ‖Θ(t)−1Lt(v) − ∑i viEi‖HS ≤ n(exp(2n²s) − 1)‖v‖₂ = δ‖v‖₂. Composing v ↦ Θ(t)−1Θ(t + v) with the local inverse of exp : 𝔤ε → U(ε) near 1 produces a strictly differentiable map Ft from a neighborhood of 0 in ℝn² to 𝔤ε whose derivative at 0 has inverse norm at most (1 − δ)−1; Banach’s contraction principle on small closed balls implies that for any 0 < λ < 1 − δ, choosing κ > 1 with κλ < 1 − δ ensures Ft(B̄(0, r)) ⊇ B̄𝔤ε(0, κλr) and hence Θ(B̄(t, r)) ⊇ {Θ(t)g : g ∈ U(ε), ‖g − 1‖HS ≤ λr} for all small r > 0. Finally, for n = 0 all exponents vanish, while for n ≥ 1 the inequalities 12n³s ≤ 1 and 8n⁵s ≤ 1 imply 3n²s ≤ 1/4 ≤ 1, 2n²s ≤ 1/6 ≤ 1, and (using eu − 1 ≤ 2u for 0 ≤ u ≤ 1) both n(exp(3n²s) − 1) ≤ 6n³s ≤ 1/2 and 2n³(exp(2n²s) − 1) ≤ 8n⁵s ≤ 1, while any two elements y₁, y₂ ∈ Θ([0, s]n²) satisfy ‖y₁y₂−1 − 1‖op ≤ exp(2n²s) − 1 ≤ 4n²s ≤ 1 and hence ‖y₁y₂−1‖op ≤ 2.

Projective endomorphism reduction and the congruence level index

Why does passing from the full finite-adelic lattice stabilizer K₀ to the principal congruence subgroup Kg of integer level g ≥ 1 multiply the quotient covolume by at most g2N = g2n²f, even when the reference 𝒪E-lattice L ⊂ En is not free over 𝒪E? Over a CM field E of nontrivial class group, L need not admit a global 𝒪E-basis, and elements of K₀ act on the finite-adelic completion Lfin rather than on L itself. Because 𝒪E is a Dedekind domain, however, every finitely generated torsion-free 𝒪E-module L is projective. Combining finite-adele strong approximation on a finite generating set with projectivity of L lifts the modulo-g action of each x ∈ K₀ to a global 𝒪E-linear endomorphism φ(x) ∈ End𝒪E(L). Left cosets of Kg in K₀ then inject into End𝒪E(L)/g End𝒪E(L), whose underlying free ℤ-module has rank at most dimℚ Mn(E) = 2n²f.

Fix an automorphic Hermitian datum of rank n ≥ 0 over a CM extension E/F of real degree f = [F : ℚ] (so [E : ℚ] = 2f), with reference finitely generated full 𝒪E-lattice L ⊂ En and finite-adelic completion Lfin = (E ⊗F 𝒪̂F) · ι(L) inside (E ⊗F 𝔸F,fin)n, where ι denotes the diagonal inclusion and 𝒪̂F = ∏v∤∞ 𝒪F,v. Let K₀ ⊂ G(𝔸F,fin) be the compact finite-adelic stabilizer of Lfin, let Kg = {x ∈ K₀ : (x − 1)Lfin ⊆ g Lfin} be the congruence level subgroup for an integer g ≥ 1, and let μfin be any Haar measure on G(𝔸F,fin), writing kg = μfin(Kg) and N = n²f.

Theorem (Congruence level index bound). For every integer g ≥ 1, the subgroup Kg contains an open neighborhood of the identity in G(𝔸F,fin), satisfies 0 < kg ≤ μfin(K₀) < ∞, and obeys

μfin(K₀) ≤ g2n²f μfin(Kg) = g2Nkg.

Proof. In the finite-adele congruence topology, K₀ is compact, whence μfin(K₀) < ∞, and the condition (x − 1)Lfin ⊆ g Lfin cuts out an open subgroup Kg ⊆ K₀, whence kg = μfin(Kg) > 0. Because L ⊂ En is a finitely generated torsion-free module over the Dedekind domain 𝒪E, L is projective over 𝒪E. For each x ∈ K₀, approximating the finite-adelic coordinates of x · ι(y) ∈ Lfin modulo g(E ⊗F 𝒪̂F) by elements of 𝒪E on a finite generating set and lifting along a projective splitting produces a global endomorphism φ(x) ∈ End𝒪E(L) such that x · ι(z) − ι(φ(x)(z)) ∈ g Lfin for all z ∈ L. Whenever x, y ∈ K₀ map to the same coset in End𝒪E(L)/g End𝒪E(L), writing φ(x) − φ(y) = gθ with θ ∈ End𝒪E(L) gives (x − y)ι(z) ∈ g Lfin for every z ∈ L, hence (x − y)Lfin ⊆ g Lfin by (E ⊗F 𝒪̂F)-linearity; since y−1 ∈ K₀ stabilizes Lfin and g Lfin, one obtains (y−1x − 1)Lfin ⊆ g Lfin, so y−1x ∈ Kg and x ∈ y Kg. Covering K₀ by at most |End𝒪E(L)/g End𝒪E(L)| left translates of Kg and bounding the ℤ-rank of End𝒪E(L) by 2n²f yields μfin(K₀) ≤ g2n²fμfin(Kg).

Projective splitting lift and ℤ-rank bound on the lattice endomorphism ring

Choose generators u₁, …, us ∈ L over 𝒪E and let π : 𝒪Es → L be the surjection sending the jth standard basis vector ej to uj. Projectivity of L over 𝒪E supplies an 𝒪E-linear section σ : L → 𝒪Es with π ∘ σ = idL. Given x ∈ K₀ and 1 ≤ j ≤ s, the vector x · ι(uj) ∈ Lfin writes as ∑k=1s αjk ι(uk) with αjk ∈ E ⊗F 𝒪̂F. By simultaneous finite-adele approximation, there exist ajk ∈ 𝒪E with αjk − ajk ∈ g(E ⊗F 𝒪̂F), so wj = ∑k=1s ajkuk ∈ L satisfies x · ι(uj) − ι(wj) ∈ g Lfin. Defining ψ : 𝒪Es → L by ψ(ej) = wj and putting φ(x) = ψ ∘ σ ∈ End𝒪E(L), every z ∈ L satisfies σ(z) = ∑j=1s bjej (bj ∈ 𝒪E) with ∑j bjuj = π(σ(z)) = z, whence x · ι(z) − ι(φ(x)(z)) = ∑j=1s bj(x · ι(uj) − ι(wj)) ∈ g Lfin.

To bound |End𝒪E(L)/g End𝒪E(L)|, select a maximal E-linearly independent tuple v₁, …, vm ∈ L (so m = dimE(E · L) ≤ n). For every z ∈ L, clearing denominators in an E-linear expression for z produces a nonzero c ∈ 𝒪E such that cz ∈ ∑j=1m 𝒪Evj; since L ⊂ En is torsion-free over 𝒪E, any θ ∈ End𝒪E(L) satisfying θ(v₁) = ⋯ = θ(vm) = 0 satisfies cθ(z) = θ(cz) = 0 and hence θ(z) = 0. Thus θ ↦ (θ(v₁), …, θ(vm)) embeds End𝒪E(L) ℤ-linearly and injectively into (En)m ≃ Em×n, a ℚ-vector space of dimension mn[E : ℚ] ≤ n²(2f) = 2n²f. Because L is finitely generated over ℤ, End𝒪E(L) is a finitely generated torsion-free abelian group—hence free over ℤ—of rank r ≤ 2n²f, so |End𝒪E(L)/g End𝒪E(L)| = gr ≤ g2n²f.

Fundamental-domain covolume and the absolute unitary volume bound

How does one bound the global fundamental-domain measure μ(𝒟) of an indefinite unitary group of signature (p, q) (p + q = n ≥ 8, q² ≤ n) without re-entering the noncompact cusp geometry? Rather than packing coordinates on the quotient Γ ∖ G(𝔸F), the proof descends by successive one-vector stabilizer steps from signature (p, q) down to a positive-definite group of signature (2, 0), picking up root-discriminant powers and convergent unramified Euler factors across ranks 3 ≤ M ≤ n. At signature (2, 0), the archimedean factor U(2)f is compact of Riemannian volume (8π³)f, and the double cosets Γ ∖ G(𝔸F) / (G∞ × K₀) are controlled by isometry classes of rank-two positive-definite unimodular Hermitian lattices over 𝒪E. Every such plane lattice contains a nonzero vector of length at most d at every complex place; splitting the plane orthogonally along that short vector reduces the class count to multiplying bounds for a real-generated scale ideal, two rank-one Hermitian line classes, and the unimodular gluings above their orthogonal sum.

1. Quotient covolume and definite double cosets. For any automorphic Hermitian datum over E/F with discrete countable rational group Γ = G(F) ⊂ G(𝔸F) and left-invariant product Haar measure μ = μ∞ × μfin (with σ-finite Haar measure μfin and μ∞ = ∏v μv, positive on nonempty open subsets), let 𝒟 ⊆ G(𝔸F) be a measurable fundamental domain for the left action of Γ. Discreteness and countability of Γ identify the quotient covolume of Γ ∖ A with μ(A ∩ 𝒟) for every Γ-left-invariant measurable subset A ⊆ G(𝔸F); in particular, covol(Γ, μ, G(𝔸F)) = μ(𝒟) > 0 since G(𝔸F) = ⋃γ∈Γ γ−1𝒟 has positive Haar measure.

Suppose now that the datum has definite signature (r, 0) at every real place v of F (with f = [F : ℚ] ≥ 1), let cv > 0 be the Riemannian Haar gauge constant of μv on U(r) so that cvμv(U(r)) = vol(U(r)) and P∞ = ∏v cv, and write hdef(E, r) for the number of 𝒪E-isometry classes of rank-r positive-definite unimodular Hermitian lattices. By the self-dual point-lattice correspondence, whenever two adelic points x, y ∈ G(𝔸F) carry E-isometric Hermitian lattices Λx and Λy, an isometry γ ∈ Γ maps Λy onto Λx, so γ−1x and y share the same rational lattice and hence the same finite-adelic completion, placing x ∈ Γ y (G∞ × K₀). Summing over at most hdef(E, r) double-coset representatives bounds μ(𝒟) ≤ (∏v μv(U(r))) · hdef(E, r) · μfin(K₀); multiplying by P∞ and dividing by μfin(K₀) > 0 yields

P∞ covol(Γ, μ, G(𝔸F)) / μfin(K₀) ≤ vol(U(r))f hdef(E, r).

2. Rank-two definite class bound and inductive assembly. Let E/F be a CM extension of degree [E : ℚ] = 2f (f ≥ 1), compatible with complex conjugation and unramified at all finite places as in the flag datum, with root discriminant d = |disc E|1/(2f) = 2νE ≥ 1.

Proposition (Rank-two definite unimodular Hermitian class bound). The number of 𝒪E-isometry classes of rank-two positive-definite unimodular Hermitian lattices satisfies

hdef(E, 2) ≤ 4f d5f (1 + f log d)5f.
Short-vector plane splitting, real-generated scale ideals, and unimodular line gluings

Let (Λ, J) be any rank-two positive-definite unimodular Hermitian lattice over 𝒪E. Minkowski’s convex-body theorem on the rank-two unimodular lattice over 𝒪E produces a nonzero vector u ∈ Λ such that Re φ(u*Ju) = φ(u*Ju) ≤ d at every complex embedding φ : E ↪ ℂ. Writing v = u*J = (v₀, v₁) in E² and setting w = (−v₁, v₀)⊤ ∈ (E · u)⊥, the orthogonal basis (u, w) diagonalizes the Hermitian form to diag(β₁, β₂) with totally positive scalars β₁, β₂ ∈ F. Intersecting Λ with the two orthogonal lines yields rank-one positive-definite Hermitian sublattices (N₁, β₁) and (N₂, β₂) in E, and unimodularity of Λ forces both lines to share the same nonzero real-generated scale ideal 𝔫 = 𝔫₀𝒪E ⊆ 𝒪E (for a nonzero ideal 𝔫₀ ⊆ 𝒪F), of absolute norm NE/ℚ(𝔫) = NF/ℚ(𝔫₀)² ≤ (df)² = d2f.

Each rank-two isometry class is therefore reconstructed from four finite choices: (i) the nonzero ideal 𝔫₀ ⊆ 𝒪F of norm NF/ℚ(𝔫₀) ≤ Y = df, for which divisor counting over F of degree f gives at most Y(1 + log Y)f = df(1 + f log d)f possibilities for 𝔫; (ii) and (iii) the rank-one Hermitian classes of (N₁, β₁) and (N₂, β₂) with fixed scale 𝔫, each parameterized by the ideal class of Ni in Cl(𝒪E) together with the parity in (𝒪E×/μ(E)) ⊗ (ℤ/2ℤ) ≃ (ℤ/2ℤ)f−1 of the unit ratio u = βi/(āβi⁰a) ∈ 𝒪E× against a class representative (Ni⁰, βi⁰) = (aNi, βi⁰)—since u = wζv² (ζ ∈ μ(E), v ∈ 𝒪E×) and total positivity force u = w v̄ v via |φ(ζ)| = 1 (at most 2f−1hE ≤ 2fhE classes per line, with Minkowski class-number bound hE ≤ df(1 + f log d)2f, hence at most (2fdf(1 + f log d)2f)² = 4fd2f(1 + f log d)4f line pairs); and (iv) the unimodular overlattice M ⊇ N₁ ⊕ N₂ inside 𝔫−1N₁ ⊕ 𝔫−1N₂, determined by the graph of an 𝒪E-module isomorphism between 𝔫−1N₁/N₁ and 𝔫−1N₂/N₂, which admits at most |𝒪E/𝔫| = NE/ℚ(𝔫) ≤ d2f gluings. Multiplying the four bounds yields 4fd5f(1 + f log d)5f.

Theorem (Absolute unitary covolume and fundamental-domain volume bound). Let the automorphic Hermitian datum of rank n = p + q ≥ 8 over E/F have common archimedean signature (p, q) satisfying q² ≤ n, and let X ≥ 2 be any real number satisfying d ≤ X and 2f d ≤ X². Then

P∞ covol(Γ, μ, G(𝔸F)) / μfin(K₀) ≤ Xn²f.

In particular, under the indefinite counting hypotheses (n ≥ 8, f ≥ 1, q ≥ 1, q² ≤ n, d ≥ 1, g ≥ 1, b ≥ 2) with N = n²f and X = nf d g b ≥ 16, combining this bound with covol(Γ, μ, G(𝔸F)) = μ(𝒟) and the congruence level index μfin(K₀) ≤ g2Nkg proves

μ(𝒟) ≤ XN g2N kg / P∞,

completing the covolume estimate (written μ(F) in the deep-cusp normalization pair).

Rank-step induction from signature (2, 0) and absorption of constants into Xn²f

Because n ≥ 8 and q² ≤ n, one has q ≤ n − 2 (indeed, at n = 8 even q ≤ 6 suffices, and (n − 1)² > n for all n ≥ 8), so p = n − q ≥ 2 and signature (p, q) dominates (2, 0) componentwise. Applying the ordered rank-step volume ratio between the datum of signature (p, q) and a same-kind definite datum of signature (2, 0) expresses the ratio of their gauge-normalized covolumes as ∏M=3n d(M−1/2)f LM, where LM is the unramified rank-M Euler product of the rank-product formula. At each base prime 𝔭 ∣ p of norm Q = pf𝔭 ≥ p, both (1 − Q−M)−1 and (1 − (−1)MQ−M)−1 are at most (1 − p−M)−f𝔭, so ∑𝔭∣p f𝔭 ≤ f gives 0 < LM ≤ ζ(M)f for every M ≥ 3; bounding ∑m>K m−M ≤ (K + 1)2−M/K by telescoping comparison at K = 30 for 3 ≤ M ≤ 8 (giving ∏M=38 ζ(M) ≤ 1.391) and at K = 2 for M = k + 9 ≥ 9 (giving ζ(k + 9) ≤ 1 + 0.00235 · 2−k) yields ∏M=3n ζ(M) ≤ 1.398 < 1.4 and hence ∏M=3n LM ≤ (1.4)f. At signature (2, 0), by the compact unitary sphere-volume formula the unit spheres in ℂ¹ and ℂ² have areas 2π and 2π², so with the normalization factor 22(2−1)/2 = 2 one has vol(U(2)) = 2 · (2π) · (2π²) = 8π³. Multiplying the base bound vol(U(2))fhdef(E, 2) ≤ (32π³)fd5f(1 + f log d)5f by df(n²−4)/2(1.4)f gives

P∞ covol(Γ, μ, G(𝔸F)) / μfin(K₀) ≤ (44.8 π³)f df(n²+6)/2 (1 + f log d)5f.

Since d ≥ 1 and f ≥ 1, log d ≤ d − 1 implies 1 + f log d ≤ 1 + f(d − 1) ≤ f d < 2f d ≤ X², whence (1 + f log d)⁵ ≤ X¹⁰. Moreover d ≤ X and 44.8 π³ < 1400 < 2¹¹ ≤ X¹¹ because X ≥ 2.
