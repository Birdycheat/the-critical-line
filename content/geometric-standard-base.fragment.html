How do the field labels determine the base of the tower?

The level and its operators are defined using a field F in the group datum. The tower is to be taken over the CM field E of the automorphic representation. Fix the supplied field isomorphism η from F to E. Before a geometric fibre power can be chosen we need a root ρ in E and a map from the integers of F into E for each label of the datum. They specify how the coefficient field acts on the base. In this construction the datum has a single label. The unitary rank can still be any of the positive odd ranks under consideration.

Standard base lemma. Let ω be the integral element in the datum, with ω + ω̄ = t and ωω̄ = n. When there is one label, the standard base exists over E. Its root and labelled homomorphism are given by the following formulas.

ρ = η(ω),   t₀(y) = η(y)   for y ∈ 𝒪F.

Indeed, expand ω² − (ω + ω̄)ω + ωω̄ to obtain zero. Applying η gives ρ² − tρ + n = 0, since a field isomorphism fixes the rational integers. The map t₀ sends ω to ρ by construction. For a general datum, the differences of the maps belonging to two distinct labels must generate the unit ideal. A single label has no distinct partner, so all conditions on the base are now verified.

These equalities are used again after choosing an embedding τ of E into ℂ and its field automorphism σ from the weight test. That test arranges σ(τ(η(y))) = τ₀(y) for the distinguished embedding τ₀ of F. For any integral y this verifies the required embedding of t₀(y). Setting y = ω verifies the required image of ρ. The Hodge comparison can therefore be applied with both labels fixed once the geometric fibre power and its comparison theorem are available.
