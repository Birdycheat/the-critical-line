Unipotent exponentials, scalar fixed-cycle blocks and Noetherian coset extraction

The cycle perturbation, row-identity transfer, and open-coset density steps of the rank-extraction argument rest on four clean algebraic lemmas that tie the formal tangent space to the global Zariski closure and the topology of a compact group. First, a square-zero tangent matrix has a linear formal exponential, placing every elementary unipotent factor directly inside the Zariski closure. Second, an annihilator of degree at most n eliminates Jordan blocks on every weight space fixed by a permutation of the projectors, forcing scalar action there. Third, adjoining a group image that normalizes a matrix subalgebra yields a multiplicatively closed linear span. Fourth, compactness of the group together with Noetherianity of the induced Zariski topology turns any nonempty open vanishing locus of a matrix polynomial into a coset of a finite-index normal subgroup.

Theorem (Unipotent Zariski membership, scalar fixed blocks, normalized spans and finite-index cosets). Let C be a field (algebraically closed of characteristic zero for the spectral statement) and let N, n ≥ 0 be integers.

Unipotent exponentials. Let S ⊆ MN(C) be a matrix submonoid whose Zariski tangent space satisfies formal exponential vanishing: for every tangent matrix E and every polynomial f vanishing on S, f(exp(sE)) = 0 in C[[s]]. If E2 = 0, then the formal exponential truncates and places I + E in the Zariski closure S̄:

exp(sE) = I + sE   ⇒   f(I + E) = 0   for all f vanishing on S.

Scalar action on fixed weight spaces. Let p0, …, pn−1 ∈ MN(C) be pairwise orthogonal nonzero idempotents summing to IN, let y ∈ MN(C) permute them via ypj = pπ(j)y, and suppose y is annihilated by a nonzero polynomial of degree at most n. Assume further that distinct cycles of π share no common eigenvalue of y and that y admits n distinct eigenvalues λ0, …, λn−1 with nonzero eigenvectors supported on the corresponding cycles. Then on every weight space fixed by π,

π(i) = i   ⇒   ypi = λi pi.

Normalized matrix spans and Noetherian coset extraction. If a subalgebra A ⊆ MN(C) is normalized by a representation ρ : Γ → GLN(C), meaning ρ(g) A ρ(g−1) ⊆ A for every g ∈ Γ, then the linear span of {a ρ(g) : a ∈ A, g ∈ Γ} is closed under multiplication:

(a ρ(g))(b ρ(k)) = (a · ρ(g) b ρ(g−1)) ρ(gk) ∈ spanC(A · ρ(Γ)).

Finally, let Γ be a compact topological group, r : Γ → GLn(C) a matrix representation, and f ∈ C[Xi,j] a matrix polynomial vanishing on a nonempty open set O ⊆ Γ. Assuming the linear polynomial pullback property, there exist a finite-index normal subgroup U ⊴ Γ and an element g ∈ Γ such that f(r(gu)) = 0 for all u ∈ U. Together with matrix-unit, idempotent-conjugacy, unit-factorization, and full scalar-corner constructions, these results assemble the representation-realization, semisimplicity, matrix-units, full-corner, and key-component instances.

Proof of unipotent membership, scalar fixed blocks, normalized spans and Noetherian coset extraction

Truncation of square-zero exponentials. When E2 = 0, induction gives Ek = 0 for every k ≥ 2. Every matrix entry of the formal series exp(sE) = ∑k≥0 (k!)−1 Ek sk therefore agrees coefficient by coefficient with the linear polynomial gp(s) = Ip + Ep s ∈ C[s]. Because the canonical inclusion C[s] ↪ C[[s]] is an injective algebra homomorphism, the vanishing f(exp(sE)) = 0 in C[[s]] implies f(I + sE) = 0 in C[s]. Evaluating at s = 1 yields f(I + E) = 0, placing I + E in the Zariski closure of S.

Scalar action on fixed weight spaces. Let q ∈ C[T] be a nonzero polynomial of degree at most n with q(y) = 0. Applying q(y) to each nonzero eigenvector vj with eigenvalue λj gives q(λj) vj = 0, so all n distinct scalars λ0, …, λn−1 are roots of q. Hence P(T) = ∏j=0n−1 (T − λj) divides q; comparing degrees deg P = n ≥ deg q shows that q is a nonzero scalar multiple of P, whence P(y) = 0. Fix i with π(i) = i. Then y commutes with pi, and so does every polynomial in y. We prove by induction on any finite subset S ⊆ {0, …, n − 1} not containing i that every vector w satisfying pi w = w and (∏j∈S (y − λj I)) w = 0 must vanish. The empty set gives w = 0 directly. For S = {a} ∪ s (with a ≠ i, a ∉ s), set w′ = (∏j∈s (y − λj I)) w. If w′ = 0 the induction hypothesis yields w = 0. Otherwise (y − λa I) w′ = 0 makes w′ a nonzero λa-eigenvector of y. Because pi commutes with ∏j∈s (y − λj I) and pk pi = 0 for all k ≠ i, one has pk w′ = 0 whenever k is not in the fixed cycle {i}; this contradicts cycle-spectrum disjointness against the λa-eigenvector va supported on the cycle of a ≠ i. Setting S = {0, …, n − 1} ∖ {i} and w = (y − λi I) pi u for any u ∈ CN satisfies pi w = w and (∏j≠i (y − λj I)) w = P(y) pi u = 0, forcing (y − λi I) pi = 0, i.e., ypi = λi pi.

Normalized spans and Noetherian Zariski cosets. For generators a ρ(g) and b ρ(k) with a, b ∈ A, inserting ρ(g−1) ρ(g) = I rewrites their product as (a · ρ(g) b ρ(g−1)) ρ(gk), where ρ(g) b ρ(g−1) ∈ A by normality; bilinearity extends closure under multiplication to the full span. Now let Γ be compact, O ⊆ Γ nonempty open, and f ∈ C[Xi,j] vanish on r(O). Pick o ∈ O; the open sets {x ∈ Γ : ax ∈ O} cover Γ (take a = ox−1), so compactness produces a finite subset s ⊂ Γ such that every x ∈ Γ satisfies ax ∈ O for some a ∈ s. Endow Γ with the topology τ pulled back from Spec(C[Xi,j]) along g ↦ ker(evr(g)). Because C[Xi,j] is Noetherian, Spec(C[Xi,j]) and hence (Γ, τ) is a Noetherian topological space with finitely many irreducible components, and the zero locus Z = {g ∈ Γ : f(r(g)) = 0} is τ-closed. By linear polynomial pullback along left multiplication by r(h), every left translation x ↦ hx is τ-continuous and therefore permutes the finite set of τ-irreducible components. Let X be the τ-irreducible component containing 1 ∈ Γ. Its Γ-orbit is finite, so its stabilizer has finite index in Γ, and the normal core U = ⋂h∈Γ h stabΓ(X) h−1 is a normal subgroup of finite index. Since X ⊆ Γ = ⋃a∈s a−1 Z is covered by finitely many τ-closed sets, irreducibility yields a ∈ s with X ⊆ a−1 Z. Every u ∈ U fixes X, whence u = u·1 ∈ X ⊆ a−1 Z and therefore f(r(au)) = 0 for all u ∈ U.

Worked check of Jordan-block exclusion. Take n = 2, N = 3, projectors p0 = diag(1, 1, 0) and p1 = diag(0, 0, 1), and π = id. The matrix y = diag(J2(2), 3) with 2 × 2 Jordan block J2(2) has disjoint fixed-cycle spectra {2} and {3}, yet fails the degree bound because its minimal polynomial (T − 2)2(T − 3) has degree 3 > 2. Once a degree-2 annihilator is imposed, (y − 2I)(y − 3I) = 0; for every u ∈ im(p0), (y − 3I)((y − 2I)u) = 0 forces (y − 2I)u = 0 because 3 is not an eigenvalue on im(p0), yielding yp0 = 2p0. Similarly, for E = ((0, 1), (0, 0)) one has E2 = 0 and exp(sE) = I + sE = ((1, s), (0, 1)), placing ((1, 1), (0, 1)) directly in the Zariski closure.

Linear chain rule, invariant tangent derivations and Lagrange spectral projectors

Every algebraic manipulation of Zariski closures and tangent spaces in the rank-extraction pipeline translates matrix multiplication into multivariate polynomial substitutions. To avoid ad hoc coordinate calculations, one isolates three universal polynomial identities: an exact chain rule for pulling matrix polynomials back along arbitrary linear maps, the equivalence between tangency at the identity of a matrix submonoid and preservation of its vanishing ideal by right-invariant derivations, and the construction of orthogonal spectral projectors via Lagrange interpolation on a degree-bounded matrix with n distinct eigenvalues. Paired with the lacunary-weight condition (unequal means on all disjoint nonempty subsets of eigenvalues), these supply the entire Lie-spectrum and tangent-algebra structure package.

Proposition (Linear chain rule, right-invariant derivations, Lagrange projectors and Lie structure). Let C be a field and N, M, n ≥ 0 integers. Write dxf(Y) = ∑p (∂p f)(x) Yp for the directional derivative of a matrix polynomial f ∈ C[Xi,j] at x along Y.

Linear polynomial chain rule. For every C-linear map φ : MN(C) → MM(C) and every f ∈ C[Xi,j] on M × M matrices, there exists a polynomial f′ on MN(C) satisfying, for all x, Y ∈ MN(C),

f′(x) = f(φ(x))   and   dxf′(Y) = dφ(x)f(φ(Y)).

Right-invariant tangent derivations. For any matrix Y ∈ MN(C), let DY be the derivation on C[Xi,j] sending the coordinate Xi,j to (YX)i,j. Then (DY f)(x) = dxf(Yx) for all f, x. Consequently, for any matrix submonoid S ⊆ MN(C) with vanishing ideal ℐ(S),

dIf(Y) = 0   (∀f ∈ ℐ(S))   ⇔   DY(ℐ(S)) ⊆ ℐ(S).

Lagrange spectral projectors and lacunary Lie structure. If X ∈ MN(C) is annihilated by a nonzero polynomial of degree at most n and has n distinct eigenvalues e0, …, en−1 ∈ C, then its minimal polynomial is ∏k=0n−1 (T − ek), and the Lagrange interpolation matrices pi = bi(X) with bi(T) = ∏j≠i (T − ej)/(ei − ej) satisfy

pi2 = pi ≠ 0,   pi pj = 0   (i ≠ j),   ∑i=0n−1 pi = IN,   X = ∑i=0n−1 ei pi.

Moreover, every eigenvector yv = μv obeys ym v = μm v and Q(y) v = Q(μ) v for all Q ∈ C[T]. Together with the lacunary-weight condition |B| ∑i∈A ei ≠ |A| ∑j∈B ej for disjoint nonempty subsets A, B, specialization to the coefficient field K, and the global degree bound, this assembles the Lie-spectrum, global degree, and complete tangent-algebra structure theorems.

Proof of the linear chain rule, tangent derivation equivalence and Lagrange projectors

Linear chain rule by polynomial induction. Decompose any matrix x ∈ MN(C) in the matrix-unit basis Er as x = ∑r xr Er; linearity gives φ(x)q = ∑r φ(Er)q xr. Define the linear coordinate polynomials ℓq = ∑r φ(Er)q Xr ∈ C[Xr] and set f′ = f(ℓ). Immediately ℓq(x) = φ(x)q and dxℓq(Y) = ∑r φ(Er)q Yr = φ(Y)q, so f′(x) = f(φ(x)). Because dx(fg)(Y) = f(x) dxg(Y) + g(x) dxf(Y) by the Leibniz rule for partial derivatives, induction over constants, sums, and multiplication by coordinate variables Xq yields dxf′(Y) = dφ(x)f(φ(Y)) for all f.

Right-invariant derivations and tangency. On constants both (DY a)(x) and dxa(Yx) vanish; on a coordinate variable Xq both equal (Yx)q; both sides are additive and obey the Leibniz product rule, so induction on f gives (DY f)(x) = dxf(Yx). Suppose now that dIg(Y) = 0 for all g ∈ ℐ(S), and fix f ∈ ℐ(S) and s ∈ S. Applying the linear chain rule to right multiplication φ(x) = xs produces f′ with f′(x) = f(xs). Because S is closed under multiplication, f′ ∈ ℐ(S), so 0 = dIf′(Y) = dsf(Ys) = (DY f)(s); hence DY f ∈ ℐ(S). Conversely, if DY(ℐ(S)) ⊆ ℐ(S), then evaluating at I ∈ S gives dIf(Y) = (DY f)(I) = 0.

Minimal polynomial and Lagrange projectors. Every eigenvalue ek of X is a root of the minimal polynomial mX(T). Since the linear factors T − ek are pairwise coprime, P(T) = ∏k=0n−1 (T − ek) divides mX. On the other hand, mX divides the given nonzero annihilator of degree at most n, so deg mX ≤ n = deg P. Both polynomials are monic, forcing mX = P. Consequently any polynomial f ∈ C[T] vanishing at all ek is divisible by mX and satisfies f(X) = 0. Since the Lagrange basis polynomials obey bi(ek) = δi,k, evaluating bi2 − bi, bi bj (i ≠ j), ∑i bi − 1, and T − ∑i ei bi at X gives all the projector identities and X = ∑i ei pi. Finally, if pi = bi(X) were zero, then mX would divide the nonzero polynomial bi, contradicting deg mX = n > n − 1 = deg bi. The spectral identities ym v = μm v and Q(y) v = Q(μ) v follow by immediate induction on m and polynomial degree, and wiring these pieces with specialization, grading, cycle counts, scalar corners, and semisimplicity constructs the full tangent-algebra structure.

Worked check of lacunarity, Lagrange projectors and invariant derivations. For n = 3, the weights e = (0, 1, 4) are lacunary: the singleton means 0, 1, 4 are distinct, and against the complementary pairs one checks 2·0 ≠ 5, 2·1 ≠ 4, and 2·4 ≠ 1 (whereas (0, 1, 2) fails because 2·1 = 0 + 2). On X = diag(0, 1, 4, 4), the minimal polynomial is T(T − 1)(T − 4) and the Lagrange polynomials b0(T) = (T − 1)(T − 4)/4, b1(T) = −T(T − 4)/3, and b2(T) = T(T − 1)/12 evaluate to p0 = diag(1, 0, 0, 0), p1 = diag(0, 1, 0, 0), and the rank-2 projector p2 = diag(0, 0, 1, 1), summing to I4 with 0·p0 + 1·p1 + 4·p2 = X. For S = SL2 cut out by f(x) = det(x) − 1, one computes (DY f)(x) = dxf(Yx) = tr(Y) det(x), which vanishes on SL2 if and only if tr(Y) = dIf(Y) = 0.

Zariski multiplicative closure, semisimple reverse embedding and unconditional rank-extraction assembly

Four final algebraic mechanisms convert the extracted n-dimensional representation r into the full unconditional lacunary rank-extraction theorem: two-stage polynomial pullback closes the Zariski closure of any matrix submonoid under multiplication; an injective intertwiner A with left inverse B and spanning intertwiners transfers semisimplicity and polynomial annihilation cleanly between ρ and r; root-multiset comparison matches the characteristic polynomial of r(s) to the prescribed annihilator Ps under either separability or product-genericity; and a constructive idempotent kernel-descent argument writes IN as a finite sum of rank factorizations through r, building the reverse embedding ρ ↪ r⊕k. Assembling all thirteen verified instances discharges the lacunary rank-extraction theorem unconditionally.

Theorem (Zariski multiplication, subrepresentation descent, polynomial matching, reverse embedding and unconditional assembly). Let K be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero, K′/K an algebraically closed extension, and Γ a compact topological group.

Zariski multiplicative closure. For any matrix submonoid S ⊆ MN(K) and its Zariski closure S̄,

x, y ∈ S̄   ⇒   xy ∈ S̄.

Subrepresentation descent and characteristic-polynomial matching. Let ρ : Γ → GLN(K) be semisimple, let r : Γ → GLn(K) admit an intertwiner A ∈ MN×n(K) (ρ(g)A = Ar(g)) with linear left inverse B (BA = In), and assume spanning: every Z ∈ MN(K) satisfying ZT = 0 for all intertwiners T : r → ρ vanishes. Then r is semisimple, rank(A) = n, r(g) = B ρ(g) A, and for every g ∈ Γ and q ∈ K[T], q(ρ(g)) = 0 if and only if q(r(g)) = 0. Furthermore, if x ∈ Mn(K) is annihilated by a monic degree-n polynomial P ∈ K[T], then the characteristic polynomial χx satisfies

χx = P   whenever   (χx is separable)   or   (P is product-generic and det(x) = (−1)n P(0)).

Semisimple reverse embedding. Under the same semisimplicity and spanning hypotheses on ρ and r, there exist an integer k ≥ 0 and equivariant matrices tj : ρ → r and t′j : r → ρ (1 ≤ j ≤ k) such that

∑j=1k t′j tj = IN,   yielding an equivariant injection   ρ ↪ r⊕k.

Unconditional lacunary rank extraction. Combining all thirteen verified components—Lie-spectrum specialization, global degree bound, tangent-algebra structure, key cycle-perturbation package, full scalar corners, semisimplicity, matrix units, representation realization, dense algebraic hull, subrepresentation descent, open-dense simple-spectrum locus, polynomial matching, and reverse embedding—discharges the lacunary rank-extraction theorem unconditionally: for every continuous semisimple ρ : Γ → GLN(K), dense subset S ⊆ Γ with monic degree-n annihilators Ps(ρ(s)) = 0, continuous character ψ matching (−1)n Ps(0) on S, and Zariski Lie-algebra matrix X ∈ MN(K′) with n distinct lacunary eigenvalues, there exists a continuous semisimple representation r : Γ → GLn(K) with r ↪ ρ, ρ ↪ r⊕k, χr(g)(ρ(g)) = 0, Ps(r(s)) = 0, det(r) = ψ, open dense separable-spectrum locus, and χr(s) = Ps at every s ∈ S where χr(s) is separable or Ps is product-generic.

Proof of Zariski multiplicative closure, subrepresentation descent, polynomial matching and idempotent reverse embedding

Two-stage Zariski closure under multiplication. First fix s ∈ S and y ∈ S̄. For any polynomial f vanishing on S, the linear chain rule applied to left multiplication z ↦ sz produces f′ with f′(z) = f(sz). Because S is a submonoid, f′ vanishes on S, so 0 = f′(y) = f(sy), proving sy ∈ S̄. Next fix x, y ∈ S̄ and pull f back along right multiplication z ↦ zy to obtain f″(z) = f(zy). By the first step, ty ∈ S̄ for every t ∈ S, so f″ vanishes on S, whence 0 = f″(x) = f(xy) and xy ∈ S̄.

Subrepresentation descent and characteristic-polynomial matching. Because BA = In, A is injective and n = rank(BA) ≤ rank(A) ≤ n, while multiplying ρ(g)A = Ar(g) on the left by B yields r(g) = B ρ(g) A. For any r-invariant subspace W ⊆ Kn, its image A(W) ⊆ KN is ρ-invariant and therefore admits a ρ-invariant complement W″ in KN; its preimage W′ = A−1(W″) is r-invariant, disjoint from W by injectivity of A, and satisfies W + W′ = Kn (writing Av = Aw + z with w ∈ W and z ∈ W″ puts A(v − w) = z ∈ W″), proving semisimplicity of r. By induction on polynomial degree, any intertwiner XT = TY obeys q(X)T = Tq(Y); thus q(ρ(g)) = 0 implies q(r(g)) = Bq(ρ(g))A = 0, and conversely q(r(g)) = 0 implies q(ρ(g))T = Tq(r(g)) = 0 for every intertwiner T, whence q(ρ(g)) = 0 by spanning. Now let x ∈ Mn(K) satisfy P(x) = 0 with P monic of degree n. Spectral mapping places every root of χx in the root set of P, and algebraic closure gives both root multisets cardinality n. If χx is separable, its root multiset has no repeated entries, so set-inclusion becomes multiset inclusion of the roots of χx inside the roots of P, and equal cardinality forces equality of multisets and hence χx = P. If instead P is product-generic and det(x) = (−1)n P(0), Vieta’s formula gives the product of the roots of χx as det(x) = (−1)n (−1)n times the product of the roots of P (with (−1)2n = 1); product-genericity forbids distinct degree-n root multisets with equal products, again forcing χx = P.

Constructive reverse embedding by idempotent kernel descent. Let 𝒢 be the set of equivariant rank factorizations t′ ∘ t ∈ EndK(V) through U = Kn, where t′ : U → V and t : V → U intertwine r and ρ. Suppose E ∈ spanK(𝒢) is an equivariant idempotent with ker E ≠ 0, and set P = idV − E (an equivariant projection with P ≠ 0). By the spanning hypothesis there exists an intertwiner t′ : U → V such that M = P ∘ t′ ≠ 0. Semisimplicity of U supplies an r-invariant complement Cc of ker M in U, so M restricts to an equivariant isomorphism from Cc onto R = M(Cc) ⊆ im P = ker E. Semisimplicity of V supplies a ρ-invariant complement D of R in V, whose projection πR,D : V → R is equivariant. Composing πR,D with the inverse of M|Cc yields an intertwiner T : V → U satisfying M ∘ T = πR,D. Define H = M ∘ (T ∘ P) ∈ 𝒢; then H = πR,D ∘ P satisfies EH = HE = 0 and H2 = H, so E′ = E + H ∈ spanK(𝒢) is an equivariant idempotent with ker E′ ⊆ ker E. Since M ≠ 0, some c ∈ Cc satisfies M(c) ∈ R ∖ {0} ⊆ ker E whereas (E + H)(M(c)) = M(c) ≠ 0, giving strict descent dimK ker E′ < dimK ker E. Starting from E = 0, induction on dimK ker E ≤ N reaches ker E = 0, hence E = idV ∈ spanK(𝒢). Expressing idV = ∑j=1k fj (t′j ∘ tj) and absorbing the scalars fj into t′j yields ∑j=1k t′j tj = IN, so stacking t1, …, tk embeds ρ ↪ r⊕k. Finally, combining the dense algebraic hull and finite-index normal coset criterion establishes openness and density of the simple-spectrum locus, and plugging all thirteen verified instances into the master assembly theorem establishes the lacunary rank-extraction theorem unconditionally.

Worked check of idempotent descent and product-genericity. Let r act on U = ℂ2 and ρ = r ⊕ r act on V = ℂ4 with canonical inclusions t′1(u) = (u, 0) and t′2(u) = (0, u). Starting from E0 = 0 (kernel dimension 4), choosing t′1 gives H1 = diag(I2, 0) and E1 = diag(I2, 0) (kernel dimension 2); next P1 = diag(0, I2) and t′2 give H2 = diag(0, I2) and E2 = I4 (kernel dimension 0), yielding ρ ↪ r⊕2 in two steps.
