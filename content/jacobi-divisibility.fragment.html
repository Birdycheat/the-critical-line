Why each Jacobi sum divides the split prime

The cyclotomic construction determines a signed ideal by testing a product of Jacobi sums at the primes above p. A zero residue proves that one such prime divides a factor. How can we tell that its square does not? Each factor has an explicit partner whose product with it is p. At a split unramified prime, the order of p is one, so the nonnegative orders of the two partners sum to one. This turns the residue test into an exact exponent. Let χ and φ be multiplicative characters of 𝔽p× taking values in a characteristic-zero integral domain R, extended by zero at zero. Assume χ, φ and χφ are all nontrivial. A second orthogonality identity says Gψ(ρ)Gψ−1(ρ−1) = p for nontrivial ρ. To see why, expand the two sums and put x = uy for y ≠ 0. At each y the summand becomes ρ(u)ψ(y(u−1)). We can include y = 0 because its total is ∑uρ(u) = 0. Summing over all y now leaves only u = 1, contributing p. Apply the first identity twice, once with ψ and once with its inverse and the inverse multiplicative characters. Multiplication and the second identity give pJ(χ,φ)J(χ−1,φ−1) = p². Cancelling p and using injectivity of the two field embeddings gives the claimed identity in R. See the two factors of 7 in the Eisenstein integers

Take the level M = 3, b = 2 and p = 7. In ℤ[ω], with ω²+ω+1 = 0, choose the cubic character determined by χ(3) = ω, since 3 generates 𝔽₇×. At the prime where ω reduces to 2 it satisfies the required reduction χ(x) ≡ x². Here the Jacobi product has one factor, J(χ,χ). The inputs 0 and 1 contribute zero. For x = 2, 3, 4, 5, 6 the five contributions are ω², 1, ω², 1, ω². The sum and its inverse-character partner are 2+3ω² and 2+3ω. Their product is 4+6(ω+ω²)+9 = 7. At ω ≡ 2 modulo 7 the first is zero and the second is one. At ω ≡ 4 their roles reverse. Each factor has order exactly one at the prime where it vanishes. In the application the two characters are χ and χj with 1 ≤ j < b < M. The reduction hypothesis gives χ order M, so the exponents 1, j and 1+j meet all three nontriviality conditions. Multiplication of the individually factored ideals then adds their prime orders. These cyclotomic elements enter the reciprocity construction behind the minus character estimate.
