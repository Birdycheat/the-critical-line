How many points lie over a point of the Hecke correspondence?

At a principal level N, write ZN for the fibre power constructed above. A Hecke correspondence gives two maps from one scheme to ZN. To apply it to a point we first take all its lifts by the source map s, then take their images by the target map t. We can count the lifts before specifying where t sends them.

Degree theorem. Let K(N) ⊆ K ⊆ K(1) and let the finer principal level M be a multiple of N. Assume the admissibility conditions of the transition theorem for each translated target map. The source map is finite étale. Every point over an algebraically closed field has exactly

d = [K : K(N)] · [K(N) : K(M)] = [K : K(M)]

lifts. The space ZN is smooth over the ground field. For the compact unitary datum used here it is projective.

Proof. For each coset c in K/K(N) take one copy of ZM. On this copy s forgets level and t uses the translated element h times the chosen representative of c. The first index in the formula counts copies. It is finite since K(N) has finite index in K(1). A point above x specifies its copy c and one lift in that copy. By the fibre calculation there are exactly [K(N) : K(M)] such lifts. Summing over the copies gives the product. Each copy maps by a finite étale morphism. Their finite disjoint union is again finite étale, since over an affine open its algebra is the finite product of the algebras of the copies and étaleness can be checked on each copy.

For instance, if the two indices are 2 and 3, take a point x and label its lifts on one copy u, v, w. Above x in the whole source are the six pairs (1, u), …, (2, w). Some of their images under t can coincide. Those images then enter the correspondence with their multiplicities.

For smoothness, start with the smooth principal-level moduli scheme and its smooth abelian scheme. Taking a further fibre factor is a base change of the abelian scheme followed by composition. Both operations preserve smoothness. The zeroth power is the moduli scheme itself. For projectivity, the compact-datum moduli theorem gives a projective base. The polarization gives a relatively ample line bundle on the abelian scheme. The exterior product of its pullbacks to the factors gives a relatively ample line bundle on the fibre power. Projectivity over the base and projectivity of the base over the field now give the asserted projectivity. This uses the compactness hypothesis at the moduli step. The degree formula uses the level indices.
