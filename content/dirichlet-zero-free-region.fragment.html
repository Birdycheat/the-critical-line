Classical zero-free region for the Riemann zeta function, regional logarithmic-derivative bound, and square-root exponential remainder for the smoothed prime sum

Why does a positivity inequality among three values of −ζ′/ζ push the zeros of ζ a distance of order 1/log(|t| + 2) to the left of the line Re s = 1, and why is this distance exactly what turns a contour shift into the saving exp(−c√(log y)) used in the square-root exponential estimate for one character? For σ > 1 the series −ζ′(s)/ζ(s) = ∑ Λ(n)n−s has nonnegative coefficients, and 3 + 4 cos θ + cos 2θ = 2(1 + cos θ)2 ≥ 0, so 3 Re(−ζ′/ζ)(σ) + 4 Re(−ζ′/ζ)(σ + it) + Re(−ζ′/ζ)(σ + 2it) is never negative. Just to the right of 1, the first term is about 1/(σ − 1) because of the pole; a zero β + it with β close to 1 makes the second term contain −1/(σ − β); and the third term is only of size log(|t| + 2), because the pole is far away. Four copies of 1/(σ − β) outweigh three copies of 1/(σ − 1) as soon as σ − β < (4/3)(σ − 1), and the terms of size log(|t| + 2) cost only a fixed fraction of 1/(σ − 1) once σ − 1 is a small multiple of 1/log(|t| + 2); this balance produces the region. Both local statements, the term −1/(σ − β) and the bound of size log(|t| + 2), come from the disk lemma for logarithmic derivatives on disks of radius 1/2, where ζ grows at most polynomially. Once the zeros are excluded from Re s ≥ 1 − c/log(|Im s| + 2), the same lemma bounds ζ′/ζ + 1/(s − 1) by a constant times (log(|Im s| + 2))2 throughout the region. The smoothed prime sum S(y) is a Perron integral of −(ζ′(w)/ζ(w))yw/(w(w − 1)) along a vertical line to the right of 1; moving that line to Re w = 1 − c/log(T + 2) crosses only the double pole at w = 1, whose residue y log y − (1 + γ)y is the main term, and with T = exp(c√(log y)/4) every remaining piece is y times a negative exponential in √(log y).

For σ > 1 and real y, the real part of the n-th term of −ζ′/ζ(σ + iy) = ∑ Λ(n)n−σ−iy is a cos(y log n) with a = Λ(n)n−σ ≥ 0. For the right-of-one bound, let λ(σ) = ∑ Λ(n)n−σ = −ζ′(σ)/ζ(σ), a nonincreasing function of σ > 1 with |ζ′(s)/ζ(s)| ≤ λ(Re s). Near the pole, ζ′(σ)/ζ(σ) + 1/(σ − 1) stays bounded, say by B, for real 1 < σ < 1 + ε. If 1 < Re s < 1 + ε/2, then λ(Re s) ≤ B + 1/(Re s − 1); if Re s ≥ 1 + ε/2, then λ(Re s) ≤ λ(1 + ε/2). For Re s = σ > 0 and s ≠ 1, the telescoping identity (s − 1)ζ(s) = s − D(s) holds with D(s) = ∑n≥0 [(n + 1)1−s − (n + 2)1−s − (s − 1)(n + 2)−s]. Why does the zero-free region for Dirichlet L-functions need a second local expansion, now centred at 11/10 + it with outer radius 3/5, when the local Dirichlet expansion already expands −L′/L about 101/100 + it, and why must the multiplicities now carry a derivative witness? The 3–4–1 argument evaluates Dχ = −L′/L on the segment 1 < σ ≤ 6/5 at one height t, and needs every zero within 1/5 of 11/10 + it, in particular every zero β + it with 9/10 ≤ β < 1, to appear in the subtracted sum with weight at least 1/(σ − β). A zero disk of radius 1/5 requires the disk lemma on radius 3/5, three times larger, and the expansion is then valid on radius 1/10, which covers exactly the segment |σ − 11/10| ≤ 1/10. The mechanism is that of the local Dirichlet expansion: a lower bound at the centre from the twisted Möbius series, polynomial growth from partial summation, and the disk lemma for logarithmic derivatives; only the constants change, to |L(11/10 + it, χ)| ≥ 1/11, the growth ratio M = 22C1q(|t| + 2) and the remainder 72 log M/(3/5) = 120 log M. What is new is that the multiplicities are tied to the derivative: m(ρ) ≥ 2 when f′(ρ) = 0 and m(ρ) = 1 when f′(ρ) ≠ 0. The real part of a finite sum ∑ m(ρ)/(s − ρ) is ∑ m(ρ) Re(1/(s − ρ)). For a nonprincipal character every zero has Re ρ < 1, because L(s, χ) has no zeros in Re s ≥ 1, so each Re(1/(σ + it − ρ)) with σ > 1 is positive and the subtracted sum in (iii) is nonnegative. Compared with the local Dirichlet expansion, whose centre 101/100 + it and outer radius 2/5 give the centre bound 1/101, the remainder 72 log B/(2/5) = 180 log B, and an inner expansion disk reaching into the critical strip to Re s = 101/100 − 1/15 = 283/300 < 1, moving the centre to 11/10 + it with outer radius 3/5 yields 1/11 and 120 log M, while the inner expansion disk |s − (11/10 + it)| ≤ 1/10 reaches left only to Re s = 1 (covering 1 < σ ≤ 6/5).

For example, take f(w) = w2(w − 1/10) on the closed disk of centre 1/5 and radius 3/4. Then f(1/5) = 1/250 ≠ 0, both zeros 0 and 1/10 lie within 1/4 of the centre, and f′(w) = 2w(w − 1/10) + w2 gives f′(0) = 0 and f′(1/10) = 1/100 ≠ 0. The derivative test therefore assigns multiplicity at least 2 to 0 and exactly 1 to 1/10, matching the analytic orders 2 and 1.

Principal-character reduction, uniform principal logarithmic-derivative bound, 3–4–1 character positivity, and exclusion of nonreal and large-height real zeros near Re s = 1

Why do the zeros of L(s, χ) avoid the region Re s ≥ 1 − c/log(q(|Im s| + 2)) for every nonreal χ, and for every real χ as soon as |Im s| is not too small, while a real zero of a real character can survive close to 1? The 3–4–1 positivity of the zeta argument has a character analogue. With a = Λ(n)n−σ and w = χ(n)n−it at a unit n, where |w| = 1, one has 3a + 4a Re w + a Re w2 = 2a(1 + Re w)2 ≥ 0, so 3 Re D1q(σ) + 4 Re Dχ(σ + it) + Re Dχ2(σ + 2it) ≥ 0. The first term carries the pole of the principal L-function, about 1/(σ − 1), and a zero β + it of L(·, χ) makes the second term contain −1/(σ − β) through the local zero sum. The third term decides the outcome. If χ2 is nonprincipal, L(·, χ2) has no pole, its own local zero sum bounds the third term by a multiple of log(q(|t| + 2)), and the contradiction goes through at every height. If χ is real, then χ2 = 1q is principal, and the third term contains the pole term Re(1/(σ − 1 + 2it)) = (σ − 1)/((σ − 1)2 + 4t2), which is close to 1/(σ − 1) when |t| is small compared with σ − 1, and then no contradiction arises; but once |t| ≥ δ/log(q(|t| + 2)) and σ − 1 is a small multiple a of 1/log(q(|t| + 2)) with a ≤ δ/2, this pole term is at most 1/16 of 1/(σ − 1), and the argument closes again. The principal character itself is reduced to ζ through L(s, 1q) = ∏p∣q(1 − p−s)ζ(s).

1. Principal reduction, character positivity, and the two exclusions. Keep q ≥ 1, the characters χ, χ1, χ2 modulo q, the principal character 1q, and Dχ = −L′/L from the local zero sum. For c > 0 say that s lies in the region of width c at modulus q if Re s ≥ 1 − c/log(q(|Im s| + 2)). Let CR ≥ 0 be the right-of-one constant and C0 the constant of the local zero sum, let Cζ denote the constant C1 of the regional bound for ζ, and put K3 = ∑ Λ(n)n−6/5, Kζ = 840 + |Cζ| log 4 + |K3| and CP = Kζ + 1.

Proposition (Principal reduction, principal bound, character positivity, and nonreal and large-height exclusions).

(i) For every q ≥ 1 and every s with Re s > 0 and s ≠ 1, L(s, 1q) = 0 implies ζ(s) = 0.

(ii) For every q ≥ 1, every real t and every 1 < σ ≤ 2, Re D1q(σ + it) ≤ Re(1/(σ − 1 + it)) + CP log(q(|t| + 2)).

(iii) For every q ≥ 1 and σ > 1, all characters χ modulo q and all real t,

0 ≤ 3 Re D1q(σ) + 4 Re Dχ(σ + it) + Re Dχ2(σ + 2it),

while 0 ≤ Re D1q(σ) + Re Dχ(σ) whenever χ2 = 1q, and 0 ≤ Re D1q(σ) + Re Dχ1(σ) + Re Dχ2(σ) + Re Dχ1χ2(σ) whenever χ12 = χ22 = 1q.

(iv) There is c > 0 such that L(s, χ) ≠ 0 for every q ≥ 1, every χ modulo q with χ2 ≠ 1q, and every s in the region of width c at modulus q.

(v) For every δ > 0 there is c > 0 such that L(s, χ) ≠ 0 for every q ≥ 1, every χ modulo q with χ2 = 1q and χ ≠ 1q, and every s in the region of width c at modulus q with |Im s| ≥ δ/log(q(|Im s| + 2)).

Proof. For (iii), let σ > 1. For every character ψ modulo q and real u, Dψ(σ + iu) = ∑ ψ(n)Λ(n)n−σ−iu, with n-th term aψ(n)n−iu and a = Λ(n)n−σ ≥ 0. When gcd(n, q) > 1 every character modulo q vanishes at n. At a unit n, put w = χ(n)n−it, so |w| = 1 and χ2(n)n−2it = w2; since Re w2 = 2(Re w)2 − 1, the real part of the n-th term of the first combination is a(3 + 4 Re w + 2(Re w)2 − 1) = 2a(1 + Re w)2 ≥ 0. When χ2 = 1q, χ(n) = ±1 on units and the n-th term of the second combination is a(1 + χ(n)) ≥ 0; when χ12 = χ22 = 1q, the n-th term of the third is a(1 + χ1(n))(1 + χ2(n)) ≥ 0, which is the four-character identity. Summing the nonnegative real parts over n proves (iii).

For (i), the factorization L(s, 1q) = ∏p∣q(1 − p−s)ζ(s) holds for s ≠ 1, and when Re s > 0 no factor vanishes, because |p−s| = p−Re s < 1. For (ii), the same factorization for Re s > 1 gives Re D1q(s) ≤ Re(−ζ′/ζ)(s) + log q, since each Euler factor changes the logarithmic derivative by at most log p and ∑p∣q log p ≤ log q. It therefore suffices to show Re(−ζ′/ζ)(σ + it) ≤ Re(1/(σ − 1 + it)) + Kζ log(|t| + 2) for 1 < σ ≤ 2, because Kζ ≥ 0, log q ≥ 0 and log(q(|t| + 2)) = log q + log(|t| + 2) then yield the constant CP = Kζ + 1. The decisive regime is |t| > 2 with σ ≤ 6/5. Apply the disk lemma to ζ at z0 = 11/10 + it with radius 3/5. On that disk Re z ≥ 1/2, |z − 1| ≥ 1 and |z| ≤ |t| + 3, so the growth bound gives |ζ(z)| ≤ 6(|t| + 2), while the reciprocal bound at Re z0 = 11/10 gives |ζ(z0)| ≥ 1/11; hence M = 66(|t| + 2). Since every zero of ζ has real part less than σ, the subtracted zero sum is nonnegative, and Re(−ζ′/ζ)(σ + it) ≤ 120 log M. As |t| + 2 ≥ 4 and 46 > 66, log M ≤ 7 log(|t| + 2), so Re(−ζ′/ζ)(σ + it) ≤ 840 log(|t| + 2), to which the nonnegative term Re(1/(σ − 1 + it)) may be added. The two other regimes, |t| ≤ 2 and |t| > 2 with σ > 6/5, cost |Cζ| log 4 and |K3| (see the details below).

For (iv), put K = |C0|, A = 6K + 5CR + 1, δ = min(1/10, 1/(2A)) and c = δ/8. Suppose χ2 ≠ 1q, so χ ≠ 1q, and L(s, χ) = 0 with β = Re s ≥ 1 − c/ℓ, where ℓ = log(q(|Im s| + 2)) ≥ log 2 > 0.69. Then β < 1, and δ/(8ℓ) ≤ 1/10 gives β ≥ 9/10. Test at σ = 1 + δ/ℓ, so 1 < σ ≤ 6/5, and write u = 1/δ, so 1/(σ − 1) = ℓu. By (i) of the local zero sum, Re D1q(σ) ≤ ℓu + CR. At height Im s, the zero s lies within 1/5 of 11/10 + i Im s, so it belongs to Z and contributes m(s)/(σ − β) ≥ 1/(σ − β) to the subtracted sum, whose other terms are nonnegative; since σ − β ≤ δ/ℓ + δ/(8ℓ) = (9/8)(δ/ℓ), this gives Re Dχ(σ + i Im s) ≤ Kℓ − (8/9)ℓu. As χ2 is nonprincipal, the local zero sum for χ2 at height 2 Im s, with its nonnegative subtracted sum, gives Re Dχ2(σ + 2i Im s) ≤ K log(q(2|Im s| + 2)) ≤ 2Kℓ. Positivity (iii) at t = Im s then yields

0 ≤ 3(ℓu + CR) + 4(Kℓ − (8/9)ℓu) + 2Kℓ = 6Kℓ + 3CR − (5/9)ℓu.

But u ≥ 2A gives ℓu ≥ 12Kℓ + 10CRℓ + 2ℓ, and ℓ > 0.69 gives 3CR ≤ 5CRℓ, so (5/9)ℓu ≥ (20/3)Kℓ + (50/9)CRℓ + (10/9)ℓ > 6Kℓ + 3CR, a contradiction.

For (v), fix δ > 0 and put K = 4 max(C0, 0) + 2 max(CP, 0) + 6CR, a = min(δ/2, 1/10, 1/(4K + 1)) and c = a/8; then Ka ≤ 1/4 and a2/(4δ2) ≤ 1/16. Suppose χ2 = 1q, χ ≠ 1q, L(s, χ) = 0, and write t = Im s and L = log(q(|t| + 2)) > 1/2, with δ/L ≤ |t| and β = Re s ≥ 1 − a/(8L). Test at σ = 1 + a/L ≤ 6/5. As in (iv), σ − β ≤ (9/8)(a/L), the zero s belongs to the local zero set at height t, and Re Dχ(σ + it) ≤ C0L − (8/9)(L/a); also Re D1q(σ) ≤ L/a + CR. Because χ2 = 1q, the third term of (iii) is Re D1q(σ + 2it), which (ii) bounds by (σ − 1)/((σ − 1)2 + 4t2) + CP log(q(2|t| + 2)). Here t2 ≥ δ2/L2 gives (σ − 1)/((σ − 1)2 + 4t2) ≤ (a/L)/(4δ2/L2) = (a2/(4δ2))(L/a) ≤ (1/16)(L/a), and CP log(q(2|t| + 2)) ≤ 2 max(CP, 0)L. With 3CR ≤ 6CRL and 4C0L ≤ 4 max(C0, 0)L, the terms without the factor L/a add up to at most KL ≤ (1/4)(L/a), and positivity (iii) gives

0 ≤ 3(L/a + CR) + 4(C0L − (8/9)(L/a)) + (1/16)(L/a) + 2 max(CP, 0)L ≤ (3 − 32/9 + 1/16 + 1/4)(L/a) = −(35/144)(L/a) < 0,

a contradiction. With the zeta region, statement (i) excludes zeros of the principal character in a region of the same shape, (iv) excludes nonreal characters, and (v) excludes real characters at height at least δ/log(q(|Im s| + 2)). Real characters below that height are treated in the same-character small-height estimate, which combines all of these into one region containing at most one character–zero pair, real and simple.

Euler-factor logarithmic derivatives, the two outer regimes of the principal bound, the doubling inequality for log(q(|t| + 2)), and positivity of the zero terms

For a prime p and Re s > 1, the logarithmic derivative of 1 − p−s is log p · p−s/(1 − p−s), of absolute value at most log p · p−σ/(1 − p−σ) ≤ log p, since p−σ ≤ 1/2. Differentiating log L(s, 1q) = ∑p∣q log(1 − p−s) + log ζ(s) gives D1q(s) = −ζ′(s)/ζ(s) − ∑p∣q log p · p−s/(1 − p−s), and ∑p∣q log p is the logarithm of the product of the distinct prime divisors of q, at most log q.

In the principal bound, if |t| ≤ 2 then every s = σ + it with σ > 1 lies in the region of the regional bound for ζ, so Re(−ζ′/ζ)(s) = Re(1/(s − 1)) − Re(ζ′/ζ(s) + 1/(s − 1)) ≤ Re(1/(σ − 1 + it)) + Cζ(log(|t| + 2))2, and (log(|t| + 2))2 ≤ log 4 · log(|t| + 2). If |t| > 2 and σ > 6/5, then Re(−ζ′/ζ)(s) ≤ ∑ Λ(n)n−σ ≤ K3 ≤ |K3| log(|t| + 2), because log(|t| + 2) ≥ 1, and Re(1/(σ − 1 + it)) ≥ 0 may again be added. In the middle regime the disk bound for ζ on |z − (11/10 + it)| ≤ 3/5 uses |Im z| ≥ |t| − 3/5 > 1 for |z − 1| ≥ 1, and 1 + 1/|z − 1| + |z|(1 + 1/Re z) ≤ 2 + 3(|t| + 3) ≤ 6(|t| + 2) for the growth bound; the point σ + it lies within (3/5)/6 = 1/10 of the centre, and 72 log M/(3/5) = 120 log M.

For any real y, q(2|y| + 2) ≤ (q(|y| + 2))2, so log(q(2|y| + 2)) ≤ 2 log(q(|y| + 2)), and log(q(|y| + 2)) ≥ log 2 because q(|y| + 2) ≥ 2. For real x, y not both zero, Re(1/(x + iy)) = x/(x2 + y2). If χ ≠ 1q, L(ρ, χ) = 0 and σ > 1, then Re ρ < 1 < σ, so Re(1/(σ + it − ρ)) = (σ − Re ρ)/|σ + it − ρ|2 > 0; this is the positivity of every term of the subtracted sums used in (iv) and (v). For the disk membership in (iv), 9/10 ≤ β < 1 gives |s − (11/10 + i Im s)| = 11/10 − β ≤ 1/5; in (v), a/(8L) ≤ 1/40 gives 1 − 1/40 ≤ β < 1 and the same conclusion.
