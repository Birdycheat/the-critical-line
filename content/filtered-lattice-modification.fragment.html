Which integral lattice do the lifted columns generate?

Suppose Y has d rows and m columns over S = W[[u]]. Fix a depth n. The columns give the lattice L = im(Y) + EnSd. We seek d columns arranged in a square matrix, so that we can also control an inverse. First pass to R = S[1/ℓ]. Its elements are series with a common bound for the denominators of their coefficients.

Over R every ideal is principal. The Weierstrass preparation theorem reduces the assertion to division of polynomials. The submodule LR generated by L is therefore free. It has rank d because it contains all the columns of EnI, which span after passage to the fraction field.

Why is the module free after inverting ℓ?

Scale a nonzero series by a power of ℓ until its coefficients lie in W and some coefficient is a unit. Preparation writes it as a unit times a monic polynomial whose degree is the first index of minimal coefficient valuation. In any nonzero ideal choose such a polynomial P of least degree. Divide every other prepared polynomial of the ideal by P over Frac(W). A nonzero remainder would belong to the ideal and have prepared degree at most its ordinary degree, below deg P. Hence P generates the ideal. R. Sharifi, Iwasawa Theory, Proposition 2.2.1 and Theorem 2.2.3.

For a submodule of Rd, project onto the first coordinate. A nonzero image has a generator whose lift splits off a free summand. The kernel lies in Rd−1, so induction gives a basis, also covering the cases of zero image and rank zero.

Let B have as columns a basis of LR. Expressing the basis in the original generators gives B = YQ₀ + EnR₀. Expressing the generators in the basis gives BQ₀′ = Y and BB′ = EnI. Multiply all five coefficient and basis matrices by one denominator ℓk, naming the resulting matrices X, X′, Q, Q′ and H in place of B, B′, Q₀, Q₀′ and R₀. Since S embeds in R, we obtain the integral identities with c = 2k.

XX′ = ℓcEnI,   X = YQ + EnH,   XQ′ = ℓcY.

These identities locate the integral columns precisely. The middle one puts im(X) inside L. The first and third put ℓcL inside im(X). In particular the two modules have the same ℓ-saturation, the set of vectors of Sd some ℓ-power of which belongs to the module. For example, over ℤ3[[u]] take Y = (3), E = u − 3 and n = 1. Then L = (3, u) has quotient 𝔽3, whereas its saturation is the whole ring. The power of ℓ in the identities records exactly the kind of denominator that can occur on returning to S.

Why does the exponent of column i equal N − hi?

Fix a nonnegative height N and an invertible square period matrix U over the de Rham field, with inverse U′. Write Filr for the elements of valuation at least r, normalizing the image of E to have valuation one. Suppose U has entries in Fil−N. Let C be a d by m matrix over the ring of integers of K and β an m by d de Rham matrix. Assume UCβ = I, column i of UC lies in Filhi, row i of β lies in Fil−hi, and |hi| ≤ N. The columns and rows thus give a decomposition of the identity, even when m exceeds d.

We want integral matrices X, X′ for which every entry of UX and X′U′ reaches FilN. Use the coefficient lift at depth 2N. In the de Rham field it reads s = ℓκC + Δ, with Δ in Fil2N entrywise. To bring column i from degree hi to degree N, set ni = N − hi, an integer between 0 and 2N. Make Y by multiplying column i of s by Eni, and apply the integral basis identities with depth 2N.

The forward product. Column i of UY is the sum of ℓκ times column i of UC multiplied by Eni and the corresponding error UΔ multiplied by the same power. Their respective lower bounds are hi + ni = N and −N + 2N + ni ≥ N. Since X = YQ + E2NH and integral series have filtration at least zero, UX has entries in FilN.

The inverse product. Over the fraction field of S, the square matrices X and X′ are inverses up to their scalar product. Thus X′X = ℓcE2NI already in S. Left multiplication of XQ′ = ℓcY by X′ gives X′Y = E2NQ′ after cancellation in this domain. Column i of Y carries Eni, so column i of X′s has degree at least 2N − ni = N + hi. The error X′Δ has degree at least 2N ≥ N + hi, and ℓ is a de Rham unit. Column i of X′C therefore has the same lower bound.

UX ∈ Mat(FilN),   X′U′ = (X′C)β ∈ Mat(FilN).

The equality uses Cβ = U′, obtained by multiplying UCβ = I on the left by U′. In each summand of the last matrix product the column contributes N + hi and its paired row contributes −hi. In the two-column calculation, try heights −1 and 2 with N = 3. The forward powers are 4 and 1, and the complementary powers are 2 and 5. Exchanging the signs preserves the scalar-product identity while changing the actual orders of both period products.
