Why does the density stay constant at deeper levels?

Lift a solution modulo ϖk by writing its next representative as X+ϖkY. For k ≥ 1 the quadratic term in Y disappears modulo ϖk+1. The correction must solve the linear equation Y∗JX+X∗JY = H in the residue algebra, with H Hermitian. It is solvable for every H. For instance, since 2 and T are invertible here, take Y = ½XT−1H. The linear map has a domain with q2Mn elements and a codomain with qn² elements. Every fibre has q2Mn−n² elements. Thus Nk+1 gains exactly the factor cancelled by the next density normalization.
