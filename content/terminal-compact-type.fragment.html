Why does the stopping parameter have a discrete-series model?

The stopping type gives a regular Harish-Chandra parameter μ for H = U(r,s). To compare its coefficients with those of a discrete series, we need one such representation at μ. Its required lowest compact weight is L = μ + ρn − ρc on K = U(r) × U(s). Write n = r + s.

Number the coordinates from zero in decreasing order of μ. In La, the correction to μa is (n−1)/2 − a, minus the number of later coordinates of that sign, plus the number of earlier ones of that sign. This counts the two half-sums of roots, and μa + (n−1)/2 is an integer by the lattice condition. If a < b are consecutive positions within one sign block, the later count drops by one and the earlier count increases by one. Hence

La − Lb = μa − μb + (b−a) − 2 ≥ 0.

Indeed, successive μ-coordinates differ by at least one, so μa − μb ≥ b−a. The compact weight is therefore integral and dominant on each block. To realize it, set C = ∑a|La| and add C to every entry. In a block with shifted entries b1 ≥ ⋯ ≥ bd ≥ 0, put bd+1 = 0. Take bi − bi+1 copies of the highest vector in the ith exterior power. Each contributes one to the first i coordinates. Their tensor generates the irreducible module with highest weight (b1, …, bd). Apply this construction to both blocks and take the tensor product. Multiplying the action of (k₊, k₋) by (det k₊ det k₋)−C subtracts C from every torus exponent. This unitary scalar character preserves the invariant subspaces, giving an irreducible type of weight L. How does one determinant correct both sign blocks?

On U(2) × U(1) take the compact weight (2, −1 | −4), with the bar separating the blocks. Here C = 7, and the shifted weight is (9, 6 | 3). On U(2), three copies of the first exterior vector and six copies of the determinant vector give exponents 9 and 6. On U(1) the character is z ↦ z³. The determinant of the full diagonal element is z₁z₂z₃, so the exponent in the last block changes from 3 to −4 as well.

z₁⁹ z₂⁶ z₃³ (z₁z₂z₃)⁻⁷ = z₁² z₂⁻¹ z₃⁻⁴.

If one sign is absent, H itself is compact. The representation just constructed has a single compact type and its continuous coefficients are square integrable for finite Haar measure. If both signs occur, put u = min(r, s) and let h be a lower bound for the absolute gaps between opposite signs. For h > max(2n, 8n u), the compact type supplies a nonzero highest vector, the normalized kernel is square integrable, and the representation generated by its coefficients is a discrete series of parameter μ.

The single sufficient bound h > 8n² + 2n works in either signature since u ≤ n. At the distant scale t, distinct coordinate centers are at least t apart. Each coordinate of μ lies within A√t of its center for a fixed A. Every opposite-sign gap is thus at least t − 2A√t, which eventually exceeds the bound. We may now choose a model σ at μ. The coefficient identity on the stopping type and the isometry on its translates will put this model inside the restriction.
