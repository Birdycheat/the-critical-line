Where the Hecke factor 3/5 enters

For a prime element whose ideal has norm at least 25 and N ≥ 3, take a continuous, arithmetic- and scalar-invariant real coefficient of mean zero in L². To estimate its Hecke average, lift its translate w and the coset average v of that translate to a common finite cover. Averaging there gives ⟨v,w⟩ = ‖v‖². The angle bound for the invariant subspaces gives |⟨v,w⟩| ≤ (2/5)‖v‖‖w‖. Cancelling ‖v‖ when positive gives a norm bound of 2/5. Both squared norms acquire the same positive covering index, so it cancels on returning to the quotient. The proof weakens 2/5 to 3/5 and preserves mean zero at each step, giving the norm factor (3/5)j after j averages. This is the upper estimate compared with (4/5)j for the stabilized periods.
