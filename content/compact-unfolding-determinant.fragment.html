Finding bounded frames for a compact set of forms

The simplex estimate ends with an integral over a compact set K of positive Hermitian forms. The period bound needs an integral on a matrix group. A positive matrix has a frame that carries the identity to it, but that frame can have any nonzero determinant. We first separate its scalar part and bound what remains.

At each infinite place of the number field use the real vector space of complex Hermitian N × N matrices, with N ≥ 1. Write V for their product and P for its positive definite cone. The action is Ag(H) = (g−1)∗Hg−1 at each place. Fix a compact subset of P. Continuity of inversion on invertible matrices and continuity of the determinant give bounds R2 on the moduli of all entries of Q−1 and R3 on det Q for all its local components Q. There are only finitely many places here.

Take Y = Q−1/2 by the spectral theorem. Then AY(I) = Q and YY∗ = Q−1. The squared Euclidean length of row i of Y is (Q−1)ii. Each entry of Y is therefore bounded in modulus by a = max(1,R2). Choose an Nth root c of det Y and set T = c−1Y. Taking determinants gives

det T = 1,   |c|2N det Q = 1.

With D = max(1,R3) we have |c| ≥ D−1/(2N) ≥ D−1. Dividing an entry of Y by c thus costs at most D. The single radius R = aD bounds the entries of both Y and T throughout the compact set. For example Q = diag(16,1) gives Y = diag(1/4,1), c = 1/2 and T = diag(1/2,2). More generally, for Q = diag(u²,1) with u ≥ 1, the entries of its inverse stay bounded by one while the second diagonal entry of T grows as √u. S. Axler, Linear Algebra Done Right, chapter 7, spectral theorem and positive square roots.

Which determinant changes the volume?

Write S = ∏w SLN(ℂ) and take the compact neighbourhood W of its identity defined by |(sw − I)ij| ≤ 1/2 for every entry. It lies in a bounded closed set of matrices with determinant one and contains an open neighbourhood of the identity. In particular 0 < ν(W) < ∞ for the chosen left- and right-invariant Haar measure ν on S. Enlarge the original compact set to K1 = {As⁻¹p ∣ s ∈ W, p ∈ K}. This is a compact subset of P and K ⊆ AsK1 for every s ∈ W.

Let μ be additive Haar measure on V. For a nonnegative measurable function f on P, the linear change of variables gives

∫K₁ f(Asp) dμ(p) = |detℝ As|−1 ∫AsK₁ f dμ ≥ |detℝ As|−1 ∫K f dμ.

Thus an upper bound D0 ≥ 1 on |detℝ As| gives the lower multiplier 1/D0 needed on the right. Continuity on W gives such a bound. Here one can even compute it. For a single matrix g with singular values σ1, …, σN, the unitary factors act isometrically on Hermitian matrices in the Hilbert–Schmidt inner product. The diagonal factor in the action scales real coordinate Hii by σi−2. It scales both real coordinates of every complex entry Hij, i < j, by (σiσj)−1. Multiplying these factors gives |detℝ Ag| = |detℂ g|−2N. The Jacobians multiply across places, hence D0 = 1 works on S. For s = diag(4/3,3/4) in W, the two diagonal coordinates scale by 9/16 and 16/9, while the complex off-diagonal coordinate stays fixed. J. Hunter, Measure Theory, §2.8, linear changes of Lebesgue measure · Axler, chapter 7, singular value decomposition.

Moving the integral onto the special linear group

Let ω be a continuous differential form of degree q on P, invariant under pullback by the independent scalar matrices at all places. Write fr ω(g) for its value at AgI pulled back to the tangent space at I by Ag. We use the maximum absolute matrix entry over all places as norm on V, with its induced operator norm on forms. Fix p in K1 and choose its frame Y = cT by the first result, using the radius R for K1. For s in W, each entry of s has modulus at most 3/2. The products sY and sT both have entries bounded by R′ = 3NR/2. Thus W T lies in the fixed compact set K′ of determinant-one matrices at all places with entries bounded by R′.

Set X = sY = csT. To recover ω at Asp = AXI from fr ω(X), apply the inverse tangent action H ↦ X∗HX to each of the q tangent arguments. Each matrix entry in that expression is a sum of N² terms of modulus at most (R′)²‖H‖. The cost for a form is therefore B = (N²(R′)²)q. Scalar invariance gives fr ω(csT) = fr ω(sT). Consequently ‖ω(Asp)‖ ≤ B ‖fr ω(sT)‖. Right invariance of ν and WT ⊆ K′ bound its integral over s ∈ W by B ∫K′ ‖fr ω‖ dν. This estimate holds for each fixed p and needs only the existence of its frame.

Integrate the real-Jacobian inequality first over W, with f = ‖ω‖. Tonelli’s theorem allows us to integrate over s first on the other side, since the joint integrand is continuous on W × K1 and nonnegative. The preceding pointwise estimate then gives

∫K ‖ω‖ dμ ≤ C2 ∫K′ ‖fr ω‖ dν,   C2 = D0 B μ(K1)/ν(W).

The sets, the measures and the degree fix this finite constant before ω is chosen. The cycle-period estimate now counts visits of the arithmetic group to K′ and applies Cauchy–Schwarz on its fundamental domain. T. Tao, An Introduction to Measure Theory, §1.7, Tonelli’s theorem.

Lean correspondence

The compact unfolding uses continuity to choose D′ on the averaging neighbourhood. The explicit singular-value calculation above supplies a sharper permissible value. Its bounded frame comes from an arbitrary Gram factor followed by a scalar root.
