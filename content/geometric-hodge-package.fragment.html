How is the same Hecke system carried into the geometric tower?

The cohomological eigenspace requires several correspondences to have prescribed eigenvalues on the same nonzero vector. There are two kinds of evidence on the automorphic side. At a finite place a Hecke polynomial records roots. At an embedding a weight predicts degrees in which a joint eigenvector should occur. We describe how these data pass to a single tower once the geometric comparison theorems are supplied.

Conditional transfer theorem. Fix a CM field E, the coefficient identification ι from the algebraic closure of ℚℓ to ℂ, and an automorphic representation of positive odd rank a. Suppose a level, a Hecke test function, and a family of Hecke operators indexed by I have been chosen for it. Write θi for their specified joint eigenvalues, κ for the auxiliary algebraic Hecke character, and mτ for its shift at the embedding τ. The level selection supplies a cofinite congruence among the permitted primes away from ℓ and may impose an additional congruence or weight test at a specified place. Assume the following geometric inputs for these data.

  • The group datum has a standard base over E. For the chosen weight the Hodge comparison supplies a fibre power. At that power there is a smooth projective tower with the prescribed finite étale correspondences at the chosen level.
  • The comparison at an integral prime sends a group-side good-reduction witness to the corresponding geometric good-reduction condition, and sends a group-side Hecke congruence to the geometric congruence with the same coefficients.
  • For this Hecke test and its spectral data, the Hodge trace comparison supplies a nonzero simultaneous eigenclass in every holomorphic degree selected by the group-side exponent test. The embedding used for base change must match the field labels of the group datum.

Then the tower carries the same congruences with the same exceptional set. At an embedding τ above any place where the weight test was imposed, each predicted jump j occurs in the shifted holomorphic degree j + mτ, with the conjugation of eigenvalues specified by that test.

Proof at a finite place. Choose the standard base, then the power supplied for this weight, and take the tower at that power. The comparison applies to all its chosen correspondences. At a permitted prime w, take the ideal of the group datum corresponding to w under the chosen field isomorphism. The spectral test supplies the Hecke coefficients, including a nonzero leading scalar c after applying ι. The geometric congruence uses the same sequence of coefficients, so the polynomial identity passes unchanged. Writing β₁, …, βa for the algebraically normalized Satake parameters, with multiplicity, the identity reads

Pw(X) = c ∏i=1a (X − βi/κ(w)),   c ≠ 0.

For a numerical check, take the three normalized roots 6, 10 and 14, character value 2 and leading scalar 3. The polynomial is 3(X − 3)(X − 5)(X − 7). Multiplying each root by 2 recovers 6, 10 and 14. The scalar 3 multiplies the polynomial once. The local character is unramified at the tested place, so its indicated value on a uniformizer is defined. The leading scalar is retained, and division by it gives the monic polynomial that specifies the roots. This reasoning works separately at every permitted place outside the original finite exception set. For an additionally prescribed congruence the same comparison applies directly at that place.

Proof at an embedding. For τ above the tested prime, the group-side weight test supplies one field automorphism σ of ℂ matching the embeddings of the coefficient datum. For each possible weight of the automorphic representation at τ and each prescribed jump j, it then supplies an exponent list h of nonnegative integers and an index i such that hi = j + mτ. The standard base ensures that σ ∘ τ agrees with the group datum on its designated elements. The Hodge trace comparison therefore applies to the base change along σ ∘ τ and to the joint eigenvalues σ(ι(θr)). It gives some cohomological degree b and a nonzero class in Hb(Ωhi) on which each correspondence r has this eigenvalue. Substitution of j + mτ gives the required holomorphic degree. For instance, if a jump is −1 and the character shift is 3, one looks in holomorphic degree 2. The second degree b is supplied by the comparison and can vary with the jump.

The ordering of the choices matters. One choice of geometric tower preserves every test carried by the initial level data. The baseline construction tests the distinguished place. A second choice of level data may carry both that test and a prescribed congruence away from ℓ. At an additional place above ℓ, the level construction may use two choices, one for the Hodge test and one for the polynomial test. Applying the transfer to each choice preserves the two conclusions with their own witnesses. The local comparisons and rank extraction explain how these witnesses are used later. Constructing the required levels and proving the geometric comparison inputs themselves remain separate parts of the reconstruction.
