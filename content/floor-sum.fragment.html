How multiplication modulo the level produces a Bernoulli value

The length argument needs an integer-like scalar whose ℓ-adic valuation is exactly that of a special value. The floor in its definition records the carry when an integer product is reduced modulo the level. This permits a direct comparison of the two quantities, one residue at a time. Keep the level F = fℓ′ℓm chosen above, put k = e + 1 ≥ 2, and let χ denote the Dirichlet character induced by ψ at level F throughout this calculation. The cyclotomic character will enter again when the sum is compared with the Galois lifts. The two successive calls for the length bound

For a unit residue, write u for its representative in 1,…,F−1. Choose b prime to F and divide bu by F, obtaining a quotient j and a remainder y between 1 and F−1. The opposite residue is represented by F−u. Its product with b is (b−j−1)F + (F−y). Subtracting the two quotients gives the signed carry θ(u) = 2j − (b−1). Define S = ∑u θ(u)ueχ(u), summed over the unit representatives. The use of ue in place of a lifted cyclotomic character changes the scalar by a multiple of ℓm. Write C(u) = Fk−1Bk(u/F)/k, where Bk(x) is the Bernoulli polynomial. Let P be the product of the primes at most k + 1 and set D = 2kP. The first two coefficients of the polynomial give

C(u) = uk/(kF) − uk−1/2 + (2F/D)nu,   nu ∈ ℤ.

Indeed the term of Bernoulli index h ≥ 2 has a factor Fh−1. The denominator bound for the Bernoulli number Bh says that PBh is an integer for h ≤ k. Thus the terms after the first two are all multiples of F/(kP). Polynomial and character normalization Expansion with its integral remainder Prime denominator bound

Compare C(y) and bkC(u) with y = bu − Fj. In the leading power, the first-order change divided by kF is −j(bu)e. In the second power, the surviving term is (b−1)(bu)e/2. Their sum is −θ(u)(bu)e/2. To keep track of the remainder, expand yk modulo F² and ye modulo F. Multiplication by D clears every denominator in the remainder, with one factor F left. In particular the following quantity is an integer multiple of F. First-order binomial difference The integral identity for one unit

D[beθ(u)ue + 2(C(y) − bkC(u))] ∈ Fℤ.

Multiply by χ(bu) and sum over u. Multiplication by b permutes the units, so ∑χ(bu)C(y) = ∑χ(u)C(u) = TF, the generalized Bernoulli value at level F divided by k. The other terms use χ(bu) = χ(b)χ(u). Every summand on the remainder side is divisible by ℓm in the coefficient ring. This gives the congruence used by the count. The permuted unit sum Bounding the whole sum by the same power of ℓ

D[χ(b)beS − 2(χ(b)bk − 1)TF] ≡ 0   (mod ℓm).

For example, take the primitive trivial character, ℓ = 5, e = 1, F = 5 and b = 2. The four signed carries are −1, −1, 1, 1, giving S = −1 − 2 + 3 + 4 = 4. Raising the level from 1 to 5 multiplies B₂ = 1/6 by 1 − 5. Hence T5 = −1/3 and D = 24. The left side is 24[2·4 − 2(4−1)(−1/3)] = 240, a multiple of 5. In this example the valuation of S is zero, as is the valuation of the primitive value 1/12. Euler factors on changing the level

In general the change of level multiplies the primitive Bernoulli value by factors 1 − ψ(p)pe. At p dividing the conductor the character vanishes, and at a newly added ℓ the subtracted term has norm less than one. Thus |TF|ℓ equals the norm of the primitive value. The unit-difference hypothesis in the character argument gives a b for which χ(b)bk − 1 is a unit. Since ℓ is odd, 2 is a unit too. The depth is chosen so that |ℓm|ℓ is strictly smaller than |D|ℓ|TF|ℓ, also allowing for the original cutoff. The congruence then forces |S|ℓ = |TF|ℓ. Replacing S by the lifted scalar z preserves that norm. This is the valuation that the successive Frobenius tests spend.
