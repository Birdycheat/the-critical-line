Which lattice points does an adelic box count?

The sum over isotropic lines leaves a rational scalar to count in each shell. We prove a bound for M coordinates over E and then use it with M = 1. Let E/F be a CM quadratic extension and d = [F∶ℚ]. Choose an F-basis of E. It writes an adelic vector in (𝔸F ⊗F E)M as 2M adelic coordinates over F. At each finite place v require every coordinate to belong to N−1𝒪Fᵥ, where N is a fixed positive integer. At each of the d real places take the absolute values of the 2M coordinates. The box BN(R) consists of vectors satisfying the finite conditions whose real absolute values sum to at most R. This sum is the ℓ¹ norm on ℝν with ν = 2dM.

BN(R) = {‖u‖₁ ≤ R} × ∏v finite (N−1𝒪Fᵥ)2M.

The finite factor is compact and open. Away from the primes dividing N, its factors are the usual integral lattices. Every finite adèle has a denominator at only finitely many places, and powers of the rational primes below them clear those denominators. Thus the open finite factors, as N varies, cover any compact family. Take a finite subcover, use a common multiple of its denominators, and bound the continuous real norm on the same family. Every compact Ω is consequently contained in some BN(r) with r > 0. Sutherland, The ring of adeles, Propositions 25.5–25.6 and Definition 25.7.

For a rational vector, each of its 2M coefficients c lies in F. The finite condition says exactly that Nc is integral at every finite place, hence lies in 𝒪F. The allowed rational vectors therefore form the additive subgroup ΛN = (N−1𝒪F)2M in the chosen coordinates. Taking differences preserves every finite condition. The real embeddings identify this subgroup with a lattice, so its intersection with a bounded real ball is finite. Sutherland, §14.2, the integer ring in its real embedding space.

How many translates of a small ball cover the rational points?

Let PN(R) count the points of ΛN in the closed real ball of radius R, including zero. For 0 < r₀ ≤ R, choose a maximal collection of these points whose pairwise distances are at least r₀. The open balls of radius r₀/2 about the chosen points are disjoint and fit inside the ball of radius R+r₀/2. Dilation by a positive scalar t multiplies volume in ℝν by tν. The number of centres is therefore at most (1+2R/r₀)ν ≤ (3R/r₀)ν.

Every lattice point in the large ball lies within distance r₀ of a centre, since otherwise it could be added to the collection. Subtracting that centre sends the point into ΛN in the small ball. Each centre therefore accounts for at most PN(r₀) points. Using the coefficient 5 in the subsequent shell estimate gives the comparison

PN(R) ≤ (5R/r₀)ν PN(r₀),   0 < r₀ ≤ R.

When does a dilated compact set contain a nonzero rational point?

Fix Ω ⊂ BN(r) as above. Choose 0 < r₀ ≤ 1 strictly smaller than the real sizes of all nonzero points in BN(1). This is possible because the list is finite, and a nonzero rational coordinate stays nonzero under a real embedding. If the list is empty, take r₀ = 1. Thus PN(r₀) = 1. For an explicit choice take r₀ = min(1, d/(2N)). Indeed, any nonzero coefficient c has |NormF/ℚ(Nc)| ≥ 1 because it is the absolute value of a nonzero integer. The arithmetic–geometric mean inequality gives ∑σ|σ(c)| ≥ d/N.

The dilation τ(x) multiplies real coordinates by exp(x) and fixes finite coordinates. Let AΩ(x) count X ∈ EM with X ≠ 0 and τ(−x)X ∈ Ω. The tested vector then has real size exp(−x)‖X‖₁ ≤ r, so X belongs to the box of radius exp(x)r. Put x₀ = log(r₀/r). Below x₀ the new radius is smaller than r₀ and the count is zero. At and above the threshold apply the two-radius comparison with R = exp(x)r. It bounds even the count including zero, and gives

AΩ(x) = 0   for x < x₀,   AΩ(x) ≤ C exp(νx)   for x ≥ x₀,   C = (5r/r₀)ν.

For example, take F = ℚ, E = ℚ(i), the basis (1,i), M = 1 and Ω = B₂(1). The lattice is ½ℤ[i] with size |a|+|b| on a+bi. Taking r₀ = ¼ gives ν = 2 and x₀ = −log 4. At the threshold the actual nonzero count is still zero, while the displayed upper bound is 25. At x = −log 2 the closed radius reaches ½ and admits exactly the four points ±½, ±i/2.

In the shell integral, pairing a tested vector with its transverse dual recovers a scalar in E. The pair and the tested vector vary in compact sets, so the image of their continuous pairing is one fixed compact Ω. Here the dimension in the count is ν = 2d, one scalar over E, and the variable is x = j + log rtest for shell index j and test radius rtest. Hence the shell sum begins at ⌈x₀−log rtest⌉ and the scalar count contributes at most C rtest2d exp(2dj).
