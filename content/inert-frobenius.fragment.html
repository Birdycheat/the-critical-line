Which Frobenius fixes the invariant, and which exchanges the CM roots?

The good model and its formal coordinate use Frobenius at two different primes. To place the singular modulus J in the unramified field at the target prime p, the proof must show that inertia at p fixes J. It tests an inertia element on the roots of an integral polynomial for J using an auxiliary prime ideal 𝔮 of the quadratic field. Chebotarev selects 𝔮 outside the finite set where two distinct roots can reduce to the same value, and its residue field has prime cardinality q. The chosen arithmetic Frobenius φ agrees with the inertia element on these roots and has the same ideal class under Artin reciprocity. That class is trivial, so 𝔮 is principal. The congruence needed is Jq ≡ J at this auxiliary place. The target inert ideal (p) has residue field of size p². How a principal ideal gives the congruence for J

Write the complex period lattice as a nonzero scalar times ℤτ + ℤ. If 𝔮 has generator α = a + bω, multiplication by α preserves the lattice. Express ατ and α in the basis (τ,1) as Aτ + B and Cτ + D. The resulting integer matrix has determinant Norm(α) = q and fixes τ under the fractional linear action. Up to a determinant-one change of basis, the q + 1 matrices indexing the degree-q correspondence send τ to qτ or to (τ + b)/q for 0 ≤ b < q. Thus one of these points τ′ has j(τ′) = j(τ) = J. Here is why a square occurs in the calculation. Put F = E₄³ and D = Δ, so j = F/D. Form the product G over these q + 1 points of F(τ)D(τ′) − F(τ′)D(τ). The equal j-values give G(τ) = 0. To compute the product modulo 1 − ζq, use the Fourier parameter y = exp(2πiτ/q) and write B = FDq − FqD for the corresponding series. Reduction sets ζq = 1 and takes place in characteristic q, where an integral series satisfies F(yq) = F(y)q. The factor for qτ becomes B(yq). Each of the other q factors becomes −B(y), so their product is −B(yq). The two groups together give the negative square. At every extension of the valuation at 𝔮 the right side of the second identity reduces to zero. Hence Jq − J reduces to zero there too. At the target prime p the calculation concerns the CM endomorphism ω = (1 + √−d)/2. Let δ = 1 − 2ω, so δ² = −d. Its reduction is a unit because p ∤ d. Inertness gives (−d)(p−1)/2 ≡ −1 modulo p. Multiplying by δ shows δp ≡ −δ. The source lifts δ into the completed unramified field by Hensel’s lemma. Its arithmetic Frobenius σ preserves δ², so σδ is one of ±δ. Reduction gives σδ ≡ δp ≡ −δ. The two signs remain distinct because 2δ is a unit. Thus σω = 1 − ω exactly, σ²ω = ω, and σω − ω is a unit. These last two properties enter the height-two formal-group comparison for the chosen coordinate. Two primes for discriminant −23. At the target p = 5, δ² ≡ 2 and δ⁵ ≡ 4δ = −δ modulo 5. For an auxiliary example take τ = ω and α = 5 + 2ω. Since ω² = ω − 6, multiplication gives ατ = 7τ − 12 and α = 2τ + 5. The matrix has determinant 7·5 − (−12)·2 = 59. Thus (α) has prime norm 59 and the congruence at this ideal is J59 ≡ J.
