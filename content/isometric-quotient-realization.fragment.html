Isometric frame realizations of arithmetic quotients and sublattices

How does the rectangular covering bound for full lattices in standard coordinate spaces transfer without metric distortion to homomorphisms A/S → R from the quotient of an ambient arithmetic lattice A ⊆ Kn by a saturated sublattice S into an arbitrary finitely generated sublattice R ⊆ Kn, as invoked in the subquotient transport above? Two linear-algebraic realizations supply standard full-rank models at every infinite place while preserving all covolumes and normalized sizes. On the source quotient, choosing an orthonormal basis of the orthogonal complement of the complex span of S inside the complex span of A at each complex place w yields a partial coisometry Qw (QwQw* = 1m) that intertwines a rational quotient matrix q ∈ Mm×n(K) with an invertible m × m frame X′w = qwXwQw*, matching every quotient covolume above S with an ordinary sublattice covolume in Km. On the target sublattice, polar decomposition of the frame-embedded rational basis matrix via the positive-definite Hermitian square root Sw = (Yw*Yw)1/2 extracts an isometric column matrix Pw (Pw*Pw = 1j) and an invertible j × j frame X″w = Sw−1 on a full lattice B ⊆ Kj, preserving the largest normalized quotient size.

1. Coisometric frame realization of a saturated quotient. Let K be a totally complex number field equipped with a ring involution (such as complex conjugation on the CM field E), let n ≥ 0 be an integer, and let X = (Xw)w be a family of invertible matrices Xw ∈ GLn(ℂ) indexed by the complex places w of K, with frame coordinates fX,w(y) = Xw−1yw ∈ ℂn for y ∈ Kn (where yw denotes the coordinatewise image of y at w, and similarly for matrices over K). Let A ⊆ Kn be a finitely generated 𝒪K-submodule and S ⊆ A an 𝒪K-submodule saturated in A under the Euclidean lattice realization ρX : y ↦ (fX,w(y))w, meaning ρX(A) ∩ ℝ · ρX(S) = ρX(S), and set m = rkK(A) − rkK(S) ≥ 0, where rkK(M) = dimK(K · M).

Theorem (Isometric realization of an arithmetic quotient). Under these hypotheses, there exist a rational matrix q ∈ Mm×n(K), invertible frames X′w ∈ GLm(ℂ), and complex matrices Qw ∈ Mm×n(ℂ) at every complex place w of K such that: (a) q vanishes on S, has kernel K · S on K · A, and maps A onto a full finitely generated 𝒪K-lattice q(A) spanning Km; (b) QwQw* = 1m, every vector Qw*z (z ∈ ℂm) belongs to U′w = spanℂ fX,w(A), and u − Qw*Qwu ∈ Uw = spanℂ fX,w(S) for all u ∈ U′w; (c) the frame coordinates intertwine on A via

(X′w)−1(qy)w = Qw fX,w(y)   (y ∈ A);

and (d) for every nonzero 𝒪K-sublattice N′ ⊆ q(A), the pullback N = A ∩ q−1(N′) satisfies S ⊊ N ⊆ A and q(N) = N′, and the normalized quotient size of N/S relative to (X, S) equals the normalized size of N′ in the frame X′.

Proof. Because K · S ⊆ K · A has K-codimension m, choose a K-linear isomorphism (K · A)/(K · S) ≃ Km and extend its composition with K · A → (K · A)/(K · S) across a K-linear complement of K · A in Kn to obtain q ∈ Mm×n(K). By construction q kills S, has kernel K · S on K · A, and maps the finitely generated module A onto an 𝒪K-submodule q(A) with K · q(A) = q(K · A) = Km, proving (a). At each complex place w, invertibility of Xw gives dimℂ U′w = rkK(A) and dimℂ Uw = rkK(S), so the Hermitian orthogonal complement Cw = Uw⊥ ∩ U′w inside ℂn has complex dimension m. Choose an orthonormal basis of Cw for the standard positive-definite inner product on ℂn and place its entrywise conjugates as the m rows of Qw ∈ Mm×n(ℂ). Orthonormality gives QwQw* = 1m and identifies the image of Qw* with Cw ⊆ U′w; for every u ∈ U′w = Uw ⊕ Cw, the vector Qw*Qwu is the orthogonal projection of u onto Cw (equivalently onto Uw⊥), whence u − Qw*Qwu ∈ Uw and ‖projUw⊥(u)‖2 = ‖Qw*Qwu‖2 = ‖Qwu‖2, establishing (b).

Next put Mw = qwXw ∈ Mm×n(ℂ). For every y ∈ S one has MwfX,w(y) = (qy)w = 0, so Mw vanishes on Uw. Setting X′w = MwQw* ∈ Mm(ℂ), every y ∈ A satisfies fX,w(y) − Qw*QwfX,w(y) ∈ Uw ⊆ ker Mw, and therefore (qy)w = MwfX,w(y) = X′w(QwfX,w(y)). Since q(A) spans Km over K, the vectors (qy)w (y ∈ A) span ℂm over ℂ, forcing the square matrix X′w ∈ Mm(ℂ) to be surjective and hence invertible, which gives (c). Finally, for any nonzero 𝒪K-sublattice N′ ⊆ q(A), the preimage N = A ∩ q−1(N′) ⊆ A is finitely generated (since 𝒪K is Noetherian and A is finitely generated), contains S, maps onto N′ because N′ ⊆ q(A), strictly contains S because N′ ≠ 0 whereas q(S) = 0, and satisfies rkK(N′) = rkK(N) − rkK(S) via (K · N)/(K · S) ≃ K · N′. Because ‖(X′w)−1(qy)w‖2 = ‖QwfX,w(y)‖2 = ‖projUw⊥(fX,w(y))‖2 at every place w, the Euclidean quotient lattice of N/S and the Euclidean realization ρX′(N′) are isometric additive images of N, giving equal covolumes and equal normalized sizes as required in (d).

2. Polar decomposition and isometric realization of a sublattice. Retain the totally complex number field K and invertible frames Xw ∈ GLn(ℂ), and let R ⊆ Kn be any finitely generated 𝒪K-submodule of rank j = rkK(R) ≥ 0.

Theorem (Isometric realization of an arithmetic sublattice). There exist a rational inclusion matrix i ∈ Mn×j(K), invertible frames X″w ∈ GLj(ℂ), isometric matrices Pw ∈ Mn×j(ℂ) satisfying Pw*Pw = 1j, and a full finitely generated 𝒪K-lattice B ⊆ Kj spanning Kj such that i maps B bijectively onto R, the frames intertwine at every complex place w via

Xw−1iw = Pw(X″w)−1   (Pw*Pw = 1j),

and the largest normalized quotient size of B in the frame X″ equals that of R in the frame X.

Proof. Choose a K-linear isomorphism Kj ≃ K · R ⊆ Kn with matrix i ∈ Mn×j(K) and choose a K-linear left inverse g : Kn → Kj satisfying g ∘ i = 1j. Set B = g(R) ⊆ Kj; since R ⊆ K · R = im i and g ∘ i = 1j, the map i restricts to an 𝒪K-linear bijection from the finitely generated module B onto R, and K · B = g(K · R) = Kj. At each complex place w, the matrix Yw = Xw−1iw ∈ Mn×j(ℂ) admits the left inverse Lw = gwXw (LwYw = 1j). Consequently Yw*Yw ∈ Mj(ℂ) is Hermitian positive definite with trivial kernel and admits an invertible positive-definite Hermitian square root Sw = (Yw*Yw)1/2 ∈ GLj(ℂ). Defining X″w = Sw−1 ∈ GLj(ℂ) and Pw = YwSw−1 ∈ Mn×j(ℂ) gives Pw*Pw = Sw−1(Yw*Yw)Sw−1 = 1j and Xw−1iw = Yw = PwSw = Pw(X″w)−1. At every place w and every y ∈ Kj, ‖fX,w(iy)‖2 = ‖PwfX″,w(y)‖2 = ‖fX″,w(y)‖2, so i induces a norm-preserving bijection across all sublattices and saturated quotients of B and R, matching their Harder–Narasimhan slopes and largest normalized quotient sizes. Combined with the quotient realization above, for any target family (Tw)w ∈ ∏w Mn(ℂ) vanishing on Uw and mapping U′w into spanℂ fX,w(R), applying the full-lattice covering estimate to (Pw*TwQw*)w between (q(A), X′) and (B, X″) and lifting Φ₀ : q(A) → B to Φ = i ∘ Φ₀ ∘ q completes the subquotient covering bound.

Orthogonal quotient covolume equality and polar rerealization of Harder–Narasimhan slopes

Write f for the number of complex places of the totally complex field K (so [K : ℚ] = 2f) and d = |disc K|1/(2f) for its root discriminant. Under Minkowski’s canonical embedding (w)w : 𝒪K → ⊕w ℂ, a ℤ-basis of 𝒪K forms an ℝ-basis of ⊕w ℂ, so for every complex place w and every scalar z ∈ ℂ an ℝ-linear combination of elements of 𝒪K embeds to z at w and to 0 at every place w′ ≠ w. Acting by such scalar combinations on S identifies the joint ℝ-span ℝ · ρX(S) inside ⊕w ℂn with the direct sum ⊕w Uw of the local ℂ-spans Uw = spanℂ fX,w(S), so the Euclidean orthogonal projection of ⊕w ℂn off ℝ · ρX(S) acts factor by factor as projUw⊥. For any nonzero sublattice N′ ⊆ q(A) with pullback N = A ∩ q−1(N′), the ℝ-linear map (uw)w ↦ (Qwuw)w vanishes on ⊕w Uw, restricts to a Euclidean isometry on ⊕w Cw, and sends the projected lattice of N/S bijectively onto {(QwfX,w(y))w : y ∈ N} = {((X′w)−1(qy)w)w : y ∈ N} = ρX′(N′). Since isometric ℤ-lattices in ℝ-inner-product spaces have equal covolumes and rkK(N′) = rkK(N) − rkK(S), dividing by (d/2)f rkK(N′) and taking the (2f rkK(N′))th root equates the normalized quotient size of N/S to sizeX′(N′).

On the target side, if z ∈ ℂj satisfies Yw*Ywz = 0, then ‖Ywz‖22 = ⟨z, Yw*Ywz⟩ = 0 gives Ywz = 0 and hence z = LwYwz = 0; thus every eigenvalue of the Hermitian matrix Yw*Yw is strictly positive, making Sw = (Yw*Yw)1/2 invertible and Hermitian. Because (vw)w ↦ (Pwvw)w is an ℝ-linear isometric embedding of ⊕w ℂj into ⊕w ℂn intertwining ρX″ with ρX ∘ i, every 𝒪K-sublattice M ⊆ B satisfies covol(ρX″(M)) = covol(ρX(i(M))), rkK(M) = rkK(i(M)), and ρX″(B) ∩ ℝ · ρX″(M) = ρX″(M) if and only if ρX(R) ∩ ℝ · ρX(i(M)) = ρX(i(M)). Consequently M ↦ i(M) bijects the proper saturated sublattices of B onto those of R with μ(B/M) = μ(R/i(M)) and equal normalized quotient sizes, giving μmin(B) = μmin(R) and (taking the supremum over proper saturated sublattices, 0 when B = R = 0) equal largest normalized quotient sizes.

Archimedean base change of the orthogonal sublattice, nilradical and center

In the first-layer rounding below and the central rounding, arithmetic approximation operates on the rational lattice intersection P = Λx ∩ W⊥ inside En, whereas the archimedean norm lifts act on the complex Hermitian orthogonal complement (Wv)⊥ ⊆ ℂn at each real place v of F. Why does the complex span of P at v coincide with (Wv)⊥, and why does diagonal archimedean embedding carry rational skew-Hermitian nilradical and central matrices into the corresponding real parabolic layers? Two algebraic facts bridge the rational and archimedean spaces: because the automorphic point lattice Λx spans En over E, clearing denominators in 𝒪E identifies the E-span of P with the rational orthogonal complement W⊥; and for any ring homomorphism ι : E → A intertwining the involutions, linear duality via an E-linear coannihilator projection shows that extending scalars commutes with taking the orthogonal complement of any E-subspace.

Fix an automorphic Hermitian datum of rank n ≥ 0 over a CM extension E/F with CM involution σE and ring of integers 𝒪E, equipped with a Hermitian matrix J ∈ GLn(E) defining the pairing ⟨y, z⟩J = ∑a,b σE(ya)Jabzb on En and a chosen complex embedding φv : E ↪ ℂ above each real place v of F intertwining σE with complex conjugation (writing Jv = φv(J) ∈ GLn(ℂ)). Let x be an adelic point whose rational point lattice Λx ⊂ En is a full self-dual 𝒪E-lattice. For any 𝒪E-submodule R ⊆ En, write W = E · R ⊆ En for its E-span with rational orthogonal complement W⊥ = {y ∈ En : ∀ w ∈ W, ⟨w, y⟩J = 0}, and put P = {y ∈ Λx : ∀ s ∈ R, ⟨s, y⟩J = 0}. At each real place v of F, set spanv(M) = spanℂ φv(M) ⊆ ℂn for any subset M ⊆ En, and write U⊥Jv = {u ∈ ℂn : ∀ w ∈ U, w*Jvu = 0} for the complex Hermitian orthogonal complement of U ⊆ ℂn.

Theorem (Archimedean span of the orthogonal sublattice and parabolic layers). At every real place v of F: (1) spanv(R) = spanℂ φv(W) and spanv(Λx) = ℂn; (2) the complex span of P equals the local Hermitian orthogonal complement,

spanv(P) = (spanv(R))⊥Jv   in ℂn;

and (3) every matrix Y ∈ Mn(E) in the rational stabilizer nilradical of W—meaning Y is skew-Hermitian for J (σE(Y)⊤J + JY = 0) and maps En → W⊥, W⊥ → W, and W → 0—embeds diagonally as (φv(Y))v into the real stabilizer nilradical of (spanv(R))v, while every Y in the rational center (additionally vanishing on W⊥) embeds diagonally into the real center (vanishing on (spanv(R))⊥Jv at every real place v).

Proof. For (1), since φv : E ↪ ℂ is a ring homomorphism, every E-linear combination of elements of R maps under φv to a ℂ-linear combination of elements of φv(R), giving spanℂ φv(W) = spanv(R); because E · Λx = En, the standard basis of ℂn lies in φv(En) ⊆ spanv(Λx), so spanv(Λx) = ℂn. For (2), first identify E · P = W⊥ in En: σE-semilinearity of ⟨·, y⟩J in its first argument ensures P ⊆ W⊥ and hence E · P ⊆ W⊥; conversely, given any y ∈ W⊥ ⊆ En = E · Λx, writing y = ∑k (ak/bk)zk with zk ∈ Λx, ak ∈ 𝒪E, and nonzero bk ∈ 𝒪E over E = Frac(𝒪E) and setting c = ∏k bk ≠ 0 in 𝒪E places cy ∈ Λx ∩ W⊥ = P, whence y = c−1(cy) ∈ E · P. Thus spanv(P) = spanℂ φv(W⊥). Next, for any field E with ring endomorphism σE, any commutative ring A with ring endomorphism σA, any ring homomorphism ι : E → A satisfying ι ∘ σE = σA ∘ ι, any matrix J ∈ Mn(E), and any E-subspace W ⊆ En, scalar extension commutes with taking orthogonal complements: (spanA ι(W))⊥ι(J) = spanA ι(W⊥) in An. Specializing to A = ℂ with complex conjugation and ι = φv yields (spanv(R))⊥Jv = (spanℂ φv(W))⊥Jv = spanℂ φv(W⊥) = spanv(P).

Commutation of orthogonal complements with scalar extension via the dual coannihilator

In An, the inclusion spanA ι(W⊥) ⊆ (spanA ι(W))⊥ι(J) follows directly from A-sesquilinearity and ι ∘ σE = σA ∘ ι, since applying ι to ∑a,b σE(wa)Jabyb = 0 for w ∈ W and y ∈ W⊥ gives ∑a,b σA(ι(wa))ι(Jab)ι(yb) = 0.

For the reverse inclusion, let D ⊆ (En)* be the E-subspace of linear forms spanned by λw(y) = ⟨w, y⟩J = ∑a,b σE(wa)Jabyb for w ∈ W, so that K = W⊥ = {y ∈ En : ∀ f ∈ D, f(y) = 0} is the dual coannihilator of D. Because En is finite-dimensional over the field E, double-annihilator duality identifies the annihilator K0 = {f ∈ (En)* : ∀ y ∈ K, f(y) = 0} with D, and choosing a subspace complement of K in En supplies an E-linear projection π : En → En onto K satisfying π(y) = y for all y ∈ K. For each coordinate index 1 ≤ r ≤ n, the linear form gr(y) = yr − π(y)r vanishes on K and therefore belongs to K0 = D. Now let u ∈ (spanA ι(W))⊥ι(J) ⊆ An, and write e1, …, en for the standard basis of En. Orthogonality against ι(w) for w ∈ W reads ∑s=1n ι(λw(es))us = 0 in A, so E-linearity gives ∑s=1n ι(f(es))us = 0 for every f ∈ D. Evaluating at f = gr (where gr(es) = δrs − π(es)r) yields ur = ∑s=1n ι(π(es)r)us for every 1 ≤ r ≤ n, hence u = ∑s=1n us ι(π(es)) ∈ spanA ι(W⊥) since each π(es) lies in K = W⊥.

Finally, for (3), applying φv entrywise to σE(Y)⊤J + JY = 0 and using φv ∘ σE = conj ∘ φv gives φv(Y)*Jv + Jvφv(Y) = 0, so φv(Y) is skew-Hermitian for Jv. Whenever Y maps an E-subspace U₁ into U₂, ℂ-linearity sends φv(Y)(spanℂ φv(U₁)) into spanℂ φv(U₂). Applying this to En → W⊥ → W → 0 and substituting spanℂ φv(En) = ℂn, spanℂ φv(W) = spanv(R), and spanℂ φv(W⊥) = (spanv(R))⊥Jv from (1) and (2) places (φv(Y))v in the real stabilizer nilradical of (spanv(R))v; if Y additionally vanishes on W⊥, then φv(Y) vanishes on spanℂ φv(W⊥) = (spanv(R))⊥Jv at every real place v, placing (φv(Y))v in the real center.
