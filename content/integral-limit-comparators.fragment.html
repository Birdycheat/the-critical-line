Which later layer represents all columns in the new coordinates?

Keep the two target bases T∗ and T from the integral intersection. In T∗-coordinates let Cm denote the earlier étale comparison, called Rm in the tower construction. We now want a matrix over 𝔖 in T-coordinates. Fix a precision n. Stabilization gives a single level m ≥ n that works for all starting columns. In particular, each of the columns of Cm is congruent modulo ℓn to a surviving column vj: start with the jth coordinate vector at level m. The earlier period bound puts yj = τHT∗vj in the intersection L. Consequently each yj has the form Y e(cj) for a coordinate column over 𝔖.

Put these coordinate columns into a matrix Rn with d rows and dm columns. Since Y = τHT and τ is a unit in the large Witt ring W, cancelling τH gives the exact equality T eW(cj) = T∗vj. Only now reduce modulo ℓn. All columns give the same comparison of projected periods:

T eW(Rn) ≡ T∗Cm ≡ ΠmTm (mod ℓn).

To compare in the opposite direction, let Sm be a right inverse to Πm modulo ℓm with ℓ-adic integer entries. The height-H pair on this layer has Pm = τHTm and PmP′m = τ2HI. Set Q = P′mSm. Its entries lie in the integral period ring A = Ainf. In W we have TmP′m = τHI. Multiply the projection congruence on the right by Q and on the left by the scalar τH. One height factor comes from the target and the other from the inverse on the layer. We get Y e(Rn)Q ≡ τ2HI modulo ℓn in W.

This holds at every precision. Multiplying by the fixed inverse of Y in W shows that the single matrix τ2HY−1 can be approximated at every order by matrices e(Rn)Q over A. Its Witt coordinates are all integral: the first n coordinates agree with those of an integral approximation at order n. It therefore comes from a matrix Y′ over A. The inclusion in W is injective, so YY′ = Y′Y = τ2HI in A. At precision n the congruence e(Rn)Q ≡ Y′ may be multiplied by Y on the right. Divisibility by ℓn for these integral entries is detected by the first n Witt coordinates in either ring. This gives

e(Rn)(QY) ≡ τ2HId (mod ℓn) in A.

The order QY is forced by the calculation. It also has the required size dm by d. The remaining descent must turn its entries into series in 𝔖 and the fixed scalar τ2H into a power of ℓ. A coordinate functional on the period ring will do both.
