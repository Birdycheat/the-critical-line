How do two different characters interact in the same detector block?

Fix a real Q ≥ 3. Take a finite set S of distinct primitive Dirichlet characters of conductors at most Q. For each χ choose one complex point ρχ with σ₀ = 399/400 < Re ρχ < 1 and |Im ρχ| ≤ Q. The zero detector assigns one such zero and one block to each selected character of conductor greater than one when Q ≥ 20. For the mean square itself the points may be anywhere in that strip, even at the same height. Fix a positive integer U and use the same U integers U < n ≤ 2U for every row. Set vχ(n) = χ(n)n−(ρχ−σ₀). For any complex coefficients b(n), the block mean square satisfies

∑χ∈S |∑U<n≤2U b(n)vχ(n)|² ≤ (U + 8|S|Q³) ∑U<n≤2U|b(n)|².

Write Gχψ = ∑ vχ(n) conj(vψ(n)). The diagonal is at most U since each term is at most one. For χ ≠ ψ let m be the product of their conductors. The sequence ξ(n) = χ(n) conj(ψ(n)) is a character modulo m. On units it is the quotient of the two induced characters. On a nonunit at least one factor vanishes. If ξ were principal, the two induced characters would agree on every unit. Uniqueness of the primitive character inducing a given character would force χ = ψ. A complete period of ξ therefore sums to zero. Multiplication of the unit classes by some a with ξ(a) ≠ 1 leaves the sum unchanged and multiplies it by ξ(a). Deleting complete periods gives |A(r)| ≤ m for A(r) = ∑0≤n<r ξ(n). Andrew Sutherland, Theorem 18.13 and Corollary 18.15.

The complex weights vary across the block. Put w = ρχ−σ₀ + conj(ρψ−σ₀), so 0 < Re w < 1 and |Im w| ≤ 2Q. For f(x) = x−w, the derivative has modulus |w|x−Re w−1 ≤ |w|/U throughout the block. Substituting ξ(n) = A(n+1)−A(n) and shifting the second sum leaves endpoint terms f(2U)A(2U+1)−f(U+1)A(U+1), followed by −∑U<n<2U (f(n+1)−f(n))A(n+1). Each endpoint costs at most m and the full difference sum costs at most m|w|. Hence |Gχψ| ≤ 2m(1+|w|) ≤ 8Q³, using m ≤ Q² and |w| ≤ 1+2Q.

It remains to pass from pairwise comparisons to the sum of squares. Write V for the row matrix and Δ = U + 8|S|Q³. Every absolute row sum of G = VV∗ is ≤ Δ, and the same holds for columns by Hermitian symmetry. For any vector z, bound each term in z∗Gz by |Gχψ| (|zχ|²+|zψ|²)/2. Summing gives ‖V∗z‖² ≤ Δ‖z‖². Take z = Vb. Cauchy–Schwarz gives ‖z‖² = |⟨V∗z,b⟩| ≤ √Δ ‖z‖ ‖b‖. For z ≠ 0 division and squaring give the stated inequality. For z = 0 it already holds.

For a small check of that matrix argument, use the unweighted quadratic characters modulo 3 and 4 on the integers 5, 6, 7, 8. Their rows are (−1,0,1,−1) and (1,0,−1,0). The off-diagonal entry equals −2 even though the complete product period of length 12 sums to zero. The diagonal entries are 3 and 2, so one may take Δ = 5. Against b = (1,0,−1,0) the row outputs are −2 and 2. Their squared sum 8 is bounded by 5‖b‖² = 10. This example checks the finite matrix inequality for arbitrary coefficients.

Why can the bound contain the number of characters on both sides?

The detector uses a common coefficient a(n) = ∑ μ(d) over de = n, 1 ≤ d ≤ Y and 1 ≤ e ≤ N. Put b(n) = a(n)n−σ₀. Then b(n)vχ(n) = a(n)χ(n)n−ρχ, exactly the term evaluated at the chosen point. Each divisor of n contributes at most one in modulus, so |a(n)| ≤ τ(n), the number of its positive divisors.

Here is a uniform constant for that coefficient bound. For ε = 1/400 let K = ⌈21/ε⌉, A = max(1,1/(ε log 2)) and D = AK. If p ≥ K, a prime power pk has k+1 ≤ 2k ≤ pεk divisors. For p < K, the inequality 1+x ≤ exp(x) gives k+1 ≤ A(1+εk log 2) ≤ A pεk. Multiplying over the prime powers in n costs at most K factors A, since only primes smaller than K need one. Thus τ(n) ≤ D n1/400. This deliberately coarse constant is independent of Y,N,U and of the family of characters.

Now |b(n)| ≤ D U−199/200 on the block. There are exactly U integers in it, giving ∑|b(n)|² ≤ D²U−99/100 ≤ C U−197/200 with C = D². Suppose every character in S selected this block among J ≥ 1 candidates. Each output then has modulus at least 1/(2J). If T = |S|, the mean square gives

T/(4J²) ≤ C U3/200 + T · 8CQ³U−197/200.

The second term can use at most half the left side once 8CQ³U−197/200 ≤ 1/(8J²). Under exactly this hypothesis we can subtract it to obtain T ≤ 8CJ²U3/200. For the selected windows X = ⌈Q⌉, Y = X⁴, J = 8(⌊log₂ X⌋+1), N = 2J and U = Y2j with 0 ≤ j < J. Since U ≥ Q⁴, the coefficient of T on the right is at most 8CQ−47/50 for every block. The factor J² grows only as the square of a logarithm. Consequently there is one threshold Q₀ ≥ 20 depending only on C above which subtraction is valid for all those windows. The sum over the selected blocks then gives the exponent 1/4, with one additional character allowed for conductor one. The bounded range of smaller heights is covered by counting all characters with conductor below the fixed threshold.
