What Ferrero–Washington supplies in this proof

A generator bound limits how many independent ℓ-adic directions can contribute to the growth of a cohomology group. The argument starts with the ideal class groups of the number fields in the finite layers of a cyclotomic tower, passes to a single module for the infinite tower, and then recovers the length at any chosen finite layer as a quotient of that module. The class group of a number field is the group of its nonzero fractional ideals modulo principal ideals.

Write Cl(L) for the ideal class group of a number field L, written additively. The number of independent characters of order ℓ depends on Cl(L)/ℓCl(L). For an abelian extension K/ℚ containing a primitive ℓth root of unity, or a primitive fourth root of unity at ℓ = 2, the cyclotomic rank bound of Ferrero and Washington gives a single integer r such that

|Cl(K(μℓⁿ))/ℓCl(K(μℓⁿ))| ≤ ℓr   for every n ≥ 0.

From class-group characters to generators

Let U∞ be the kernel of the ℓ-adic cyclotomic character on the absolute Galois group, and put A = ℚℓ/ℤℓ. For an integer s ≥ 1, let Ds be the group of locally constant homomorphisms U∞ → A vanishing on its intersections with every inertia group and with each decomposition group above a prime dividing s. Define Xs = Homℤℓ(Ds, A). The earlier cokernel calculation uses s = ℓ. The generator proof starts at s = 1. Local conditions on the characters Definition of the character module Its ℤℓ-linear dual

At the finite field K(μℓⁿ), n ≥ 2, class field theory pulls the class-group characters onto the everywhere unramified ℤ/ℓ-characters. Their number is bounded by the cardinality of Cl/ℓCl, since they factor through that finite vector space and its dual has the same dimension. At infinite level every such character extends to a finite level. The extension theorem first extends the locally constant homomorphism and then enforces the local conditions after moving higher. Compactness of the relevant sets of decomposition and inertia elements makes a single higher level suffice. The images under restriction from successive levels are nested and cover the infinite-level characters. Any ℓr + 1 distinct characters would therefore already occur together at one level, contradicting the finite bound. Adding the decomposition conditions for s can only reduce their number. Every element of Ds has ℓ-power order. Its domain is compact, so its locally constant image is finite, and every element of A is killed by a power of ℓ. Further, Ds[ℓ] has at most ℓr elements by the character bound and the identification A[ℓ] ≅ ℤ/ℓ. Embed this vector space in Ar using at most r coordinates. Each coordinate extends to all of Ds because A is divisible and hence injective over the principal ideal domain ℤℓ. Their joint map is still injective. Indeed a nonzero kernel element of order ℓk would yield a nonzero kernel element of order ℓ by multiplication by ℓk−1. Any map Ds → A now extends to Ar. Each endomorphism of A is scalar multiplication by an ℓ-adic integer, as seen by its compatible values on the cyclic groups A[ℓk]. The r extended coordinates thus generate Xs. This proves the precise generator conclusion used in the layer bound, a surjection ℤℓr → Xs. Why restriction recovers the locally trivial classes

Fix a field F in one of these towers. Let G be its absolute Galois group, χ its cyclotomic character, U = ker χ, and e a nonzero integer. The coefficient module A = ℚℓ/ℤℓ carries the action ρ(g) = χ(g)−e. For a class in degree one, inflate to G and restrict a cocycle z to U. Since U acts trivially, z restricts to an additive character φ, and every coboundary restricts to zero. Conjugation of the cocycle identity gives φ(gug−1) = ρ(g)φ(u). Local triviality forces φ to vanish on inertia and on decomposition groups above ℓ, in each case after intersection with U. We will recover exactly the equivariant characters with these vanishing conditions. Transport of the infinite character group and its fixed-point condition Image of the locally trivial subgroup

For injectivity, suppose φ = 0. The cocycle then factors through the cyclotomic quotient. In our fields this quotient is procyclic. Choose g whose image topologically generates it. The value χ(g)−e − 1 is nonzero. Indeed the cyclotomic degrees over a fixed number field grow without bound, whereas the ℓ-adic roots of a fixed nonzero power are finite. Multiplication by this nonzero scalar is surjective on A, so choose a with (ρ(g) − 1)a = z(g). Subtract its coboundary. The resulting cocycle vanishes on g, hence by the cocycle rule on all its powers, and by continuity on the quotient. This is where the condition e ≠ 0 enters. Injectivity and the nontrivial cyclotomic power

For surjectivity let φ be one of the equivariant characters. Write C for the closure of the powers of a lift of a cyclotomic generator. Its compact image surjects onto χ(G), so G = CU. The character φ vanishes on C ∩ U. In group-theoretic terms the pro-ℓ part of the procyclic group C maps onto an infinite pro-ℓ group. That part is ℤℓ, and all its nonzero closed subgroups have finite index. Its kernel is therefore zero. The intersection has only prime-to-ℓ finite quotients, so its continuous characters into ℓ-primary torsion vanish. The code proves this vanishing directly by congruences in finite quotients. We may consequently define

z(cu) = ρ(c)φ(u),   c ∈ C,   u ∈ U.

Two decompositions differ by an element of the intersection, making the formula independent of the choice. Equivariance gives the cocycle identity on multiplying two decompositions. Continuity follows because C × U → G is a continuous surjection of compact Hausdorff spaces, hence a quotient map. Away from ℓ, inertia already lies in the cyclotomic kernel, where φ vanishes. Above ℓ, the restricted cocycle vanishes on the kernel of the local cyclotomic character. Its image on the decomposition group is open in ℤℓ×, and the allowed roots of unity give the same procyclic condition. The preceding generator argument therefore subtracts a local coboundary. Inflation from the group unramified outside ℓ now supplies a locally trivial class, whose restriction is φ. The inverse action and the Tate twist

Return to the base field L0 and one of its cyclotomic layers Lm of relative degree ℓm, embedded in the same algebraic closure. The two fields have the same infinite cyclotomic kernel. At the reference layer the proof also needs a comparison with degree two. The Tate dual of A(−e) has integral coefficient twist e + 1. Under finite-level Poitou–Tate duality, local finiteness and local Tate duality, the two locally trivial groups have equal length. Take a decreasing filtration on the locally trivial degree-two group and an increasing torsion-level filtration on degree one. The former is separated and the latter exhaustive. At all sufficiently high levels the finite quotients of the first and the finite pieces of the second have equal cardinality by finite-level duality and control of localization. Their lengths therefore agree, including the case of unbounded lengths. The degree-two group embeds into full H², giving the upper bound used for the exceptional twists.
