How many powers of ℓ divide an iterate of the parameter?

The Frobenius matrix of the limit lattice has entries in a power-series ring. To compare it with its value at the constant term, we shall apply Frobenius repeatedly. The following estimate explains why the resulting matrices can converge even when a power of ℓ must be divided out at every step.

Fix a prime ℓ, a finite extension K of ℚℓ and its chosen Breuil–Kisin datum. Write 𝔖 = W(kK)[[u]] for the coefficient ring over the residue field kK, E for its monic Eisenstein polynomial of degree b ≥ 1, and e for evaluation into Ainf. Write ι for the natural map into R = Acris, and put v = ι(e(u)) and η = ι(e(E)). The evaluation commutes with Frobenius, which takes u to uℓ and fixes ℓ. In particular φ(v) = vℓ.

Growth of the parameter. For every pair of integers c, M ≥ 0 there exists n₀ such that for every n ≥ n₀,

vℓn−1 ∈ ℓcn+MR.

Proof. Fontaine's map θ kills e(E). The construction of R adjoins the divided powers of elements of ker θ to Ainf and completes for the ℓ-adic topology. In particular ηℓ/ℓ! belongs to R. Multiplying by (ℓ − 1)! gives ηℓ/ℓ in R. Since E ≡ ub modulo ℓ, it follows that vbℓ ≡ ηℓ ≡ 0 modulo ℓ. Raising this divisibility to the power cn + M gives the claim as soon as ℓn − 1 ≥ bℓ(cn + M). The left side grows exponentially and the right side grows at most linearly. The exponent ℓn − 1 leaves one factor of v available when we apply the estimate to φn(v). We will also use that R is ℓ-torsion-free, complete and separated. Brinon and Conrad, CMI Summer School Notes on p-adic Hodge Theory, §9.1, (9.1.1) and Proposition 9.1.1.

How do the two Frobenius evaluations compare?

Keep R = Acris and v = ι(e(u)). The first evaluation takes a series s to f(s) = ι(e(φ(s))). The second retains only its constant term before applying the first, so f₀(s) = f(s(0)), with constants included in 𝔖. For matrices both evaluations act entrywise. Let H, d ≥ 0 and let A, A′ be square matrices of size d over 𝔖 satisfying AA′ = E2HI. The two evaluations send the scalar on the right to different elements. We first put both products in the same form.

Normalization lemma. Both f(E) = ℓw and f₀(E) = ℓw₀ are ℓ times units in R. Put D = ℓ2H, and define

α = w−Hf(A), α′ = w−Hf(A′), β = w₀−Hf₀(A), β′ = w₀−Hf₀(A′).

Then αα′ = α′α = ββ′ = β′β = DI. The difference ℓ(α − β) is divisible by vℓ entrywise.

Proof. The constant term of E is ℓ times a unit in W(kK). This gives the assertion about w₀. For w use φ(E) = Eℓ + ℓδ(E), where δ(E) is a unit by the chosen datum. Put y = ηℓ/ℓ, integral by the divided-power calculation. Its ℓth power belongs to ℓR. Indeed ηℓ²/(ℓ²)! is also a divided power, and (ℓ²)! contains at least ℓ + 1 factors of ℓ, one from each of ℓ, 2ℓ, …, ℓ² and one more from ℓ². Thus w = ι(e(δ(E))) + y is a unit modulo ℓ, as the sum of a unit and a nilpotent in a commutative ring. It is a unit in R as well. Indeed an inverse modulo ℓ gives a product 1 − ℓz, whose inverse is the convergent geometric series in ℓz.

Applying the two evaluations to AA′ and cancelling the unit factors gives αα′ = ββ′ = DI. Over R[1/ℓ], the scalar D is a unit. Taking determinants shows that both factors of each product have unit determinant, so each pair also has product DI in the reverse order. The resulting equalities hold in R by ℓ-torsion-freeness.

Finally, s − s(0) is divisible by u in 𝔖. Frobenius and evaluation therefore give f(s) − f₀(s) ∈ vℓR. Apply this first to E to obtain ℓ(w − w₀) ∈ vℓR. Since both factors are units, the difference w−H − w₀−H is a multiple of w − w₀. Apply the series calculation again to each entry of A. In ℓ(α − β), both the difference of evaluations and the difference of normalizing units now carry vℓ. This accounts for the factor ℓ retained in the lemma.

How do the Frobenius iterates give an integral section?

Integral Frobenius section theorem. Let A, A′ be matrices over 𝔖 of the same size d with AA′ = E2HI. For the two evaluations f and f₀ there exist an integer κ ≥ 0 and matrices Q, Q′ over R = Acris such that

QQ′ = ℓ2κI,  Q ≡ Q′ ≡ ℓκI (mod v),
f(E)HQf₀(A) = f₀(E)Hf(A)φ(Q).

Thus ℓ−κQ becomes an invertible change of basis over R[1/ℓ], with inverse ℓ−κQ′. Its residue modulo v is the identity. We construct both matrices integrally so that their reductions are available before inverting ℓ.

Proof. Use the normalized matrices α, α′, β, β′ and the scalar D = ℓ2H from the preceding lemma. Start with U₀ = U′₀ = I, and define the two sequences by

Un+1 = αφ(Un)β′,  U′n+1 = βφ(U′n)α′.

The first difference satisfies ℓ(U₁ − DI) = ℓ(α − β)β′ ∈ vℓ Matd(R). Each subsequent difference comes from the preceding one by applying φ, multiplying by α on the left and by β′ on the right. Because φ(v) = vℓ and φ(D) = D, induction gives

ℓ(Un+1 − DUn) ∈ vℓn+1 Matd(R).

A second induction gives ℓn(Un − DnI) ≡ 0 modulo v. Indeed the difference at n + 1 is the sum of Un+1 − DUn and D(Un − DnI). After multiplication by ℓn+1, the first term is divisible by v by the displayed estimate and the second by induction. The same two arguments apply to U′, starting from ℓ(β − α)α′.

Choose n₀ by the growth bound with c = 2H + 1 and M = 1. For n ≥ n₀, separate one factor v from vℓn+1. The remaining power absorbs ℓ(2H+1)(n+1)+1. By cancelling ℓ from the difference estimate in the torsion-free ring we obtain

Un+1 − DUn ∈ v ℓ(2H+1)(n+1) Matd(R).

Set S₀ = Un₀. We construct integral matrices Sj with DjSj = Un₀+j. To extend the sequence, take n = n₀ + j and divide the difference in the last display by Dj+1. This gives an integral correction Sj+1 − Sj in v ℓ(2H+1)n₀+j+1 Matd(R). The exponent tends to infinity. Factor out v and sum the remaining corrections in the complete ring R. The result gives a limit S with S ≡ S₀ modulo v. The same construction gives S′ from the primed sequence.

The products at finite stages identify the limit. Substituting the two recurrences brings β′β = DI together in the middle of Un+1U′n+1. Induction then gives UnU′n = D2nI, using αα′ = DI for the outside factors. Multiplication of the first recurrence on the right by β gives Un+1β = Dαφ(Un). Substitute the expressions in Sj and cancel the common powers of ℓ. This gives SjS′j = D2n₀I and Sj+1β = αφ(Sj). Both identities pass to the limit. Matrix multiplication is continuous and φ preserves every ideal ℓjR.

Take κ = (2H + 1)n₀, Q = ℓn₀S and Q′ = ℓn₀S′. The product becomes ℓ2n₀D2n₀I = ℓ2κI. To check the residue of Q, replace S by Un₀ modulo v. The second induction gives ℓn₀Un₀ ≡ ℓn₀Dn₀I = ℓκI. It gives the same residue for Q′.

Finally Qβ = αφ(Q). Substituting the definitions and multiplying by f(E)Hw₀H yields the asserted Frobenius identity. The choice of κ depends only on the height and the fixed datum. For example when A = A′ = EHI, the evaluations normalize to α = β = ℓHI, so Q = Q′ = I already works with κ = 0.
