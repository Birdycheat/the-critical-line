How does a bound in L² control the period of one cycle?

A low-dimensional simplex can have zero volume in the surrounding space. An L² estimate on the ambient quotient therefore gives no direct estimate for restriction to that simplex. What makes the argument work here is that a closed cycle has the same period after its vertices are averaged. The resulting integral can be estimated using ambient volume.

Fix a finite bar cycle z of degree m ≥ 1 in GLN(𝒪L′). Write F(y₀,…,ym) for the integral of the form ω on the affine simplex with those vertices in the cone of positive Hermitian matrices. The proof assumes that F is invariant under the arithmetic group and that its alternating sum over the faces of every (m + 1)-simplex vanishes. For a bar symbol [g₁|…|gm], set h₀ = 1 and hj = g₁⋯gj. Choose independent points Y₀,…,Ym with normalized volume on a closed ball about the identity matrix I, small enough to lie inside the cone. Then

⟨ω,z⟩ = ∑g z(g) · 𝔼[F(h₀·Y₀,…,hm·Ym)].

To see why, allow each vertex to carry one of two marks, “fixed at I” or “averaged.” Removing a vertex leaves the same product probability law on the others. The alternating face sum of the marked cochain is therefore the expectation of a face sum of F, hence zero. The prism between the two choices of basepoint has coboundary equal to the difference between all-averaged and all-fixed evaluations. On z this difference is zero because ∂z = 0. A one-dimensional check. Let ℤ act on ℝ by translations, put P(x) = x + sin(2πx), and take F(x,y) = P(y) − P(x). Integer translation leaves F unchanged and its three face terms telescope. The bar cycle [2] has period F(0,2) = 2. Take independent Y₀,Y₁ uniform on {0,1/4}. The four values of F(Y₀,2 + Y₁) are 2, 13/4, 3/4 and 2. Their mean is 2. This discrete example checks the averaging identity. The volume estimate uses the ball with its continuous distribution.

For that estimate, fix a bar symbol. Let Aj be the invertible real linear action of hj on the ambient Hermitian space of real dimension d. A point of the random simplex is ∑j τjAjYj, with nonnegative barycentric coordinates summing to one. Some τi ≥ 1/(m + 1). Hold all the other vertices fixed. Changing variables in Yi gives a density bounded by (m + 1)d/(vol(B) |det Ai|), where B is the ball. The edge lengths are uniformly bounded and the possible simplex points fill a compact set K inside the cone. Integrating in the simplex parameters now bounds the expected absolute simplex integral by a constant times ∫K ‖ω‖. Central-scalar invariance lets the proof unfold this last integral into a bound by the integral of the moving-frame norm ‖fr ω‖ on a compact set K′ inside ∏w SLN(ℂ). Write Λ for the image of SLN(𝒪L′) and D for its fundamental domain. If λ₀x and λx both belong to K′, then λλ₀⁻¹ lies in the finite set Λ ∩ K′(K′)⁻¹. Its cardinality bounds the number of visits from any one orbit. Arithmetic invariance therefore bounds the integral on K′ by this cardinality times ∫D ‖fr ω‖. Cauchy–Schwarz supplies the last factor vol(D)1/2 ‖fr ω‖2. Summing over the finite support of z with weights |z(g)| gives a constant Cz independent of the form. Apply this bound to ωj = Hjω₀. The Hecke contraction acts on each scalar coefficient in a fixed basis at the identity. Finite-dimensional norm comparison bounds the squared norm of the whole form by Kbasis times the sum of its squared coefficients. If S₀ is the sum of their initial L² norms squared, then for every j ≥ 0,

|⟨Hjω₀,z⟩| ≤ Cz √(KbasisS₀) (3/5)j.

The local estimate holds for each fixed cycle and every admissible square-integrable form. Mean zero, N ≥ 3 and a prime element of norm at least 25 enter when applying the contraction. The second estimate on the stabilized cycles recovers the period before iteration.
