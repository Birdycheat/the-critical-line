How can residue points on the marked fibre power be compared?

The preceding comparisons give an equality of quotient moduli points and an equality of images of points on the abelian varieties. The tower uses a space with additional coordinates. Let Y → M record the auxiliary level structure, with universal family AY pulled back from AM. A point on the k-fold fibre power 𝒵 consists of a point on Y and a tuple of k points in its abelian fibre. Even if two isogenies have the same underlying target in M, they still have to agree on the marking and on that tuple.

Separation by evaluation. Two maps from a test scheme to 𝒵 agree if they have the same composite to M and give the same values in AM on all of the following tests:

  • For every lattice label y, evaluate the auxiliary level section η(y) at the marked base point and project to AM.
  • For each of the k coordinates, project that coordinate from AY to AM.

Indeed the two maps to Y now classify the same object over M and the same marking on it. To compare the markings, pull both objects back to the test scheme. The Cartesian square of universal abelian schemes identifies sections over a fixed base map by their images in the underlying universal family. The equalities on every label therefore identify the marked objects. The representing property of Y gives the same map to Y. Over that common map the same Cartesian square identifies each coordinate. The universal property of the fibre product then gives equality in 𝒵. For k = 0 only the marking tests are needed.

Transporting the tests. Write ψ for the isogeny on the correspondence. If a lifted source point has tuple (x₁,…,xk), the target tuple has entries ψ(xi). For a marking η(y), pull its section back along the source square of the correspondence. The defining compatibility of the marked isogeny says that applying ψ to this pulled-back section gives the target marking. Thus all the tests are evaluated by applying the same isogeny to known points on its source. Formation of these images commutes with passage to the residue field since the square defining each lifted point is Cartesian.

Suppose two subspaces missing the ordinary line have the same image modulo the line. The reduced quotient comparison gives the same base in M and the same images for all the tests just listed. The separation statement gives the same residue target in 𝒵. If one subspace contains the line and the other misses it, the relative Frobenius comparison gives those same equalities after the specified residue twist on the second target. Applying separation to that twisted point gives equality in 𝒵 again. In particular, the conclusion also identifies tuples whose coordinates lie outside the torsion group used to label the kernel.
