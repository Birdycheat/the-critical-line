Why do finitely many regions suffice for the cusp integral?

Let L be totally complex and set Γ = SLN(𝒪L). In the product of SLN(ℂ) over the complex places, a single matrix can travel arbitrarily far out a cusp. What becomes finite is the choice of arithmetic coordinate system. For fixed L and N ≥ 1, there are finitely many matrices cj and one region S such that for every x, some γ ∈ Γ and some j satisfy cj−1γx ∈ S. Inside S the diagonal parameters can still grow. First choose finitely many rational matrices. Append the identity at the finite places to an archimedean point x. Adelic reduction supplies a rational matrix η such that ηx has a bounded upper triangular factor, a positive diagonal and a compact factor. The positive diagonal equals the identity at the finite places. Thus the finite part of η belongs to one compact set C, the finite projection of the product of the compact triangular set and the maximal compact subgroup. The subgroup U of matrices integral and invertible at every finite place is open. Its left cosets cover C, and finitely many suffice. In each of these cosets that occurs for a rational η, choose one rational representative a. Then a−1η and its inverse are integral at every finite place. Each entry therefore lies in 𝒪L. Writing δ = a−1η, we have δ ∈ GLN(𝒪L) and δx equals a−1 times the reduced factors. The finite list for this GL cover consists of the inverses g = a−1. Correct the determinant. The unit det δ can be written vNu₀. Here v is a unit and u₀ comes from a finite list. Indeed, express a unit as a root of unity times powers of the fundamental units and reduce each exponent modulo N. The list has at most |μ(L)|Nrank 𝒪L× entries. Put D(u) = diag(u,1,…,1). This gives an integral change of coordinates of determinant one.

γ = v−1D(u₀−1)δ,   det γ = v−Nu₀−1 det δ = 1.

The chart now depends only on (g, u₀). Set G = D(u₀−1)g. At each complex place w choose a scalar zw with zwN det w(G) = 1 and take cj,w = zww(G). This is the precise rationality used for the height bound. The underlying matrix G is defined over L, while the scalar can depend on the place. A determinant correction over the Gaussian integers

Test the correction with N = 2, L = ℚ(i), x = g = I and δ = [[i,1],[0,1]]. Here v = 1 and u₀ = i work. Then γ = [[1,−i],[0,1]]. Choose z = exp(πi/4), so z² = i and c = z diag(−i,1) has determinant one. In this chart c−1γ = z−1δ = [[1,1],[0,1]] diag(i/z,1/z). The last factor is unitary of determinant one. This tests the correction on one admissible triangular factorization. The full list of charts may contain other representatives.

How are the chart bounds shared across places?

Before the determinant correction, write the positive diagonal entries as rᵢ, with t ri+1 ≤ rᵢ for fixed t > 0. Compactness bounds the entries of each triangular factor Bw by M and bounds its diagonal moduli below by M−1. After the correction the local scalar is αw = w(v−1)/zw. Use the same diagonal coordinates at every place by setting

sᵢ = log rᵢ + meanw(log |αw| + log |Bw,ii|).

The local determinant-one equations give ∑isᵢ = 0. Subtracting two places’ determinant equations also gives |log |αw| − log |αw′|| ≤ 2 log M. After removing exp(sᵢ) and moving the diagonal phases into the unitary factor, every triangular entry has modulus at most M⁴ and each diagonal modulus is at least M−4. In each diagonal position their product over the places is one, by the definition of the mean. The common region uses si+1 ≤ sᵢ + 4 log M − log t. These bounds hold across all charts for this field and matrix size. Integrate over the cover. Write F for a Haar fundamental domain, and let f be a nonnegative Γ-invariant integrand. Moving pieces of F by Γ gives an upper bound by the integral over the union of the charts. Subadditivity and left invariance of Haar measure then give

∫F f dμ ≤ ∑j ∫S f(cjy) dμ(y).

Overlaps are allowed. Each chart has its own finite height constant, and there are only finitely many summands. For f = heightA the remaining integral on each chart is finite when N ≥ A + 2. This last convergence comes from the decay in the unbounded diagonal variables.
