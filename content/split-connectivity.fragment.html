When does every cycle of ordered split pairs bound?

A split pair in Rn is a vector v with a specified linear functional λ satisfying λ(v) = 1. An ordered list of pairs is split if λi(vj) = δij. Write Cp for the free abelian group on such lists of length p. Its boundary deletes one pair at a time, starting with sign +. Thus a vertex has boundary the empty list, and an edge has boundary its last vertex minus its first. The stability argument needs to know when these signed lists can fill a cycle. This holds for every commutative ring with identity and all nonnegative integers n, p, s satisfying the ring’s stable-range hypothesis at s. That hypothesis says that whenever d ≥ s and the entries of (r₁,…,rd,t) generate the unit ideal, one can find a₁,…,ad so that the entries ri + ait still generate it. Length zero gives surjectivity onto the augmentation group ℤ. For p ≥ 1 the conclusion is vanishing of reduced integral homology in degree p − 1. Filling the link of an empty prefix

Let Ln be the span of the first n coordinate vectors (numbered from zero), and let j ≥ n. The permitted letters lie in S = Ln ∪ (eⱼ + Ln). Suppose that, for every unimodular prefix σ, cycles of q further letters in Ln bound when q + s + |σ| ≤ n. We show that a cycle of length p in S, starting with empty prefix, bounds when p + s ≤ n + 1.

First remove letters outside the low slice from the cycle, working from right to left. At the rightmost position still containing an outside letter, group the offending words by their prefix τ ending at that position. For a fixed τ, its tails form a cycle in the low link of τ. Indeed a face obtained by deleting a prefix letter has its last outside letter strictly earlier, so the component of the boundary still beginning with τ sees only tail deletions. If x is the last letter of τ, then xⱼ = 1. Move x to the front for the unimodularity test and replace every other prefix letter u by u − uⱼx. This splits off the summand generated by x. Every low tail letter has coordinate j zero and is fixed. Thus the very same low tails extend a unimodular prefix of length |τ| − 1. Their filling is available since (p − |τ|) + s + (|τ| − 1) = p + s − 1 ≤ n.

Attach each filling after its original prefix with sign (−1)|τ| and subtract its boundary. Its tail faces cancel the offending terms, and its prefix faces have their last outside letter earlier. Iterating leaves a homologous cycle c supported in the low slice. Prepending eⱼ to each term preserves unimodularity because its coordinate splits off and all low letters have zero j-th coordinate. The signed boundary is ∂[eⱼ,c] = c − [eⱼ,∂c] = c. This includes length zero, where ∂[eⱼ] = [ ].

A nonempty prefix in the passage from rank n to rank n + 1

Fix n ≥ s ≥ 1 and j ≥ n + 1. Suppose that links with tail letters in Sₙ = Lₙ ∪ (eⱼ + Lₙ) are exact for every unimodular prefix σ in the range q + s + |σ| ≤ n + 1. For a nonempty unimodular prefix τ we want to fill cycles of length p in its Sn+1-link when p + s + |τ| ≤ n + 2. Choose a letter v of τ and a form f with f(v) = 1. Write h = f − ∑i<n f(eᵢ)δi, where δi reads coordinate i. The first n coordinates of v together with h(v) generate 1. Stable range gives t ∈ Lₙ such that the first n coordinates of w = v + h(v)t generate 1.

The shear x ↦ x + h(x)t carries v to w and has inverse obtained by subtraction, since h(t) = 0. Choose a linear combination λ of the first n coordinate forms with λ(w) = 1 − wₙ. The second shear x ↦ x + λ(x)eₙ is invertible for the same reason. Both maps and their inverses add vectors in Ln+1, so preserve each allowed coset there. They transport the entire link to one in which the chosen prefix letter has vₙ = 1.

For any extension σ in this transported link with |σ| ≤ p, restrict the letters after τσ to Sₙ. These letters have coordinate n zero because j ≥ n + 1. Splitting off v as above fixes each restricted letter and shortens the combined prefix to length |τ| + |σ| − 1. The rank-n hypothesis fills tails of length p − |σ| because the sum in its range is p + s + |τ| − 1 ≤ n + 1. For nonempty σ these fillings move the last outside position to the left by the preceding boundary argument. For empty σ the same bound fills the remaining cycle in Sₙ. This proves the rank step for the whole link.

To see how the induction starts each new rank, first obtain the low-slice conclusion in Ln+1. Use the two slices Lₙ ∪ (eₙ + Lₙ) within it. For an extension σ the smaller-rank affine hypothesis applies directly to the concatenated prefix τσ, since (p − |σ|) + s + |τσ| = p + s + |τ| ≤ n + 1. The same boundary argument fills the full low link. The empty-prefix cone then gives the affine conclusion for the empty prefix at the new rank. For a nonempty prefix use the two shears when n ≥ s and the singleton companion at the first admissible rank. At rank zero with s ≥ 1 the low range is empty, and only the empty-prefix cone can be required.

How does stable range give a companion in the prescribed translate?

Write Ln for the span of the first n coordinate vectors in the module of finitely supported sequences. Fix j ≥ n ≥ s and choose a form f with f(v) = 1. Put aᵢ = f(eᵢ). Remove from f its first n coordinates and its j-th coordinate to obtain φ. With b = φ(v) + aⱼvⱼ, we have 1 = ∑i<n aᵢvᵢ + b. Stable range therefore supplies tᵢ such that the shortened entries vᵢ + tᵢb generate the unit ideal.

Take x = eⱼ − aⱼ∑i<n tᵢeᵢ and v′ = v − vⱼx. By construction x ∈ eⱼ + Ln, its j-th coordinate is one and φ(x) = 0. Each shortened entry is vᵢ′ + tᵢφ(v′), so it belongs to the ideal generated by the coordinates of v′. Those coordinates therefore generate 1. Choose a form h with h(v′) = 1. Three edges over the field of two elements. In rank two, each of the three nonzero vectors has exactly two possible covectors taking value one on it. A vertex (v,λ) has a unique compatible neighbor. Its vector w is the nonzero element of ker λ, and its covector is uniquely determined by taking value zero on v and one on w. The six vertices therefore form three disjoint edges when directions are forgotten. In particular, A = (e₁,e₁*) and C = (e₁,e₁* + e₂*) are in different components. In rank three, extend these pairs by zero. With U = (e₃,e₃*) the integral boundary of [A,U] + [U,C] is [C] − [A]. Thus one extra coordinate fills this particular cycle.
