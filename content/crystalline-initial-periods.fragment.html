How does Frobenius act on a basis of crystalline periods?

Let V be a continuous crystalline representation of the absolute Galois group GK of a finite extension K of ℚℓ. Fix a stable lattice and use its basis to write the action as ρ(g). With k the residue field and K₀ = W(k)[1/ℓ], the crystalline comparison carries a K₀-basis of invariant periods to the columns of an invertible matrix U over Bcris. Brinon and Conrad, CMI Summer School Notes on p-adic Hodge Theory, Proposition 9.1.9.

ρ(g) g(U) = U   (g ∈ GK),   f = U−1φ(U),   f′ = φ(U−1)U.

Frobenius commutes with g and fixes ρ(g), so φ(U) and U obey the same Galois law. Their change of basis f and its inverse f′ are therefore fixed entrywise and lie in K₀. Choose a, b ≥ 0 so that F = ℓaf and F′ = ℓbf′ have Witt-vector entries. Clearing the two denominators gives

ℓc₁ φ(U) = UF,   FF′ = ℓc₃I,   c₁ = a,   c₃ = a + b.

An unramified quadratic character. Over ℚ₃ let ρ be −1 on arithmetic Frobenius and 1 on inertia. Let i be the Teichmüller lift of a root of x² + 1 over 𝔽₉, so i² = −1. The Witt vectors of 𝔽₉ lie in Bcris, and both arithmetic Frobenius and Witt Frobenius send i to i³ = −i. Thus U = i is invariant for the combined action. The two signs cancel on Frobenius, and inertia fixes i. Since U−1 = −i, F = F′ = −1 and both denominator exponents are zero.

Extracting a basis from spanning invariant columns

Express the standard vectors as finite Bcris-linear combinations of invariants. A K₀-basis selected from those columns still spans every standard vector over the ring. For independence, suppose there is a shortest relation over Frac(Bcris), normalized to have one coefficient 1. Subtracting the relation from any Galois conjugate gives a shorter one unless all coefficients are fixed. They would then lie in K₀, contradicting independence there. Brinon and Conrad, §9.1, Theorem 9.1.5 and Proposition 9.1.6.

How can the period matrix and its inverse be made integral at the same height?

Use the Breuil–Kisin ring 𝔖 = W(k)[[u]], the Eisenstein polynomial E of the chosen uniformizer and the compatible evaluations in the period rings. Since Bcris = Acris[1/t], one exponent N₀ clears the t-denominators of both U and U−1. We can increase it to meet the filtration bounds as well.

The filtered comparison writes each standard vector as a sum of invariant columns of degrees at least ki with coefficients of degrees at least −ki. In the basis U these columns have entries in BdRGK = K. Clear the denominators of each column by a nonzero ℤℓ-scalar and divide its coefficient by the same scalar. It is a unit in BdR+, so both degrees are preserved. This gives UCβ = I for an integral, possibly rectangular matrix C over 𝒪K and a matrix β of coefficients. Brinon and Conrad, §9.1, construction of Bcris and Proposition 9.1.9.

Take N ≥ N₀ bounding every |ki| and every de Rham pole order of U. The filtered modification yields square matrices X, X′ over 𝔖 and c₂ ≥ 0 with

XX′ = ℓc₂E2NI,   UX, X′U−1 ∈ Matd(FilN BdR).

For every target prism over 𝒪K and every pair of maps f, h to it from the Breuil–Kisin prism, the two-map comparison supplies j ≥ 0 such that f(E)2N divides every entry of ℓjf(X′)h(X). The exponent can depend on the pair.

Let Z, Z′ lift tNU, tNU−1 to Acris. Then ZZ′ = t2NI, and ZX, X′Z′ both have degree at least 2N because t has degree one. Substituting the lifts in the action laws for U gives the following equalities in Acris by injectivity into Bcris.

tNℓc₁φ(Z) = φ(t)NZF,   tNρ(g) g(Z) = g(t)NZ.

Why can the initial Galois correction be the identity on the entire disc?

Fix a primitive Breuil–Kisin period τ and write e for the image of E on the closed period disc. The bounded comparison γ sends integral crystalline elements of filtration degree at least r into (er) and satisfies γ(t) = νeτ for a unit ν. We may therefore divide both γ(Z)X and X′γ(Z′) by e2N. The normalized quotients W, W′ satisfy WW′ = τ2NI.

Put P = WX′ and b = eτ. For any g ∈ GK, take any norm-nonincreasing disc maps Φ, G extending Witt Frobenius and g on Ainf, respectively. Applying γ to the integral action laws gives

ℓc₁ Φ(P) = Φ(e)N PF,   bN ρ(g) G(P) = G(b)N P.

The general disc law allows an extra right factor κD(g) after P. Here the equality holds with κD(g) = I for every g, including in the nontrivial quadratic example. For the pole bound use the two prism maps ev and g ∘ ev. Since ℓ is a unit on the disc, cancellation of ℓj gives e2N dividing every entry of X′G(X). At every depth the exponent 0 and the single witness Λ = I make both approximation errors zero for all g and all compatible G.

In the integral frame, the change of basis itself transforms under g. Its evaluated entries agree with their conjugates modulo ev(u), since g fixes the constant coefficients and preserves the ideal of the origin. The integral correction calculation thus gives ε(g) ≡ I modulo ev(u) in Ainf for every g. This proves the crystalline test on the integral layer. The de Rham test uses the transported witnesses, and a common height across layers requires a common bound on their filtration jumps.

Normalizing the two quotients and their actions

Let Y, Y′ be the quotients by e2N in the order above. In the numerator of YY′, XX′ contributes ℓc₂e2N and γ(ZZ′) contributes (νeτ)2N. Thus W = ν−NY and W′ = ℓ−c₂ν−NY′ have the stated product. Multiplying γ(Z)X = e2NY by X′ on the right gives P = ν−Nℓc₂γ(Z).

The bounded comparison commutes with both disc maps. Substitute this expression for P and γ(t) = νb into the integral action laws and cancel ν and its transforms. In the Frobenius law, φ(τ) = ev(E)τ gives Φ(b) = Φ(e)b. Its common factor bN cancels because the disc ring is a domain and b is nonzero.
