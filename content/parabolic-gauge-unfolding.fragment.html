Subalgebra Haar asymptotics, conjugation modulus on the isotropic nilradical, and parabolic integral factorization

Passing from ambient Haar integration on G = U(p, q) to product coordinates (u, m, k) ∈ N × M × K in the real parabolic integration formula rests on two local-to-global bridges. First, each closed subgroup S among G, N, M, K, and L = K ∩ M is cut out inside G by a condition g − 1 ∈ A for a real matrix subalgebra A ⊆ Mn(ℂ). Because orthogonal projection onto the tangent intersection A ∩ Lie(G) is isometric to first order near the identity, normalizing a left Haar measure μ on S against Hilbert–Schmidt small balls forces rescaled integrals of compactly supported bump functions at scale r → 0+ to converge to Euclidean Lebesgue integrals on ℝd, and pins μ down uniquely. Second, when a normalizing element m ∈ M conjugates S = N by T(u) = m u m−1, left Haar uniqueness scales the pushforward T∗μN by a constant c > 0, while submultiplicativity of the Frobenius norm sandwiches T−1 of a small group ball between two concentric small balls; sending r → 0+ against a rescaled bump function equates c with |detℝ(Ad(m)|Lie(N))|−1. Completing the totally isotropic flag by signed-form orthogonals to a self-dual (2a + 1)-step chain then evaluates dimℝ N = dN and matches that inverse real determinant with the one-place flag layer product P1(s), after which left-(NM)- and right-K-invariance yield the parabolic integration formula up to a positive finite constant κ.

1. Rescaled small-ball asymptotics on matrix-algebra subgroups. Let G = U(p, q) (n = p + q ≥ 0) be defined by a diagonal signature matrix Jε = diag(ε1, …, εn) ∈ GLn(ℂ) (εl = ±1), and equip Mn(ℂ) with the real trace pairing ⟨X, Y⟩ = Re tr(X Y∗) and Hilbert–Schmidt norm ‖X‖HS = (Re tr(X X∗))1/2. Let A ⊆ Mn(ℂ) be a real linear subspace closed under matrix multiplication (X, Y ∈ A ⇒ X Y ∈ A), and let E1, …, Ed be a Hilbert–Schmidt orthonormal real basis (Re tr(Ei Ek∗) = δik) spanning A ∩ Lie(G). On the closed subgroup S = {g ∈ G : g − 1 ∈ A}, define the tangent projection t : G → ℝd by ti(g) = Re tr((g − 1)Ei∗), and call a left-S-invariant Borel measure μ supported on S a d-gauge left Haar measure on S if μ({g ∈ S : ‖g − 1‖HS ≤ r}) / rd → ωd as r → 0+, where ωd is the Lebesgue volume of the unit Euclidean ball in ℝd.

Proposition (Subalgebra Haar uniqueness and local rescaling). Every d-gauge left Haar measure μ on S is σ-finite, finite on compact subsets, and uniquely determined on S; moreover, there exists a threshold r0 > 0 such that for every 0 < r1 ≤ r0 and every continuous compactly supported function φ : ℝd → ℝ,

limr → 0+ r−d ∫{g ∈ S : ‖g − 1‖HS < √r1} φ(t(g)/r) dμ(g) = ∫ℝd φ(t) dt.

2. Inverse-determinant conjugation modulus. Retain S = {g ∈ G : g − 1 ∈ A}, its d-gauge left Haar measure μ, and the orthonormal basis E1, …, Ed of A ∩ Lie(G). Suppose m ∈ G normalizes S (m S m−1 = S) and acts on A ∩ Lie(G) by Ad(m)(Ek) = m Ek m−1 = ∑i=1d Lik Ei with det L ≠ 0, and write T : S → S for conjugation T(u) = m u m−1.

Lemma (Conjugation pushforward modulus). The pushforward measure T∗μ satisfies T∗μ = |det L|−1 μ.

Proof. Since T is a topological group automorphism of S (with inverse u ↦ m−1u m), T∗μ is a left Haar measure supported on S, so Haar uniqueness gives T∗μ = c μ for a scalar c > 0. For every u ∈ S, closure of S under inversion and u−1 = Jεu∗Jε split u − 1 = X(u) + Z(u), where X(u) = ((u − 1) − (u−1 − 1))/2 ∈ A ∩ Lie(G) and Z(u) = ((u − 1) + (u−1 − 1))/2 satisfies Z(u)∗Jε = JεZ(u) and is Hilbert–Schmidt orthogonal to Lie(G). Hence X(u) = ∑k=1d tk(u)Ek, while X(m u m−1) = m X(u)m−1 and Z(m u m−1) = m Z(u)m−1 give t(m u m−1) = L t(u) identically on S. Moreover, writing h(u) = ‖u − 1‖HS² and C = ‖m‖HS² ‖m−1‖HS² + 1 ≥ 1, submultiplicativity of the Hilbert–Schmidt norm on m(u − 1)m−1 yields h(T(u)) ≤ C h(u) and symmetrically h(u) ≤ C h(T(u)). Choose a nonnegative continuous compactly supported bump φ : ℝd → [0, ∞) with ∫ℝd φ(x) dx > 0, set ψ(x) = φ(L x) (so ∫ℝd ψ(x) dx = |det L|−1 ∫ℝd φ(x) dx), and fix 0 < ρ ≤ r0. Changing variables under T∗μ = c μ gives

c ∫{h < ρ/C} φ(t(u)/r) dμ(u) = ∫T−1{h < ρ/C} φ(t(T(u))/r) dμ(u) = ∫T−1{h < ρ/C} ψ(t(u)/r) dμ(u).

Because {h < ρ/C²} ⊆ T−1{h < ρ/C} ⊆ {h < ρ} and ψ ≥ 0, multiplying by r−d and sending r → 0+ via the local rescaling limit at r1 = ρ/C, ρ/C², and ρ forces c ∫ℝd φ = ∫ℝd ψ = |det L|−1 ∫ℝd φ, whence c = |det L|−1.

3. Self-dual doubled flag, nilradical dimension, and parabolic Haar factorization. Let 0 = W0 ⊂ W1 ⊂ ⋯ ⊂ Wa be a totally isotropic flag in (ℂn, Jε) of positive rank jumps ji = dimℂ(Wi/Wi−1) ≥ 1 (1 ≤ i ≤ a) and total isotropic rank J = ∑i=1a ji ≤ min(p, q) (so 2J ≤ n). For m ∈ M, put Ds = |detℂ(m|Fs)| > 0 and write si = (Di/Di−1)−1/ji (1 ≤ i ≤ a) for the local inverse graded sizes.

On S = {g ∈ G : g − 1 ∈ A}, closure of A under multiplication makes exp : A ∩ Lie(G) → S map into S (via power-series convergence in the finite-dimensional subspace A), with strict derivative at 0 equal to the Hilbert–Schmidt isometric inclusion ℝd ≃ A ∩ Lie(G) ↪ Mn(ℂ). Maximal compact product volume, dimension balance, and small-ball slab squeeze for the Iwasawa constant

Once the parabolic integral is known up to a scalar κ, calibrating the normalized one-place Iwasawa constant cIw = c κ requires comparing small-ball asymptotics on G and on N × M × K despite the positive-dimensional fiber L = K ∩ M of the multiplication map (u, m, k) ↦ u m k. Because L acts freely via (m, k) ↦ (mℓ, ℓ−1k), the parameter dimension dN + dM + dK exceeds dimℝ G = n² by exactly dL = dimℝ L, so an unrestricted small ball Br ⊂ G lifts to a non-localized tube along L. Intersecting K with a thin transverse slab Sρ(r) of radius r in the dL coordinates along Lie(L) and fixed small radius ρ overall cuts every L-fiber down to scale r: on one hand, second-order tangent expansion u m k − 1 = X + Y + Z + O(s²) linearizes the slab-localized integral Iρ(r) to a definite Euclidean gauge body of volume 2dN/2 ωn² ωdL rn²+dL; on the other hand, unfolding Iρ(r) over L replaces the slab indicator by the coset slice measure νL({ℓ ∈ L : ℓk ∈ Sρ(r)}), which — after centering the coset L k at a minimum-distance representative ℓ1k perpendicular to Lie(L) — is squeezed between two L-balls of radii r/(1 + η) and (1 + η)r. Dividing both sides by rn²+dL as r → 0+ cancels ωn² ωdL and isolates c κ = 2−dN/2 V(p)V(q) / vol(L).

1. Parabolic dimension balance and maximal compact volume. Retain signature (p, q) (n = p + q) and positive isotropic flag jumps j1, …, ja ≥ 1 with J = ∑i=1a ji ≤ min(p, q). Write dN = 4 ∑1≤i<k≤a jijk + 2J(n − 2J) + ∑i=1a ji² = 2J² − ∑i=1a ji² + 2J(n − 2J), dM = (n − 2J)² + 2 ∑i=1a ji², dK = p² + q², and dL = (p − J)² + (q − J)² + ∑i=1a ji² for the real dimensions of N, M, K ≃ U(p) × U(q), and L = K ∩ M. Expanding J² = ∑i=1a ji² + 2 ∑i<k jijk gives the exact dimension identities

n² = 2dN + dM,   p² + q² = dN + dL,   dN + dM + dK = n² + dL.

For each integer r ≥ 0, let V(r) = 2r(r−1)/2 S(r) (with V(0) = 1 and S(r) = ∏l=1r (2πl / (l − 1)!)) be the Hilbert–Schmidt volume of U(r) established in the compact unitary volume formula via the Riemannian Haar gauge. Countable slice covolume inequality, finiteness of the unitary quotient volume, and five-factor flag constant

Closing the global passage from the single-stabilizer slice bound to marked-flag unfolding and the short-flag probability estimate requires four structural links. First, covering the short-flag locus in G(𝔸F) = G∞ × G(𝔸F,fin) by countably many marked-stabilizer slices need not partition the quotient cleanly, yet choosing near-minimal measurable covers on each real slice and taking their union with finite-level cosets xcK bounds the global covolume subadditively by μ2(K) times the sum of slice covolumes. Second, dividing the unnormalized mass inequality H M(B) ≤ Aj V0 I(B) by H V = V0 RJ requires knowing unconditionally that the ambient quotient volume V = μ(𝒟) is strictly positive and finite whenever p ≥ 2, which follows by stepping (p, q) down to definite signature (2, 0) via the Hermitian rank-step ratio and invoking the rank-two unimodular class bound. 1. Countable product-slice covolume inequality and finiteness of the ambient volume.
