How one Bernoulli value bounds a module length

Fix a primitive character ψ of conductor f, an odd prime ℓ prime to its order, and a twist e ≥ 1 with ψ(−1) = (−1)e+1. Write O for the coefficient ring, a discrete valuation ring finite as a module over ℤℓ, v for its additive valuation with v(π) = 1 at a uniformizer π, and A for the cleared Bernoulli value at the conductor. The goal is lengthO ≤ v(A) for this character component. An integer can kill a whole module without controlling its length. For example, 3 kills (ℤ/3)d for every d. The proof needs local tests that measure how much length remains after each one. Let χ be the ℓ-adic cyclotomic character and ψG the Galois character attached to ψ. The scalar action on the cocycles in the count is α = (χeψG)−1. First consider the case where α(g) − χ(g) is a unit for some g. Choose a nonzero cutoff a₀ ∈ O. Work with cocycles c in the torsion coefficient module O ⊗ℤℓ (ℚℓ/ℤℓ) whose values are killed by a₀. Normalize them to have c(δ) = 0 for a fixed δ with α(δ) − 1 a unit. Subtracting the coboundary of c(δ)/(α(δ) − 1) achieves this. At primes outside the level they vanish on inertia. At primes in the level they are locally coboundaries with a witness killed by the cutoff. As a₀ runs through powers of π, the images of these spaces cover the locally trivial classes. A bound uniform in the cutoff will therefore bound the entire length. The evaluation theorem arranges a₀z ∣ ℓm and z ∣ A. Here is the valuation principle behind the second divisibility. The unit-difference hypothesis gives a residue b for which ψ(b)be+1 − 1 is an ℓ-adic unit. The congruence for the floor sum compares it, after explicit scaling, with the nonzero Bernoulli value divided by e + 1. The source takes m large enough that the approximation error is strictly smaller than the norm of the comparison term after scaling. In a nonarchimedean norm, |x−y| < |y| forces |x| = |y|. The resulting norm of z is |Be+1,ψ/(e+1)|. Multiplying by integral factors to get the cleared value A can only increase its valuation. What a Frobenius test measures

Choose a base prime r ≡ 1 modulo F. Let L be a list of distinct auxiliary primes q ≡ 1 modulo F, different from r, at which the multiplicative order of r is prime to ℓ. Call the space at this cutoff, before imposing the base-prime condition, Sbefore. Write SL for its cocycles vanishing on the entire decomposition groups over r and the primes in L. Even S∅ imposes vanishing over r. With each list comes a second function νL in the dual character χα−1. On the subgroup fixing the Fth roots of unity its values are additive and killed by ℓm. Their annihilator is a principal ideal, with generator aL. Put jL = v(aL) and J = v(ℓm). Thus jL ≤ J. The induction uses lists for which the cutoff a₀ divides aL. The initial list has this property, and each new test preserves it. If SL contains a nonzero c, the prime selection theorem chooses a new q with two properties at once. Evaluation on a Frobenius element φ at q detects c, meaning c(φ) ≠ 0. Every value of νL on the subgroup fixing the roots of unity is an O-multiple of νL(φ). For the longer list L′ there is then an inertia element τ at q for which νL′(τ) equals νL(φ) times a unit. The two values have identical annihilators. Since the entire next system contains this inertia value, aL divides aL′. Denote their quotient by d. Reciprocity controls the image of the linear evaluation map c ↦ c(φ). In the finite coefficient quotient the characters of the two cocycles multiply to χ. Their cup product is a global degree-two class. At the other primes in the finite ramification set the local coboundary condition makes its restriction trivial. The global sum of local invariants thus leaves zero at q. Local unramified orthogonality, with c zero on inertia there, makes the product of the inertia coefficient of the dual class and the value c(φ) vanish. This conclusion holds in the finite coefficient quotient. The proof first tests it with linear functionals which separate its elements. Concretely, choose a generator w of the cyclic coefficient subgroup killed by aL′ and write νL′(τ) = y w. Its annihilator is (aL). If the inertia value is nonzero, its order in a cyclic module over a discrete valuation ring gives v(y) = v(d). Thus the reciprocity relation y c(φ) = 0 implies d c(φ) = 0. If the inertia value is zero, its annihilator is the unit ideal and the cutoff dividing aL forces the cutoff space to vanish. The chosen nonzero evaluation therefore forces v(d) > 0. The image of evaluation, lying in cyclic d-torsion, has length at most v(d). An element in its kernel vanishes at the whole decomposition group over q. On the roots-of-unity subgroup it is additive, and the quotient of the decomposition group by inertia is topologically generated by Frobenius. Conjugation transports vanishing to the other places over q. Thus the kernel lies in SL′. In ℚ₃/ℤ₃ take w = 1/27 modulo ℤ₃. Its annihilator is (27), and the annihilator of 3w is (9). Their quotient is 3. Returning to the character length

Whenever SL is nonzero, the next integer jL′ is strictly larger. It cannot exceed J. Induction on J − jL ends with a zero space and proves length SL + jL ≤ J at every admissible state reached, even before finiteness of the starting length has been established. It remains to remove the condition at the base prime r. Take k = ℓm/z. The initial inertia value is a unit times z w for a coefficient element with annihilator (ℓm), so that value has annihilator (k). Write a∅ = k d₀. The same test at r gives length Sbefore ≤ length S∅ + v(d₀). Since J = v(k) + v(z) and j∅ = v(k) + v(d₀), cancellation leaves at most v(z) ≤ v(A). Every cutoff has now received the same final bound. In the complementary case α agrees with χ modulo the maximal ideal at every group element. Primitivity and the prime-to-ℓ order imply that α is unramified away from ℓ. At ℓ, an inertia element acting by −1 on ℓth roots of unity gives a unit α(δ) − 1 since ℓ is odd. For a normalized cocycle killed by π, the local coboundary witness over ℓ must be zero. Evaluating its defining equation at δ multiplies the witness by the unit α(δ) − 1 and gives zero. Away from ℓ the cocycle already vanishes on inertia. Reduction to scalar tests on the π-torsion

To see what these local conditions force globally, apply an additive functional from the π-torsion to the field of ℓ elements. The resulting scalar cocycle s has cyclotomic action. Kummer theory realizes it by a radical β with βℓ = a ∈ ℚ× and gβ/β = ζs(g) for a primitive ℓth root ζ. Vanishing on inertia makes every prime exponent of a divisible by ℓ. Unique factorization in ℚ and oddness of ℓ now give a = uℓ for a rational u, including its sign. For example, −8/125 has exponents 3 and −3 and cube root −2/5. Thus β = uζi, so s(g) = i(χ̄(g) − 1), where χ̄ is the action modulo ℓ. At δ the left side is zero and χ̄(δ) − 1 is nonzero. Hence i = 0. All the scalar tests vanish, and linear functionals separate the π-torsion points. Finally, if πk+1c = 0, apply this first step to πkc and descend on k. The original length here is zero.
