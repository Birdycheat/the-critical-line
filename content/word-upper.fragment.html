An upper bound for the scale of every word

Relative variation alone can leave all the words equally large. To bound their absolute scale, start again from the mass identity used for the lower bound. It gives fk log Xu as a sum of six terms. For an upper bound, a term of known negative sign can be discarded. The remaining positive contributions must be estimated even when the word and the family member vary. Assume 44 ≤ m ≤ k, r = k + m − 1, L ≥ 9 and log(2k) ≤ L. Write CY for the bound log gi ≤ CYL on each of the two auxiliary primes. The following budget is divided throughout by fkmL, so its sum bounds log Xu/(mL). The selected family and the range of its variables · Six terms before taking the upper estimate

  • The coefficient of log(v/π) is f[(m − 1)k + m(m − 1)/2]. Since 0 ≤ log(v/π) ≤ L and m ≤ k, the normalized contribution is at most 3/2. The term in −log(2v) is nonpositive.
  • At most m Euler values enter. Each logarithm is at most f by the comparison with ζ(M) at integers M ≥ 2. Together they cost at most 1/(kL). The finite corrections log(1 − gi−j) are nonpositive.
  • The positive prime term has coefficient fm(2k + m − 1)/2. Since log(g0g1) ≤ 2CYL, it costs at most 3CY.
  • At each infinite place, separate the logarithm of the ratio of representation dimensions from the factorial products in Weyl’s formula. The gap estimate for the real-place degree and the column estimate bound the first by km(log 3 + 5 log(2k) + 5L + 10). For the factorials there are r − k additional factors j!. Each is at most rr. Since r ≤ 2k, their logarithms total at most 2kmL. Summing over f places costs at most 12/L + 12 after normalization.

These four bounds imply

log Xu/(mL) ≤ 3/2 + 1/(kL) + 3CY + 12/L + 12 ≤ 15 + 3CY.

For the last inequality even the looser k ≥ 5 suffices. The constants independent of CY sum to at most 3/2 + 1/45 + 4/3 + 12 = 1337/90, strictly below 15. Take the maximum of the thresholds for this estimate, the lower estimate, the real-place gap and the mass inputs. Thus the same threshold works for all words. To pass to the chosen prime Tu, its window has center XuΓ−2/(fk) and upper multiplicative allowance 1 + 1/(fk). With Γ ≥ 1 this gives log Tu ≤ log Xu + 1. The construction absorbs the additive allowance into its common counting constant. The existence of a prime in the window is a separate arithmetic input.
