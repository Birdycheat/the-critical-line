Why is the map from the tower to the generic fibre finite étale?

Work over the valuation ring O at the chosen place, with fraction field E. Keep the integral models Y → M and their universal abelian schemes. Write 𝒵 = AYk and 𝒫 = AMk for the two fibre powers. A subscript E denotes the generic fibre. At the admissible principal level N = cN₀, choose the generator a and the quotient with its two level markings as in the invariance lemma. This gives the comparison πN from the tower space ZN over E to 𝒵E.

Finite étale comparison. The map πN is finite étale for every such quotient and every k ≥ 0. Let K₀ be the level subgroup defined by the integral model’s level ideal. If two geometric points z, z′ of ZN have the same image, then some u ∈ K₀ carries z to z′ by the tower transition Tu. The word “separation” here describes what the level marking can distinguish in a geometric fibre.

The comparison at the reference level. At N₀ the generic moduli comparison identifies the tower base with an open and closed subscheme of ME, and its universal abelian scheme with the pullback of the integral one. Taking k factors preserves this pullback. It gives an immersion ρ ∶ ZN₀ → 𝒫E onto an open and closed subscheme. This is the sense in which the reference-level comparison is trivial. Each point of its image has a single lift and points on the complementary components have none.

Let t ∶ ZN → ZN₀ forget the refinement of principal level, and let v ∶ 𝒵E → 𝒫E forget the auxiliary marking. The maps satisfy

v ∘ πN = ρ ∘ t.

To see the equality, first compare the induced maps on the moduli bases. Forgetting the auxiliary marking of the chosen quotient and first descending to N₀ give the same quotient object. The identity transition identifies the abelian schemes and sends the sections labelled cy at level N to those labelled y at level N₀. The representing property of M thus makes the two classifying maps equal. Over this common map rigidity of the quotient gives equality of the maps of universal abelian schemes. Taking the same k-fold product gives the displayed equality on the tower.

The map t is a finite étale change of principal level. The immersion ρ is finite because it is closed and étale because it is open. The map v is finite étale because AY is the pullback of AM along the finite étale map Y → M. The same pullback description holds for the fibre powers and after base change to E. Thus both v and v ∘ πN are finite étale. For finiteness, the graph of πN over 𝒫E is a closed immersion because v is separated. The projection from its ambient fibre product to 𝒵E is finite by base change. For étaleness the same graph is an open immersion because v is unramified, and the projection is étale. Their composite is πN. Stacks Project, finiteness of a morphism over a separated target and the graph argument for étaleness.

Separating the points in a fibre. Suppose πN(z) = πN(z′) over an algebraically closed field. Applying v and using the injectivity of ρ gives t(z) = t(z′). The subgroup K(N) is normal in K(N₀). Indeed, if an element acts as the identity modulo N on the lattice, conjugating by a lattice automorphism preserves this property. By transitivity in a principal-level fibre, we obtain u ∈ K(N₀) with z′ = Tu(z). We still have to show that u preserves the auxiliary marking.

Project z to the moduli base XN by forgetting the k fibre coordinates. Projecting z′ gives the translate of the same base point. Equality of the πN-images forces the two corresponding marked quotients to have the same base point in Y. Pull their universal objects back to this geometric point. By rigidity their quotient maps agree there. In particular the level sections indexed by ay agree for every lattice vector y. The pointwise description of the level structure says exactly that u fixes those marked vectors modulo N in the completed lattice. The congruence criterion for the auxiliary ideal identifies this condition, together with u ∈ K(N₀), with u ∈ K₀. These are the two markings compared in the quotient calculation.

How do level transformations split the pullback of the cover?

Let π = πN ∶ ZN → 𝒵E be the finite étale comparison. Assume also that the chosen level satisfies K(N) ⊆ K₀. The latter group lies in K(N₀), since its level ideal is N₀ times an integral ideal. It preserves the lattice and hence normalizes K(N). The quotient Δ = K₀/K(N) is finite, since its order divides the finite index [K(N₀) ∶ K(N)].

Splitting theorem. There is a right action of Δ on ZN over 𝒵E. A pair of points in the fibre product consists of two lifts of the same point. The second lift and the unique transformation which carries it to the first specify that pair. On schemes this gives the isomorphism

∐δ∈Δ ZN ≅ ZN ×𝒵E ZN,   (δ, z) ↦ (z · δ, z).

The action and its fibres. On a fixed principal level the transitions obey Tv ∘ Tu = Tvu, and Tu is the identity when u ∈ K(N). For δ represented by u set z · δ = Tu−1(z). Replacing u by another representative gives the same transformation by the identity at principal level. The inverse in the definition gives (z · δ) · ε = z · (δε). The invariance of the quotient gives π(z · δ) = π(z) as an equality of maps. The preceding separation argument gives a transformation between any two geometric lifts. On a given geometric point two transformations can agree only if the quotient of their representatives fixes that point. Freeness of the principal-level action puts this quotient in K(N), so their classes in Δ agree.

From points to schemes. The second projection of the fibre product is étale by base change from π. The map from each copy of ZN in the display is a section of that projection. Its graph is an open immersion, and the map from the disjoint union is therefore étale. We just proved that it is bijective on points valued in every algebraically closed field. For an étale map, uniqueness makes the diagonal surjective on underlying points. Its diagonal is also an open immersion, hence an isomorphism. The map is therefore an étale monomorphism, an open immersion. Existence of a preimage for each geometric point makes this open immersion surjective, hence an isomorphism. Stacks Project, Lemma 35.25.2, on universally injective étale maps.

Every nonempty geometric fibre of π now has exactly |Δ| points. Outside its open and closed image the fibre is empty, and both sides of the graph isomorphism are empty there as well. The restriction to the image is the step that produces a surjective cover. The base model 𝒵 is smooth and projective over O. The self-product in the display and its splitting are over its generic fibre.

For example, take L = ℚ(√2) and let Spec L map to the first of two copies of Spec ℚ. Its two transformations over ℚ are the identity and conjugation. Over the first component, L ⊗ℚ L ≅ L[T]/(T² − 2), where T comes from the first factor. Evaluation at √2 and −√2 gives L × L, since their difference is invertible in L. Thus the self-product consists of the two graphs even though the original generic cover is the spectrum of a field. Over the second component the fibre is empty.

Which Hecke operators realize the transformations? Assume the list of operators contains, for every u ∈ K₀, an entry at level N whose subgroup is exactly K(N), whose translating element is u, and whose prescribed scalar is 1. Choose such an entry for a representative of δ−1. Its correspondence has one coset, so its source map sj is an isomorphism. Changing that coset’s representative multiplies the translating element on the right by an element of K(N), which acts trivially. With aδ(z) = z · δ, the two legs consequently satisfy

tj = aδ ∘ sj,   θj = 1.

This equality identifies the target as a morphism on the whole correspondence. Every simultaneous eigenclass with the prescribed values is therefore fixed by the action of these cover transformations.
