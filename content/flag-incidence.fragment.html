How a finite incidence count bounds an angle

Let π be the prime element used in the Hecke average, 𝒪 the ring of integers of the field, and k = 𝒪/(π), of order q ≥ 25. Take N ≥ 3. In SLN(L′) consider three conjugates of SLN(𝒪). The group Kᵣ is Dᵣ−1SLN(𝒪)Dᵣ, where the diagonal matrix Dᵣ has π in its first r positions and 1 elsewhere, for r = 0,1,2. Put Eᵢⱼ = Kᵢ ∩ Kⱼ and B = K₀ ∩ K₁ ∩ K₂. At each vertex Kᵣ, the two incident subgroups stabilize a pair of nested subspaces after reduction modulo π. Such a pair is a flag. The three groups · Reading subspace conditions from scaled entries

Use coordinates 0,…,N − 1 after conjugating by Dᵣ. The following pairs are in the order used by the angle bound. The first subgroup in each pair is called E and the second E′.

  • In K₀, E₀₁ fixes the line spanned by e₀ and E₀₂ fixes the plane spanned by e₀,e₁.
  • In K₁, E₁₂ fixes the line spanned by e₁ and E₀₁ fixes the hyperplane x₀ = 0.
  • In K₂, E₁₂ fixes the hyperplane x₁ = 0 and E₀₂ fixes its subspace x₀ = x₁ = 0.

Here “fixes” means preserves the subspace as a set. The bipartite graph has a vertex for each subspace of the first or second type, and one edge for each incident pair. Equivalently, take the translates of the two coordinate subspaces under Kᵣ. The stabilizer of their common edge is B. In the first two cases a matrix outside E moves the distinguished line to a different line. Row additions preserving the first line can take that new line to a fixed second coordinate line. Thus all such matrices form one double coset of E. Transposing gives the same result for the distinguished hyperplane in the third case. Count the flag choices in a finite group

At any one vertex, the proof passes to the finite group G = Kᵣ/core(B), where core(B) is the intersection of all Kᵣ-conjugates of B. Use the same letters for subgroup images. A real function f constant on the cosets Ek has a value at each vertex of the first type. Suppose its mean is zero. Use the uniform measure on G and form its correlation c(s) = meank f(sk)f(k). It equals ‖f‖² on E. Elsewhere it is constant because it depends only on the double coset. Its mean over s is zero, so its value outside E is nonpositive. Average f over left multiplication by E′, writing P′f for the result. Expanding its squared norm and reindexing gives the mean of c(s) over s ∈ E′. Only the elements of E′ ∩ E = B can give a positive contribution. Their proportion is 1/[E′:B]. For a function g constant on the cosets E′k, averaging preserves the pairing with g. Cauchy–Schwarz gives

|⟨f,g⟩| = |⟨P′f,g⟩| ≤ ‖f‖‖g‖ / √[E′:B].

At vertex 0, the matrices I + t e₁₀ in E₀₂ move the line to span(e₀ + t e₁) inside the same plane. Distinct t modulo π give distinct flags, so [E₀₂:B] ≥ q. Likewise I + t e₂₁ in E₀₁ sends e₁ to e₁ + t e₂ while fixing e₀. This supplies [E₀₁:B] ≥ q, the other needed index. Thus every pairing has coefficient at most 1/√q ≤ 1/5. On the arithmetic quotient the first vector is orthogonal to all Kᵣ-invariant functions. Subtracting its local Kᵣ-average gives a representative with zero mean on each finite fiber. Integrating the finite estimate gives the three angles used in the Hecke norm calculation. A calculation at the threshold. In size N = 3 over 𝔽₂₅, the graph at vertex 0 joins points to lines in the projective plane. There are (25³ − 1)/(25 − 1) = 651 points and as many lines. Each line contains 26 points. Give one point value +1, a second point value −1, and all other points zero. Averaging on each line gives +1/26 on the 25 lines through just the first point, −1/26 on the 25 through just the second, and zero elsewhere. The common line cancels. The ratio of output norm to input norm is √(50/26²)/√2 = 5/26. In fact this is the norm on the entire mean-zero subspace. If A is the line-by-point incidence matrix, ATA has diagonal entries 26 and off-diagonal entries 1, since two points determine one line. It acts by 25 on vectors with zero sum. The averaging matrix is A/26. Here the general bound 1/√26 and its uniform relaxation 1/5 both exceed the exact value 5/26.
