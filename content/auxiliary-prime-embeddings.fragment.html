How can the same copy of the number field be used at infinity and at p?

Let E be the CM field of the arithmetic datum, with its chosen embedding τ₀ ∶ E → ℂ. We shall construct a second embedding τ₁ ∶ E → Ωp, where Ωp is an algebraic closure of ℚp. The geometric datum records both maps as realizations of one marked copy of E.

Fix an algebraic closure Ω of ℚ. Choose an embedding s ∶ E → Ω. The map s(x) ↦ τ₀(x) on s(E) extends to an embedding ιℂ ∶ Ω → ℂ, since Ω is algebraic over s(E) and ℂ is algebraically closed. Now extend ℚ → ℚp → Ωp to any embedding ιp ∶ Ω → Ωp. The extension theorem used here adjoins a root of the transported minimal polynomial at each simple algebraic extension and takes a maximal compatible extension. Extending a field embedding to an algebraic extension.

ιℂ ∘ s = τ₀,   τ₁ = ιp ∘ s.

The first equality prescribes the complex value of every element of E. A choice of ιp fixes all the p-adic values at once. In particular it fixes the image of the quadratic generator ω before we select a root in ℤp.

Which p-adic root agrees with the chosen embedding?

Let P(X) = X² − TX + U be the monic quadratic of ω. Its discriminant T² − 4U is negative. Thus P has no real root and is irreducible over ℚ. In particular K = ℚ(ω) is imaginary quadratic and each element has a unique expression a + bω with a, b ∈ ℚ. Assume p avoids the discriminant and P has a root modulo p. The quadratic place criterion identifies the two residual roots.

Hensel’s lemma lifts one simple root to r ∈ ℤp. The other root is T − r. Let j ∶ ℚp → Ωp denote inclusion and put z = τ₁(ω). Since τ₁ fixes ℚ, P(z) = 0. Factoring gives

(z − j(r))(z − j(T − r)) = 0.

There is therefore exactly one ρ ∈ {r, T − r} with j(ρ) = τ₁(ω). The factors are distinct since the discriminant is a unit at p. The auxiliary choice of the first lift r can be made in either order. Hensel lifting of a simple root.

u(a + bω) = a + bρ,   j ∘ u = τ₁|K.

This gives the required embedding u ∶ K → ℚp. Indeed composition with the injective j preserves sums, products and 1 because it equals τ₁ on K. The same identity proves uniqueness for this τ₁. The argument uses only the quadratic splitting condition at p.

How can the orbit representatives retain the chosen embedding?

Fix u as above. Let S be the embeddings τ ∶ E → Ωp satisfying τ|K = j ∘ u. The group Gp = Gal(Ωp/ℚp) acts by τ ↦ σ ∘ τ. Each embedding of the finite extension E/K is determined by the images of finitely many generators, each with finitely many possible roots. Thus S and its set of orbits are finite. The distinguished τ₁ belongs to S. Counting embeddings of a finite field extension.

In the orbit of τ₁ choose τ₁ itself. Choose one representative in every other orbit. Enumerate the orbits by 0, …, m − 1 and let w be the index of the orbit containing τ₁. Writing ei for the representative at index i gives

ew = τ₁ = ιp ∘ s,   S = ⨆0 ≤ i < m Gp · ei.

Every τ is σ ∘ ei for a unique index i. The choices of σ differ by the stabilizer of ei. Equality at w identifies the two maps on every element of E. This is why the later comparison carries w along with s and ρ.

For E = ℚ(i, √2) at 5, fixing the image of i leaves two embeddings. They lie in one orbit, so the family still has only one representative.

Why do the two embeddings above the same root at 5 form one orbit?

Take E = ℚ(i, √2), K = ℚ(i), and p = 5. Suppose τ₁(i) is the root ρ ≡ 2 mod 5 of X² + 1. Above this root there are two embeddings of E, sending √2 to the two roots ±α of X² − 2. The nonzero squares modulo 5 are 1 and 4. A root in ℚ₅ would be a unit and give a square root of 2 modulo 5. Thus ℚ₅(α)/ℚ₅ is quadratic, and its automorphism α ↦ −α extends to Ω₅. It interchanges the two embeddings while fixing ρ. They form one orbit, so m = 1 and w = 0. The other image of i gives another orbit of two if one counts all four embeddings before fixing u.
