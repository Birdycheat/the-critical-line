How does marking a flag change the volume being counted?

Fix the positive rank jumps j₁,…,jₐ and let B be a measurable region of admissible ordered sizes. A point whose canonical flag has those ranks and sizes supplies one saturated isotropic flag. If we count all such flags over the point, the indicator of the event is bounded above by the number of markings. For instance, if three points have probabilities 1/4, 1/2 and 1/4 and carry respectively zero, one and two eligible markings, the points with a marking have probability 3/4. Counting all markings gives 1/2 + 2(1/4) = 1. This inequality permits the geometric proof to use arithmetic stabilizers.

Choose a basis adapted to a marked flag. Its data include one full lattice class for each successive quotient, of rank jᵢ, and a finite-adelic double class in the fixed residual Hermitian model supplied by the volume-ratio theorem, of signature (p−J, q−J), where J = ∑ jᵢ. On that space two finite representatives are equivalent when a rational isometry on the left and an element of the compact lattice stabilizer on the right identify them. The construction of an integral frame and the comparison of two markings put any marking with the same data into the chosen representative by these changes of basis. The archimedean flag sizes are preserved under the corresponding simultaneous change of frame. For each realized tuple of data it therefore suffices to integrate on the quotient by the subgroup preserving its marked flag. These slices cover the original event. Counting an overlap twice gives an upper bound.

The sum is countable. Each block lattice has finitely many generators in a countable number field, and the open compact subgroup at the finite places has at most countably many cosets in a second-countable group. The residual double quotient is a further quotient of that countable set.

Why does the probability lose the residual volume?

For one stabilizer the real group is integrated in unipotent, block diagonal and compact coordinates. For the fixed marked flag the sizes at umk equal those at m, with the unipotent element u on the left and the compact element k on the right. The block diagonal coordinate gives the size variable τ and its density, whose integral on B we denote by I(B). The mass of a single marked slice is at most the product of this integral, a common factor from the unipotent covolume and the real Haar measures, one lattice weight for each block, and a stabilizer covolume on the residual Hermitian space. All these quantities are nonnegative.

We can now sum over every tuple of block classes and residual class, including tuples for which no marked flag was found. The sum of products separates. The sum over coefficient-lattice classes evaluates the weight of each block. At rank jᵢ this gives 2f jᵢ² times the normalized general linear volume of that rank. The sum of residual stabilizer covolumes with their real gauge factors is bounded by the normalized residual volume V₀. Write Aj for the common factor multiplied by all the block bounds. If M(B) is the unnormalized mass in the original fundamental domain, and H is the product of the archimedean Haar gauge factors divided by the measure of the finite compact stabilizer, then

H M(B) ≤ Aj V₀ I(B),   H V = V₀ RJ.

The second equality is the Hermitian volume-ratio theorem, with V the full fundamental-domain volume and RJ the product of its rank factors from n−2J+1 through n. Both volumes and the gauge are positive and finite. Dividing gives Pr(event) = M(B)/V ≤ (Aj/RJ) I(B). The quotient of these constants is precisely κj. This is the use of the smaller Hermitian space in the probability estimate. To resume the dimension argument from here, sum over the possible rank lists to bound the volume lost in the trace. To check the arithmetic constant, follow the general linear class masses in its numerator and the Hermitian rank factors in its denominator.
