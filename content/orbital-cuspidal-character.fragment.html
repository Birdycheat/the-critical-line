Why does the initial Hecke test have zero constant term on every slice?

At the split place w₀ write the away-from-p group as Γ = GLn(k) × H, where k is a nonarchimedean local field of characteristic zero. Write pr for the projection onto the matrix group. The local type consists of a compact open subgroup J and a character χ of J with open kernel. Every smooth irreducible complex representation containing a nonzero vector of character χ is cuspidal. The construction of the local type gives this property. Choose the given compact open levels K₀ ⊆ K of finite index with pr(K) ⊆ J, pr(K₀) ⊆ ker χ and (J,1) ⊆ K. The fine level K₀ is a product in this splitting. With convolution counted over right cosets qK₀, the initial test is

d = [K : K₀],   e(x) = d−1χ(pr x)−1   for x ∈ K,   e(x) = 0 otherwise.

To check the normalization, take x ∈ K. Each of the d cosets in K/K₀ contributes d−2χ(pr x)−1 to (e ∗ e)(x). Outside K all products vanish. Thus e ∗ e = e. The inverse character also gives the correct action on a χ-vector, since its χ-factor cancels the weight of each summand. For h ∈ H, the slice g ↦ e(g,h) is e(1,h) times the local function b equal to χ−1 on J and zero off J. Indeed, for g ∈ J, multiplication by (g,1) preserves membership in K. For g outside J the support condition gives zero.

Cancellation by one translation. Take any proper two-block unipotent radical with coordinates u(X) and any matrix t conjugating it. There exists a coordinate Y with j = t−1u(Y)t ∈ J and χ(j) ≠ 1. For the explicit type, the rank-one construction produces an element whose image generates the character quotient. The argument for an arbitrary cuspidal type is given below. Fix a matrix g and put F(X) = b(gt−1u(X)t). Addition of rectangular matrices is the group law in the radical. Since b(zj) = χ(j)−1b(z), translation invariance of any additive Haar measure μ gives

F(X + Y) = χ(j)−1F(X),   (1 − χ(j)−1) ∫ F(X) dμ(X) = 0.

The scalar in parentheses is nonzero. Multiplying this vanishing integral by e(1,h) gives the required zero on each slice. For n = 1 there is no proper two-block radical.

Why a cuspidal type detects every conjugated radical

Suppose χ were trivial on J ∩ U for one such radical U. The integral of b over U would be the positive finite volume of this compact open intersection. In the span of all left translates of b, quotient by the functions whose integrals over every left translate of U vanish. The class of b is a nonzero cyclic generator. A maximal proper submodule exists because no union of a chain of proper submodules contains the generator. The resulting smooth irreducible quotient contains its nonzero image v, on which J acts by χ under left translation. Hence the type hypothesis makes this quotient cuspidal.

Write π for this representation. As a smooth irreducible complex representation of GLn(k), it is admissible by the admissibility theorem of Bernstein and Zelevinsky, Theorem 3.25. Any locally constant compactly supported function a with zero integral on every set xUy annihilates v under integration against π. Indeed, a ∗ b is a finite sum of right translates of a after choosing a common compact open subgroup of invariance. All its integrals over left translates of U vanish, so its image in the quotient is zero. To contradict this, choose a smooth linear functional λ with λ(v) ≠ 0 by averaging a functional over a compact open subgroup fixing v. Take c(g) = λ(π(g)v) on the set where det(g) has valuation zero, and zero elsewhere. Cuspidal matrix coefficients have compact support modulo the centre. The determinant restriction bounds the scalar valuation, so c has compact support. Its integrals on xUy vanish because the Jacquet module for U is zero. A sufficiently large compact subgroup of U averages π(y)v to zero. The determinant restriction is constant on xUy. Applying the annihilation just proved to the complex conjugate of c and then applying λ gives the integral of |c|² equal to zero. But c(1) = λ(v) ≠ 0 and c is locally constant. This contradiction proves the required nontriviality. Casselman, Cuspidal representations, Theorem 1.1.

What survives convolution. Let f be a Hecke function at the compact open product level K₀ whose slices all have zero constant term. For any Hecke function α at that same level, its support meets finitely many right cosets. If q−1 = (q₁′,q₂′) in the product, then

(α ∗ f)(g,h) = ∑qK₀ α(q) f(q₁′g, q₂′h).

Each summand takes a left translate of a slice at another fixed point of H. Its constant term vanishes by the hypothesis for all slices and all left translates. The restrictions to the closed radical translates are locally constant with compact support, so their integrals commute with finite sums and scalar multiplication. Thus left convolution and finite linear combination preserve the condition. This proves it for every polynomial in the Hecke operators applied to an iterated left convolution of e. The second-place test supplies regular support for the definite orbital sum.

A three-element check. On the additive quotient ℤ/3ℤ take χ(1) = ζ with ζ a primitive cube root of unity. With counting measure, e takes the values (1, ζ², ζ)/3. Translating its argument by 1 multiplies every value by ζ². The sum vanishes since 1 + ζ + ζ² = 0, while the three terms in (e ∗ e)(1) each equal ζ²/9. This gives back e(1) = ζ²/3.
