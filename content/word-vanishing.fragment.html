Why can a pair of words have a zero orbital term?

Fix two distinct words u and v in the selected family. Their local unitary representations have different weight parameters at every infinite place. Examine a single nonidentity term with Gram label zero in Buv. It compares a flag with a translate of the other flag. Call the pair relevant when the differentials of their two characters agree on every common infinitesimal unipotent direction. There are two ways for its orbital integral P to vanish. If relevance fails, one direction gives unequal phases. In fact the proof rescales that direction so that, with N infinite places, the difference in the two character exponents is πi/N at each place. The product of the first character with the conjugate of the second therefore takes the value exp(πi) = −1. This character is trivial on the rational lattice. Averaging over its compact quotient in the common unipotent group gives zero, since translating the character average by that element changes its sign. For a relevant pair with Gram label zero, the geometric normal form gives a positive orthonormal frame perpendicular to both flag subspaces at each infinite place. Write its columns as W and the ambient Hermitian form as J, so W∗JW = I. Every M ∈ U(k) extends to a symmetry fixing those subspaces pointwise. Indeed, put

H = I + W(M − I)W∗J.   HW = WM,   Hy = y whenever W∗Jy = 0.

On the frame this map preserves the inner product because M is unitary, and on its orthogonal complement it is the identity. The other flag sees the action as CMC∗ for a fixed unitary matrix C at that place. Invariance of the orbital integral under the pointwise stabilizer gives covariance between these two actions. This uses the existence of the fundamental domain and integrability of the orbital kernel. Choose one infinite place and let M vary only there. Fix arbitrary vectors in every other tensor factor on each side. Let Su and Sv insert a local vector into those fixed tensors. For A = Su∗PSv, covariance reads

πu(M)A = Aπv(CMC∗),   πu(M)R = Rπv(M),   R = Aπv(C).

A nonzero R would be an isomorphism. Its kernel and image are invariant, and the two finite-dimensional representations are irreducible. An isomorphism preserves the weights of the diagonal torus, including the distinguished extremal weight that determines each type. The unequal parameters rule this out. Hence R = 0 and A = 0. Varying the fixed tensors shows that every matrix coefficient of P between pure tensors vanishes. They span both spaces. Can a scalar unitary distinguish these two types of U(2)?

On the determinant line and on Sym²(ℂ²), the scalar zI acts by z². Suppose an intertwiner sends a generator of the determinant line to a quadratic tensor w. Every diag(z,z−1) with |z| = 1 fixes w. In the basis e₁², e₁e₂, e₂², the three eigenvalues are z², 1 and z−2. Thus w is a multiple of e₁e₂. Swapping the basis vectors fixes that monomial and multiplies the determinant by −1. Equivariance gives w = −w, so w = 0.

Both cases kill each selected term individually, so their sum Buv is zero. The other terms still contribute to Kuv. The separate different-word estimate bounds the squared norm of Kuv − Buv, and substituting zero gives the bound for the full block.
