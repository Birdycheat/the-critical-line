How do we round a map on the middle quotient?

Keep the isotropic lattice R from the gap in the flag. Let W be its rational span, let Λ be the self-dual Hermitian lattice at x, and put P = Λ ∩ W⊥. Write j = dim W and n = dim V. A nilpotent infinitesimal displacement T kills W and takes W⊥ into W. Restriction to W⊥ therefore gives a map W⊥/W → W. Adding a central displacement leaves that map unchanged. The first rounding takes place in the lattice of integral maps P/R → R.

Covering estimate for maps between lattices. If R has largest normalized size at most h and every lattice M with R ⊊ M ⊆ P has quotient M/R of normalized size at least l > 0, then the restrictions of any family T as above (using the archimedean span of P) admit one rational approximation Φ. It takes P into R and kills R. At each real place choose an extension Dᵥ of the difference (Tᵥ−Φv)|Wᵥ⊥ to the whole space. The extensions can be chosen so that

‖D‖x ≤ j(n−2j) √f d h/(2l).

This is the matrix covering bound for a quotient and a sublattice. Its dimension factor is the product of the two ranks. Nondegeneracy gives dim W⊥ = n−j, so the source has rank n−2j and the target rank j. The theorem realizes both lattices isometrically in their spans, applies a covering estimate for the pair, and carries the approximating map back by the quotient and inclusion. There is one complex place of the CM field above each real place of its totally real subfield. Thus the summed norm uses f places and the discriminant factor is the CM root discriminant d. The covering estimate for the pair of Hermitian lattices is the geometry-of-numbers input here.

Lifting the integral map. We need a skew-Hermitian matrix on all of Λ that still agrees with Φ on P. Since P is the intersection of Λ with a rational subspace, Λ/P is torsion-free. It is finitely generated over the ring of integers, a Dedekind domain, and hence projective. The quotient map therefore splits. Subtracting its splitting composed with the quotient map from the identity gives a projection π from Λ onto P which fixes P. Extend π to the rational vector space and set Ψ = Φπ. Then Ψ(Λ) ⊆ R, Ψ agrees with Φ on P, and Ψ kills W. Torsion-free finite modules over a Dedekind domain · Finite locally free and projective modules.

Write Ψ† for the adjoint relative to the original Hermitian form. Self-duality of Λ makes Ψ† integral. Indeed ⟨z,Ψ†y⟩ = ⟨Ψz,y⟩ is integral for all y,z ∈ Λ. Moreover Ψ† kills W⊥, since Ψ has image in W. Its image lies in W⊥, since Ψ kills W. It follows that Y₀ = Ψ − Ψ† is skew-Hermitian and lowers every step of 0 ⊂ W ⊂ W⊥ ⊂ V. On P it is exactly Φ. This construction preserves integrality even at primes above two.

Why does the lift cost only √2 in norm?

At one real place, use the frame at x. In its ordinary positive definite inner product the original form is represented by a Hermitian involution J, with J² = 1. Isotropy makes W and JW orthogonal for the positive definite product. Let H be their joint orthogonal complement. Then JH = H and the original form has W⊥ = W ⊕ H. Restrict D to W ⊕ H and extend by zero on JW. The resulting matrix B has just one block, H → W, because D kills W and maps W⊥ into W. Restriction by an orthogonal projection gives ‖B‖HS ≤ ‖D‖HS.

Let B* be the adjoint for the positive definite product. The matrix A = B − J B*J is skew-Hermitian for the original form and agrees with D on W⊥. Its second block goes from JW to H. These two blocks occupy disjoint entries in an adapted orthonormal basis, and conjugation by the unitary J preserves their Hilbert–Schmidt norms. Therefore ‖A‖HS² = 2‖B‖HS². Sum over the real places and take square roots to get ‖A‖x ≤ √2 ‖D‖x. Now T−Y₀−A is a skew-Hermitian nilpotent matrix that vanishes on W⊥. Its image lies in (W⊥)⊥ = W, so it is a real central matrix C. This gives exactly the approximation T = Y₀ + C + A required by the first round.

‖A‖x ≤ r₁ = n²√f d u/(8√2),   2g r₁ = n²√f g d u/(4√2).

Indeed j(n−2j) ≤ n²/8 because the difference is (n−4j)²/8. Substitute this and h/l ≤ u in the quotient bound and multiply by √2. The orbit calculation takes T = Y/(2g), so its displacement after restoring the original scale is 2gA.

Check the two blocks in dimension four

Let J exchange the first and fourth standard basis vectors and fix the middle two. Take W spanned by the first vector and choose B with its sole unit entry in row 1, column 2. The matrix J B*J then has its sole unit entry in row 2, column 4. Subtracting gives squared Hilbert–Schmidt norm 2 and leaves the prescribed map from the second vector to the first. Here j = 1 and n = 4, so the rank factor 1·(4−2) = 2 attains the bound n²/8.

Lean correspondence

The first-layer proof uses separate integral and archimedean lifts of the same quotient map. The integral lift only needs to preserve the lattice. The norm estimate is applied to the chosen archimedean residual, and their difference on the whole space is central.
