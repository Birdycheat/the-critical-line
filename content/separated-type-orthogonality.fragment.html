Can the original highest weight appear again in a later derivative layer?

The comparison of compact types uses a fact about one representation. Let V be a nonzero irreducible smooth compact type of H = U(r,s), with highest weight L = μ+ρn−ρc and full Casimir scalar ‖μ‖²−‖ρ‖². Assume μ is strictly decreasing, the opposite-sign gaps are at least h, and h > nrs+1 with n = r+s. The claim is that every compact highest vector of weight L among the derivative words on V already belongs to V.

Set F₀ = V. Build Fk+1 by adding to Fk all vectors Eabu, with u ∈ Fk and a,b of opposite signs. Each layer is finite-dimensional. Commuting a same-sign matrix unit past an opposite-sign unit introduces only opposite-sign units, so induction shows that all layers are stable under compact derivatives. Every derivative word belongs to some layer. Let Pk be the orthogonal projection onto Fk. The adjoint of a compact matrix unit is again a compact matrix unit, so the perpendicular of each layer is compact-stable as well. The projections commute with the compact derivatives and preserve the highest-weight equations. P. Etingof, compact orthogonal complements, §2.1.

Write cK(γ) = ‖γ+ρc‖²−‖ρc‖² for the compact Casimir of highest weight γ. The quantitative estimate needed here concerns a nonzero compact highest vector w in Fk orthogonal to Fk−1, with k ≥ 1 and highest weight γ. It gives

cK(γ) − cK(L) ≥ 2k(h−n+1).

To obtain the factor k, represent w modulo the previous layer by an array A of coefficients in V, indexed by words of exactly k noncompact matrix units. Applying the words and projecting off Fk−1 maps these arrays onto the new orthogonal layer. Choose the preimage perpendicular to the kernel. The map respects the compact action, so its restriction to the kernel’s perpendicular is an isomorphism of compact modules. Hence A is nonzero and has highest weight γ.

Here the one-position estimate supplies the representation-theoretic input. In this perpendicular space, the mixed term between the compact action on V and the action on any one word position has expectation at least (h−n+1)‖A‖². More explicitly, hold all the other positions fixed. In this one-position space the mixed operator splits into negative and positive eigenspaces, with positive eigenvalues ≥ h−n+1. A second input says that an array with only negative components at that position evaluates into the previous layer. Test the chosen A against its own negative component, supported on just the fixed slice. This test lies in the kernel just removed. Their inner product is the squared norm of that component, hence is zero. Only positive slices remain. Summing their squared norms over all choices of the other positions gives ‖A‖². Expand the compact Casimir for the diagonal action on the array. Its part on V contributes cK(L)‖A‖². Its part on the word indices is nonnegative, being a sum of squared norms. Twice the mixed expectation occurs at each of the k positions. The total expectation is cK(γ)‖A‖² because A is highest. Dividing by ‖A‖² proves the estimate.

Now let u have weight L and satisfy the compact highest-weight equations in some Fm. Decompose it as P₀u plus the successive differences (Pk−Pk−1)u. A nonzero difference would again be highest of weight L. Its compact Casimir would have to exceed the same scalar by 2k(h−n+1). This is positive when both signature indices are nonzero, since then rs ≥ 1. If one index is zero there are no noncompact derivatives at all. In either case u = P₀u ∈ V.

A rank-two calculation. Continue the U(1,1) example with L = (21,−20) and gap 40. Take an orthonormal sequence e₀, e₁, … of weights (21+j,−20−j). Set E₁₂ej = √((j+1)(41+j)) ej+1 and E₂₁ = −E₁₂*. Their commutator on ej is (41+2j)ej, as required by the two diagonal weights. Starting with e₀, layer k is the span through ek. Its new line has compact Casimir increment 82k+2k², compared with the lower bound 78k. For k = 1 these numbers are 84 and 78. The full Casimir stays 800 because the two noncompact products cancel the growth of the compact scalar.

Why does orthogonality to the compact type persist under translation?

Keep the type V and its gap from the layer estimate. In its unitary representation τ, let B be a linear map from V to the Hilbert space that commutes with the maximal compact group K. Assume its image is orthogonal to V. We will prove that for every g ∈ H and u,v ∈ V,

⟨Bu,τ(g)v⟩ = 0.

Fix a nonzero highest vector u₀ of weight L in V. On a finite layer Fk, the functional x ↦ ⟨Bu₀,x⟩ is represented by the vector z = PkBu₀. Since V is a finite-dimensional compact representation, compact equivariance makes B commute with differentiation along its compact action. The layers are finite-dimensional and stable under this action because exponentiating a compact generator preserves its layer, and the two compact unitary factors are generated by such exponentials. Thus Pk also commutes with K. The compact highest-weight equations of u₀ carry over to z. By the layer estimate, z belongs to V. Since Bu₀ is orthogonal to V,

‖z‖² = ⟨Bu₀,z⟩ = 0.

The translates of u₀ by K span V by compact irreducibility. Commuting PkB past each translate gives PkBu = 0 for every u ∈ V. This works for every finite layer, so Bu is orthogonal to their union. For fixed u, the subspace perpendicular to Bu is closed, so it contains the Hilbert closure of the whole derivative module.

It remains to put τ(g)v in that closure. The analytic input used here is the invariance theorem for a smooth finite-dimensional compact type of fixed full Casimir. For any vector of its derivative module and any noncompact Hermitian tangent direction Y, translation by exp(Y) lies in the closure of that module. The derivative estimates establishing this assertion are a separate analytic step. On the compact subgroup each finite layer is already invariant. Every g has a polar decomposition g = k exp(Y). Applying the assertion to exp(Y), followed by the bounded operator τ(k), puts τ(g)v in the derivative closure. This proves the displayed orthogonality. For the diagonal compact type in the comparison of two representations, the orthogonal map B changes the sign of the second coordinate.
