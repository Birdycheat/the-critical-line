Where do the forms satisfying the local tests come from?

Fix the algebraic weight W of the definite unitary group of rank a ≥ 1, a split auxiliary place w0 above ℓ, and a level K away from w0. The level stays fixed away from w0 throughout the approximation. At w0 we may replace a compact open subgroup by a smaller one. This variation gives the additional forms needed for the approximation.

At each place w of a finite set S, prescribe a nonzero polynomial Φw in the a Satake parameters. Each w is split, lies over a different prime of the real subfield from w0, and has hyperspecial local level. Call a representation admissible here if Φw is nonzero on every ordering of its spherical Satake parameters at every w in S. Write Mgood for the algebraic span of the K-fixed forms of weight W in these representations.

Density theorem at fixed weight and level away from one place. For every K-fixed form f of weight W and every δ > 0, there exist G ∈ Mgood and a scalar function e on the adelic group such that, for every weight vector v and group point g,

f(v, g) − G(v, g) = e(g)f(v, g),   |e(g)|ℓ ≤ δ.

The multiplier is the same for all v at a given g. In particular, every evaluation that vanishes for f also vanishes for G. We derive this statement from two estimates on a finite quotient at w0.

Counting the representations that fail a test. By smoothness choose a compact open subgroup J0 at w0 that fixes f, and a descending congruence tower inside it. The tower construction supplies normal subgroups Jn with finite ℓ-group quotients Hn = J0/Jn. At the full level obtained by adjoining Jn to K, let Bw,n be the span belonging to representations that fail the test at w. The one-place counting estimate asserts that for every η > 0 and every sufficiently large n, this space is finite dimensional with dim Bw,n ≤ η|Hn|. Take η = 1/(2(|S| + 1)) and choose a common depth for the finitely many places. Since each representation failing at least one test belongs to one of these spaces, their sum contains the whole failing span Bn. Hence 2 dim Bn ≤ |Hn|.

One correction. Write H = Hn. The tower provides a label L(g) in H for each adelic point. Right translation by j in J0 changes the label to L(g) times the class of j in H. Multiplying by a function of the label gives a linear map Ψ from functions on H to forms at the refined level,

Ψ(φ)(v, g) = φ(L(g))f(v, g).

Let P be the inverse image under Ψ of the admissible span at this refined level. The automorphic decomposition writes every form there as the sum of admissible and failing contributions. After quotienting by the admissible span, the image of Ψ therefore has dimension at most dim Bn. Its kernel in this quotient is P, so dim P ≥ |H| − dim Bn ≥ |H|/2. Moreover P is stable under right translations of H. Indeed, lift a translation to J0. It fixes f, normalizes the refined level and translates a form inside the same automorphic representation.

Finite-group approximation lemma. A right-translation-invariant subspace of functions on a finite ℓ-group H, of dimension at least |H|/2, contains a function φ satisfying |1 − φ(h)|ℓ ≤ ℓ−1/2 for every h ∈ H. The norm uses the fixed identification of the complex coefficients with the algebraic closure of ℚℓ. Apply this lemma to P. The form Ψ(φ) is admissible by the definition of P. Its error multiplier is 1 − φ(L(g)), so a single correction has a bound strictly smaller than one.

For a two-point example take ℓ = 2 and H = {1, σ}. The sign character spans a translation-invariant line, exactly half the dimension of all functions on H. Taking φ to be that character gives errors 0 at 1 and 2 at σ. The second error has 2-adic absolute value 1/2, smaller than the required 1/√2. Thus a line that contains only functions with values of opposite signs can nevertheless approximate the constant function 1 in this norm.

Iteration. Begin with G0 = 0 and e0 = 1. If the residual is f − Gm = emf, apply one correction to this residual, which still has weight W and level K away from w0. Write B for the resulting admissible form and b(g) for its error multiplier. Set Gm+1 = Gm + B. Then f − Gm+1 = b emf. After m corrections the multiplier has absolute value at most ℓ−m/2 at every point. A finite m achieves any prescribed δ.

By definition of the algebraic span, the resulting G is a finite sum of admissible forms. Group the terms belonging to the same automorphic representation and omit zero sums. When the selected Hecke operators act by scalars on the K-fixed vectors of each representation, each surviving term is a simultaneous eigenform for those operators. With |eg,x(f)|ℓ ≤ R in the integral frame and R ≥ 1, take δ = ε/R. The same list then has residual bounded by ε in every evaluation, as required for the polynomial argument. Simultaneous approximation for all integral polynomials.

The proof uses two different norms. On the tower of auxiliary levels, its separation property allows the complex norm of the test operator to bound the dimension of the failing forms. The resulting dimension inequality is the input to a finite-group estimate in the ℓ-adic norm. That estimate produces the multiplier in the relative error above.
