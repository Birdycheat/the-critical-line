How can a distant ambient representation predict a smaller weight?

The descent in the signed parameters needs a starting pair at which the smaller group actually occurs. Write G = U(p,q), H = U(p−1,q) and n = p+q−1. The subgroup fixes a positive unit vector. Choose distinct signed directions d in the big space and d′ in the small one, with the signed interlacing rule. The compression calculation below supplies a fixed g ∈ G for which the small diagonal matrix with entries d′ is the upper block of g diag(d) g⁻¹ in the subgroup coordinates.

Fix a scale t ≥ 1 at which λ + td is a regular Harish-Chandra parameter with opposite signs separated by at least 200(n+1)³. Let π be a discrete series with that parameter. Write Dab for the derived action of the matrix unit in the smaller block. For a smooth vector x define its error in following the small diagonal by

Qt(x) = ∑a,b ‖Dabx − δabtd′ax‖².

Take a unit highest-weight vector v in the lowest compact type of π. Its diagonal weights are L + td, where L = λ + ρnoncompact − ρcompact and each ρ is the half-sum of the indicated positive roots. We claim that η = π(g)v has Qt(η) ≤ C₀t for one constant independent of the scale and the chosen model. For a compact root the raising operator kills v, and commuting it with the opposite lowering operator bounds the latter’s squared norm by the absolute difference of the two weights. For noncompact roots the sum of the squared norms is the difference of the compact and full Casimir scalars. This follows by summing the signed adjoint identity for each pair of opposite matrix units. The two quadratic scalars have the same leading term t²∑ di², so their difference is affine in t. Thus every off-diagonal derivative of v has norm at most √(P+Rt) for fixed nonnegative P,R. Lowest compact type.

Conjugate the small matrix unit by g⁻¹ and call the resulting big matrix Y. Its diagonal contribution to the derivative on v is ∑ Yii(Li + tdi). The compression identity gives ∑ Yiidi = δabd′a by taking a trace. The entire order-t term is exactly the prescribed center in the displayed error. What remains has norm at most (∑ |Yij|)√(P+Rt) + ∑ |YiiLi|. Squaring, using (a+b)² ≤ 2a²+2b², and summing the n² terms proves the claim.

How can a compact-finite trial vector retain the same error?

The preceding translate is smooth. The next step needs a vector that lies in a finite-dimensional space under the maximal compact subgroup of H. We can approximate η in a finite sum F of smooth compact types of G. The density used here follows by projecting a smooth vector onto its compact types by character integration and applying the Peter–Weyl theorem. Each projection is smooth by differentiation under the compact integral. P. Etingof, Representations of Lie Groups, lecture 4, Corollary 4.7. Let x be the orthogonal projection of η onto F and w = η − x. The subspace F is stable under the compact Casimir ΩK. If c is the scalar of the full ambient Casimir, the signed adjoint identity from the preceding argument gives

∑i,j ‖DGijw‖² = Re ⟨(2ΩK − c)η, w⟩ ≤ A‖w‖,   A = ‖(2ΩK − c)η‖.

Indeed twice the sum over compact pairs minus the signed sum over all pairs is the unsigned sum. Replacing w by η inside the first slot adds a vector of F, orthogonal to w. The superscript G includes all ambient matrix units. The corresponding sum over the smaller block is bounded by this full sum. Put B = ∑a|td′a|². For this particular scale choose F large enough that ‖w‖ is less than min(1/2, t/(4A+1), t/(4B+1)). Then the derivative sum for w and the scalar error B‖w‖² are each at most t/4. Applying the elementary square bound twice to x = η − w gives Qt(x) ≤ 2C₀t + 4(t/4) + 4(t/4). Since ‖x‖ ≥ 1/2, we obtain

x ≠ 0,   Qt(x)/‖x‖² ≤ Ct,   C = 8C₀ + 8.

Restriction to H leaves the vector and its norm unchanged and identifies the derivatives in the small block. Since its compact subgroup lies in the big compact subgroup, x is compact-finite on restriction. The approximating subspace can depend on t and π. The constant in the quotient is common to all of them.

Why does the trial vector give an actual discrete constituent?

Distant occurrence. Suppose the entries of d′ are decreasing with gaps at least one. For all sufficiently large t with λ + td admissible as above, each ambient discrete series π at that parameter contains, on restriction, a discrete series with some smaller parameter μ′ satisfying the following estimate. The constants are uniform in π. If another realization of that same smaller discrete series is chosen, it too admits a nonzero bounded intertwiner into π.

|μ′a − td′a| ≤ (√(2C) + J + n)√t,   J = ⌈C⌉ + 1.

Set ξ = td′, S = Ct and s = √(2S). The proof follows one vector through three steps. First, select a nonzero compact component whose error is at most the trial bound. Its weight ν records how the diagonal torus acts, and its compact Casimir scalar κ measures the sum of its compact derivative squares. The invariant cutoff gives the lower bound κ − 2S for the full Casimir on every unit vector in a space stable under differentiation. A vector with the original compact data survives in that space.

Next, raise the compact weight to a highest weight L at distance at most s from ξ. Now lower across opposite signs when the first derivative layer supplies the required next highest vector. Each lowering moves a coordinate by at most one and costs at least 2(t−2(s+J))−2n−2 in compact Casimir. For J = ⌈C⌉ + 1 the cost of J moves would exceed 2S for sufficiently large t. The Casimir lower bound forces a stopping type.

Finally, convert its compact weight to the parameter μ′ by the half-root shift. This costs at most n per coordinate. The three errors add to √(2Ct)+J+n, which gives the displayed estimate for t ≥ 1. At large enough separation a discrete series of this parameter exists. The isometry on translates of the terminal type puts a copy inside the restriction. The integer descent can then start at a large square scale and return to the requested pair. Hongyu He, Theorem 0.1 on signed branching.

Constructing a compression with the prescribed signs

Let D = diag(di) in a basis of squared lengths εi = ±1. Seek a positive unit normal z so that the compression of D to z⊥ has the specified eigenvalues d′a and signs ε′a. In the merged decreasing list all values are distinct. Multiply each sign coming from the smaller group by −1. The signed interlacing rule says these modified signs alternate. There are n+1 positive modified signs and n negative ones, so the first sign is positive.

Form the two monic polynomials P(X) = ∏i(X−di) and R(X) = ∏a(X−d′a). Canceling each pole gives the partial fractions below. After multiplication by P, both sides agree at all n+1 roots of P and have degree at most n, proving equality. Leading coefficients give ∑ ci = 1.

R(X)/P(X) = ∑i ci/(X−di),   ci = R(di)/P′(di).

The sign of ci is (−1) to the number of entries above di, by counting negative factors. Thus it equals εi. Take zi = √|ci|. Its signed squared norm is ∑ ci = 1. For each small root u = d′a put v(u) = (uI−D)⁻¹z. Its pairing with z is R(u)/P(u) = 0. Since Dv(u) = uv(u) − z, its projection onto z⊥ is uv(u). Distinct roots give orthogonal vectors by self-adjointness of the compression. Their signed squared norms are ∑ ci/(u−di)² = −R′(u)/P(u). Counting signs of factors again gives ε′a, including the minus sign for the small group. Normalize these n vectors and append z at the distinguished positive coordinate. The inverse of this isometric basis change is the g required in the moment calculation.

For U(2,1) take the big directions (5,4,3) with signs (+,−,+), and the small directions (2,1) with signs (+,−). The residues of (X−2)(X−1)/((X−5)(X−4)(X−3)) are 6, −6 and 1. The normal (√6,√6,1) has signed squared norm 1. The two vectors from the resolvent have signed squared norms 1/6 and −1/24. Thus both the eigenvalues and the signature of the compressed block can be read directly from this one rational function. For these directions take λ = (2,1,0). At every sufficiently large integer t, the ambient parameter λ + t(5,4,3) has minimum opposite-sign gap t + 1. The occurrence estimate then gives a constituent within C√t of (2t,t) in each coordinate.
