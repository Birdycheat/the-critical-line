Which coefficient of the holomorphic trace detects a prescribed degree?

Let g be an admitted Hecke test at the chosen level which satisfies the regularity and vanishing conditions of the definite trace formula. Denote its definite automorphic trace in the given weight by D(g). For a sufficiently large fibre power there is a finite family of coefficients cα, chosen from the weight and its exponent list, independent of g. The label α specifies a nonzero even algebraic integer on each factor of the abelian fibre power. These entries act on their respective factors and give a self-map of the fibre power. In bidegree (a, b), let Ca,b be the sum of its pullbacks over all labels, weighted by cα. Write Ta,b(g) for the Hecke action on Hb(Z, Ωa).

Weighted trace identity. The finite étale fixed-point formula and the comparison of the marked fixed-point count with the definite automorphic trace give the following identity of polynomials in y.

∑a=0d (−y)a ∑b=0d (−1)b tr(Ta,b(g) Ca,b) = D(g) ∑i yhi.

Geometric inputs and their use. Decompose g into a finite sum of double-coset indicators supported where its regularity condition holds. Each indicator and each fibre label give a correspondence on a deeper level. Its two maps to Z are finite étale. At a coincidence of the maps, use the first map to identify tangent spaces and write A for the tangent action of the second. The orbit description identifies the base coincidences. The marked root calculation checks transversality, and the fibre trace sums their tangent contributions. For form degree a the holomorphic fixed-point formula gives the local contribution [Xa]det(I + XA)/det(I − A). The notation in square brackets extracts a coefficient. Multiplying this contribution by (−y)a and summing in a simply evaluates the numerator at −y, giving det(I − yA)/det(I − A). In dimension one, a tangent multiplier of 2 contributes 2y − 1. At y = 1 this fixed point contributes one to the count. The constant coefficient is −1, its local contribution to the alternating trace on the cohomology of the structure sheaf (form degree zero). The local numerator is the exterior-power trace appearing in the holomorphic Lefschetz formula. M. F. Atiyah and R. Bott, A Lefschetz fixed point formula for elliptic complexes. II. Applications.

Passing from the deeper level to the normalized Hecke operator divides this sum by the subgroup index. The count of admissible lifts on the deeper level contains the same factor. It cancels before summing over the indicators. On the group side the weights cα sum the contributions over the marked roots of each regular characteristic polynomial. The interpolation identity and the sum of marked alternants give the character of the specified algebraic representation times ∑i yhi. The equality between the weighted fixed-point count and the definite pair count, followed by the calculation of the diagonal trace, now gives the displayed identity by linearity. This use of the trace identity depends on the geometric fixed-point count, the construction of the weights and both group-side comparisons.

Extracting the coefficient. Number the weight coordinates from 0 to n − 1. The admissible exponent lists have one of two forms, up to an additive constant independent of i. The first requires the conjugate weight list to be constant. It gives hi = constant + λi + n − 1 − i. The second requires the weight at the distinguished embedding to be constant. It gives hi = constant − λ′i + i, using the weight at the conjugate embedding. The weight lists are weakly decreasing. The first exponent list strictly decreases and the second strictly increases. Each exponent therefore occurs once. The fibre power used in the eigenclass theorem can be chosen uniformly. For each embedding and conjugated weight these two formulas give at most two lists, with their constants specified by the weight data. The embeddings of the number field form a finite set and field automorphisms only permute them. Take the maximum of the finitely many thresholds for these lists.

Now suppose D(g) ≠ 0 and put h = hi. The right-hand coefficient of yh is D(g). The left side has degree at most d, so h ≤ d. At that degree the identity reads

∑b=0d (−1)b tr(Th,b(g) Ch,b) = (−1)h D(g) ≠ 0.

At least one summand has nonzero trace. The eigenclass argument applies this calculation to a test g chosen to separate the prescribed Hecke system. This is what gives the nonzero summand its spectral meaning.

How does a trace give a class with all the prescribed Hecke eigenvalues?

Let Z be a smooth projective complex fibre power in the unitary tower, of dimension d. The two indices in Hb(Z, Ωa) record different information. The sheaf Ωa consists of differential a-forms, and b is its cohomological degree. The automorphic weight prescribes the first index. To find the second we use an alternating trace over the possible values of b.

Eigenclass theorem. Use the compact unitary datum of the tower. Its rank is odd, its three parameters a, m and the linked group’s a are all 1, and its complex base is the standard base. Fix a weight whose coordinates are nonnegative integers in decreasing or equal order at each embedding. Assume the geometric and group-side comparisons in the weighted trace identity above. A single fibre power works for all admissible levels and conjugates of this weight. At such a level let f and the Hecke functions ψj be admitted by the tower, and suppose they pass the spectral trace test for the specified scalars θj. For each exponent hi in an admitted list there are an integer 0 ≤ b ≤ d and a class x such that

0 ≠ x ∈ Hb(Z, Ωhi),   Tjx = θjx for every j.

Here Tj is the cohomological action of ψj. The same class satisfies all the equations for the chosen i. The degree b and the class can change with i.

Where the operators commute. Fix h = hi. For 0 ≤ b ≤ d write Wb = Hb(Z, Ωh), Fb for the action of f there, and Tb,j for that of ψj. These spaces are finite-dimensional by coherent cohomology of a proper scheme. Let Ub be the span of the vectors w(Tb)Fbz for z ∈ Wb and every finite word w in the operators, including the empty word. Prepending a letter shows that each Tb,j preserves this space. Convolution of words acts by composition on cohomology. The spectral test permits the first two letters in any word applied to f to be interchanged. Thus the operators commute on every generator of Ub, and hence on that whole space. The Stacks Project, Proposition 30.19.1, finiteness of coherent cohomology.

One polynomial for all the degrees. On the direct sum of the Ub, put all the commuting actions side by side. Adjoin a one-dimensional summand on which operator j acts by θj. This extra line keeps track of scalar evaluation while we choose a polynomial in the operators. If some operator minus its prescribed scalar is not nilpotent on the current space, project to its generalized eigenspace for that scalar. To construct the projection, factor its minimal polynomial as (X − θj)rq(X) with q(θj) ≠ 0. The extra line ensures r ≥ 1. Bézout's identity supplies a multiple of q congruent to 1 modulo (X − θj)r. It fixes the generalized eigenspace, maps the current space into it, and still evaluates to 1 on the extra line. All other operators preserve the image by commutativity.

Every such restriction decreases dimension. After finitely many restrictions, even for an infinite family of operators, the product of the projections is a polynomial s with s(θ) = 1. Its image in each Ub consists of simultaneous generalized eigenvectors with the prescribed scalars. The extra line alone could have survived. We now use the trace to show that one of the cohomological summands survives too.

Finding the class. The spectral test applied to s supplies a second polynomial m such that the function g obtained by applying the convolution words ms to f is admissible for the trace formula and has nonzero definite trace. The coefficient at the prescribed holomorphic degree then gives a b for which

tr(m(Tb) s(Tb) Fb Ch,b) ≠ 0.

The rightmost factor Ch,b is the weighted sum of geometric pullbacks in the trace identity. A nonzero trace implies the composite is nonzero, hence s(Tb)Fb is nonzero. Its image lies in the intersection of the prescribed generalized eigenspaces in Ub. This intersection is stable. On it each difference Tb,j − θjI is nilpotent. Whenever such a difference is nonzero, its kernel is a proper nonzero subspace preserved by all the other differences. Restrict to that kernel and repeat. Dimension decreases until all the differences vanish on a nonzero space. Any nonzero vector there gives the required class.

For example, let T act diagonally on ℂ² with diagonal entries 2 and 3. Its trace is 5. If we prescribe θ = 5, the polynomial s(X) = (X − 2)(X − 3)/6 satisfies s(5) = 1 and s(T) = 0. Every product with this last operator has zero trace. Thus this example fails the spectral test at θ = 5.

For a tower originally defined over a field embedded in ℂ, the comparison also transports the result through the chosen embedding and the complex field automorphism σ of the weight test. The individual correspondence functions count cosets and take natural-number values, so σ fixes these functions. It sends the prescribed scalars to σ(θj). Compatibility of pullback and finite étale trace with base change identifies the resulting equations with those for the actual geometric correspondences. This is the occurrence used in the shifted Hodge degree of the tower.
