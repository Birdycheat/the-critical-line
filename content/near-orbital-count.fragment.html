What can contribute close to the identity?

In the four contributions to the trace error, the first is an integral of a count with weights. At a point x of the fundamental domain, let A(x) be the sum of |φ(x⁻¹γx)| over nonidentity rational elements γ whose summand is nonzero and passes the near test. At each infinite place the Euclidean length of the vector of logarithms of the singular values of x⁻¹γx must be at most ε = 10−6n−8f−2. This test measures stretching. A unitary matrix has singular values one even when it moves vectors. The integral trace argument turns small stretching into closeness to the identity for these rational elements. The test coefficient imposes the finite lattice condition. The resulting bound is ‖x⁻¹γx − I‖HS ≤ t = 3√(nε) at every infinite place.

Near-term estimate. In signature (p, q) put n = p + q, with q ≥ 1 and q² ≤ n. Fix the level-growth exponent. Under the field, level and spectral-gap hypotheses of the dimension comparison, for all sufficiently large n one has

∫moderate A(x) dμ(x) ≤ M exp(−U/4),   U = nf log n.

Here M = c₀V is the identity contribution, with V the volume of the fundamental domain. To describe the integration region put X = nf d g b, where d is the root discriminant, g the finite-level parameter and b the height parameter. A point is moderate when its least normalized sublattice size is at least τ∗ = X−11q. This lower cutoff matters because more rational transformations can preserve a lattice with a very small subspace.

First consider a point whose least size is at least ½. Let L≤s denote the step in its size filtration generated by sublattices of largest normalized size at most s. Self-duality puts the entire lattice in L≤2. The filtration estimate for an integral near transformation gives (γ − I)L≤2 ⊆ L≤2t. Since 2t < ½, the latter step is zero. Thus γ fixes a spanning lattice and is the identity. Here A(x) = 0. At every remaining moderate point there is a nonempty canonical flag of short isotropic subspaces. Write j₁,…,ja for the successive increases in rank, and τ₁ < … < τa for the normalized sizes of the successive quotients. Then τ∗ ≤ τ₁ and τa < ½. The total rank J = ∑i ji is at most q, since projection onto the negative subspace is injective on an isotropic subspace.

How much does one flag type contribute?

Fix the ordered ranks j = (j₁,…,ja). Two estimates use the same factors in the size variables. The lattice count bounds the number of possible group elements for those sizes. The flag-measure theorem bounds the volume of points with those sizes. Their product is small enough to integrate even when the count alone becomes large.

The remaining integral is over ordered sizes between τ∗ and ½. Enlarge this simplex to the whole box [τ∗,½]a. Each coordinate now integrates separately, with ∫τ∗½ ds/s = log(1/(2τ∗)). If Ij is the near integral for this flag type, it follows that

Ij ≤ 2M κj ∏i [2fji log(1/(2τ∗))] ≤ 2M κj Ya,   Y = 22f q² log X.

For the second inequality use ji ≤ q and τ∗ = X−11q. To check the box enlargement alone, take f = 1, ordered ranks (1,2) and lower endpoint ⅛. The integral of (2/s)(4/v) on the square [⅛,½]² is 8(log 4)². Exchanging s and v preserves this integrand. The triangle s < v therefore gives 4(log 4)². The example isolates the integration step from the choice of arithmetic parameters.

Why do all flag types together cost at most exp(−U/4)?

The arithmetic estimate for the flag constant gives κj ≤ exp(−JU/2). The scale bounds give Y ≤ exp(U/8) and 2q+1 ≤ exp(U/8) beyond a single rank threshold for the fixed level-growth exponent. The first inequality pays for integrating the sizes. Since every rank increment is positive, a ≤ J. Thus −JU/2 + aU/8 ≤ −3JU/8 ≤ −3U/8.

The second scale inequality pays for summing the flags. Ordered positive ranks of total J are compositions of J. Choose which of the J − 1 gaps between consecutive units carry a divider. This gives 2J−1 choices and hence 2q − 1 possible types in total. For q = 3 there is one type of rank one, two of rank two, and four of rank three, including both (1,2) and (2,1). Every nonzero near contribution has one of the counted types. Summing the nonnegative upper bounds gives (2q − 1)·2M exp(−3U/8) ≤ M exp(−U/4). This is the near part on the moderate region in the trace inequality. The deep region has its own volume and kernel-mass estimates.
