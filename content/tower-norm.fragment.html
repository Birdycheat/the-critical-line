Which degree counts the conjugates, and which degree counts the ideal?

Put N = Fd and enlarge the level to qN by a fresh prime q coprime to N. The construction chooses its two cyclotomic fields inside one algebraic closure. Even if the two embeddings were initially chosen separately, every Nth root of unity is a power of a primitive root of order qN. This puts the smaller field inside the larger one in precisely one way compatible with the chosen embeddings. Restricting the same place above the rational prime r then gives primes 𝔭′ above 𝔭. The field norm counts conjugates. A relative automorphism fixes the roots of order N and can act by any nonzero residue on a primitive qth root. Indeed, for a generator g modulo q, the Chinese remainder theorem gives an exponent equal to 1 modulo N and to g modulo q. The resulting power map on roots gives an automorphism τ of KqN over KN. Its first q − 1 powers are distinct on the primitive qth root. They exhaust the relative Galois group since the degree is φ(qN)/φ(N) = q − 1, where φ counts invertible residues. Hence the product of τi(Xqd) for 0 ≤ i < q − 1 is its field norm. The relative degree · The exponent on the two sets of roots · All the conjugates in the norm

The ideal norm counts residue-field degree. For r ∤ qN, write ordn(r) for the least positive k with rk ≡ 1 modulo n. At either level the prime is unramified, and Frobenius acts on the roots by the rth power. Its order is the absolute residue degree. Write κ(𝔭) for the residue field at 𝔭. Degrees multiply in the tower of residue fields, giving

s = [κ(𝔭′) : κ(𝔭)] = ordqN(r) / ordN(r),   Norm(𝔭′) = 𝔭s.

Thus Xds satisfies the two generator conditions for Norm(𝔭′). Raising to the power s multiplies each ideal exponent by s and preserves the identity with complex conjugation. This explains its exponent in both the norm formula and the congruence. The denominator in the norm formula uses the inverse of the qth-power action on the lower roots. The admissible auxiliary primes have orders of r prime to ℓ, and r ≡ 1 modulo F. By the Chinese remainder theorem their least common multiple is ordqN(r), so ℓ ∤ s. Check the two norms at levels 21 and 273

Take F = ℓ = 3, d = 7, q = 13 and r = 307. Both auxiliary primes are 1 modulo 3. Modulo 3, 7 and 13 the residues of 307 are 1, −1 and 8, of orders 1, 2 and 4 respectively (8² ≡ −1 modulo 13). The absolute residue degrees at levels 21 and 273 are therefore 2 and 4. The relative ideal norm is 𝔭². The residue field at 𝔭′ has 307⁴ elements and (307²)² = 307⁴. On elements the relative norm has twelve factors. For a primitive thirteenth root ζ these give Norm(1 − ζ) = ∏j=112(1 − ζj) = 13 by evaluating 1 + T + ⋯ + T12 at 1.
