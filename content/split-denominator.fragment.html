How one denominator works for every residue test

The stabilized cycle is chosen before its reductions. Each test must identify the reduction of the coefficient module with one copy of the residue field for each Galois automorphism and each coordinate. Here is the field calculation for one coordinate. Let F/L′ be the finite Galois extension in the construction and write G for its Galois group. Given a ring map r from 𝒪F to a finite field k, denote its restriction to 𝒪L′ by r0. Assume r0 is onto. We want to prescribe all entries of the tuple (r(σa))σ∈G and identify its kernel. Which single element may we exclude from every later test? Start with a basis ui of F over L′ and its dual ui∨ for the pairing (x,y) ↦ Tr(xy). The pairing is nondegenerate because the extension is separable. Clear the denominators in both finite lists with one nonzero integer m. Put ei = m ui∨, fi = m ui and b0 = m². Multiplying trace-dual expansion by m² gives the first identity below. For the second, apply σ and expand the trace as a sum over τ ∈ G. Linear independence of distinct field automorphisms as functions of x identifies the coefficient of τ(x) on both sides.

The trace formula constructs the required coefficients without choosing an integral basis over the base ring. A linear basis over the field and a common denominator suffice. Prescribe the two conjugate residues in the Gaussian integers

For ℚ(i)/ℚ take the basis (1,i), trace dual (1/2,−i/2), m = 2 and b0 = 4. Reduce at 5 by sending i to 2. The two rows send a + ci to a + 2c and a − 2c. To obtain (0,1), add the equations to get 2a = 1 and subtract them to get 4c = −1 in 𝔽5. Thus a = 3 and c = 1 work. The kernel consists exactly of multiples of 5. At 2 the two readings coincide, and b0 reduces to zero. This shows why the invertibility assumption matters.

The lattice also has a divisor 𝔡 prescribing lower bounds for the orders of its coordinates. Enlarge b0 to B = b0∏𝔓∈supp 𝔡 Norm(𝔓). Each norm lies in its prime ideal and is nonzero. Every prime in the finite support now contains B. Away from B, the prescribed order and the ordinary integrality condition agree. At a prime dividing B, multiplying by B raises the order by at least one. For any vector integral away from B, one large enough power Bn therefore brings all its finitely many coordinates into the prescribed lattice. This works for either lattice transported by a gluing map. After inverting B, the map and its inverse preserve the same module. The fixed B works across the maps and future residue tests, while n can depend on the vector. For any allowed reduction, r0(B) ≠ 0 implies r0(b0) ≠ 0, so the split tuple above is available for the cycle test.
