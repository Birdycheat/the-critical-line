How can a Hecke trace detect a prescribed system of eigenvalues?

Let K be the compact open subgroup at the integer level N. A Hecke function at this level is bi-invariant under K and supported on finitely many right cosets. Write ∗ for convolution. Let ψj be the functions attached to the chosen correspondences and θj their prescribed complex scalars. A polynomial in words is a finite linear combination of ordered products of the ψj. The empty word acts as the identity. For such a polynomial s, let s(ψ)f mean the result of applying its convolutions to a test function f, and let s(θ) mean its scalar evaluation.

Spectral trace test. Use the odd-rank unitary datum and the nonzero automorphic form of the prescribed local type at w₀. The rational prime below w₀ is good for the datum, and its chosen quadratic generator generates the same field as √(−d). No prime above p occurs in the level support of the form, and the form has a nonzero value at an argument integral with integral inverse above p.

Given any finite set of excluded rational primes, the auxiliary-prime construction chooses a good prime r outside this set, distinct from p and away from the level support of the form. Take an admissible multiple N of r whose principal level lifts to the stabilizer with trivial type character. Use the stabilizing deck operators belonging to the principal subgroup at r, with scalar 1, or the spherical operators at eligible places away from r, with their prescribed index and character normalization. The assembly of the local tests specifies which operators to include. For each such family there is a Hecke function f such that g−1 carries the integral lattice into itself whenever f(g) ≠ 0. The ψj commute on all words applied to f. There is one polynomial A such that whenever s(θ) ≠ 0, the test (As)(ψ)f satisfies the regularity and vanishing conditions of the trace formula and has nonzero definite automorphic trace.

From the form to a finite space. The local type gives a Hecke idempotent e, so e ∗ e = e. Transport of the given automorphic form supplies a nonzero vector φ fixed by e on which each ψj acts by θj. It belongs to one of the spaces of definite automorphic forms at this level and weight. Take their direct sum M over the finitely many definite Hermitian classes in the trace formula. For each class the double coset set at the specified level is finite. Evaluation on representatives determines a form, by its rational transformation law on the left and level invariance on the right. Each summand therefore embeds in a finite product of copies of the coefficient space. In particular M is finite dimensional, and the sum of the traces on its summands is the trace on M. Finite class number and evaluation at representatives.

For a Hecke function h, write T(h) for its action on M. Convolution becomes composition. Put M₀ = im T(e). The local Hecke relations imply that each T(ψj) preserves M₀ and that their restrictions Tj commute. At the chosen auxiliary places a second construction supplies functions u and u′, with e ∗ u = u ∗ e = u and u′ ∗ u = e. On M₀ the operator B induced by u commutes with every Tj and has a left inverse. It is thus invertible on this finite-dimensional space.

Choosing the multiplier. Start with M₀, which contains φ. Whenever Tj − θjI fails to be nilpotent on the remaining space, restrict to its generalized θj-eigenspace there. The dimension decreases and φ survives. Each restriction is a polynomial projection. Indeed, on M₀ factor the minimal polynomial of Tj as (X − θj)kb(X) with b(θj) ≠ 0. The Euclidean algorithm gives a multiple of b congruent to 1 modulo (X − θj)k. Evaluated at Tj it fixes the kernel of (Tj − θjI)k and maps into it, since the full minimal polynomial annihilates Tj. These projections commute. Their finite product A(T) projects onto a nonzero space L on which every Tj − θjI is nilpotent. The product uses finitely many indices even when the family of correspondences is infinite, and A(θ) = 1.

Choosing the function. The operator B preserves L and is invertible there. Put tm = tr(Bm|L). Some positive m has tm ≠ 0. In fact the minimal polynomial of B|L has nonzero constant coefficient. Taking the trace of its annihilating identity shows that if all positive power traces vanished, its constant coefficient times t₀ would vanish as well. But t₀ = dimℂ L > 0. Fix such an m and take f = u∗m ∗ e. On L the difference s(T) − s(θ)I is a polynomial without constant term in the finitely many commuting nilpotent differences occurring in s. In a sufficiently high power every resulting monomial contains a power of one of these differences large enough to vanish. Thus the difference is nilpotent. Its product with the commuting Bm is also nilpotent and has trace zero. The trailing e removes the kernel of T(e), and A(T) removes the complementary summand to L in M₀. Consequently

trM T((As)(ψ)f) = s(θ) trL(Bm).

For example, let one operator have diagonal entries 2, 2, 5 and let B have diagonal entries 1, −1, 1 in the same diagonal basis. To detect θ = 2 take A(X) = (5 − X)/3. On the selected two-dimensional space the first power of B has trace zero and the second has trace 2. With s(X) = X + 1 the resulting trace is 6. The common eigenvector φ establishes that the selected space is nonzero. The scalar-valued function f supplies a trace that detects that space.

The passage from this trace identity to a geometric test uses two local results. The auxiliary Hecke function puts the support of each positive convolution power into a regular set. The inverses of points in the support of each power carry the integral lattice into itself. The regular set is stable under left multiplication by the supports of the ψj, so their words retain regular support. Cuspidality of the type at w₀ and separability of the local characteristic polynomial at an auxiliary place kill the orbital terms from marked pairs with reducible polynomial and from elements of the definite forms outside the regular irreducible locus. The construction of these local functions and the orbital vanishing theorems enter here as inputs. The Hodge comparison uses the resulting trace test to obtain a joint eigenclass.
