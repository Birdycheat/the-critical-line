When does the crystalline congruence survive passage to the limit?

Let v = e(u) = [b] ∈ Ainf, where b is the chosen tilt element of positive valuation. The crystalline condition on a layer says that each Galois correction acts as the identity after reduction modulo v. We now have an integral correction ε₀(g) on the limit frame. We use the same prime, local field and Breuil–Kisin datum on every layer.

Transfer of the integral crystalline condition. Suppose εm(g) ≡ I modulo v in Ainf for every layer m and every g ∈ GK. Assume that the target frame is linked to the tower at every ℓ-adic precision, with one exponent r as in the uniform comparison. Then ε₀(g) ≡ I modulo v in Ainf for every g ∈ GK.

First consider a power series s ∈ 𝔖. Write it as s = w + u q, with w ∈ W(k). The action fixes e(w). It also preserves the ideal generated by v. Indeed g preserves the valuation on the tilt field F = ℂℓ♭, so g(b)/b is a unit of its valuation ring. Multiplicativity of Teichmüller lifts gives g(v) = v[g(b)/b]. Consequently g(e(s)) and e(s) have the same residue modulo v, even when g moves the chosen parameter.

Fix g and a precision n, and choose a linked layer m ≥ n. Put C = e(R) and B = e(G) for the two comparison matrices. The link gives CB ≡ ℓrI modulo ℓn. The calculation of the integral correction gives ε₀(g)g(C) ≡ Cεm(g) modulo ℓn in Ainf. Here we may contract the congruence from the larger Witt ring because equality of the first n Witt coordinates detects reduction modulo ℓn in either ring. Multiply the latter congruence on the right by g(B). Since g fixes ℓ, the left side becomes ℓrε₀(g) modulo ℓn. On the right, reduce also modulo v. The layer hypothesis gives εm(g) ≡ I, and the power-series calculation gives g(B) ≡ B. The right side reduces to CB, hence to ℓrI. Thus, entrywise,

ℓr(ε₀(g) − I) ∈ (ℓn, v)   for every n.

Let x be one entry of ε₀(g) − I. For every n, the displayed containment gives an integral an with ℓrx ≡ van modulo ℓn. In W(F) the Teichmüller element v has inverse [b−1]. There ℓr(x/v) ≡ an modulo ℓn. Take n = i + r + 1 for each i. Comparing Witt coordinate i+r on the two sides identifies (zi)ℓr with an integral coordinate of an, where zi is coordinate i of x/v. It follows that the coordinate itself has nonnegative valuation. Hence x/v ∈ Ainf, giving the required divisibility in the integral ring. Brinon–Conrad, §4.2, Witt coordinates and reduction modulo powers of ℓ.

The argument uses integral corrections and their layerwise congruence. It applies to a linked frame without assuming any de Rham witnesses. To conclude that the same limit frame also satisfies the de Rham condition, the combined limit theorem assumes that each layer has de Rham witnesses and transfers those witnesses separately.
