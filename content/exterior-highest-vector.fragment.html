Why does the exterior tensor contain a compact highest vector of weight β?

The stopping-type calculation and the Dirac step of the layer bound use the following lemma. Coordinates are ordered by decreasing μ, with r positive and s negative ones, and a compact operator Eab is one whose two coordinates have the same sign. Let V be finite-dimensional and irreducible under the compact operators, with a highest-weight vector of weight L = μ+ρn−ρc for a strictly decreasing μ. Only the compact commutation relations are used, and V needs no positive form. The exterior algebra S has one generator eia for each positive coordinate i and negative coordinate a. The compact operators act on the generators as on the root vectors Eia, and on products as derivations. For positive a, b, the operator Eab sends eby to eay, and for negative a, b it sends eia to −eib.

Lemma. V ⊗ S contains a nonzero compact highest-weight vector x of weight β = L+θ−ρn = μ−ρc+θ. Here θ, written ρhol in the kernel chapter, has value s/2 on positive coordinates and −r/2 on negative ones.

The extreme vector. The wedge products eT over sets T of generators form a basis of S, and eT has weight ∑(i,a)∈T (ei−ea). For the form in which this basis is orthonormal, contraction is adjoint to exterior multiplication, so Eab* = Eba on S. Let T0 consist of the generators eia with a < i, whose negative coordinate comes first. The weight of eT0 is θ−ρn. On a positive coordinate c both equal the number of negative coordinates before c, and on a negative coordinate c both equal minus the number of positive coordinates after c. Every compact lowering operator kills eT0. For positive coordinates b < a, the operator Eab replaces a factor eby by eay. Then y < b < a, so eay is already a factor and the product vanishes. Two negative coordinates are handled in the same way. Thus eT0 is a lowest-weight vector of weight ν = θ−ρn.

Dominance. The weight L+ν is again dominant for the compact operators. Take coordinates a < b of the same sign with no coordinate of that sign between them, and let k be the number of coordinates of the opposite sign between them. Then ρc,a−ρc,b = 1, ρn,a−ρn,b = k and θa = θb, so La−Lb = μa−μb+k−1. This difference is a nonnegative integer m, by the 𝔰𝔩₂-string through the highest vector of V. Since μa > μb, we get m > k−1, hence m ≥ k and (L+ν)a−(L+ν)b = m−k ≥ 0. This is the only use of strict decrease.

The component lemma below, applied to V, to S and to its lowest vector eT0, now gives the tensor x of weight L+ν = β.

What does the lemma give for U(2,1)?

Take signs (+,−,+) and μ = (2,0,−1). Then ρc = ρn = (½,0,−½), so L = μ, while θ = (½,−1,½). The generators are e12 and e32, and T0 = {e32}, of weight (0,−1,1) = θ−ρn. The compact group U(2)×U(1) acts on the coordinates 1, 3 and on the coordinate 2. There V has highest weight ((2,−1),0), and β = (2,−1,0) is the weight ((2,0),−1). The two generators span the standard representation of U(2) tensored with the weight −1 of U(1). By the Pieri rule, (2,−1) ⊗ (1,0) contains the component of highest weight (2,0), so β occurs in V ⊗ S in exterior degree one. The dominance margin is m−k = 3−1 = 2 = β1−β3.

Why does a lowest weight added to a highest weight give a component of the tensor product?

The component lemma is the following. Let V be finite-dimensional and irreducible under the compact operators, with a highest-weight vector w of weight Λ. Let U be a finite-dimensional space with a positive form, on which the compact operators satisfy the same relations and Eab* = Eba. Suppose that f ∈ U is a nonzero lowest-weight vector of weight ν and that Λ+ν is dominant. Then V ⊗ U contains a nonzero highest-weight vector of weight Λ+ν. For compact groups this is the component of Parthasarathy, Ranga Rao and Varadarajan. The proof uses the commutation relations and two facts about a highest-weight vector w. The module it generates is spanned by products of lowering operators applied to w, and in it the weight Λ occurs only on the line ℂw. Indeed the commutation relations move raising operators to the right, and every nonempty product of lowering operators strictly lowers the weight.

An auxiliary module. Within each sign block the coordinates of Λ differ by integers, and so do those of ν, by 𝔰𝔩₂-strings through w and f. Hence the fractional part t of Λ+ν is constant on each block, and the integral part ⌊Λ+ν⌋ is dominant. Take the irreducible representation of U(r)×U(s) with highest weight ⌊Λ+ν⌋, and add ta to each diagonal operator Eaa. The commutation relations survive because t is constant on blocks. The result is a module T with a positive form for which Eab* = Eba, and with a highest-weight vector wT of weight Λ+ν. It need not integrate to the group, and only these relations are used. The construction of the terminal compact model gives an explicit realization of this integral representation.

A graph. Let U* carry the dual action. The functional ⟨·,f⟩ is a highest-weight vector of weight −ν, because Eab* = Eba turns the lowering equations for f into raising equations. Thus x0 = wT ⊗ ⟨·,f⟩ is a highest-weight vector of weight Λ in P = T ⊗ U*, where again Eab* = Eba for the product form. Let Γ ⊆ P ⊕ V be the submodule generated by (x0,w), a highest-weight vector of weight Λ. If Γ met 0 ⊕ V nontrivially, irreducibility of V would put (0,w) in Γ. By the second fact, (0,w) would then be a multiple of (x0,w), which is impossible because x0 ≠ 0. So Γ is the graph of an equivariant map from the submodule Z generated by x0 to V, sending x0 to w. The orthogonal complement of Z is a submodule because Eab* = Eba. The map therefore extends by zero to an equivariant Φ : P → V with Φ(x0) = w.

The tensor. Choose a basis (ei) of U with dual basis (ei*), and put xi = Φ(wT ⊗ ei*). The tensor ∑i xi ⊗ ei is the image of wT under the equivariant map T → V ⊗ U corresponding to Φ. It is therefore a highest-weight vector of weight Λ+ν. It is nonzero, because pairing its second factor with f gives ∑i ⟨ei,f⟩ xi = Φ(x0) = w.
