Why testing the first degree can miss an inadmissible parameter

For q = 3 and n = 2 the counting variables are (3,1). There are four sublattices of colength one and thirteen of colength two. Try the scaled candidate (b₀,b₁) = (4,−3/4). Its degree-one sum is 13/4, which passes the bound of four. At degree two the sum is 16−3+9/16 = 217/16, larger than thirteen. The second degree already rules out this candidate for a bounded spherical coefficient nonzero at the identity. For the trivial coefficient, the scaled variables are (3,1) up to order and all the inequalities are equalities. Its largest unscaled parameter attains √3.
