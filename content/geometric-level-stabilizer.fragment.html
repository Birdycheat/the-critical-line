How deep must a level be to fix the chosen automorphic form?

The automorphic form Φ carries a local character χ at a distinguished place w₀. An element of its level can therefore act by a scalar. To make Φ invariant we must also put its component at w₀ in the kernel of χ. We now choose a single depth that enforces both conditions before passing to the geometric tower.

Let E be the CM field, n the positive rank and p the auxiliary rational prime. At a finite place u write 𝒪u for the local ring of integers, 𝔪u for its maximal ideal and Ku0 = GLn(𝒪u). The chosen form specifies a finite set S containing w₀ and open subgroups Ku for u ∈ S. At w₀ put J = Kw₀. The character χ of J has open kernel. In its global level the similitude multiplier is a unit at every rational prime, the components at u ∈ S lie in Ku, and the components at places outside both S and its CM conjugate lie in Ku0. Right translation by a level element h multiplies Φ by χ(hw₀).

The comparison of lattices gives a surjection j from the finite adelic unitary similitude group to the group used for the level away from p. Its kernel consists of the elements whose matrix components away from p are all the identity. The comparison also supplies local changes of basis au, integral with integral inverse at almost every place and at all places above p. The choice of p keeps every place in S away from p. In the construction one also chooses p so that some nonzero value of Φ occurs at an argument integral above p. The stabilizer calculation below uses the disjointness of S from the places above p.

Uniform stabilization lemma. Put T = S ∪ {u ∣ au ∉ Ku0}. This is a finite set away from p. There is an integer e ≥ 0 with the following property. Let g be an adelic similitude whose image j(g) preserves the level-one lattice. In lattice coordinates write

Cu = au gu au−1.   Assume Cu ≡ I modulo 𝔪ue for every u ∈ T.

Then j(g) has a lift h such that its inverse-transpose transform belongs to the global level of Φ and has w₀-component in ker χ. In particular, writing h∨ for that transform, Φ(z h∨) = Φ(z) for every z. This involution inverts the similitude multiplier.

Choosing the depth. Preservation of the level-one lattice is equivalent to Cu ∈ Ku0 for every u away from p. In these coordinates the lattice consists of the integral local columns. Applying the lattice action and its inverse to the coordinate columns tests integrality of every entry of Cu and its inverse. Conversely, these entry conditions preserve every integral column in both directions.

Every open neighbourhood of I in GLn(Eu) contains all integral matrices sufficiently close to I modulo a power of 𝔪u. Indeed, powers of the maximal ideal are a neighbourhood basis at zero. If C ∈ Ku0 and C − I has entries in 𝔪ur, so does C−1 − I = −C−1(C − I). Thus both matrices approach I entry by entry as r increases. This gives the assertion in the topology of the group of units. At each u ∈ T apply this observation to the conditions that au−1C au be integral with integral inverse and, if u ∈ S, that its inverse transpose belong to Ku. These conditions define an open neighbourhood of I. Conjugation and inverse transpose are continuous, and the valuation ring is open, so entrywise integrality for a matrix and its inverse is open. Call the resulting depth ru. At w₀ use also the preimage of ker χ under the same conjugation followed by inverse transpose. This kernel is open in the open subgroup J, hence in the full matrix group. Write r₀ for the resulting depth. We may take

e = r₀ + maxu ∈ T ru.

For a local example, let a be diagonal with entries ϖ and 1, where ϖ is a uniformizer, and put C = I + ϖrE12 with r ≥ 1. Here E12 has its only nonzero entry, a one, in position (1, 2). Undoing the change of basis gives I + ϖr−1E12. Its inverse transpose is I − ϖr−1E21. A condition requiring congruence to I modulo ϖ² in the original coordinates thus requires r ≥ 3 along this family.

Constructing the lift. The level-one condition controls the coordinates away from p. To prescribe those above p, let ε be the rational finite adèle equal to 1 at p and 0 elsewhere. Its image over E is fixed by CM conjugation since it has the same value at conjugate places. Apply x ↦ εx + (1 − ε)I to the matrix of g, and apply the scalar version to its multiplier. This defines a homomorphism πp of the similitude group. Indeed ε² = ε and ε(1 − ε) = 0, so multiplication and the similitude relation hold separately on the two components. The new element agrees with g above p and equals the identity elsewhere. Consequently

h = g πp(g)−1,   j(h) = j(g),   hu = I above p,   hu = gu away from p.

The two lifts have the same image because πp(g) lies in the kernel of j. At u ∈ T the chosen depth now puts h∨u in Ku0, in Ku when prescribed, and in ker χ at w₀. At u away from both p and T, the two integral matrices au and Cu belong to the group Ku0. Therefore hu = au−1Cuau and its inverse transpose belong to that group as well. Above p the inverse transpose is the identity.

It remains to check the multiplier of h∨. At each place of E, the unitary similitude relation expresses the multiplier times I as a product of the conjugate transpose matrix from the conjugate place and the local matrix. Both factors have integral entries. A diagonal entry therefore shows that the multiplier is integral, since the rank is positive. Applying the calculation to (h∨)−1 proves that its reciprocal is integral. Every rational prime has a place of E above it. At that place the additive valuation of a rational local scalar is its rational valuation multiplied by the positive ramification index. Thus the multiplier has valuation zero at every finite rational prime. All the global level conditions now hold. The transformation law of Φ evaluates the remaining scalar at w₀ as χ(h∨w₀) = 1, proving invariance. The Stacks Project, Lemma 10.36.17, lying over for integral extensions.
