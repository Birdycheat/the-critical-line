The integral Weierstrass formal group law and its invariant logarithm

How does one construct the formal addition series FW(U₁, U₂) ∈ R[[U₁, U₂]] of a general Weierstrass equation over an arbitrary commutative ring R—including rings of mixed characteristic or rings with torsion—without ever dividing by U₂ − U₁, and how does one establish its associativity without expanding a combinatorial configuration of four secant lines? Two observations bypass both difficulties. First, in the local coordinates T = −x/y and w = −1/y at infinity, the cubic equation becomes a contractive fixed-point equation for w, and the secant slope between two parameters U₁ and U₂ is written directly by expanding monomial differences term by term without division. Second, differentiating Vieta’s cubic secant relation along the invariant vector fields fw(Ui)∂Ui proves invariance of the differential ωW(FW)∂UiFW = ωW(Ui); over the fraction field of the universal polynomial ring ℤ[A₁, A₂, A₃, A₄, A₆], integrating ωW identifies logW(FW(U₁, U₂)) = logW(U₁) + logW(U₂), and specializing the five universal coefficients to R transfers commutativity, associativity, and the formal inverse iW to every commutative ring R at once.

1. Contractive fixed point and regularized coordinates. Let R be any commutative ring and let W be a Weierstrass equation y² + a₁xy + a₃y = x³ + a₂x² + a₄x + a₆ with coefficients a₁, a₂, a₃, a₄, a₆ ∈ R. On the ideal TR[[T]] of formal power series with zero constant term, consider the operator Φ(v) = T³ + a₁Tv + a₂T²v + a₃v² + a₄Tv² + a₆v³. For any a, b ∈ TR[[T]], factoring Φ(a) − Φ(b) = (a − b)[a₁T + a₂T² + a₃(a + b) + a₄T(a + b) + a₆(a² + ab + b²)] multiplies a − b by a series in TR[[T]], sending every difference divisible by Tk into one divisible by Tk+1. Iterating Φ from 0 stabilizes coefficient by coefficient and yields a unique series wW(T) ∈ R[[T]] with wW(0) = 0 satisfying

wW(T) = T³ + a₁TwW(T) + a₂T²wW(T) + a₃wW(T)² + a₄TwW(T)² + a₆wW(T)³.

Inspecting degrees modulo T², T³, and T⁴ gives wW(T) = T³wU(T) with wU(T) ∈ 1 + TR[[T]]. Because wU(0) = 1 is invertible in R, its multiplicative inverse defines the regularized coordinate series A(T) = xS(T) = wU(T)−1 ∈ 1 + TR[[T]] and yS(T) = −xS(T) (representing T²x and T³y). Likewise −1 + a₁T + a₃wW(T) ∈ −1 + TR[[T]] is a unit in R[[T]], defining the formal negation series iW(T) = T(−1 + a₁T + a₃wW(T))−1 ∈ −T + T²R[[T]], while evaluating the vertical partial derivative of f(u, w) = w − u³ − a₁uw − a₂u²w − a₃w² − a₄uw² − a₆w³ along w = wW(T) produces the unit series fw(T) = 1 − a₁T − a₂T² − 2a₃wW(T) − 2a₄TwW(T) − 3a₆wW(T)² ∈ 1 + TR[[T]] and the invariant differential series ωW(T) = fw(T)−1 ∈ 1 + TR[[T]].

2. Division-free secant slope and Vieta’s third intersection. In two independent formal variables U₁, U₂ over R, define the secant slope and intercept series by λ(U₁, U₂) = ∑e₀,e₁≥0 ([Te₀+e₁+1]wW) U₁e₀U₂e₁ ∈ (U₁, U₂)²R[[U₁, U₂]] and ν(U₁, U₂) = wW(U₁) − λ(U₁, U₂)U₁ ∈ (U₁, U₂)³R[[U₁, U₂]]. Since (U₂ − U₁)∑e₀+e₁=n−1 U₁e₀U₂e₁ = U₂n − U₁n for all n ≥ 1 and wW(0) = 0, the identity λ(U₁, U₂)(U₂ − U₁) = wW(U₂) − wW(U₁) holds in R[[U₁, U₂]] without division, so the secant line w(u) = λu + ν passes through both (U₁, wW(U₁)) and (U₂, wW(U₂)). Substituting w = λu + ν into −f(u, w) yields a cubic polynomial P(u) ∈ R[[U₁, U₂]][u] with invertible leading coefficient c₃ = 1 + a₂λ + a₄λ² + a₆λ³ ∈ 1 + (U₁, U₂)²R[[U₁, U₂]] and quadratic coefficient c₂ = a₁λ + a₂ν + a₃λ² + 2a₄λν + 3a₆λ²ν ∈ (U₁, U₂)²R[[U₁, U₂]]. Subtracting P(U₁) = 0 factors P(u) = (u − U₁)Q₂(u) in R[[U₁, U₂]][u], and evaluating at u = U₂ gives (U₂ − U₁)Q₂(U₂) = P(U₂) = 0; since U₂ − U₁ is a non-zero-divisor in R[[U₁, U₂]] over any commutative ring R (comparing coefficients of U₁a+1U₂b by induction on b ≥ 0), Q₂(U₂) = 0 and hence P(u) = c₃(u − U₁)(u − U₂)(u − u₃) in R[[U₁, U₂]][u], where Vieta’s sum of roots gives the third abscissa u₃ = −U₁ − U₂ − c₂c₃−1 ∈ (U₁, U₂)R[[U₁, U₂]]. Evaluating P(u₃) = 0 shows that w₃ = λu₃ + ν satisfies the contractive fixed-point equation for the zero-constant series u₃, so w₃ = wW(u₃) by uniqueness, and applying formal negation defines the addition series

FW(U₁, U₂) = iW(u₃) = u₃(−1 + a₁u₃ + a₃w₃)−1 = U₁ + U₂ + O((U₁, U₂)²).

3. Flow of the third point, invariant logarithm, and universal base change. Over any integral domain—such as a field K or the universal polynomial ring Z = ℤ[A₁, A₂, A₃, A₄, A₆]—differentiating the secant factorization along the invariant vector fields transports ωW across u₃ and FW.

Derivation along the secant line and sign reversal under negation

Over an integral domain, apply the derivation D₁ = fw(U₁)∂U₁ (holding the polynomial indeterminate u fixed, so D₁u = 0, D₁U₁ = fw(U₁), and D₁U₂ = 0) to f(u, λu + ν) = −c₃(u − U₁)(u − U₂)(u − u₃). The chain rule in the second argument gives fw(u, λu + ν)(uD₁λ + D₁ν) = −(D₁c₃)(u − U₁)(u − U₂)(u − u₃) + c₃fw(U₁)(u − U₂)(u − u₃) + c₃(u − U₁)(u − U₂)(D₁u₃). Evaluating at u = U₂ and cancelling the unit fw(U₂) yields D₁ν = −U₂D₁λ; evaluating at u = U₁ and cancelling the unit fw(U₁) together with U₁ − U₂ ≠ 0 yields D₁λ = c₃(U₁ − u₃), hence u₃D₁λ + D₁ν = (u₃ − U₂)D₁λ = −c₃(u₃ − U₁)(u₃ − U₂). Evaluating at u = u₃ (where λu₃ + ν = w₃ = wW(u₃)) and cancelling the nonzero product c₃(u₃ − U₁)(u₃ − U₂) (whose factors have linear parts −2U₁ − U₂ and −U₁ − 2U₂) gives fw(U₁)∂U₁u₃ = −fw(u₃, w₃), and symmetry in (U₁, U₂) gives fw(U₂)∂U₂u₃ = −fw(u₃, w₃).

At U₂ = 0 one has ν(U₁, 0) = 0 and λ₀ = λ(U₁, 0) = U₁²wU(U₁). Dividing the fixed-point equation for wW(U₁) = U₁λ₀ by U₁ reads c₃(U₁, 0)U₁² + c₂(U₁, 0)U₁ = λ₀ with c₂(U₁, 0) = a₁λ₀ + a₃λ₀², so c₃(U₁, 0)U₁² = −λ₀(−1 + a₁U₁ + a₃wW(U₁)); since c₃(U₁, 0)U₁u₃(U₁, 0) = −λ₀, cancelling c₃(U₁, 0)U₁ identifies u₃(U₁, 0) = iW(U₁). Setting U₂ = 0 in fw(U₁)∂U₁u₃ = −fw(u₃, w₃) therefore gives ωW(iW(T))iW′(T) = −ωW(T), and applying the chain rule to FW(U₁, U₂) = iW(u₃) concludes ωW(FW)∂UiFW = (−ωW(u₃))(−ωW(Ui)ωW(u₃)−1) = ωW(Ui) for i = 1, 2.

Over any field K of characteristic zero, define the formal logarithm by termwise integration logW(T) = ∫₀T ωW(s) ds = ∑n≥1 ([Tn−1]ωW)Tn/n ∈ T + T²K[[T]], with compositional inverse expW(z) ∈ z + z²K[[z]]. By the formal chain rule, ωW(FW)∂UiFW = ωW(Ui) and ωW(iW(T))iW′(T) = −ωW(T) force ∂U₁, ∂U₂(logW(FW(U₁, U₂)) − logW(U₁) − logW(U₂)) = 0 and (d/dT)(logW(iW(T)) + logW(T)) = 0; since FW(0, 0) = 0, iW(0) = 0, and K has characteristic zero,

logW(FW(U₁, U₂)) = logW(U₁) + logW(U₂),   logW(iW(T)) = −logW(T).

On the short model y² = x³ − (g₂/4)x − g₃/4 (where a₁ = a₂ = a₃ = 0, a₄ = −g₂/4, a₆ = −g₃/4), the Weierstrass uniformization x(z) = ℘(z), y(z) = ½℘′(z) satisfies dx/(2y) = dz, while differentiating f(T, wW(T)) = 0 identifies d(T/wW)/(−2/wW) = ωW(T) dT; thus the analytic coordinate ratio T(z) = −x(z)/y(z) = z + O(z⁵) satisfies logW(T(z)) = z and coincides with expW(z), matching logW with the curve logarithm λ above. Finally, embed the universal polynomial ring Z = ℤ[A₁, A₂, A₃, A₄, A₆] into its characteristic-zero fraction field K₀ = Frac(Z). Applying expW to the logarithm identities over K₀ proves FW(T, 0) = FW(0, T) = T, commutativity FW(U₁, U₂) = FW(U₂, U₁), associativity FW(FW(U₁, U₂), U₃) = FW(U₁, FW(U₂, U₃)), and the inverse law FW(T, iW(T)) = 0 = FW(iW(T), T) over K₀, hence already in Z[[U₁, U₂, U₃]] where all coefficients of FW and iW lie. Specializing (A₁, A₂, A₃, A₄, A₆) ↦ (a₁, a₂, a₃, a₄, a₆) along the unique ring homomorphism Z → R establishes that FW is a commutative formal group law with formal inverse iW over every commutative ring R. In particular, for any subring R ⊆ K of a characteristic-zero field containing the coefficients of W—such as the closed unit ball of an ultrametric valued field K when |ai| ≤ 1, and on the short model y² = x³ − (g₂/4)x − g₃/4 whenever |g₂| ≤ 1, |g₃| ≤ 1, and |2| = 1—the series FW, iW, every iterated multiplication series [n]W(T) (including [p](T) in the height-two descent above), and formal halving [½]W(T) = ([2]W)−1(T) when 2 ∈ R× all have coefficients in R and match addition, negation, and scalar multiplication in logW.

Universal two-variable chord factorization on the cubic

Clearing denominators in the affine abscissa difference x(U₁) − x(U₂) = U₁−2A(U₁) − U₂−2A(U₂) produces the cross-difference U₂²A(U₁) − U₁²A(U₂). Why does this two-variable series factor over any commutative ring R as the exact product FW(U₁, U₂)FW(U₁, iW(U₂)) times an integral unit series c(U₁, U₂) of constant term −1? Geometrically, two points share the same Weierstrass abscissa precisely when they are opposite or equal; algebraically, Vieta’s product of the three roots U₁, U₂, u₃ of the secant cubic expresses the intercept ν in terms of U₁U₂u₃ while (U₂ − U₁)ν produces the cross-difference multiplied by U₁U₂, allowing U₁U₂ to be cancelled inside the universal integral domain Z[[U₁, U₂]].

Let R be any commutative ring and let W be a Weierstrass equation over R with regularized coordinate series A(T) = xS(T) = wU(T)−1 ∈ 1 + TR[[T]], formal group law FW(U₁, U₂), and formal inverse iW(T). Then there exists a formal power series c(U₁, U₂) ∈ R[[U₁, U₂]] with constant term c(0, 0) = −1 such that

U₂²A(U₁) − U₁²A(U₂) = c(U₁, U₂) FW(U₁, U₂) FW(U₁, iW(U₂)).

To prove the identity, work first over the universal polynomial ring Z = ℤ[A₁, A₂, A₃, A₄, A₆], so that Z[[U₁, U₂]] is an integral domain. Because FW(U₁, iW(U₁)) = 0, subtracting U₁i+j from each monomial U₁iU₂j degree by degree factors out the diagonal as FW(U₁, iW(U₂)) = (U₂ − U₁)Q(U₁, U₂) in Z[[U₁, U₂]]; since FW(U₁, iW(U₂)) = U₁ − U₂ + O((U₁, U₂)²), one has Q(0, 0) = −1 and −Q ∈ 1 + (U₁, U₂)Z[[U₁, U₂]]. Next, evaluating the secant factorization P(u) = c₃(u − U₁)(u − U₂)(u − u₃) at u = 0 equates P(0) = −ν(1 − A₃ν − A₆ν²) to −c₃U₁U₂u₃, so c₃U₁U₂u₃ = ν(1 − A₃ν − A₆ν²). Using ν = wW(U₁) − λU₁, λ(U₂ − U₁) = wW(U₂) − wW(U₁), and wW(T) = T³A(T)−1, one computes (U₂ − U₁)ν = U₂wW(U₁) − U₁wW(U₂) = U₁U₂A(U₁)−1A(U₂)−1(U₁²A(U₂) − U₂²A(U₁)).

Multiplying c₃U₁U₂u₃ = ν(1 − A₃ν − A₆ν²) by U₂ − U₁, substituting the identity for (U₂ − U₁)ν, and cancelling the nonzero element U₁U₂ in the integral domain Z[[U₁, U₂]] gives U₂²A(U₁) − U₁²A(U₂) = −A(U₁)A(U₂)c₃(1 − A₃ν − A₆ν²)−1u₃(U₂ − U₁). Finally, u₃ = −(1 − A₁u₃ − A₃w₃)FW(U₁, U₂) and U₂ − U₁ = −(−Q(U₁, U₂))−1FW(U₁, iW(U₂)), while A(U₁), A(U₂), c₃, 1 − A₃ν − A₆ν², 1 − A₁u₃ − A₃w₃, and −Q(U₁, U₂) all belong to 1 + (U₁, U₂)Z[[U₁, U₂]] and are therefore invertible in Z[[U₁, U₂]]. Setting c(U₁, U₂) = −A(U₁)A(U₂)c₃(1 − A₃ν − A₆ν²)−1(1 − A₁u₃ − A₃w₃)(−Q(U₁, U₂))−1 ∈ Z[[U₁, U₂]] gives c(0, 0) = −1 and proves the universal factorization over Z. Specializing Z → R extends the identity to every commutative ring R; in particular, when R is the closed unit ball of a characteristic-zero ultrametric valued field K and W has coefficients in R, the logarithm homomorphism identifies FW and iW with F(X, Y) = l−1(l(X) + l(Y)) and [−1]l(T) = l−1(−l(T)), supplying the integral unit series c used in cancelling the poles of the sigma parallelogram above.
