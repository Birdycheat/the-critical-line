How many perpendicular line lattices can a fixed level distinguish?

The hyperplane estimate needs a smaller gluing allowance than one obtains by counting arbitrary submodules. Keep the orthogonal decomposition V = U ⊕ W, with U a hyperplane and W a line. Fix integral full intersections P ⊂ U and Q ⊂ W. Count full integral lattices L with precisely these intersections. Assume a nonzero algebraic integer T ∈ R = 𝒪E, kills L∨/L. The form is totally definite and the CM extension is unramified at finite places, as in the row count. Write q for the projection onto W.

The projection Q′ = qL can be larger than Q. To bound its possibilities, record its dual C in W. A vector of W pairs integrally with qL exactly when it pairs integrally with L, since it is orthogonal to U. Thus C = L∨ ∩ W. We have Q ⊂ C by integrality, and TC ⊂ L ∩ W = Q by the level. The label therefore satisfies

Q ⊆ C = (qL)∨ ⊆ T−1Q,   Q′ = C∨.

Every finite quotient between two lattices on a line is cyclic over R, by the rank-one case of the generator bound. Fix y generating T−1Q/Q. For each intermediate C, its quotient by Q has one generator, a multiple ry modulo Q. Replacing r by r + Ts adds an element of Q. All candidates are therefore covered by the |R/(T)| residues of r. This is N((T)), the absolute norm of the level ideal. The cyclic modules here are finite quotients. The line lattice Q itself can be a nonprincipal ideal in line coordinates.

Lean correspondence
  • One relative generator for a rank-one lattice quotient
  • The level interval and its generator modulo the smaller lattice
  • Residues of the level cover every intermediate line lattice
  • Dual of the projected line as an intersection with the ambient dual
  • The level kills the quotient for the dual line label
Why does just one hyperplane residue finish the gluing?

Fix the label C of a candidate lattice. Its dual recovers the projected line lattice Q′. Choose once a vector yC generating Q′/Q. Because Q′ is the image of L, some a ∈ U makes a + yC ∈ L. Pairing this vector with P shows a ∈ P∨. Two choices for that lift differ in L ∩ U = P. The residue of a modulo P is therefore uniquely determined once L and the line generator have been chosen.

L = (P ⊕ Q) + R(a + yC).

Indeed the right side is contained in L. For the reverse inclusion, take x ∈ L. Subtract a suitable multiple of the chosen lift so that its line projection belongs to Q. Subtract that element of Q as well. What remains lies in P. This proves the identity even when Q′ = Q, in which case one may choose the zero generator. Counting the two labels now gives

# gluings ≤ N((T)) · |P∨/P| = t²d²  in the row.

Here the row field has degree 2f, T is its specified rational level prime, t = Tf and the hyperplane discriminant order is d². The argument uses the annihilator T and the one-dimensional perpendicular. Counting both discriminant modules by all possible generating tuples would lose this sharper exponent.

In the plane with Gram matrix diag(1,2), keep u = (1,1), w = (−2,1), P = Ru and Q = Rw. Take T = 2, which kills the ambient dual quotient. The projected lattice is Q′ = (1/3)Q. Because h(w,w) = 6, its dual is C = (1/2)Q. A generator for Q′/Q is w/3, with lift (w−u)/3 = (−1,0). Together with (1,1) this lift generates R². The two data used by the proof are the line label (1/2)Q and the hyperplane residue −u/3 modulo P. In this example Q′ itself is outside the level interval, since w/3 does not lie in (1/2)Q. Dualizing the projected lattice before counting is essential.

Lean correspondence
  • A fixed generator for each projected line and a residue in the hyperplane dual
  • An integral lift of the line generator pairs integrally with the hyperplane lattice
  • Recovering the entire glued lattice from one lift
  • Product of the number of level labels and the hyperplane discriminant order
  • Using the sharper rank-one fibre estimate in the hyperplane mass
