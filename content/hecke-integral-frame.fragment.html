Which evaluations keep a Hecke error small?

At weight zero a Hecke operator is a finite sum of right translations. If all its coefficients have ℓ-adic absolute value at most one, the ultrametric inequality bounds the sum by its largest term. At higher weight, translating the point also changes the vector on which the form is evaluated. We construct a family of test vectors and compute how small each Hecke coefficient must be to compensate for this change.

Let G be the definite unitary group of rank a ≥ 1 attached to the CM field E, viewed over ℚ by restriction of scalars. Fix an identification of the algebraic closure of ℚℓ with ℂ, and use it to read all absolute values ℓ-adically, with |ℓ| = ℓ−1. Realize the weight representation ρ on a finite-dimensional space V of polynomials. Write ‖x‖ for the largest absolute value of a coefficient of x, taking ‖0‖ = 0, and let Λ consist of the polynomials in V with ‖x‖ ≤ 1. A form f(x, g) is linear in x ∈ V and evaluated at an adelic point g.

To transport Λ, record two matrices at each infinite place. The first, c∞(g), reads the archimedean component at the chosen complex embedding. For the second, κ(g), read the finite component at the place above ℓ induced by that embedding and the fixed field identification, then transport its entries back to ℂ. Both constructions respect multiplication. They agree on rational group elements, and κ takes the value 1 on the archimedean subgroup. Put

M(g) = c∞(g)−1κ(g),   eg,x(f) = f(ρ(M(g))x, g),   x ∈ Λ.

Integral evaluation theorem. These evaluations detect every nonzero algebraic form, and each form has a finite bound on all its evaluations. Let λτ,i be the weight entries over all complex embeddings τ of E, with the dual weight at the conjugate embedding. Put D = a ∑τ,i |λτ,i|, using ordinary absolute values of integers in this sum. Consider a Hecke operator with finite support on the finite adelic group and coefficient t(h) at a translation h. For each such h, choose d ≥ 0 so that ℓd clears the denominators of the entries of both h and h−1 at every place over ℓ. If |t(h)| ≤ ℓ−dD for every term, the operator preserves every common bound on the evaluations.

Proof. First we estimate the effect of a matrix on the weight. Suppose every entry of A and A−1 has norm at most B ≥ 1 in every archimedean matrix block, using the transported ℓ-adic norm. Substitution of linear forms into a monomial of degree m gives coefficients of norm at most Bm, by expanding the product. In the block of weight λ₁ ≥ ⋯ ≥ λa, the polynomial part has degree at most ∑i(λi − λa), the sum of its column degrees. The determinant twist contributes at most Ba|λa| by expanding the determinant of A or of its inverse. For each i, (λi − λa) + |λa| ≤ 2∑j|λj|. There is one polynomial block for each conjugate pair of embeddings. The conjugate weight reverses and negates the entries, so the pair contributes 2a∑j|λj| to D, enough for the polynomial degree and the determinant twist together. Adding over the blocks therefore gives ‖ρ(A)x‖ ≤ BD for x ∈ Λ.

For example, take rank two, one infinite place and weight (0, −1). The polynomial part has degree one. The determinant of a two-by-two matrix, or its inverse, costs at most two more powers of B. The two embeddings carry weights (0, −1) and (1, 0), so the uniform formula gives D = 4. Estimating the polynomial and the determinant separately gives B³, within the uniform allowance B⁴.

For detection, take a point g and vector v at which f(v, g) ≠ 0. Multiply ρ(M(g))−1v by a sufficiently high power ℓk to put all its polynomial coefficients in the unit ball. The corresponding evaluation is ℓkf(v, g), which is still nonzero.

For boundedness, take a compact open finite level C fixing f. Borel’s class-number theorem gives finitely many representatives s such that every g has the form γsuk, with γ rational, u archimedean and k ∈ C. Indeed, intersecting the standard finite level with C gives a subgroup of finite index in the standard level, so each standard class splits into finitely many classes. A. Borel, Some finiteness properties of adele groups over number fields, Theorem 5.1.

Left invariance under γ, covariance under u, and invariance under k remove these three factors. In the specified order the matrices satisfy c∞(u)M(γsuk) = M(s)κ(k), which gives eg,x(f) = f(ρ(M(s))ρ(κ(k))x, s). The single exponent d clearing all k ∈ C exists because the denominator conditions form an increasing open cover of the compact group C. Thus ‖ρ(κ(k))x‖ ≤ ℓdD. At each representative s, the functional y ↦ f(ρ(M(s))y, s) is bounded in the coefficient norm. Indeed, coefficient functionals separate points of the finite-dimensional space V, so finitely many form a basis of its dual. Taking the largest bound over the representatives proves the assertion.

Finally take a translation h in the operator. Its archimedean component is the identity, so M(gh) = M(g)κ(h) with the product understood in each block. To evaluate at gh using the vector that was transported to g, apply the inverse change of coordinates and absorb the Hecke coefficient into the vector. This gives

yh = t(h)ρ(κ(h)−1)x,   ‖yh‖ ≤ ℓ−dDℓdD = 1.

By linearity in the test vector, eg,x(Tf) = ∑h egh,yh(f). Every summand is again one of our evaluations. The ultrametric inequality now preserves their common bound, even for a function that is assumed only linear in its weight argument. This allows the same estimate to be applied to every residual in the polynomial approximation.

For an operator supported away from ℓ we can take d = 0. At places over ℓ, any single finite sum can be made integral for this estimate by multiplying it by ℓk. Choose k large enough that ℓ−k|t(h)| ≤ ℓ−dD at each of its finitely many terms. The same multiplier scales its eigenvalue. This accounts for the factors attached separately to the auxiliary Hecke coordinates.
