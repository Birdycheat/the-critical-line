Where does the eigenvector on a definite class come from?

Let Φ be the original automorphic vector in the odd-rank CM unitary datum of the spectral test. Its prescribed cuspidal type is at a split place w₀ above a good prime. No place above p lies in the original level support, and there is an argument g₀ with Φ(g₀) ≠ 0 whose components above p are integral with integral inverses. The quadratic element in the arithmetic datum and its distinguished generator generate the same subfield of the CM field. Choose the auxiliary prime r outside the prescribed finite set and distinct from p, and fix the half-set above it. The argument works for every admissible multiple N whose principal subgroup K₀ stabilizes the data with trivial type character. Write e for the normalized projector onto the prescribed type. We seek one vector fixed by e that carries all the observed eigenvalues.

The frame projection j forgets the factors above p. Its multiplicative section s gives them identity components. Write ϑ for inverse transpose on the matrix component and inversion on the multiplier. On the group away from p set φ(h) = Φ(ϑ(s(h))). If g is integral with integral inverse above p, factor it as s(j(g))k. The factor k is trivial away from p and integral with integral inverse above p. Its image under ϑ acts through the original level and has component one at w₀. Thus the right transformation law for Φ gives φ(j(g)) = Φ(ϑ(g)). The involution ϑ preserves the integral group above p. In particular φ(j(ϑ(g₀))) = Φ(g₀) ≠ 0. This is where integrality at that particular argument is needed.

Right multiplication by the transported type group acts on φ through its type character. Its restriction to K₀ is trivial, and averaging against the inverse character over the finite quotient K/K₀ gives eφ = φ. Here K denotes the type subgroup and each coset has weight 1/[K:K₀]. There are two kinds of observation. An eligible deck transformation stabilizes the original datum and has eigenvalue one. For a spherical observation at a free split place w above a prime different from r, assume its local test subgroup stabilizes the datum. Lifting the right cosets recovers the original spherical sum. If B is the Satake multiset and 0 ≤ i ≤ n, the eigenvalue is idx(g) (Nw)i(n−i)/2 ei(B) κ(w)−i. Here ei is the elementary symmetric polynomial, idx(g) counts the finer level cosets per summand, and κ is the scalar character of the original datum used in the spherical normalization. The observations may form an infinite family. The same φ satisfies each identity.

How does the vector extend across the missing local group?

Use the standard definite Hermitian form for the frame. Its rational group Γ embeds by (ι₁,ι₂) in H₁ × H₂, where H₁ is the group away from p and H₂ is the local factor at p. Denote its compact level there by Kp and its rational action on the coefficient space by ξ₀. The covariance of Φ descends to φ as soon as ι₂(γ) belongs to Kp. For such γ one has φ(ι₁(γ)h) = ξ₀(γ)φ(h). This restricted covariance is enough to extend φ.

F(h,t) = ξ₀(γ)φ(ι₁(γ)−1h) whenever ι₂(γ)−1t ∈ Kp.

Give F value zero on every coset tKp outside the rational orbit. If γ and δ both represent the same coset, ι₂(δ−1γ) ∈ Kp. Applying the restricted covariance to δ−1γ identifies their two values. An element α of Γ sends the representing choice γ to αγ, giving rational covariance for F. Right Kp-invariance follows because the formula only depends on the coset, and the identity coset recovers φ.

The Hecke sums use right translates in the first variable. On a coset represented by γ, such a sum is ξ₀(γ) times the sum for φ evaluated at ι₁(γ)−1h. It vanishes on all other cosets. Thus eF = F and every observed operator retains its eigenvalue. Nonvanishing follows by evaluating on the identity coset.

A four-coset local group

Let Γ = H₁ = {1,−1}, take H₂ = {1,−1}² and embed γ as (γ,(γ,1)). Use trivial levels and trivial coefficients. For φ(h) = h, the extension gives F(h,(t,1)) = th and F(h,(t,−1)) = 0. Replacing h by −h changes the sign on each occupied coset. On the empty cosets the same equation reads 0 = −0. Thus the nonzero function F is an eigenvector with eigenvalue −1.

Why does the spectral sum contain this eigenvector?

The trace sum uses one fixed representative for each definite class, while the form F was constructed on a particular Hermitian datum. Let β give that datum and β′ the representative selected for its class c₀. Their equivalence supplies a rational unit y and a positive rational scalar a with β′ = a yβy*. Put Y = (y,1). If (x,t) and (x′,t′) are the two adelic trivializations, the component away from p of the change d is (x′yx−1, t′a−1t−1). Together with its local component this conjugates the first rational embedding to the second. In formulas, ι′(YγY−1) = dι(γ)d−1.

F′(h) = ξ(Y)F(d−1h).

The conjugation identity proves left covariance for F′. Its right level and every right Hecke sum commute with the change of argument. Multiplication by ξ(Y) is invertible and left translation is a bijection, so F′ is still nonzero. Place F′ in the c₀ component of the class-indexed space and put zero in all other components. The actions are componentwise, hence this vector has all the prescribed eigenvalues and is fixed by e. On that vector the joint projector is the identity. The positive-power argument then gives a nonzero sum of traces, even when a trace on an individual class can vanish.

Which polynomial gives the weight of a definite class?

The spectral test uses the trace of a rational similitude on the dual coefficient space. Write A for its invertible matrix over the CM field E, ν for its rational multiplier, and P for its minimal polynomial. The weight is tested when P is separable of degree n, the size of A. By Cayley–Hamilton, P divides the characteristic polynomial. Both are monic of degree n, so they coincide. In the coefficient field of the spectral test identify the two fields by the prescribed isomorphism and form the monic reciprocal

Q(X) = XnP(1/X)/P(0).

Invertibility gives P(0) ≠ 0. At a chosen complex embedding τ above an infinite place v, let t₁,…,tn be the roots of P. They are nonzero and distinct. The similitude equation reads ĀtBA = νB for an invertible form matrix B. Thus at the conjugate embedding the roots of P are ν/ti. The roots of Q at the two embeddings are respectively 1/ti and ti/ν. This order matters in the character formula.

Write wv for the integral highest weight at v. Choose a nonnegative integer mv such that each entry of wv + mv is nonnegative. The two polynomial weights are the constant tuple (mv,…,mv) at τ and wv + mv at its conjugate. For Dv = ∑i(wv,i + mv), the product of their Schur polynomials evaluates to

s(m,…,m)(t−1) sw+m(t/ν) = ν−Dv χw(t).

Here m and w refer to this place. The scalar ν contributes its power by homogeneity. Adding m to every entry of a highest weight multiplies its character by detm, as one sees by taking the same factor from every column of the Weyl numerator. The rectangular character at t−1 contributes the inverse of exactly that determinant. This leaves the ordinary character χw on the roots of A.

In the dual coefficient action the argument is the inverse transpose of A and the multiplier is ν−1. Taking the dual replaces that argument by its inverse inside the trace, and transposition preserves the characteristic polynomial. The tensor product over infinite places multiplies the traces, while their powers of ν add. The result is the product of the prescribed Schur polynomials on the roots of Q over all complex embeddings. This proves the weight comparison used once the definite vectors are placed in the spectral test.

The two root lists for a similitude over the Gaussian field

Take the diagonal entries 5, 3 + 4i, 4 + 3i for the standard definite form over ℚ(i). Each has squared absolute value 25, so ν = 25. The roots of Q in the identity embedding are 1/5, (3 − 4i)/25 and (4 − 3i)/25. In the conjugate embedding the last two imaginary signs reverse. For w = (1,0,0) take m = 1. Its two partitions are (1,1,1) and (2,1,1). Their characters are the determinant and the determinant times the trace, respectively. The determinant factors cancel, leaving (12 + 7i)/25⁴. Interchanging the embeddings would conjugate this value. The minimal polynomial splits over ℚ(i). This example checks the weight before the separate orbital cancellation for reducible classes.
