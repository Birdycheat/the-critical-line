Which parts of a tangent matrix can the weights distinguish?

Let K be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Write V for the space of ρ and 𝔤 for the tangent Lie algebra of the Zariski closure of its image. Suppose that every matrix in 𝔤 has minimal polynomial of degree at most m. Fix X ∈ 𝔤 with m distinct lacunary eigenvalues e₁, …, em. Its minimal polynomial must have exactly these simple roots. Write pi for the projection onto the ei-eigenspace along the others. This eigenspace can have dimension greater than one.

Proposition. For every Y ∈ 𝔤 the block piYpj belongs to 𝔤 whenever i ≠ j. Each diagonal block of Y acts by a scalar on its eigenspace.

To distinguish the off-diagonal blocks, take the commutator with X. In a block mapping eigenspace j to eigenspace i, multiplication by X on the left gives the output weight, and multiplication on the right gives the input weight. Thus

[X, piYpj] = (ei − ej) piYpj.

Each nonzero difference belongs to a unique ordered pair. Indeed, an equality ei − ej = ek − el says that the pairs (i, l) and (k, j) have the same mean. If the pairs share an index, cancellation and distinctness give the same ordered difference. Otherwise their supports are disjoint. A repeated entry in either pair has the mean of its singleton support. In every case disjointness would contradict lacunarity. For example, weights 0, 1, 4 produce the six distinct nonzero differences ±1, ±3 and ±4.

Why is the tangent space closed under this bracket? For a tangent Y let DY differentiate a polynomial at M along M(I + sY). As in the formal exponential argument, translation shows that each such derivation preserves the ideal of relations of the image. On a coordinate entry of M, the commutator of the two derivations for Y and Z takes the value of that entry in M(YZ − ZY). Derivations are determined by the coordinates, so this commutator is the derivation for YZ − ZY. It still preserves the ideal. Evaluating at the identity gives the tangent condition for the matrix bracket.

Let Δ be the set of all differences, including zero. For d = ei − ej ≠ 0 form the polynomial Ld(T) = ∏c∈Δ, c≠d (T−c)/(d−c). It takes value one at d and zero at every other difference. Decompose Y as the sum of all its blocks. Applying Ld to the operator [X, ·] selects precisely piYpj. This matrix belongs to 𝔤 because 𝔤 is a vector space closed under commutators.

Subtract these off-diagonal blocks from Y. The remaining block-diagonal matrix Z lies in 𝔤, as does X + tZ for every t ∈ K. On block i the latter has eigenvalues ei + tλ, where λ runs over the eigenvalues of Z on that block. Choose a nonzero t for which the spectra of different blocks are disjoint. Each pair of eigenvalues from different blocks excludes at most one value of t because their intercepts differ. The minimal polynomial of the direct sum is then the product of the block minimal polynomials. Indeed an annihilator must be divisible by each of these pairwise coprime polynomials. Each of the m nonzero blocks costs at least one degree, and the total degree is at most m. Every block therefore has a linear minimal polynomial. It follows that Z, hence the diagonal of Y, acts there by a scalar.

  • The bracket of invariant derivations and closure of the tangent space
  • Disjoint means distinguish the nonzero ordered differences
  • Polynomial interpolation in the commutator isolates one block
  • Separating the shifted block spectra forces scalar diagonal actions
