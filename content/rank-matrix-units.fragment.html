Which parts of a tangent matrix can the weights distinguish?

Let K be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero. Write V for the space of ρ and 𝔤 for the tangent Lie algebra of the Zariski closure of its image. Suppose that every matrix in 𝔤 has minimal polynomial of degree at most m. Fix X ∈ 𝔤 with m distinct lacunary eigenvalues e₁, …, em. Its minimal polynomial must have exactly these simple roots. Write pi for the projection onto the ei-eigenspace along the others. This eigenspace can have dimension greater than one.

Proposition. For every Y ∈ 𝔤 the block piYpj belongs to 𝔤 whenever i ≠ j. Each diagonal block of Y acts by a scalar on its eigenspace.

To distinguish the off-diagonal blocks, take the commutator with X. In a block mapping eigenspace j to eigenspace i, multiplication by X on the left gives the output weight, and multiplication on the right gives the input weight. Thus

[X, piYpj] = (ei − ej) piYpj.

Each nonzero difference belongs to a unique ordered pair. Indeed, an equality ei − ej = ek − el says that the pairs (i, l) and (k, j) have the same mean. If the pairs share an index, cancellation and distinctness give the same ordered difference. Otherwise their supports are disjoint. A repeated entry in either pair has the mean of its singleton support. In every case disjointness would contradict lacunarity. For example, weights 0, 1, 4 produce the six distinct nonzero differences ±1, ±3 and ±4.

Let Δ be the set of all differences, including zero. For d = ei − ej ≠ 0 form the polynomial Ld(T) = ∏c∈Δ, c≠d (T−c)/(d−c). It takes value one at d and zero at every other difference. Decompose Y as the sum of all its blocks. Applying Ld to the operator [X, ·] selects precisely piYpj. This matrix belongs to 𝔤 because 𝔤 is a vector space closed under commutators.

Subtract these off-diagonal blocks from Y. The remaining block-diagonal matrix Z lies in 𝔤, as does X + tZ for every t ∈ K. On block i the latter has eigenvalues ei + tλ, where λ runs over the eigenvalues of Z on that block. Choose a nonzero t for which the spectra of different blocks are disjoint. Each pair of eigenvalues from different blocks excludes at most one value of t because their intercepts differ. The minimal polynomial of the direct sum is then the product of the block minimal polynomials. Indeed an annihilator must be divisible by each of these pairwise coprime polynomials. Each of the m nonzero blocks costs at least one degree, and the total degree is at most m. Every block therefore has a linear minimal polynomial. It follows that Z, hence the diagonal of Y, acts there by a scalar.

Passing to the full image. Let A be the unital algebra generated by the tangent matrices. Two structural facts enter here. First, A contains the projectors, has scalar diagonal corners piApi = Kpi, is preserved by conjugation by ρ(Γ), and preserves each Γ-invariant subspace. The scalar conclusion for individual tangent matrices just proved has to extend to their products to give this assertion about A. Second, if piaρ(g)pi is nonzero for some a ∈ A, there is w ∈ A with piρ(g) = piw. This last assertion concerns all of Γ, including the components that move weight spaces. The proofs of these structural inputs remain to be supplied.

Given these facts, take R to be the linear span of all products aρ(g). It contains the identity, A and ρ(Γ). It is closed under multiplication because ρ(g)bρ(g)−1 belongs to A for each b ∈ A. Each generator also preserves every Γ-invariant subspace. To compute its diagonal corner, write aρ(g) = ρ(g)b, with b = ρ(g)−1aρ(g) ∈ A. If the corner is nonzero, the second structural fact replaces piρ(g) by piw. The corner becomes piwb pi, a scalar multiple of pi by the first fact. The zero corner is already scalar. Taking linear combinations proves the same assertion on R.

We also need to detect nonzero elements of R. Decompose V into irreducible Γ-subspaces using semisimplicity. If x ∈ R is nonzero, choose a summand W and v ∈ W with xv ≠ 0. Since R preserves W, the vector xv lies in W. Its Γ-translates span a nonzero invariant subspace of W, hence span W. In particular some linear combination b of group matrices sends xv to v. We have b ∈ R and bx fixes the nonzero vector v. No power of bx can be zero. This proves the nilpotence test used to construct the matrix units below.

How do the blocks give a representation of dimension m?

Proposition. Assume the structure theorem just stated. Then ρ contains an m-dimensional representation r and embeds in a finite direct sum of copies of r. The construction identifies exactly which multiplicities the two embeddings retain.

Consider the space piRpj of block maps from Vj to Vi, where Vi = piV. Any nonzero member x has an inverse between these two spaces. To see this, choose b ∈ R with bx nonnilpotent, using the criterion above. Then xbx ≠ 0, since otherwise (bx)² = 0. Put z = pjbpi. Both xz and zx lie in diagonal blocks, so each is a scalar times its projector. Their scalars coincide and are nonzero because (xz)x = x(zx) = xbx ≠ 0. Divide z by that scalar to obtain the inverse y. For any other map w in the same block, wy is scalar on Vi and w = (wy)x. Thus a nonzero block space is one-dimensional, even when its maps act on large eigenspaces.

Group indices into classes by declaring i and j related when their block space is nonzero. Identity maps, inverse maps and compositions give reflexivity, symmetry and transitivity. In each class C choose a base index b and isomorphisms ui from Vb to Vi in their block spaces, taking the identity at b. Within the class set Eij = uiuj−1. These are matrix units, with EijEjk = Eik. Write each block of a ∈ R as cij(a)Eij when the indices belong to one class. Take the coefficient to be zero between classes. Inserting the identity ∑ pj between a and b gives

cik(ab) = ∑j cij(a) cjk(b).

The identity has coefficient matrix Im. The displayed rule therefore makes r(g) = (cij(ρ(g))) a representation on Km, with inverse r(g−1). It is continuous for the ℓ-adic topology when ρ is continuous. For a nonzero block, choose an entry where Eij is nonzero. Dividing the corresponding entry of piρ(g)pj by that fixed entry expresses the coefficient as a continuous linear function of ρ(g).

Let rC denote the action on the coordinates of class C. Choosing a nonzero vector v ∈ Vb gives one copy of rC inside V. Its coordinate vectors are the independent vectors uiv in the distinct spaces Vi, and the formula for ρ(g) on them is exactly the coefficient matrix. Taking a whole basis of Vb gives a direct sum of dC = dim Vb such copies and spans all the corresponding eigenspaces. Thus

r = ⊕C rC,   ρ ≃ ⊕C rC⊕dC.

Every dC is positive. Taking one copy per class gives r ↪ ρ. With k the largest multiplicity, assign the copies of each class to the first dC of the k available slots to get ρ ↪ r⊕k. Since any subrepresentation of a semisimple representation is semisimple, the first injection also gives semisimplicity of r. Indeed, the intersection with r of an invariant complement in ρ to any subrepresentation of r is a complement in r.

For an example with unequal multiplicities, take A ∈ GL₂(ℤ₅) and u ∈ ℤ₅× acting on a five-dimensional space by diag(A, A, u). The three-dimensional r is diag(A, u). The first injection sends (v, z) to (v, 0, z). The second sends (v, w, z) to ((v, z), (w, 0)). The two-dimensional action occurs twice in ρ and the character occurs once. At the elements A = diag(1, t), u = t⁴ for t ∈ ℤ₅×, these are the weights 0, 1, 4 seen above. In the geometric application the two injections transfer different local information.
