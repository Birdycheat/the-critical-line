How does the same change of lattice compare two prism maps?

The filtration argument evaluated both matrices in the de Rham field. For descent the two matrices are evaluated by different maps. Fix any target prism with ring A and two maps f, g from the coefficient prism. We show that one exponent J, chosen for this pair, works for every entry of the ordered product

ℓJf(X′)g(X) ∈ Mat((f(E)2N)).

Write D = diag(Eni), so Y = sD as in the filtration calculation. In A, both f(E) and g(E) generate the prism ideal. Choose v with g(E) = v f(E). Work modulo f(E)2N and retain the same letters for the images. Now g(E)2N = 0, and g(D) = f(D) diag(vni). The integral identity X = YQ + E2NH therefore gives

g(X) = g(s)f(D) diag(vni) g(Q).

The coefficient comparison supplies j₀ with ℓj₀g(s) = ℓj₀f(s) in this quotient. After multiplying the displayed factorization by that power, the adjacent factors f(s)f(D) become f(Y). Multiply also by ℓc and use ℓcf(Y) = f(X)f(Q′). Finally, left multiplication by f(X′) gives zero because f(X′)f(X) = ℓcf(E)2NI. This proves the required divisibility with J = j₀ + c, even when the target has ℓ-torsion. All cancellations of nonzero scalars used to construct the matrices took place earlier in the domain S.

Thus the matrices X, X′ serve both the filtration test and every comparison of prism maps. The crystalline lattice construction uses the latter assertion before forming the matrices on the coefficient disc.
