How many distant terms can survive at a point?

In the trace estimate, the variable x describes a lattice and γ runs over rational group elements. Fix x for now. At a real place v, take the polar decomposition of the matrix of x⁻¹γx as k exp(X), with k unitary and X Hermitian. Write zv(γ) = ‖X‖HS. This is the square root of the sum of the squared logarithms of its singular values. A nonzero summand is far when some zv exceeds the chosen radius ε. Equality with ε belongs to the inner interval. Zero summands may be dropped throughout.

Rational point count. Write tx = min(½, least(x)), where least(x) is the smallest normalized sublattice size, interpreted as an infimum. Among rational elements whose finite component after conjugation lies in the supporting compact subgroup, the number with zv(γ) ≤ Rv at every place, for prescribed nonnegative radii, satisfies

# {γ in the box} ≤ P(x) exp(κ ∑v Rv),   P(x) = (3√n/tx²)2n²f,   κ = 2n².

Here n is the matrix size and f is the number of real places. To see the lattice in this count, subtract two distinct rational elements from the box. Their finite support condition makes the difference a nonzero map from the lattice at x into itself. The lattice size inequality, using self-duality, gives a lower bound for the geometric mean of the operator norms of this difference across all places, namely tx². Thus the matrices form a separated set, even though two of them may approach one another at an individual place.

Why does the exponent contain 2n²f?

At place v the matrices lie in a ball of operator radius exp(Rv), since the largest log singular value is at most zv. Put R̄ = (∑ Rv)/f. Rescale that space of matrices by exp(R̄ − Rv). The product of the rescalings is one, so geometric-mean separation persists, while every coordinate ball now has radius exp(R̄). In the maximum norm on the product, distinct centers are therefore at least tx² apart. Open balls of radius tx²/2 around them are disjoint and fit in the concentric ball of radius exp(R̄) + tx²/2. The product space has real dimension 2n²f. Scaling its Lebesgue volume bounds the count by (1 + 2 exp(R̄)/tx²)2n²f. Since n ≥ 1, tx ≤ ½ and R̄ ≥ 0, the base is at most 3√n exp(R̄)/tx². Raising this to the indicated power yields the box bound.

Why does the decay beat the number of distant terms?

Lowest-type coefficient bound. Use the unit vectors of the lowest compact type of the discrete series at the real places, with signature (p,q), 1 ≤ q ≤ p. Let h be a common lower bound for |λa − λb| whenever the two parameter entries have opposite signature signs, with h ≥ 120nq3/2. The polar matrix coefficient estimate at each place gives the following bound for the absolute kernel summand. The formal degrees and the maximum of the finite projector give the identity density c₀.

|φ(x⁻¹γx)| ≤ c₀ ∏v exp[−(h/8) min(zv², zv/√(2q))].

Sum outside the inner box. Assume 0 < ε ≤ 1/√(2q), κε ≤ log 2, 32κ ≤ hε and T = hε²/16 ≥ 1. Use index zero for [0, ε] and index j ≥ 1 for (2j−1ε, 2jε]. For a fixed tuple of indices, use the outer radius of each interval to count the terms and the inner radius to bound each coefficient. In a positive interval, both z² and z/√(2q) are at least εz. Their common lower bound exceeds 2j−1ε². After including the counting factor exp(κ2jε), a positive interval costs at most exp(−T2j−1), because κε ≤ T/2. The zero interval costs at most exp(κε) ≤ 2.

Write a for the zero-interval bound and b₊ for the sum of the positive-interval bounds. Since T ≥ 1, successive positive terms decrease by a factor at most exp(−T) ≤ ½. Hence b₊ ≤ 2 exp(−T) ≤ 1. A far element has a positive index at some place. The sum of the product bounds over the allowed profiles is (a + b₊)f − af. Factoring the difference of powers makes this at most fb₊(a + b₊)f−1. Every finite set of summands obeys this same estimate, so its nonnegative infinite sum converges and satisfies the slightly enlarged bound

∑γ far |φ(x⁻¹γx)| ≤ c₀P(x) · 2f·3f exp(−T).

With two places, temporarily allow only indices zero and one. Radii (ε, 2ε) have indices (0, 1), so a term at those radii is far. The surviving profiles are (0, 1), (1, 0) and (1, 1). With bounds a = 2 and b₊ = ¼ their weights sum to ½ + ½ + 1/16 = 17/16, also (2 + ¼)² − 2². This checks the counting identity on two intervals, independently of the arithmetic parameters.

Where does the factor exp(−n²f) enter the trace?

Let d be the CM root discriminant, g the level parameter, b the height parameter and Cg ≥ 0 its level-growth exponent. In the indefinite counting family, n ≥ 8, q² ≤ n, log f ≤ √n, d ≥ 1, b ≥ 2 and 1 ≤ g ≤ bCg. The moderate set consists of points of the fundamental domain with least(x) ≥ τ, where τ = (nfdgb)−11q ≤ ½. Thus tx ≥ τ and the packing factor is bounded on the entire moderate set. Put ε = 10−6n−8f−2. The family takes its spectral gap at least 8·1014(Cg + 4)n19f⁵ log(bd). These choices satisfy the shell hypotheses and give

∫moderate ∑γ far |φ(x⁻¹γx)| dμ(x) ≤ M exp(−n²f).
How does the gap pay for a possibly small τ?

Write s = √n and L = log(bd) ≥ log 2. The gap gives T ≥ 50(Cg + 4)n³fL. We compare this with the logarithms of the other factors. Since log n ≤ s, log f ≤ s and log g ≤ CgL, one has log(1/τ) ≤ 11s[2s + (Cg + 1)L]. Consequently the logarithm of (3s/τ²)2n²f · 2f·3f, plus the desired margin n²f, is at most

2n²f [2 + s + 22s(2s + (Cg + 1)L)] + 4f + n²f.

Divide by n³f, use s² = n and n ≥ 8. The result is at most 93 + 44(Cg + 1)L ≤ 50(Cg + 4)L, since 156 log 2 > 93. The shell sum at each moderate x is therefore bounded by c₀ exp(−n²f). Integrating uses only that the moderate volume is at most the full volume V and M = c₀V.

This supplies the term δM in the four-term trace inequality. The rare-flag volume and the concentration of the projection on the deep region enter through separate integrals.
