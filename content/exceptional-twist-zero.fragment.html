Why only one quadratic twist can keep a near-one zero

In the fixed-height mean square, the prime number theorem for a residue class modulo M can contribute a term involving li(Uβ). Here β is a real zero of a nonprincipal quadratic Dirichlet character. Two such terms at different moduli might both be large. To compare them, the proof first puts both characters at the same modulus. The analytic reason this works, as in the 3–4–1 positivity for characters, is that two nearby zeros would force a sum of four logarithmic derivatives to be negative, while every coefficient in its Dirichlet series is nonnegative. The optional zero in the progression formula · The zero-free theorem used by the twist estimate

Fix a positive modulus Q and two distinct nonprincipal characters χ₁, χ₂ with χ₁² = χ₂² = 1. Their product is nonprincipal because each character is its own inverse. Write Dψ(σ) = Re(−L′(σ,ψ)/L(σ,ψ)). At a real σ > 1, this is an absolutely convergent sum with coefficients Λ(n) Re ψ(n), where Λ is the von Mangoldt function. On units modulo Q, χ₁ and χ₂ take the values ±1. At every nonunit all four characters, including the principal character 1 modulo Q, vanish. Consequently

D₁ + Dχ₁ + Dχ₂ + Dχ₁χ₂ = ∑(n,Q)=1 Λ(n) (1+χ₁(n))(1+χ₂(n))/nσ ≥ 0.

Each unit has a multiplier of 0 or 4. Suppose both characters have real zeros β₁, β₂ near 1. Put ℓ = log(2Q). In the interval 1 < σ ≤ 6/5 the expansion over zeros in the closed disk of radius 1/5 about 11/10 bounds Dχ by Cℓ minus a sum of Re(1/(σ−ρ)), counted with multiplicity. Every summand is positive since Re ρ < 1. A real zero β in [9/10,1) thus subtracts at least 1/(σ−β). The product character contributes at most Cℓ, and the principal character contributes at most 1/(σ−1)+C′, with C′ ≥ 0. Both constants are uniform in Q. Set H = 3|C| + 2C′ + 1, e = 1/(20Hℓ), and test at σ = 1+2e. Since ℓ ≥ log 2 > 1/2, we have e ≤ 1/10. If β₁, β₂ ≥ 1−e, each contributes at least 1/(3e). The upper bound for the same four-term sum is then

3Cℓ + C′ + 1/(2e) − 2/(3e) ≤ (H−1)ℓ − (10/3)Hℓ < 0.

This contradiction excludes two distinct characters. A single zero is compatible with the calculation: the pole allowance 1/(2e) exceeds the one guaranteed subtraction 1/(3e). Test point and two zero contributions · Principal pole and the contradiction

A second estimate handles two zeros of the same real character at small imaginary part. At its test point σ = 1+1/T, the local sum over zeros is strictly less than 8T/5, and each zero in the restricted region contributes at least 4T/5. Two distinct zeros or one zero of multiplicity at least two exceed the available bound. A nonreal zero would occur with its complex conjugate, so a surviving zero is real and simple. Combining these estimates with the exclusions for the principal character, nonreal characters and larger imaginary parts produces an absolute c > 0. For every Q ≥ 1 the closed region Re z ≥ 1−c/log(Q(|Im z|+2)) contains at most one character–zero pair, with the pole of the principal character excluded. Now take levels M₁, M₂ ≤ D with D ≥ 3 and induce both characters to Q = M₁M₂. The change-of-level formula preserves a zero by multiplying its L-value by Euler factors. Since log(2Q) ≤ 3 log D, any two real zeros above 1−c/(3 log D) lie in the same region. Their induced characters must agree on every common unit. For example, take m = 8 and squarefree denominators 3 and 5. At the levels 24 and 40, the common unit 41 is 1 modulo 8, but (41/3) = −1 and (41/5) = +1. Thus characters agreeing there with the respective quadratic symbols cannot both have such a zero. The example uses no assumption that either zero exists. Induced characters and the factor of three · The alignment test and choice of the exceptional denominator

Fix 0 < ε ≤ 1 and K ≥ 1. For every sufficiently large Y and every admitted progression with modulus m, take D = mY800K and c₀ = c/3. A denominator s can produce an uncontrolled contribution only if the progression formula at ms supplies a pair with 1−β < c₀/log D and its character agrees with (·/s) on units congruent to 1 modulo m. For a misaligned character the weighted sum of its main terms vanishes. For an aligned character with 1−β ≥ c₀/log D, the ratio li(Uβ)/li(U) is at most 4 log U exp(−c₀ log U/log D), small enough at the common threshold for Y. At most one odd squarefree s > 1 coprime to m, s ≤ Y800K, can survive both tests. The proof chooses it (or 0 if none) before the height y and coefficients κ. Its pairs are included in the quadratic estimate. A separate use of Siegel’s lower bound gives 1−β ≥ c₁/m at the original modulus and supplies the lower bound on the number of primes.
