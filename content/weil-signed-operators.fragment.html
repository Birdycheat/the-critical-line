How can one integral matrix represent either direction of Frobenius?

Fix a Weil element σ. Its signed exponent α counts powers of the absolute Frobenius on unramified scalars. In the local convention it is the residue degree of the place times the signed degree of σ. To work in the integral ring R = Acris, where we can always apply positive powers, write n₁ = max(−α, 0), n₂ = max(α, 0) and φj = φnj. We want a matrix that carries φ₂(U) to a common scalar times φ₁(U), where U is the basis of invariant period columns. At α = 1 the input has already been acted on by Frobenius. At α = −1 Frobenius acts on the output.

Use the height H of the frames, their common section exponent κ, and the image a of the primitive period τ under Witt Frobenius followed by inclusion into R. Write y, y′ for the same images of the integral period matrices, and Q, Q′ for the section matrices. Their paired products are a2HI and ℓ2κI. Set

Z = yQ,   Z′ = Q′y′,   ZZ′ = Z′Z = hI,   h = a2Hℓ2κ.

Multiplying the paired products gives the first order ZZ′. For square matrices over a commutative ring a one-sided inverse is two-sided when its scalar is a unit. Here the equality in the other order can be checked after inclusion into Bcris, where h is invertible, and brought back through the injective inclusion. In that ring we also have Z = aHU. This suggests composing φ₁(Z) with φ₂(Z′). An extra integral factor will preserve precision when we compare two ranks.

V = φ₂(h) φ₁(Z) φ₂(Z′),   s = φ₂(a)3H ℓ4κ φ₁(a)H.

Indeed multiply V by φ₂(U) = φ₂(a)−Hφ₂(Z). The adjacent factors φ₂(Z′Z) give a second copy of φ₂(h). Replacing the last remaining φ₁(Z) by φ₁(a)Hφ₁(U) gives

Vφ₂(U) = sφ₁(U)   in Bcris.

Both a and ℓ are units there, so s is a unit there as well. Its integral formula involves the local datum, the two exponents, H and κ. Once these are fixed, we have the same s on the target and on every layer, even when their ranks differ.

The rank-one calculation lets you change the sign of the exponent and follow the same column through both powers of Frobenius.

Why do the integral operators agree after projection?

Let M be the requested precision. Choose a layer as in the frame comparison, at least as deep as M and as the precision needed for its integral matrices. Use the subscript 1 for that layer. We have ΠZ₁ ≡ ZD modulo ℓM, where D = f₀(C) is the constant evaluation of the rectangular comparison. We will need this congruence twice, with the same Π and D after each application of Frobenius.

Frobenius fixes the entries of Π in ℤℓ. For D, the coefficients of a constant evaluation are fixed by the local Galois group. Inside Bcris the fixed ring is the maximal unramified subfield K₀ of the base field K. The embedded Weil element fixes K₀ and acts on unramified scalars by the absolute Frobenius to the signed power α. Thus φ|α| fixes K₀, also when α is negative, since Frobenius is invertible on unramified scalars. One of n₁, n₂ is zero and the other is |α|. By injectivity of R → Bcris, both iterates therefore fix D already in R. Each preserves the ideal ℓMR. The field of fixed crystalline scalars.

Write W = φ₁(Z), T = φ₂(Z), T′ = φ₂(Z′) and b = φ₂(h). The two errors ΠT₁ − TD and ΠW₁ − WD are now divisible entrywise by ℓM. The matrices T′T and T₁T′₁ equal bI on their respective modules. Expand the following expression in the indicated order.

VΠ − ΠV₁ = WT′(ΠT₁ − TD)T′₁ − b(ΠW₁ − WD)T′₁.

The first product contributes bWT′Π − bWD T′₁. The second cancels the latter term and contributes −bΠW₁T′₁. Each original summand contains one of the known errors and has only integral factors outside it. Thus VΠ ≡ ΠV₁ modulo ℓM. The extra factor b in the operator has cleared the paired products before either error is estimated. Increasing M changes the chosen layer, while its scalar s stays fixed.
