Why the real-place projection has positive mass

Fix one real place τ of a step of the iterated theta chain. Its integral pairs a Weil matrix coefficient with the conjugate of a discrete-series coefficient. Even when both vectors have norm one, the integral can vanish if the two coefficients detect different parts of the representation. Here the Fock model realizes vectors using polynomials with a Gaussian inner product. Restricting a representation to a compact subgroup breaks it into finite-dimensional types. A joint highest-weight polynomial selects a specific type for each of the two compact groups. We must show that this choice survives projection onto the component detected by the source vector. The chain chooses a polynomial Φ of the prescribed highest weights for both compact groups and a unit source vector of its prescribed highest weight. Its real-place comparison identifies the polynomial with a vector in the Fock model of the oscillator representation. Build the projection. Let G̃ be the double cover of the real unitary group U(p,q) with Haar measure ν. Let σ be the source discrete series on this cover, v its chosen unit vector, and dσ its formal degree for ν. We use the inner product linear in its second entry. Write mv(g) = ⟨v,σ(g)v⟩. For unit v, Schur orthogonality gives ∫ |mv(g)|² dν(g) = 1/dσ. This fixes the scalar that makes the projector send v to itself in σ. For any unitary representation π of the cover define the operator

Pvx = dσ ∫G̃ mv(g) π(g)x dν(g).

To see the contradiction, compress π(g) and σ(g) to E, writing A(g) = j₁*π(g)j₁ and B(g) = j₂*σ(g)j₂. In an orthonormal basis, F(g) = tr(B(g)*A(g)) is the sum of exactly those mixed entries, so its integral would vanish. Left compact translation changes the common basis and preserves F. On the noncompact Cartan directions g = exp Y, positivity of the Fock joint harmonics and of the lowest compact type of σ makes both compressed operators positive semidefinite. Diagonalizing one shows tr(B*A) ≥ 0. Since each g is a compact translate of some exp Y, this proves F ≥ 0 everywhere. At the identity B(1) = 1 and F(1) = ∑j ‖j₁ej‖² > 0 because η belongs to the image. Continuity and positive Haar measure on open sets contradict a zero integral. Thus Pvη ≠ 0. When the partner is definite, the stronger local theorem gives Pvη = η and the cover integral equals 1/dσ. Return to the real integral. Let the cover map send ν to c₀μ for the measure μ chosen on the real group. Let CF > 0 compare the theta pairing with the Fock inner product, and write η = n · ι(κΦ), where κ puts the polynomial in local model coordinates, ι embeds it in Fock space and n = 1/‖ι(κΦ)‖. The original Weil coefficient thus contributes CF/n² times the normalized Fock coefficient. On the cover, both the Weil and discrete-series coefficients carry the same unit-modulus splitting character. Complex conjugation on the discrete-series factor cancels that phase.
