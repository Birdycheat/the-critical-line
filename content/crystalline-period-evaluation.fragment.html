How do periods of a matrix lattice reach the original representation?

The invariant columns give periods for the rationalized matrix lattice W. The representation we set out to study acts on a different space V = La, with L a finite coefficient extension of ℚℓ. Regard both as vector spaces over ℚℓ. The identity matrix in the lattice provides a way to reach every vector of V.

Fix v ∈ V. A vector of W with coordinates x represents a matrix Φ(x) in the global coefficient field. If its entries lie in a larger field than L, choose an L-linear retraction π from the algebraic closure onto L. To obtain one, extend the vector 1 to an L-basis of the algebraic closure and take the coefficient of 1. On entries already in L this map is the identity. Apply π to each coordinate after multiplication. Stacks Project, §11.2, on extending an independent set to a basis.

Fv(x) = π(Φ(x)v),   Fv(e) = v,

where e is the lattice vector representing the identity. For a local Galois element g, the lattice action gives Φ(g x) = r(g)Φ(x). The entries of the local matrix r(g) belong to L, so its action commutes with π. This proves Fv(g x) = r(g)Fv(x). The choice of v stays fixed throughout this calculation.

Descent through a family of maps. Suppose the crystalline periods of W span Bcris ⊗ W. Expand 1 ⊗ e in its basis of invariant columns. Apply 1 ⊗ Fv to that expansion. Each column goes to a fixed vector by equivariance, so 1 ⊗ v belongs to the span of the crystalline periods of V. Varying v gives every simple tensor after multiplication by a scalar of Bcris. Simple tensors generate the whole module, and the period-ring criterion now gives crystallinity of V. Brinon and Conrad, Theorem 5.2.1 and Remark 5.2.3.

For instance, take the scalar matrix space ℚℓ·I acting on ℚℓ², with trivial group action. Evaluating at the first coordinate vector gives one line and evaluating at the second gives the other. Their images span the plane. The same reasoning permits a different map for each target vector, even when the source has smaller dimension than the target.
