Which powers of the flag sizes enter the volume?

Fix one saturated isotropic flag with its integral lattices. Assume the Hermitian space has the same signature (p,q) at every real place. Write E for the CM field and f for its number of pairs of complex embeddings. A flag with successive rank jumps j₁,…,jₐ determines a decomposition into an isotropic part, a residual space and the dual isotropic part. In that order the block ranks are r = (j₁,…,jₐ, n−2∑jᵢ, jₐ,…,j₁). Let τ⁰ denote the positive sizes in this fixed frame. The height of the ith isotropic block is hᵢ = (τᵢ⁰)2f jᵢ. Its dual has reciprocal height, and the residual block has height one.

An upper triangular unipotent matrix can move one block into each earlier block. Group those rectangular entries by their distance k above the diagonal. For a pair of blocks b < c, the rectangle has rbrc entries and represents a map from block c to block b. The determinant contribution of its integral lattice is hbrc/hcrb. To see the two powers, a change of basis in the range acts on each of the rc columns. The inverse change of basis in the domain acts on each of the rb rows. The arithmetic normalization for the coefficient lattice is νEf per entry, where 2νE is the root discriminant of E.

dₖ = ∑c−b=k rbrc,   Cₖ = νEf dₖ ∏c−b=k hbrc/hcrb.

Here Cₖ is the covolume of the full complex block lattice across all places. Write P(τ⁰) for the positive square root of the product of the height ratios over all pairs. For two isotropic pieces i < j, pairing them and their duals gives (τᵢ⁰/τⱼ⁰)2f jᵢjⱼ(τᵢ⁰τⱼ⁰)2f jᵢjⱼ in P. Pairing piece i with the residual block of rank r0 gives (τᵢ⁰)2f jᵢr₀. Pairing it with its own dual gives ((τᵢ⁰)²)f jᵢ². The next step explains why that square root also occurs in the real covolume.

From integral matrices to unitary matrices on one layer

Use orthonormal skew-adjoint coordinates on the layer. Its full lattice L lies in ℂs, with s = f dₖ, and is stable under complex conjugation. A skew-adjoint leading term has real coordinates in this basis. Put A = L ∩ ℝs and iB = L ∩ iℝs. For x + iy ∈ L, adding its conjugate gives 2x ∈ A. In the quotient by A ⊕ iB, the class of x modulo A determines the entire class. Indeed, if x ∈ A, subtraction leaves iy ∈ iB. The quotient therefore injects into (½A)/A, of order 2s. The index formula gives cov(A) cov(B) ≤ 2sCₖ.

Why can we take a square root? The two covolumes are equal. In a CM extension unramified at finite places, for each rational prime p there is a purely imaginary algebraic integer whose absolute norm is prime to p. For this existence assertion write the scalar as z − conj(z). At a split pair of primes above p, choose the residues of z to be 0 and 1. At an inert prime choose a residue moved by conjugation in its quadratic residue field. The Chinese remainder theorem realizes these choices simultaneously. The difference is a unit above p. Multiplying by it exchanges the two real coordinate spaces and maps their lattices into one another. If D is the absolute determinant of this real scaling and I, J are the two indices, then I = D cov(A)/cov(B) and J = D cov(B)/cov(A). Their product D² is the dₖth power of that norm, prime to p. Consequently the positive rational number I/J = (cov(A)/cov(B))² has zero valuation at every prime. It is one. For a planar example of the index bound, the lattice of x + iy with integral coordinates of the same parity has area 2. Both axis lattices have spacing 2, attaining √(2 · 2) on the real axis.

To use the real lattice in the group, each of its leading terms must lift to an integral unitary matrix. The same unramified hypothesis gives an integral w with w + conj(w) = 1, where conj denotes conjugation. Indeed the residue trace is onto at every finite prime, both for the split pair and for the unramified quadratic residue field, including residue characteristic two. The ideal of integral traces is therefore contained in no maximal ideal and equals the base integer ring. If an integral block matrix Y has the required real leading coordinates, then X = wY − (wY)† is skew-adjoint and has the same leading coordinates. Start with g = 1 + X. Its defect D = g†g − 1 equals X†X, already in a deeper layer. Replace g by g(1 − wD). Its new defect is (1 − conj(w)D)(1 + D)(1 − wD) − 1. The linear term vanishes because w has trace one. All remaining terms lie deeper. The filtration ends after finitely many diagonals, so this produces an integral unitary lift.

Reduce the first nonzero layer modulo its real lattice, then reduce the next. A correction in a deeper layer preserves the earlier coordinates since the cross term in (1 + X)(1 + Y) lies strictly deeper. The product of the layer domains thus covers the quotient. In these triangular coordinates Haar volume is the product of the real coordinate volumes with the chosen orthonormal normalization. Multiplying the bounds √(2f dₖCₖ) gives, with dN = ∑dₖ,

covolume of the integral unipotent radical ≤ (2νE)f dN/2 P(τ⁰).

From block determinants to the volume of a marked slice

Next let the block-diagonal element m vary. In block i, set Dᵢ = ∏v |det mᵢᵥ|² over the complex embedding pairs, and Nᵢ = 2f jᵢ. Write bᵢ for the volume of the quotient of this general linear block by its lattice automorphisms in the slab 1 ≤ Dᵢ < exp(1). The determinant of a lattice automorphism has absolute norm one. Scaling a block by a positive real scalar translates log Dᵢ by any prescribed amount and preserves its Haar measure. Translation invariance on the line therefore makes the determinant measure bᵢ dDᵢ/Dᵢ. The fixed frame gives τᵢ = τᵢ⁰ Dᵢ−1/Nᵢ. Reversing the integration limits changes dDᵢ/Dᵢ to Nᵢ dτᵢ/τᵢ.

The unitary metric counts a general linear tangent block twice, once in the block and once in its dual. The squared norm doubles in 2jᵢ² real dimensions at each place. Its volume factor across all places is 2f jᵢ². The residual group contributes the gauge-normalized covolume g₀ of its one chosen lattice class. The block domains and that residual domain cover the required quotient, giving the product of these factors as an upper bound for nonnegative tests.

Finally write the real group element in unipotent, block-diagonal and compact coordinates. The change of Haar measure in these coordinates contributes a positive factor δᵥ at each real place. Block sizes multiply under the block-diagonal change of frame, so their height ratios give P(τ⁰)∏vδᵥ(mᵥ) = P(τ(m)). The initial height factor from the radical has now become the variable weight in the integral. For a measurable subset B of 0 < τ₁ < ⋯ < τₐ < 1/2, let cIw be the one-place Iwasawa normalization for this signature and flag type, with compact mass one. Then the gauge-normalized mass of this single marked slice with sizes in B satisfies

mass ≤ cIwf (2νE)f dN/2 g₀ (∏i 2f jᵢ²bᵢ) ∫B P(τ) ∏i (2f jᵢ/τᵢ) dτ.

To check the size density by itself, take one isotropic line in dimension four and formally set f = 1. Then the ranks are (1,2,1) and the heights are (τ²,1,τ−2). Each of the three pairs contributes τ⁴ before the square root. Thus P(τ) = τ⁶, and the size density is 2τ⁵ dτ. Integrating from zero to 1/2 gives 1/192 before multiplication by the arithmetic and measure constants. The remaining sum over the marked classes is where the residual covolume is absorbed into the total volume.
