Which central directions still need averaging?

The form ω realizing the cocycle already has two symmetries. It is invariant under Γ = GLN(𝒪L′) and under a common positive scaling at all infinite places. The next estimate uses independent scalar changes at every place. Write c(t) for the central matrix whose component at w is exp(tw)I. A complex scalar zw acts on a positive Hermitian matrix by Hw ↦ |zw|−2Hw, since the action uses the inverse matrix on the right and its adjoint on the left. Let r be the number of complex places of the totally complex field L′. The line of constant vectors in ℝr acts trivially on ω by pullback. A unit u gives another symmetry because uI ∈ Γ. Its logarithm vector ℓ(u) = (log |w(u)|)w lies in V = {t ∈ ℝr ∣ ∑w tw = 0} by the product formula. Dirichlet’s unit theorem gives a lattice Λ of rank r − 1 in V, generated by the logarithms of fundamental units. Subtracting the coordinate mean from any t leaves a vector in V. Consequently the central parameters still to be averaged form the compact torus V/Λ. For r = 1 the initial symmetries already suffice. The proof integrates one unit direction v = ℓ(u) at a time. For the pullback of the form, including its tangent arguments, it takes

Avω = ∫₀¹ c(τv)∗ω dτ.

Unit invariance makes the integrand 1-periodic in τ. Translating the interval of integration therefore preserves the average, so every real multiple of v fixes Avω. Central scalars commute, which preserves the directions already treated. Iterating uses ordinary Lebesgue measure of mass one on each unit interval. In the coordinates of the fundamental units their product induces Haar probability on V/Λ. The output ω′ is invariant under every scalar tuple. Why does the average have exactly the original periods? Fix a finite bar cycle z based at the identity matrix I. For fixed τ, centrality moves the basepoint of every simplex from I to c(τv)·I, keeping the same group labels. Join the old and new vertices by the simplices of a prism. Its side faces cancel on the cycle because ∂z = 0. The remaining two evaluations agree because ω integrates to a cocycle on simplices. Thus for every τ the sum over the cycle already equals its original value. Interchanging the finite sum, simplex integrals and the unit-interval integral preserves that value. The averaged form still integrates to a cocycle by the same interchange on a boundary. Its moving-frame bound retains the exponent am for a form of degree m, since the path τ ↦ c(τv) is compact and right translation there changes the height by a bounded factor. Check the factor of two and a period

In L′ = ℚ(i,√2), label the two complex places by the sign of √2. For u = 1 + √2 and a = log(1 + √2), the absolute values of u at these places are exp(a) and exp(−a). Take N = 1 and work with the cyclic subgroup generated by u. On the pair of positive numbers (H₁,H₂) set s = (log H₂ − log H₁)/(4a). Under c(τℓ(u)) log H₁ decreases by 2aτ and log H₂ increases by 2aτ, so s goes to s + τ. Averaging (2 + cos(2πs)) ds gives 2 ds. The cycle [u] moves s from 0 to 1 at the identity basepoint. The two primitives 2s + sin(2πs)/(2π) and 2s both increase by 2 along that cycle. On an open arc from 0 to 1/4 their increments differ by 1/(2π).
