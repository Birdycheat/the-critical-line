Counting one inert Euler factor

At a good inert place v, the Euler product needs the integral of a spherical section times a local matrix coefficient. Why should an integral over infinitely many matrices give a rational function with paired factors? Each coset of the integral lattice stabilizer has volume one, so the integral becomes a weighted count. In a rank reduction by two, the integer that measures the outer diagonal block can increase or decrease. Those two directions will supply the two factors. Where the Euler product uses this particular inert calculation.

Write n for the source rank and b for the target rank. The local coefficient field is complete and discretely valued. Its valuation ring 𝒪 has residue field of size q², where q ≥ 2 is the base residue size. The involution preserves the valuation, fixes a uniformizer ϖ of valuation one and acts nontrivially on the residue field. Let Kn be the integral unitary group, with Haar volume one. Take the normalized spherical doubling section for an unramified character, and a coefficient w invariant under Kn on both sides, with w(1) = 1 and the specified Satake eigenvalues. Write ε for the character at ϖ and α₁,…,α⌊n/2⌋ for the nonzero spherical parameters of the unitary representation. Put

X = ε q−(2s+n),   y = ε q−(2s+1) = qn−1X.

We use |y| < 1, |yαi| < 1 and |y/αi| < 1 for every parameter. In the arithmetic application |ε| = 1. For a coefficient with nonzero value at the identity, the bounds |αi|, |αi−1| ≤ qn give these strict inequalities when Re s > n. What does a cell count?

On the embedded unitary group the section takes the value Xℓ(g). Write Λ = 𝒪n for the standard row lattice and Λ(g) = Λ + Λg for its enlargement. Then ℓ(g) is the length over 𝒪 of Λ(g)/Λ. If k ∈ Kn, then Λk = Λ and Λ(gk) = Λ(g)k. The map sending a row v to vk induces an isomorphism of the two quotients. Hence the length, and therefore the integrand, is constant on each coset gKn. A quotient isomorphic to 𝒪/ϖh𝒪 has length h. The proof computes the sum using a normalized induced spherical function φ. The Satake eigenvalue identity makes its sums over each double coset agree with those of w. For a double coset D, sum over its finitely many cosets of the form gKn. These are the orbits of multiplication on the right by integral unitary matrices. Both the length ℓ and the original coefficient w are constant on D. Thus the sum of |Xℓw| there is |X|ℓ|∑w| = |X|ℓ|∑φ| ≤ ∑|Xℓφ|. Summing this bound over the double cosets transfers absolute convergence from φ to w. Suppose n = m + 2. After a representative is fixed for each smaller coset, each coset gKn has a unique label consisting of an integer a, a smaller coset g₀Km, and a coset of the upper unipotent radical modulo its integral points. Before multiplying by the radical representative, the diagonal blocks are ϖa, g₀, ϖ−a. Fix the first two labels. Write h = ℓ(g₀), a₊ = max(a,0), and H = |a| + h. Every resulting lattice has length at least H. Block and radical coordinates, bijection of the cell labels.

Let Cj count radical cosets of length at most H + j. The lattice length test permits row denominators at level e = a₊ + ⌊j/2⌋ and a corrected upper corner at level f = 2a₊ + j. The number of row classes is q2(h+me). Each of the m row coordinates uses the extension residue field of size q², and the fixed middle lattice accounts for h. Every admissible row lifts because 2e ≤ f. With a row fixed, the difference between any two corners has trace zero. There are qf classes of such differences modulo integral ones. Multiplying the two counts gives

Cj = c q2m⌊j/2⌋+j,   c = q2h+(2m+2)a₊.

Three steps explain the count. The length calculation for one matrix derives the two levels e and f. At those levels, the row calculation counts the admissible rows and constructs a lift of each one. The trace-zero calculation counts the corners above that lift. The counting proof chooses a lift of each row and constructs a bijection with the product of the row and trace-zero quotients. Pass from balls to shells

For j = 2k, substitution in the count gives C2kX2k = c(y²)k, since y = qm+1X. For j = 2k + 1 the row level stays fixed and the corner gains one level. Its term therefore gains the factor qX. Adding the two geometric series gives c(1 + qX)/(1 − y²). A coset of length H + 2 occurs in every ball with j ≥ 2. To count it exactly once, subtract the preceding ball. The shell counts are d₀ = C₀ and dj = Cj − Cj−1 for j ≥ 1. Subtracting the shifted series multiplies by 1 − X. Restoring the length offset gives

∑j≥0 djXH+j = c XHJm(X),   Jm(X) = (1 − X)(1 + qX)/(1 − y²).

Taking |X| in the same shell calculation proves absolute convergence. It authorizes regrouping individual cosets by length. Even and odd series, first differences, absolute convergence over all radical cosets.

Sum the positive and negative diagonal valuations
