Why does the Frobenius inclusion hold on the chosen deformation?

Let z be the generic point of a component of the special fibre of the smooth unitary moduli scheme M over the discrete valuation ring R. Write κ = κ(z) and let k be the algebraically closed field of our geometric point above z. Both fields have characteristic q. Keep the algebra degree m = 1 and the level N ≥ 3 prime to q. Suppose the ordinary count fails there. The pointwise inclusion then holds for the abelian variety A0 over k. The realization of the prescribed Hodge deformation gives a marked abelian scheme A′ over Δ = k[ε]/(ε²) and an identification of its special fibre with A0. We need the inclusion for this particular A′.

Representability gives a Δ-point of M. Its reduction represents the given marked object, so its closed point maps to z. Its structure map R → Δ factors through k and kills the maximal ideal of R. The factorization at a generic point applies both to the k-point and to the Δ-point. Pull back the universal object first to Spec κ and call its abelian variety Aκ. We obtain two scalar maps s ∶ κ → k and τ ∶ κ → Δ. The marked objects A0 and A′ are the respective pullbacks of Aκ, with their coefficient actions. Reduction of τ gives s. Nevertheless, τ can have a nonzero first-order term. This is why we pass through κ.

Base change of the inclusion. For any of these three base rings B, let PB denote primitive first de Rham cohomology, LB its first Hodge subspace or submodule, and ΦB its q-semilinear Frobenius. Let EB and DB act on cohomology by the fixed local coefficients e and d. Here the subscripted operator DB means multiplication by the coefficient d on cohomology. The direction used to construct the graph acts on the dual space. Set WB = spanB{DBΦBfu ∣ u ∈ PB}, where Q = qf is the size of the chosen residue field 𝒪F/𝔴. The implication we need is

EkLk ⊆ Wk   ⇒   EκLκ ⊆ Wκ   ⇒   EΔLΔ ⊆ WΔ.

Both scalar maps start from the field κ, so tensoring with their targets is exact. To see the base change on cohomology, choose a finite affine cover of the separated variety Aκ. Tensor its Čech complexes of differential forms with the target. Relative differential forms commute with base change, and exactness preserves the cohomology of the total complex and the image defining the Hodge filtration. It also preserves the kernel of the equation expressing primitivity under addition. Thus PB = B ⊗κ Pκ and LB = B ⊗κ Lκ, for B = k or Δ using its indicated scalar map. The Stacks Project, Lemma 30.5.2, flat base change and its affine-cover proof.

Functoriality of the coefficient action and Frobenius gives DBΦBf(λ ⊗ u) = λQ ⊗ DκΦκfu. Hence every generator of WB belongs to B ⊗κ Wκ. The generators with λ = 1 give the reverse inclusion after taking the B-span. For l ∈ Lκ, the hypothesis over k now says that 1 ⊗ [Eκl] vanishes in k ⊗κ (Pκ/Wκ). A κ-linear functional on k taking 1 to 1 exists by extending 1 to a basis. Applying it to the first tensor factor gives [Eκl] = 0. This proves descent. Extending the resulting inclusion along τ proves it over Δ. The span accommodates an imperfect residue field κ as well as nilpotents in Δ.

Why does the projected functional have a lift with zero first-order coefficient?

Keep the marked reduction A0 of A′ over Δ. Let x ∈ Pk∨ be the separating functional. It vanishes on Wk and satisfies x ∘ Ek = x. We want a lift of x in the dual Hodge filtration of this deformation. The new condition is that its coefficient of ε should vanish when evaluated on every constant primitive class.

Comparing primitive classes. Give (ε) in Δ its square-zero divided powers, with every divided power of order at least two equal to zero. Write CΔ for the evaluation of primitive first crystalline cohomology of A0 on this thickening. The crystalline comparison for abelian schemes supplies two maps into this single Δ-module. Constant base change gives j ∶ Pk → CΔ and identifies CΔ with Δ ⊗k Pk. The marked lifting gives a Δ-linear comparison isomorphism J ∶ PΔ → CΔ. Write r ∶ PΔ → Pk for reduction and ι ∶ k → Δ for the inclusion of constants. The two comparisons are functorial for the coefficient action. Messing, The Crystals Associated to Barsotti-Tate Groups, Chapters IV–V, on crystalline evaluation and the Hodge filtration of a lift.

The equality (a + εb)q = aq means that the scalar Frobenius on Δ factors through reduction, Frobenius on k, and inclusion of constants. On the closed fibre, absolute Frobenius commutes with the marked reduction map. These two identities give the same crystalline pullback along the two composites, so JΦΔ = jΦkr. Also rΦΔ = Φkr. Applying the first identity to ΦΔf−1u and iterating the second proves

JΦΔfu = jΦkf(ru)   (u ∈ PΔ,   f ≥ 1).

Let λ be the Δ-linear extension of x to CΔ, so λ(jc) = ι(x(c)). Evaluating a generator with the comparison just proved gives λ(JDΔΦΔfu) = ι(x(DkΦkfru)) = 0. By Δ-linearity λJ annihilates WΔ. Let Ecrys be the action of e on CΔ. The functional λ ∘ Ecrys corresponds on A′ to λJEΔ. The inclusion on the deformation makes this functional zero on LΔ, so it belongs to the dual Hodge filtration. On jc its value is ι(x(Ekc)) = ι(x(c)). This is the required filtered lift with first-order coefficient zero. The vanishing of ε in a scalar Frobenius was used precisely at the first of the f iterates.

Which first-order coefficient is intrinsic to the marked deformation?

Fix the identification of A0 with the reduction of A′ and put H = Pk∨, Fil = Lk⊥. In the constant coordinates CΔ∨ = Δ ⊗k H, the filtration of A′ is a direct summand ℱ reducing to Fil. These are the Hodge subbundles in the crystalline comparison. For a fixed x ∈ Fil suppose x + εy belongs to ℱ. Then

x + εy′ ∈ ℱ   ⇔   y′ − y ∈ Fil.

Proof. Choose a Δ-linear projection onto ℱ. Its reduction is a projection onto Fil. Applied to εh, the projection gives ε times its reduction applied to h, because ε² = 0. Thus εh is fixed by the projection exactly when h ∈ Fil. Subtract the known lift x + εy from the proposed lift to obtain the equivalence. In particular the coset of y in H/Fil depends only on x and on the chosen marked deformation.

Apply this to the deformation realizing the prescribed direction. Write b for the value at x of the Kodaira–Spencer direction. Its construction gives b ∉ Fil, and the realized filtration contains x + εb. The projected crystalline functional gives x + ε·0 in this same ℱ. The criterion implies −b ∈ Fil, hence b ∈ Fil, a contradiction.

The local alternative between the ordinary count and the Frobenius inclusion holds at each geometric point satisfying the unitary hypotheses. The factorization of the chosen deformation through κ(z) used the genericity of z in the special fibre. At every geometric point over such a generic point, the failure of the count would have produced the two incompatible lifts above. Consequently #A0[𝔴](k) = Qn−1. This is the generic ordinarity theorem used in the integral congruence.
