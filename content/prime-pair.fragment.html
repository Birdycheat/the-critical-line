Finding two primes that work for every required twist

The host construction applies the following prime-pair theorem to a fixed primitive character’s window. For a fixed primitive ψ, take M equal to its conductor and A equal to the cohomological window exponent J. Fix 0 < ε < 1/52 and A ≥ 1. For every sufficiently large Y one pair of primes q,d has to work at every y between Y and YA, for every required character. A zero-free box of height H means that L(s,χ) never vanishes for Re s > 1 − 1/400 and |Im s| ≤ H. In either selection step write U for the upper bound on the candidate prime p. The candidates form a set P in one reduced progression with √U < p ≤ U. Removing failed twists

First remove primes for which some such character has a zero in the box of height H. Choose one offending character for each removed prime. Its conductor determines the prime uniquely. Indeed if fp = f′p′, primality forces p to divide f′ or p′. The first possibility contradicts 0 < f′ ≤ F < p, so p = p′. Making both choices at the same threshold

Take τ = ε/2, η = 1/2 and work at base modulus N₀ = 8M. In the first progression take U = exp z, F = N₀ and the primitive characters inducing the characters modulo N₀. At least half of a nonempty set of primes survives. Choose any surviving q. For the second progression take U = exp z′, F = N₀ exp z, and the primitive characters inducing ξ and ξ(·/q) for all ξ modulo N₀. Whatever q was chosen, this family has at most twice the number of characters modulo N₀. The second-stage theorem verifies the modulus and conductor bounds uniformly in q. In particular q > (YA)400 for large Y, while the other odd prime factors of B are at most z. At least half of this second nonempty set also survives. The two thresholds were fixed before choosing either prime. The first selection controls the twist by q alone. The second controls the twist by d and the twist by both primes. For a real base character of conductor dividing 8M, change of level places it in the same family, hence the same rectangle excludes all zeros for the induced primitive character, with height 8M exp(z + jz′) if j records the presence of d. For a base character modulo M these estimates bound the centered sum by y1/2+ε/2. The logarithmic derivative at one satisfies the sharper bound 4 log y. The source first proves 1000 log log H + C from the zero-free box and then uses log H ≤ 3Yε/5(log Y)². The leading coefficient in units of log Y is at most 1000/(5 · 52) = 50/13 < 4, so the log log Y and constant terms fit in the remaining margin for large Y.
