How a compact part of the cone supplies a partition everywhere

Let L be totally complex, Γ = GLN(𝒪L), and h a positive Hermitian form at each infinite place. Write qh(v) for the sum of the local quadratic forms. The minimum λ(h) is the least value on a nonzero integral vector, a squared length. Write λ∨ for the corresponding minimum of the dual form. The partition must work even where one of these minima becomes small. It sends each input equivariantly to a region where both minima are bounded below, normalizes a smooth bump there, and pulls the resulting weights back to the input. Finding compact representatives. Suppose λ(h), λ∨(h) ≥ c > 0. The transference estimate gives a constant C such that every real vector z has a lattice point v with qh(z−v) ≤ ρ = C/c. The points with qh(v) ≤ 10ρ span LN. To prove this, suppose a nonzero L-linear functional kills them all. Use its coefficients at the infinite places and the inverse matrices of h to choose a real vector z orthogonal to every lattice point in the kernel. Scale it to qh(z) = 4ρ. A lattice point v within squared distance ρ cannot lie in that kernel, since orthogonality would give squared distance at least 4ρ. But the parallelogram identity gives

qh(v) + qh(2z−v) = 2qh(z) + 2qh(z−v) ≤ 10ρ.

This puts v among the short points the functional kills, a contradiction. Take N independent short columns as a matrix A. These columns form a basis over L, but the integral lattice they generate can have index greater than one. Hadamard’s determinant bound at each place and a lower bound for the product of the determinants of h give a uniform bound on |NormL/ℚ(det A)|. Integral changes of coordinates then put A on a finite list of matrices B. For each B the defining conditions qh(v) ≥ c for every nonzero lattice vector and qh(Bj) ≤ 10ρ for every column are closed. They bound all entries of the Gram matrices in those columns and keep the form positive. Each such set is compact, and their finite union K meets every orbit in the region. Dividing by the orbit sum. On a compact neighborhood K′ of K inside the cone, choose a nonnegative smooth bump b supported in K′ and scaled to be at least 1 on K. Form S(k) = ∑γ∈Γ b(γ⁻¹k). Only finitely many terms occur near any positive k. In fact, if a matrix and its translate both lie in a fixed compact set, the columns of γ⁻¹ belong to a fixed finite set of lattice points. Reindexing makes S invariant. Choose a smooth function g with g(s) ≥ ½ everywhere and g(s) = s for s ≥ 1. Then ψ = b/g(S) is smooth, even where S = 0. Whenever an orbit meets K, one bump is at least 1, so its orbit sum is large enough for the exact normalization

∑γ∈Γ ψ(γ⁻¹k) = S(k)/g(S(k)) = 1.

To reach these orbits from any h, first remove its common scale by multiplying it by [∏w(det hw)mult(w)]−1/(N[L:ℚ]). Call the result h₀. Next take a smooth arithmetic-invariant approximate dual minimum μ with λ∨/4 ≤ μ ≤ 4λ∨. The source adds positive semidefinite corrections to μ(h₀)h₀. At each scale s = 4j the correction averages outer products of h0,wvw over short lattice vectors, with smooth cutoff and a factor s−2. The corrected form F(h) has both minima bounded below uniformly in h. Thus the translated weights ψ(γ⁻¹F(h)) sum to one on the whole cone. The pulled-back support can extend towards the cusp. Equivariance and the compact support of ψ still bound the possible relative labels of overlapping translates by a finite set. Why the exponent is one. Write H(h) = 1/(λ(h)λ∨(h)). Common rescaling preserves H. A perturbation of relative size δ changes every quadratic value by at most δ times that value. The normalization h ↦ h₀ increases this relative error by at most a factor 3 for δ ≤ ¼. For the averaged outer product at scale s, the change is bounded by a constant times δqh₀(z)/s in each test direction z. The active scales for two nearby inputs lie above 3λ(h₀)/64. Summing their reciprocals as a geometric progression costs at most 256/[9λ(h₀)]. To measure this error relative to the output, use qF(h) ≥ μ(h₀)qh₀ and μ ≥ λ∨/4. The reciprocal product of the two minima is exactly H. Including the variations of μ and of the cutoffs gives a total relative error ≤ C(1+H)δ for sufficiently small δ. The bump is Lipschitz for this relative error. Passing to derivatives in the moving frame costs a further factor N, and the positive lower bound H ≥ c₀ gives 1+H ≤ (1+1/c₀)H. The multiplicative constants may depend on L and N. The exponent a = 1 works in every rank N ≥ 1. For L = ℚ(i) and N = 1, write h = [t], t > 0. Then qh(z) = t|z|² on ℤ[i], λ = t, λ∨ = 1/t, and H = 1. The determinant product is t², so normalization sends every t to 1. All four units ±1, ±i fix every Hermitian scalar. Their four translated partition values are therefore equal, and the orbit sum forces each value to be ¼.
